The Social Security Solvency and Fairness Act of 2027

A neighbour's draft bill, written from across the border, to stop the automatic 22 per cent cut projected for Social Security in late 2032. It asks something of workers, employers and higher earners, protects everyone already retired or within about five years of it, strengthens the floor for low earners, and leaves the retirement age at 67.

Drafted by Stephen Gordon, CanadianSnowmanSays, with Claude. Not legislative counsel; figures are estimates, not an official score.

Where it stands. The 120th Congress convenes on January 3, 2027, with Democratic majorities in both chambers and a Republican president holding the veto. Neither side can pass a Social Security bill without the other. This draft is written for exactly that arrangement, the same one that produced the 1983 rescue.

What each side gets, and what each gives up

Republicans

Get
  • Slower benefit growth for higher earners (progressive price indexing, endorsed by President George W. Bush in 2005)
  • The Cassidy–Kaine-style investment fund
  • A commission with expedited procedures, the approach of the TRUST Act
  • A benefit credit that keeps the earned-insurance principle
Give up
  • Two gradual tax increases: a higher ceiling and a higher rate
  • A retirement-age increase

Democrats

Get
  • A much higher taxable ceiling
  • A real minimum benefit and caregiver credits, drawn from the Social Security 2100 Act
  • No increase in the retirement age
  • Benefit changes confined to the top 40 per cent of earners
Give up
  • Progressive price indexing, a real reduction for upper-middle earners
  • An investment fund many economists doubt
  • A commission some advocates see as a route to cuts

Full text of the draft bill

120th Congress · 1st Session
A Bill

To amend title II of the Social Security Act and the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 to restore long-range solvency to the Social Security trust funds, to protect current and near-term beneficiaries, to strengthen the minimum benefit, to establish a supplementary investment fund, and for other purposes.

Be it enacted by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled,

Sec. 1Short title; table of contents

(a) SHORT TITLE. This Act may be cited as the "Social Security Solvency and Fairness Act of 2027".

(b) TABLE OF CONTENTS. Sec. 1. Short title; table of contents. Sec. 2. Findings. Sec. 3. Protection of current and near-term beneficiaries. Title I: Restoring the revenue base (Secs. 101–103). Title II: Fairer benefits (Secs. 201–203). Title III: Social Security Supplementary Investment Fund (Secs. 301–302). Title IV: Solvency safeguard (Secs. 401–402).

Sec. 2Findings

Congress finds the following:

(1) The 2026 Annual Report of the Board of Trustees of the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance and Federal Disability Insurance Trust Funds projects that the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund will be depleted in the fourth quarter of 2032, after which continuing income would be sufficient to pay only about 78 per cent of scheduled benefits.

(2) The same report projects a 75-year actuarial deficit of 4.42 per cent of taxable payroll.

(3) In 1983 about 90 per cent of covered earnings fell below the contribution and benefit base. Because earnings at the top of the distribution have grown faster than average earnings, that share has since fallen to about 82 per cent.

(4) Life expectancy at older ages differs substantially by lifetime income, so that an increase in the retirement age falls most heavily on workers with the lowest earnings and the shortest retirements.

(5) The Social Security Amendments of 1983, enacted with the support of both parties on the recommendation of a bipartisan commission, secured the programme for half a century and demonstrate that shared, gradual and clearly scheduled changes are achievable.

(6) Inaction is itself a decision, producing an immediate and indiscriminate reduction in benefits for every beneficiary.

Sec. 3Protection of current and near-term beneficiaries

(a) HOLD HARMLESS. Notwithstanding any other provision of this Act, no amendment made by this Act, and no provision of any bill reported under section 401, shall reduce the monthly benefit payable under title II of the Social Security Act to, or on the basis of the wages and self-employment income of, any individual who:

(1) is entitled to a benefit under such title on the date of enactment of this Act; or

(2) attains age 57 on or before December 31, 2027.

(b) DEPENDANTS AND SURVIVORS. The protection in subsection (a) extends to every benefit payable to a spouse, divorced spouse, child or survivor on the basis of the record of an individual described in subsection (a).

(c) RULE OF CONSTRUCTION. Nothing in this section limits any increase in benefits under this Act or affects the application of cost-of-living adjustments under section 215(i) of the Social Security Act.

Title I: Restoring the revenue base
Sec. 101Restoring the 90 per cent contribution and benefit base

(a) IN GENERAL. Section 230 of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 430) is amended by adding at the end the following:

"(e) PHASED RESTORATION. (1) For each calendar year from 2028 through 2037, the contribution and benefit base shall be the greater of the amount determined under subsection (b) and the amount determined under paragraph (2).

"(2) The amount determined under this paragraph for a calendar year is the amount determined under subsection (b) for that year, plus the product of (A) one-tenth of the difference between the target base and the amount determined under subsection (b), and (B) the number of years elapsed since 2027.

"(3) The target base is the amount that the Chief Actuary of the Social Security Administration determines would cause 90 per cent of all wages and self-employment income in covered employment to fall below the contribution and benefit base.

"(4) For calendar years after 2037, the contribution and benefit base shall be the amount that the Chief Actuary determines maintains the 90 per cent share described in paragraph (3)."

(b) CONFORMING AMENDMENTS. Sections 3121(a)(1) and 1402(b)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986 shall apply using the contribution and benefit base as determined under subsection (e) of section 230 of the Social Security Act.

(c) EFFECTIVE DATE. The amendments made by this section apply to remuneration paid, and taxable years beginning, after December 31, 2027.

Sec. 102Modest benefit credit for earnings above the former base

(a) IN GENERAL. Section 215(a)(1)(A) of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 415(a)(1)(A)) is amended so that, in computing the primary insurance amount, the portion of an individual's average indexed monthly earnings attributable to earnings above the base that would have applied under section 230(b) without regard to section 230(e) is credited at a rate of 5 per cent, in place of the rates otherwise applicable.

(b) EFFECTIVE DATE. The amendment made by this section applies to earnings credited for calendar years after 2027.

Title II: Fairer benefits
Sec. 201Progressive price indexing for higher earners

(a) NEW BEND POINT. Section 215(a)(1)(B) of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 415(a)(1)(B)) is amended by establishing, for individuals who initially become eligible for old-age or disability insurance benefits in 2033 or later, an additional bend point equal to the average indexed monthly earnings at the 60th percentile of newly retired workers, as determined by the Chief Actuary.

(b) PROTECTION BELOW THE BEND POINT. For average indexed monthly earnings at or below the bend point established under subsection (a), the primary insurance amount shall be computed exactly as under current law, including wage indexing of the existing bend points.

(c) SLOWER GROWTH ABOVE THE BEND POINT. For average indexed monthly earnings above that bend point, the 32 and 15 per cent factors in section 215(a)(1)(A) shall be reduced for each successive cohort so that the primary insurance amount of a worker with average indexed monthly earnings equal to the contribution and benefit base, as determined without regard to section 230(e), grows from cohort to cohort with the Consumer Price Index rather than with the national average wage index.

(d) EFFECTIVE DATE. This section applies to individuals who initially become eligible for benefits after December 31, 2032, subject to section 3.

Sec. 202Enhanced minimum benefit with caregiver credits

(a) IN GENERAL. Section 215(a)(1)(C) of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 415(a)(1)(C)) is amended to read as follows:

"(C)(i) For an individual with 30 or more years of coverage, the primary insurance amount shall be not less than one-twelfth of 125 per cent of the annual poverty guideline for a single individual, as published by the Secretary of Health and Human Services for the year in which the individual initially becomes eligible.

"(ii) For an individual with more than 10 but fewer than 30 years of coverage, the minimum amount under clause (i) shall be reduced proportionately for each year of coverage fewer than 30.

"(iii) For purposes of this subparagraph, a year of coverage is any year in which the individual is credited with four quarters of coverage.

"(iv) An individual shall be credited with a caregiving year, counted as a year of coverage for this subparagraph only, for any year in which the individual was the primary caregiver for a child under age 6, or provided at least 960 hours of care to a family member who is disabled or aged 65 or over, as certified under regulations prescribed by the Commissioner. Not more than five caregiving years may be credited to any individual.

"(v) For individuals who initially become eligible after 2028, the amount in clause (i) shall be increased by the national average wage index."

(b) EFFECTIVE DATE. The amendment made by this section applies to individuals who initially become eligible for benefits after December 31, 2027.

Sec. 203Preservation of the retirement age

(a) NO INCREASE. Nothing in this Act amends the definition of retirement age in section 216(l) of the Social Security Act (42 U.S.C. 416(l)), which shall remain 67 for individuals attaining age 62 after 2021.

(b) SENSE OF CONGRESS. It is the sense of Congress that, because of the substantial differences in life expectancy by lifetime income, an across-the-board increase in the retirement age would place a disproportionate share of any reduction on workers with the lowest earnings and shortest retirements, and that solvency should be restored by other means.

Title III: Social Security Supplementary Investment Fund
Sec. 301Establishment, capitalisation and governance

(a) ESTABLISHMENT. There is established in the Treasury of the United States the Social Security Supplementary Investment Fund (in this title referred to as the "Fund"), separate from, and in addition to, the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund and the Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund.

(b) CAPITALISATION. There is appropriated to the Fund, out of any money in the Treasury not otherwise appropriated, $300,000,000,000 for each of fiscal years 2028 through 2032. The Secretary of the Treasury shall report the cost of this capitalisation, including the associated federal borrowing, in each annual budget submission.

(c) BOARD. The Fund shall be governed by the Social Security Investment Board, consisting of seven members:

(1) six members appointed by the President, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate, of whom one each shall be selected from lists submitted by the Speaker of the House of Representatives, the minority leader of the House, the majority leader of the Senate and the minority leader of the Senate, and two shall be selected after consultation with all four; and

(2) a chair appointed by the President, by and with the advice and consent of the Senate, after consultation with the four leaders named in paragraph (1).

(d) QUALIFICATIONS AND TERMS. Members shall have substantial experience in the management of large investment portfolios, shall serve staggered terms of seven years, may not hold any other federal office, and may be removed only for cause.

(e) FIDUCIARY DUTY. Members and employees of the Board shall act solely in the interest of Social Security beneficiaries, under fiduciary standards equivalent to those governing the Federal Retirement Thrift Investment Board under section 8477 of title 5, United States Code.

Sec. 302Investment, transfers and safeguards

(a) INVESTMENT POLICY. The Board shall invest the Fund in a diversified portfolio of broad market index funds, including domestic and international equities and fixed-income securities, through external managers selected by competitive process. Neither the Board nor any officer of the United States may direct the selection of individual securities.

(b) PROXY VOTING. Proxies shall be voted by external managers under standards published in advance, and no officer of the United States may direct any vote.

(c) TRANSFERS. Beginning in fiscal year 2043, the Board shall transfer to the Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund and the Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund, in proportion to their respective costs, an amount each year equal to 4 per cent of the average value of the Fund over the preceding five fiscal years.

(d) NO OTHER USE. Amounts in the Fund may not be appropriated, transferred, pledged or used for any purpose other than those described in subsection (c), and the Fund shall not be counted toward any other federal programme.

(e) ACCOUNTABILITY. The Fund shall be audited annually by an independent auditor and by the Comptroller General, and each audit, together with the Fund's holdings, costs and returns, shall be published online.

(f) BENEFITS NOT AFFECTED. Investment gains or losses of the Fund shall not affect the amount of any benefit payable under title II of the Social Security Act.

Title IV: Solvency safeguard
Sec. 401Solvency trigger and bipartisan commission

(a) TRIGGER. If any annual report of the Board of Trustees issued after the date of enactment of this Act projects, under intermediate assumptions, that the combined Federal Old-Age and Survivors Insurance Trust Fund and Federal Disability Insurance Trust Fund will be depleted within 10 years of the date of the report, the Chief Actuary shall so certify to Congress within 30 days.

(b) COMMISSION. Within 60 days of a certification under subsection (a), there shall be convened a National Commission on Social Security Solvency of 12 members, three appointed by each of the Speaker of the House of Representatives, the minority leader of the House, the majority leader of the Senate and the minority leader of the Senate. The Chief Actuary shall serve as a non-voting adviser.

(c) HEARINGS. The Commission shall hold public hearings and publish all actuarial analyses on which it relies.

(d) REPORT. Within 180 days of convening, the Commission shall report a bill that, as certified by the Chief Actuary, restores 75-year actuarial balance. The bill may be reported only with the approval of at least 8 members, including at least 3 appointed by leaders of each party.

(e) LIMITATIONS. A bill reported under this section:

(1) shall comply with section 3; and

(2) shall not provide for an across-the-board increase in the retirement age under section 216(l) of the Social Security Act.

Sec. 402Expedited congressional consideration

(a) INTRODUCTION. A bill reported under section 401 shall be introduced in each House by the majority and minority leaders, by request, within 3 legislative days.

(b) DISCHARGE. If a committee has not reported the bill within 15 legislative days of introduction, it shall be discharged.

(c) NO AMENDMENTS. No amendment to the bill shall be in order in either House.

(d) DEBATE AND VOTE. Debate in the Senate shall be limited to 20 hours, and debate in the House to 10 hours, equally divided between the parties. A vote on final passage shall occur immediately after the close of debate, and the bill shall pass on the affirmative vote of a majority of members voting.

(e) RULEMAKING POWER. This section is enacted as an exercise of the rulemaking power of each House, with full recognition of the right of either House to change its rules at any time.
