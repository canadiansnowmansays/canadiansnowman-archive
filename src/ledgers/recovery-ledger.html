How to read the levers

A Democratic Congress can pass almost anything and enact very little on its own. Standalone bills meet a veto unless they win Republican votes. That makes five levers matter, roughly in this order:

  • Appropriations: funding levels and binding riders in the Labor-HHS bill, which the president has to sign to keep government open.
  • Oversight: hearings, subpoenas, Inspector General and GAO referrals. Needs no signature.
  • Bipartisan bill: where MAHA Republicans and Democrats already agree, a veto-proof margin is conceivable.
  • Courts: suits already running, such as AAP v. Kennedy, which has frozen the January 2026 schedule.
  • States: governors and legislatures acting where Washington will not.
