Where they went

The gain column: programmes recruiting researchers leaving the United States, with named recruits where the reporting gives them.

The Times's Canada line, reconciled. Ottawa's official $1.7 billion programme targets more than 1,000 researchers over 12 years; the $1 billion figure is only its research-chairs strand, which funds about 100 chairs. The Times attached one number to the other. Separately, some outlets call the package $1.2 billion; no official source supports that.
No one has a reliable count of scientists who have left the United States. The 10,109 figure counts STEM PhDs who left federal jobs, not the country. The closest real departure data: physics PhD graduates leaving the US at the highest rate in 30 years, 13% of surveyed NIH-funded researchers saying they lost lab staff to other countries, and a survey showing young biomedical researchers' plans to stay in the US falling from 93% to 72% in six months. In a 2025 Nature poll of 1,600 US-based scientists, 75% said they were considering leaving.

The Vought Gap

What the White House asked for, what Congress gave, and what is actually reaching researchers. Congress refused the deep cuts for fiscal 2026. The gap opens in the last column.

AgencyFY26 requestFY26 enactedWhat is actually happeningFY27 request
Status: No fiscal 2027 science bills have passed. A stopgap law signed 2 Sep 2026 holds fiscal 2026 levels until 11 Dec 2026, and it also bars the White House from finalising its grant-rule overhaul before then. After the 3 Nov midterms: Democrats won control of both chambers from January: at least 54 Senate seats, and a large House majority (CNN projects over 250 seats). In Maine, Susan Collins, chair of Senate Appropriations, conceded to Troy Jackson on 3 Nov. The outgoing Congress returns 9 Nov and must pass funding before the stopgap expires; the grant-rule block lapses with it unless renewed. First Senate FY27 science bill: on 12 Nov Senate appropriators released their Commerce-Justice-Science bill, giving NASA science $7.43B, against $7.25B enacted for FY26, $6.0B in the House committee bill and $3.89B requested. It has not had a committee vote.

Payoff horizon

How long past federal research took to pay off, and when today's cut work would have paid off on the same clock. Wartime urgency squeezed ENIAC to three years. In peacetime the lags below run from 13 to 46 years, typically about 25, which puts today's missing payoffs between roughly 2038 and 2071.
