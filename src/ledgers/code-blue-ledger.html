The American Health Security Act of 2027

A neighbour's draft of a U.S. health bill, rebuilt after review. It takes the steps that have already won support from both parties, pays for them with savings the Congressional Budget Office has scored, and keeps universal coverage as the long-term goal. The bigger reforms are set out separately, with their costs, under "Going further".

Drafted by Stephen Gordon, CanadianSnowmanSays, with Claude. Not legislative counsel; all figures are estimates from scores of similar provisions, not an official score of this bill.

Why this was revised. The version published on December 18, 2025 claimed to be "10 titles, 52 sections, fully scored". It had 5 titles and 22 sections, had never been scored, and its arithmetic did not add up: it counted savings to employers and households as federal deficit reduction, cited the wrong Swiss and German statutes, and listed senators as sponsors who had never agreed. This ledger replaces it. What changed, section by section, is set out below.

What each side gets, and what each gives up

Republicans

Get
  • Site-neutral payment and price transparency, both long championed by Republican health leaders
  • A bill paid for with spending savings, not tax increases
  • No public option, no mandate, no all-payer price controls
  • A pay-as-you-go safeguard on new spending
Give up
  • Three more years of the enhanced subsidies they let expire
  • Restoring the Medicaid expansion incentive their 2025 law repealed

Democrats

Get
  • The subsidy extension the House passed 230–196 in January 2026
  • A renewed push for Medicaid expansion
  • An end to surprise ambulance bills
  • More doctors in underserved areas
Give up
  • The public option, Medicare at 60 and a $2,000 cap, moved to "Going further"
  • New taxes on high earners, also moved there
  • A large payment cut to hospitals, which many of their members' hospitals oppose

Section-by-section outline

This is an outline for legislative counsel, not finished statutory text. Each section names the law it would amend where that is known with confidence.

Title I · Keeping people covered
Sec. 101Restore the enhanced premium tax credit, 2027–2029
Does
Re-enacts the 2021–2025 subsidy schedule for plan years 2027–2029: no one pays more than 8.5% of income for a benchmark plan, and the lowest-income enrollees pay nothing. A minimum monthly premium and income ceiling, as in the Senate's stalled two-year framework, could lower the cost.
Amends
Internal Revenue Code §36B(b)(3)(A)(iii).
Estimate
About +$80.6B over ten years; +3.0M insured in 2027 and +4.0M in 2028. Proxy: CBO's estimate for H.R. 1834 (three years, 2026–2028), January 8, 2026.
Status
The enhanced credits expired December 31, 2025. The House passed H.R. 1834 on January 8, 2026; the Senate has not voted. As of October 2026, no extension has become law.
Sec. 102Restore the Medicaid expansion incentive
Does
For states that newly expand Medicaid, raises the federal match on their regular Medicaid population by 5 points for two years, as the American Rescue Plan did before the 2025 reconciliation law repealed it.
Amends
Social Security Act §1905, restoring subsection (ii), added by ARPA §9814 and repealed by the 2025 reconciliation law (§71114) effective January 1, 2026.
Estimate
About +$14.0B; about +100,000 insured, depending on states choosing to expand. Proxy: the mirror image of CBO's score of the repeal.
Sec. 103Simpler enrolment
Does
Lets marketplaces pre-fill applications with IRS and Social Security data, with consent, and funds a multi-state demonstration that enrols SNAP and unemployment recipients in a $0-premium plan unless they opt out.
Amends
ACA §1411 (42 U.S.C. 18081); IRC §6103 for data sharing.
Estimate
Not scored. Automatic enrolment raises subsidy spending, so this must be scored before it counts.
Title II · Honest prices
Sec. 201Price transparency, codified
Does
Puts the hospital and insurer price-transparency rules into statute, extends them to labs, imaging and ambulatory surgery centres, requires insurers to publish claims-denial, prior-authorisation and overhead data, and raises penalties. Follows the Lower Costs, More Transparency Act (H.R. 9393), reported 45–0 by the House Energy and Commerce Committee on July 21, 2026.
Amends
Public Health Service Act §2718(e) and §2715A; ERISA §715; IRC §9815.
Estimate
About −$2.2B (upper bound). Proxy: CBO's score of the same provision in the 2023 version, H.R. 5378 §106.
Sec. 202Site-neutral Medicare payment
Does
Pays hospital outpatient departments the physician-office rate for services commonly and safely provided in a doctor's office, phased in over four years. Rural sole community hospitals and critical access hospitals are exempt. Does not duplicate CMS's 2026 rule on drug administration.
Amends
Social Security Act §1833(t).
Estimate
Up to −$156.9B. Source: CBO budget option "Reduce Payments for Hospital Outpatient Departments", December 12, 2024. A 2028 start saves less within the window.
Note
This is the bill's main offset and its hardest vote. Hospitals oppose it strongly.
Sec. 203End surprise ambulance bills
Does
Brings ground ambulances under the No Surprises Act, caps patient cost-sharing at in-network levels, and uses state-set rates where they exist, as recommended by the federal Advisory Committee on Ground Ambulance and Patient Billing. Existing contracts are grandfathered for 24 months.
Amends
Public Health Service Act title XXVII, part D, with parallel ERISA and IRC amendments.
Estimate
Not scored. Likely small federal effect.
Title III · Drug prices and pharmacy benefit managers
Sec. 301Ban spread pricing in Medicaid
Does
Requires Medicaid managed-care pharmacy benefit managers to pass through what the pharmacy is paid, plus a fixed administrative fee. This piece was dropped from the PBM reforms enacted February 3, 2026.
Amends
Social Security Act title XIX (subsection to confirm).
Estimate
About −$1.1B. Proxy: CBO's score of H.R. 5378 §202 (2023).
Sec. 302Protect the drug negotiation schedule
Does
Makes no change to the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program (10 drugs in 2026, 15 in 2027, 15 in 2028, then up to 20 a year), and asks GAO to report on the 2025 law's widened orphan-drug exclusion.
Estimate
None.
Title IV · Workforce
Sec. 40114,000 new residency positions
Does
Adds 14,000 Medicare-funded graduate medical education positions over seven years, prioritising rural, underserved and primary-care programmes, following the Resident Physician Shortage Reduction Act.
Amends
Social Security Act §1886(h) and §1886(d)(5)(B).
Estimate
About +$25.2B (rough, not scored): CMS's estimate of $1.8B per 1,000 positions over ten years, scaled by us.
Title V · Accountability
Sec. 501Public reporting
Does
HHS publishes an annual report and public dashboard on coverage by state, age and income; prices; premiums; and the effects of the 2025 Medicaid work requirements and eligibility checks.
Sec. 502Pay-as-you-go safeguard
Does
Sections 101 and 401 take effect only if CBO certifies that Titles II and III, as enacted, offset them over ten years. If not, Section 101 is shortened to two years. Replaces the original's demand for "dynamic scoring"; CBO sets its own methods.

Going further: what more would cost, and how it could be paid for

The core bill is deliberately modest. Universal coverage on the Swiss or German model would take much more. These options are not in the bill; they are here so readers can see the price of ambition honestly.

The foreign models, correctly cited

Switzerland · KVG/LAMal (in force 1996)

  • Art. 3: compulsory health insurance
  • Arts. 24–33: the benefits covered, and the test that care be effective, appropriate and economical
  • Art. 61: how premiums are set
  • Art. 65: cantonal premium reductions for lower-income residents, with a federal contribution under Art. 66

Germany · Social Code Book V

  • §87: the uniform fee schedule for doctors (EBM)
  • §35a: early benefit assessment of new drugs
  • §130b: negotiated reimbursement amounts for new drugs
  • §72, cited in the original, concerns securing physician supply, not prices

Swiss and German article numbers checked October 12, 2026. These laws are models to adapt, not text to copy.

Members who have championed these ideas

None of these members has endorsed this bill. Each has publicly supported one of its components.

  • Premium tax credits: Sens. Lisa Blunt Rochester, Ron Wyden and Maria Cantwell; Sen. Bernie Moreno joined bipartisan talks on a two-year version. Reps. Brian Fitzpatrick, Mike Lawler, Ryan Mackenzie and Rob Bresnahan Jr. signed the discharge petition.
  • Price transparency: Reps. Brett Guthrie and Frank Pallone.
  • Site-neutral payment: Sens. Bill Cassidy (leaving the Senate in January 2027) and Maggie Hassan.
  • Residency positions: Sens. Raphael Warnock and John Boozman; Reps. Terri Sewell and Brian Fitzpatrick.
