WELCOME. The United States Triumphal Arch commemorates the 250th anniversary of the signing of the Declaration of Independence, an anniversary that occurred on 4 July 2026. Excavation began in September 2026. The Arch is expected to open in 2028 or 2029. The National Park Service asks visitors to consider this less a delay and more an extended, contemplative approach to the milestone, in the spirit of the founders, who also took a while to get things properly ratified.

LOCATION. The Arch rises from Memorial Circle, a traffic roundabout on the western end of Arlington Memorial Bridge, sited precisely between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery — a location the Interior Department has described as honouring both the nation’s greatest advocate for reconciliation and its largest concentration of people who did not get one. A pending legal matter concerns whether the Arch obstructs the sightline between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington House; the National Park Service takes no position on this matter and asks that visitors also take no position on this matter, at least not out loud, and not near the excavation crew.

HEIGHT AND DESIGN. The Arch will stand approximately 166 feet before the addition of the winged figure, which will add a further, currently unconfirmed amount. The figure — gilded, torch-bearing, crowned, broadly reminiscent of Lady Liberty but under new management — is flanked by two eagles. An earlier design featured four lions; these were removed at the recommendation of the Commission of Fine Arts, which noted that lions are “not native to the United States,” a standard the Commission has not yet been asked to apply to the eagles, the torch, the crown, or the general concept of a 166-foot triumphal arch, none of which are native to the United States either.

THE OBSERVATION DECK. The Arch’s fifth and uppermost level is designated, in official Interior Department correspondence, the military observation deck, situated approximately 161 feet above ground level. Visitors should be aware that “observation” in this context refers to the viewing of things from the deck, rather than the viewing of things on it, though the Department has not yet finalised guidance on this distinction and the Office reserves the right to update this brochure accordingly. Visitors hoping to observe Arlington National Cemetery, the Potomac River, or the Lincoln Memorial from the deck are encouraged to do so. Visitors hoping to observe artillery are asked to direct that inquiry to the Department of Defense, whose involvement in the deck’s programming remains, as of this printing, undetermined but not, the Office stresses, ruled out.

GETTING THERE. The roadway around Memorial Circle will be narrowed to twenty feet during and after construction, in the interest of pedestrian safety and, the Office notes without further comment, easier crowd management for whatever the observation deck ends up being used for. A barricade wall will surround the Arch’s plaza. Visitors are advised that a monument built to commemorate liberty will, upon completion, be more heavily fortified than most buildings visitors are used to entering freely, and are asked to regard this as consistent with the broader architectural themes of the project rather than as an irony requiring further comment.

INSIDE THE ARCH. Five floors are planned: — Ground level: security screening, lobby, back-of-house operations. — Two mezzanine levels: staff only, not open to the public, contents undisclosed. — Gallery level: exhibit space, with a planned café and gift shop. — Observation deck: see above.

THE GIFT SHOP. Located on the gallery level, the gift shop will offer a curated selection of merchandise reflecting the Arch’s dual commemorative and observational functions, including but not limited to: scale replica pins of the winged figure (lions sold separately, while stocks of the discontinued design last); a commemorative snow globe depicting the Arch as originally designed, with lions, for collectors interested in the road not taken; a “166 Feet and Rising” T-shirt, sizing to be finalised pending final height of the winged figure; and a postcard set featuring the view from the observation deck, contents of view subject to change without notice.

DID YOU KNOW? — The Arch will be taller than the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, a comparison the project’s chief proponent has personally and repeatedly requested visitors make. — When asked in the Oval Office who the Arch was for, the same proponent answered, without elaboration, “Me.” — Granite for the Arch will be sourced from quarries in Vermont, North Carolina, or California — a genuinely bipartisan sourcing decision, the Office notes, whatever else may be said of the project. — The Arch’s construction was announced three days after the Supreme Court ruled, 5–4, that opponents of a separate White House renovation project lacked standing to challenge it in court — a ruling Justice Department lawyers have since cited in seeking dismissal of the lawsuit challenging this Arch as well. The Office declines to speculate on whether this is a coincidence, a pattern, or simply an efficient use of a single favourable precedent, and reminds visitors that speculation of this kind is best conducted well outside Park Service property.

JUNIOR RANGER PROGRAM. A Junior Ranger activity booklet is in development for young visitors, covering the history of triumphal arches, the difference between native and non-native species as determined by the Commission of Fine Arts, and — pending final programming decisions for the fifth floor — an activity page currently marked, in internal drafts, “TBD, ask a ranger, ranger also awaiting guidance.”

HOURS. To be determined. The Arch is not yet open. This brochure will be updated as information becomes available, or as the anniversary it commemorates recedes further into the past, whichever happens first.
