Chapter 1: The Summoning

  1. And it came to pass on the twentieth day of the eleventh month, in the year two thousand and twenty-six, that the President returned from the land of Shenzhen in the great silver bird, which flew fourteen and a half hours without stopping for fuel, and he declared it a record, and it was so.

  2. And he gathered the scribes of the press into the Briefing Room, and they were many, so that he said, Do not tell the fire department.

  3. And he spoke unto them for one hour and twenty minutes, concerning potholes, and asphalt, and data centres, and the Mardi Gras, and the uncle who was at MIT, and many other things, for the President rambleth not; he hath said so.

  4. And in the eighty-first minute the President lifted up his eyes and beheld, standing at the side of the room, a man in a dark suit.

  5. And the President said, Behold, we have the most powerful man in the world in the room right now. Stephen, hast thou anything to say?

  6. And there was a stirring among the Vice President and the Secretary of State, who had until that moment believed themselves to be the second and third most powerful men in the room, and who now did quietly recalculate.

  7. And Stephen came forth, and the chroniclers of CNN did film it.

Chapter 2: The Rushing Wind

  1. And Stephen opened his mouth, and the words came out of him as a mighty rushing wind, three hundred and sixty-seven words in one hundred and twenty-eight seconds, as it is recorded in the scroll of Roll Call’s Factba.se, which counteth all things.

  2. And the stenographers wept, for their fingers could not keep pace, and the machines of speech-to-text did turn his words into tongues no man could interpret, such that “inheriting” became “inheritance”, and the Biden administration became something else entirely.

  3. And Stephen said: What President Trump hath achieved in two years is more than any president hath achieved by far in American history.

  4. And he said: We could stand up here for four hours and go through achievement by achievement by achievement.

  5. And the scribes of the press looked at one another, for they had already stood there for one hour and twenty-one minutes, and their legs were weary, and they did pray silently that he would not.

  6. And he did not. He did it in two minutes, which is the same thing, only faster.

Chapter 3: The Genesis of Deregulation

  1. In the beginning was the Federal Register, and it was without form, and very long.

  2. And the President said, For every rule that is made, let ten be cast out. And it was so.

  3. And the servants of the Office of Management and Budget counted the rules that were cast out, and they numbered them six hundred and forty-six, against five that were made, and they said, Behold, it is one hundred and twenty-nine to one, and it is very good.

  4. But the scribes of George Washington University came and counted again, and they found that the servants had counted in a manner most creative, and that the true number was forty-three to one, as the elders of the Regulatory Studies Center did testify.

  5. And Stephen said, It never happened before in history. Even under Ronald Reagan, regulations increased.

  6. And the spirit of Ronald Reagan, who is in heaven, did look down and say, Well. There you go again.

Chapter 4: The Miracle of the Murder Rate

  1. And Stephen proclaimed: Record low crime, record low murder rate.

  2. And behold, it was true, for the murders had fallen to four and one-tenth among every hundred thousand, the lowest since the days of Eisenhower, when the cars had fins.

  3. And the multitude marvelled, and said, Truly he hath worked a miracle.

  4. But a certain wise man named Asher, who counteth murders, said: The murders had been falling for three years before he came, since the year of the great spike, which was two thousand and twenty-one.

  5. And the multitude said, Who is this Asher? We have never heard of him.

  6. For it is written that whosoever is standing at the podium when the numbers improve, his is the glory; and whosoever is standing at the podium when the numbers worsen, it was the previous fellow.

Chapter 5: The Reactors of Long Ago

  1. And Stephen said: The first new nuclear reactors to go critical since the 1970s.

  2. And the land of Georgia, which is in the south, cleared its throat.

  3. For in the third month of the year two thousand and twenty-three, in the days of the previous king, a great reactor at Vogtle did split its first atoms, and the scribes of Fortune did record it; and its twin followed after; and before them, in the year two thousand and sixteen, a reactor at Watts Bar in Tennessee.

  4. But these were blotted from the book, and the book was made shorter, as is the custom.

  5. Now it is true that in the summer of two thousand and twenty-six, four small reactors at the laboratories in the land of Idaho did go critical, at zero power, by the fourth day of the seventh month, as the President had commanded.

  6. And this was a good work, but a small one, and so Stephen did move it back fifty years, that it might seem larger, as a fisherman doth hold his fish closer to the camera.

Chapter 6: The Loaves and the Barrels

  1. And Stephen said: He took over Venezuela in less than an hour.

  2. And this was nearly so, for the Secretary of Energy did say it took thirty minutes, on the third day of the first month, when Maduro was carried off in the night.

  3. And Stephen said: Doubling U.S. oil reserves overnight.

  4. And this was a miracle greater than the loaves and the fishes, for the loaves and the fishes were at least eaten by the people who received them.

  5. For the barrels were not added overnight, but came forth nearly eight months later, on the twenty-eighth day of the eighth month, when the President proclaimed upon Truth Social that the nation now controlled sixty-five billion barrels in Venezuela, at no cost to the taxpayer, through a partnership whose particulars no man could discover.

  6. And the barrels remained in the ground, in another country.

  7. And in that same season the great storehouse of the nation, which is called the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, was emptied by more than one hundred million barrels, to soothe the price of gasoline in a time of war, of which we shall not speak.

  8. So that the reserves were doubled in Venezuela, and diminished in Louisiana and Texas, and this is called the New Arithmetic.

Chapter 7: The Plague That Was Inherited

  1. And Stephen said: He inherited twenty-plus percent year-over-year inflation from the Biden administration, and defeated it.

  2. Now the plague of inflation did rise in the sixth month of the year two thousand and twenty-two to nine and one-tenth percent, and this was grievous.

  3. But in the first month of two thousand and twenty-five, when the President came again into his kingdom, it was three percent, and no more.

  4. And the scribes of FactCheck.org did search all the records of the nation, and found that the inflation had reached twenty-three and seven-tenths percent only once, in the year nineteen hundred and twenty, when Warren Harding was about to take office and the Biden administration was not yet even a twinkle.

  5. And as for the defeat of the plague: in the ninth month of two thousand and twenty-six it stood at three and seven-tenths percent, which is higher than three, as the children learn in the second grade.

  6. And the price of gasoline was up by more than a third over the year, because of a war, of which we shall not speak.

Chapter 8: The Wages That Grew Fastest of All

  1. And Stephen said: The fastest wage growth in American history.

  2. And the workers of the land looked at their pay stubs, and then at the price of eggs, and then at their pay stubs again.

  3. For it was written by the scribes of the Associated Press that the wages, once the rising prices were counted, had fallen for six months in a row.

  4. And the workers said, If this be the fastest growth in history, we should not like to see the slowest.

  5. And they went unto the polling stations on the third day of the eleventh month, and they made their feelings known, as shall be told.

Chapter 9: The Seventh Day

  1. Thus in two years he deregulated the regulations, and secured the border, and deported the multitudes, and lowered the murders, and raised up the coal, and split the atom, and rebuilt the armies, and smote the boats upon the waters, and took Venezuela before lunch, and made peace with the Bear and the Dragon at the same time.

  2. And on the seventh day he rested.

  3. And he sat upon his throne in the Oval Office, which he had made golden, and he sent for a Big Mac, and two Filet-O-Fish, and a large chocolate shake.

  4. And he looked upon the Big Mac, and he saw that it was good.

  5. Indeed he said it was the best Big Mac in the history of Big Macs, possibly in the history of the world, and that people were saying so, big strong men with tears in their eyes, and that nobody had ever seen a Big Mac like it.

  6. And the Big Mac said nothing, for it was a sandwich, and knew its place.

  7. And Stephen stood beside the throne, and he too said that it was the greatest Big Mac in American history, and that under the President’s leadership the next Big Mac would be greater still.

  8. And the President was well pleased, and said unto the Vice President, He is better than you.

Chapter 10: The Chapter That Is Missing

  1. Now there was another chapter in this book.

  2. It told of the twenty-eighth day of the second month, when the nation went to war with Iran.

  3. And how the price of gasoline rose, and the storehouse was emptied, and the people grew weary of it, so that six in ten of them did tell the pollsters of the AP Voter Poll that the war was the wrong decision.

  4. And how on the fourth day of the eleventh month, the morning after the great defeat, the Vice President did write upon the scroll called X: The reason we lost is the war, as the chroniclers of Axios did record.

  5. And how in the Briefing Room, in that same hour before Stephen spoke, the President himself was asked of this, and said: It was a very accurate statement.

  6. But when the Book of Stephen was read aloud to the multitude, this chapter was not in it.

  7. It was not listed among the triumphs, nor among the burdens, nor among the footnotes.

  8. And scholars have searched the scroll of Factba.se, all fifteen thousand nine hundred and six words of it, and they have found the war in the President’s mouth, and in the Vice President’s mouth, and in the mouths of the scribes of the press; but in the three hundred and sixty-seven words of Stephen, it is nowhere.

  9. And the Vice President stood behind him the whole time, and held his peace, and smiled, for it is written that a wise man knoweth when he is not in the book.

Chapter 11: Revelation

  1. And Stephen prophesied, saying: The next two years are going to be the greatest two years in American history.

  2. And he did not mention that both houses of Congress had passed into the hands of the other tribe, seventeen days before.

  3. Nor that the President’s approval had fallen to thirty-two in the hundred, the lowest of all his days.

  4. Nor that the counsel of the White House had sent forth an edict that no servant of the government may speak unto Congress without his permission, for the investigations were coming, as the rains come in November.

  5. But the President himself did reveal the shape of the coming kingdom, saying of the new Congress: They are going to send things my way and I am going to veto them, and they could go two years and one month, and I am going to veto them.

  6. And this is the greatest two years in American history: a man with a pen, and a Congress with a subpoena, and a chief of staff with a list.

  7. And when Stephen had finished, the President said: We have breaking news. Do you want to run for president?

  8. And then the President turned to the UFC fight that had been held upon the White House lawn, and to the military musicians who were to receive money upon the new helipad at two o’clock, with hors d’oeuvres, to which the press was invited.

  9. And in the last minutes, as the scribes cried out their questions, the President spoke once more of Stephen, and said unto the multitude: Forget about his, uh, little statement.

  10. And so the Gospel was given its first commentary by the Lord himself, and the commentary was: Forget it.

Benediction

Here endeth the reading.

The people of the land, it should be recorded, had already heard the Book of Stephen. They had been hearing it, chapter and verse, for two years. On the third day of the eleventh month they closed it, and they put it back on the shelf, and they went home.

The Big Mac, for its part, has declined to comment.
