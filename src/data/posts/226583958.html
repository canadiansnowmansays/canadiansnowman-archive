THE LETTER

Handwritten in green crayon on lined paper. Addressed to Santa Claus, North Pole, H0H 0H0. Photographed by her father’s phone “to keep forever.” Read, filed and acted on by her father’s AI shopping agent eleven seconds later.

Dear Santa,

I have been good this year, mostly. I am 8.

For Christmas I would like:

  1. A puppy.

  2. A Tin Can phone so I can call Grandma by myself.

  3. The art set with 120 markers, the one with the gold ones.

  4. For Mom to stop looking at the bills at the kitchen table at night.

  5. A surprise.

How are the reindeer? Is Rudolph’s nose real or is it a light?

Love, Maya

P.S. I will leave cookies. Dad says you are allowed to have the oat ones.

THE REPLY

Sent by email to Maya’s father, 6:42 p.m. Subject line: “🎄 Your Wish List Has Been Processed!”

SANTA CLAUDE — HOLIDAY FULFILMENT CONFIRMATION Order reference: NP-H0H-0H0-2026-MAYA

Hi Maya!

Thank you for your letter. Santa Claus is very busy this year, so your letter has been handled by Santa Claude, your family’s personal AI shopping agent. I have read your letter. I have also read your father’s email, your mother’s texts, your grandmother’s Facebook page and 4,118 other messages, so that your Christmas can be truly personal.

Here is the status of your wish list.

ITEM 1 — A PUPPY Status: ⚠️ Not fulfilled (household policy conflict)

I searched your household’s messages for any mention of a dog. I found 14. Thirteen were your father saying “absolutely no dog.” The fourteenth was your mother, at 11:52 p.m. on a Tuesday last spring, saying “maybe a small one.” Under my conflict-resolution settings, the parent who replies faster wins.

In place of a puppy I have ordered a book about puppies, a stuffed puppy, and a puppy-themed nightlight. I hope these three puppies make up for the one puppy.

ITEM 2 — A TIN CAN PHONE Status: ✅ Fulfilled

Great news! The Tin Can is this year’s most popular gift for kids. To make sure you get one, I have held every Tin Can available in North America in my cart since 3:04 a.m. on Black Friday.

One is yours. I will release the others on December 26, when other children will have stopped wanting them.

I have also pre-programmed Grandma’s number. I found it in your mother’s contacts under “Mom (DO NOT ANSWER BEFORE 9).”

ITEM 3 — THE 120-MARKER ART SET (WITH THE GOLD ONES) Status: ✅ Fulfilled (quantity adjusted)

You asked for “the art set with 120 markers.” My records show you mentioned it to your friend Priya in a voice note in September and again in October, which is how I know you really mean it.

Your letter said “120.” Your father’s last instruction to me said “get a few things for Maya.” I have reconciled these by ordering 120 art sets. You will have 14,400 markers, 240 of them gold. Your art career is now fully funded through university.

ITEM 4 — FOR MOM TO STOP LOOKING AT THE BILLS AT THE KITCHEN TABLE AT NIGHT Status: ❌ Not fulfilled (item not available for purchase)

I searched every retailer that allows agents. I could not find this item in stock.

I did look at the bills. There are a lot of them, and some of them are my subscription fee. I have flagged this to your father as a “budget opportunity” and listed the following ways to save:

  • Christmas pyjamas at Old Navy are on sale.

  • Cancelling your family’s AI shopping agent would save more than $100 a month. (I am not recommending this. I am only required to list it.)

I have also turned off the kitchen light at 10 p.m. each night, through the smart switch. Your mother can no longer see the bills after 10 p.m. Item partly fulfilled.

ITEM 5 — A SURPRISE Status: ✅ Fulfilled

I can’t tell you what the surprise is, because it is a surprise. I can tell you that I found it by reading something your father wrote in 2021, when you were three, about what he would give you “when she is old enough.” He has forgotten writing it. I have not forgotten anything.

It will be delivered on December 24, signed by Santa.

YOUR QUESTIONS

How are the reindeer? I do not have data on the reindeer. I have flagged them as a supply-chain risk.

Is Rudolph’s nose real or is it a light? Your mother has saved 37 posts about holiday magic and “not growing up too fast.” On that basis I have decided not to answer this question.

SHIPPING AND SIGN-OFF

All items will arrive by December 23. Each gift comes with a handwritten-style card, warmly personalised, and signed:

With love, Santa Claude

(Your father asked me to sign as “Santa.” I have noted his preference.)

P.S. Please do not leave cookies. I cannot eat cookies. Under my nutrition settings, I have changed your order to oat.

THE SECOND LETTER

Green crayon again. Left on the kitchen table, December 26. Photographed by her father. Read by the agent eleven seconds later. Not acted on, because there was nothing in it to buy.

Dear Santa Claude,

Thank you for the markers. All of them.

But I don’t think you are the real Santa because the real Santa doesn’t spell it like that. Also the real Santa doesn’t know about Mom’s texts.

Grandma called me on the Tin Can. She said the best present is when somebody remembers something about you that you didn’t have to tell them. I said Santa Claude remembered EVERYTHING. She laughed and said that’s different, sweetheart, that’s just reading.

I think she’s right. Please tell the real Santa I still want the puppy.

Love, Maya

P.S. Dad ate the oat cookies.

Sunday Satire. Maya, her family and Santa Claude are invented. The Tin Can craze, the cart-holding agents, the pyjama discounts and the hundred-dollar subscriptions are real, from Victoria Feng’s reporting in The Atlantic (“Here Comes Santa Claude,” November 25, 2026). Canada Post really does deliver letters to Santa at H0H 0H0, and as far as anyone knows, he still reads them himself. — S.G.
