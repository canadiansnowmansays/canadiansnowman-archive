A Sunday satire

ARE YOU TIRED of freedom of navigation just... happening, for free, the way it has since 1982 under a UN convention nobody in this administration has read?

Introducing HORMUZ STRAIT GUARDIAN™ — the revolutionary new subscription service that turns 21 miles of the most contested shipping lane on Earth into a premium, monetizable experience! Launched Monday, July 13, 2026, by a management team with zero maritime law background and a demonstrated allergy to keeping any single position for more than 36 hours, GUARDIAN™ asks the question the shipping industry never knew it needed answered: what if the fifth of the world’s oil and gas that transits this strait had to pay a cover charge, like a nightclub, but for tankers, and the bouncer was the U.S. Navy?

HOW IT WORKS:

Step one: Announce, via personal social media account, that the United States is now “THE GUARDIAN OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT” (capital letters ours, patent pending, meaning undefined). Step two: propose a 20% toll on all cargo transiting the strait, effective immediately, with no consultation with the Pentagon, the State Department, the shipping industry, or anyone who might point out that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had, three weeks prior, told the region “no country is allowed to charge tolls or fees on an international waterway. That’s existing international law.” Step three: watch, with apparent surprise, as this becomes news.

★★★★★ “I found this idea to be entirely reasonable, more reasonable than I expected, and honestly a little validating.” — Abbas Araghchi, Foreign Minister of Iran, the one man on Earth for whom the toll proposal functioned as total vindication. Tehran had spent the war insisting it had the right to charge exactly this kind of fee, an idea Washington called illegal, destabilizing, and grounds for continued bombing. When the identical idea arrived wearing an American flag, Araghchi did not gloat, exactly. He simply agreed. “Whoever provides secure and safe passage of commercial vessels through the Strait of Hormuz should be compensated for this service,” he said, adding, generously, that 20 percent seemed a touch steep — the first and only instance of fiscal restraint anyone has shown in this entire episode.

LIMITED TIME OFFER, VERY LIMITED, ABOUT ONE DAY:

GUARDIAN™’s launch window coincided with peak market conditions: a fourth consecutive day of U.S.-Iran strikes, two UAE-linked tankers set ablaze near Oman, a Dutch vessel’s engine room on fire, one mariner dead, and Brent crude spiking 9.6% in a single session — its sharpest one-day jump in six years — to $83.30 a barrel. Perfect timing to introduce a cover charge on the one thing everyone was already too afraid to use.

BUT WAIT — CALLS CAME IN:

Reviews arrived swiftly from an unexpected focus group: kings and emirs. Gulf leadership phoned in what the company describes as “highly productive conversations,” and what industry analysts describe as everyone involved in global energy markets calling to ask what on Earth this was. One call reportedly involved condolences for the death of the Emir of Qatar’s father, with the Strait toll somehow still on the agenda — GUARDIAN™’s customer service team apparently incapable of leaving any conversation, however solemn, without pitching the product.

PRODUCT DISCONTINUED, EFFECTIVE THE VERY NEXT DAY:

Less than 24 hours after launch, GUARDIAN™ was quietly rebranded. “Based on highly productive conversations with Middle East leadership,” the announcement read, “I have decided to replace the 20% United States Reimbursement Fee with Trade and Investment Deals... [which] will be MASSIVE.” Whether these investments are new, or simply the same $2 trillion in Gulf pledges from the previous year wearing a fresh coat of press-release paint, remains, per company policy, none of your business.

A WORD FROM MANAGEMENT ON WHY THIS WAS NEVER A MISTAKE:

“I like that actually,” the President told reporters, of the plan to not do the thing he had proposed 48 hours earlier, “because I don’t think anybody should be able to charge a fee for the strait.” This is, technically, the position Rubio held in June, the position Araghchi mocked in real time, and the position the President himself abandoned on a Monday and rediscovered, as a fresh insight, on a Tuesday — a closed loop of principle so tight it has begun to resemble a toll booth of its own, charging nothing but requiring you to sit in traffic anyway.

TERMS AND CONDITIONS (please read, or don’t, it won’t matter):

Safe passage is guaranteed, except when it isn’t. The fee is illegal, except when proposed by the guarantor, at which point it becomes an investment, which is legally distinct and also somehow larger. Signatories to prior joint statements rejecting tolls remain bound by those statements, unless the signatory’s boss forgets signing them, in which case the statement continues to bind everyone except him. Gulf states are required to invest MASSIVE, unspecified sums on an unspecified timeline, some or none of which may already have been pledged last year. This offer is subject to change based on the most recent phone call. Void where inconvenient, which, this week, was everywhere.

GUARDIAN™: because the alternative — a stable, published, non-negotiable freedom-of-navigation policy — was apparently too boring to try even once.

CNN, reporting on the episode, called it emblematic of “the freewheeling, transactional nature of Trump’s approach to foreign policy, even in the midst of a prolonged war that he has no clear idea how to bring to an end.” GUARDIAN™ prefers the term “agile.” The strait, for its part, remains on fire in several places, blockaded again as of midnight Wednesday, and no longer accepting new toll payments, memberships, or explanations.

Offer void in Tehran. Also void everywhere else, as of Tuesday.
