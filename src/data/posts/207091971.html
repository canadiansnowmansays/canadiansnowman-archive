EMPLOYEE: Todd Blanche CURRENT TITLE: Acting Attorney General of the United States PROMOTION SOUGHT: Attorney General (Permanent) REVIEW PERIOD: April 2026 – Present REVIEW DATE: Confirmation Hearing, Senate Judiciary Committee, Wednesday, July 15, 2026 NOTABLE SCHEDULING CONFLICT: A separate federal hearing in Virginia, on the legality of a fund the candidate insists no longer exists, is scheduled for the identical day.

SECTION 1: SELF-ASSESSMENT (candidate’s own words, submitted this review cycle)

On his relationship to his direct superior: “Continuing duty of loyalty” to President Trump, maintained as an ongoing attorney-client relationship even while serving as the nation’s chief law enforcement officer. Reviewer note: most performance rubrics list this under “conflict of interest.” This packet will list it under “consistency,” since the candidate has never claimed otherwise.

On the disputed $1.776 billion “anti-weaponization” fund: “We are not moving forward with the fund, period.” Reviewer note: candidate declined, the following month, to submit a sworn declaration saying so in federal court, calling the request “unnecessary.” Self-assessments submitted outside of sworn testimony will be weighted accordingly, i.e., not much.

On his approach to personnel matters at DOJ and FBI: privately described, per multiple reports, as helping to “clean house” of staff connected to the now-shuttered special counsel investigations into his current employer. Reviewer note: “clean house” is doing a great deal of euphemistic labor here and the committee would like it defined before voting.

SECTION 2: EXTERNAL AUDIT FINDINGS (submitted, unsolicited, by U.S. District Judge Kathleen Williams, two days prior to this review)

The committee received, on Monday, July 13, a 56-page independent audit of one representative work product from the review period: the candidate’s Department’s handling of Trump v. IRS, a $10 billion suit the President filed against an agency he controls, which the Department then declined to defend, settled instead, and used to award the President personal immunity from tax audits plus a $1.776 billion fund for his allies.

The auditor’s findings, excerpted for the file:

  • The suit was “brought for an improper purpose: to gain the imprimatur of judicial legitimacy for a ‘settlement’ that had no viable basis in law or fact.”

  • “It is risible to suggest that there was ever adverseness between the Parties.”

  • The Department, under the candidate’s leadership, was “abdicating its responsibility to zealously defend the interests of the United States.”

  • The audit trail was serious enough that the auditor forwarded findings directly to two professional licensing bodies — the New York State Bar, of which the candidate is a member, and the D.C. Bar, covering his deputy.

Reviewer note: it is unusual for an external auditor to escalate findings to an employee’s own licensing board. This typically happens only when internal HR — in this case, the Department itself — cannot be trusted to act on the findings independently. See also: Section 3.

SECTION 3: MANAGEMENT’S RESPONSE TO THE AUDIT (verbatim, via Department spokesperson)

“There was no collusion in this case, and the partisan judge who speculated otherwise has disregarded decades of precedent.”

Reviewer note: this is the entirety of the Department’s substantive response to 56 pages of findings. No line-by-line rebuttal was offered. No factual correction was submitted. The word “partisan” was, in this instance, asked to do the work usually performed by an argument. The committee finds this response itself worth including in the file, as a data point about how the candidate’s Department responds to unfavorable review: not by engaging it, but by renaming the reviewer.

SECTION 4: 360-DEGREE PEER FEEDBACK

From a former colleague (Gene Rossi, former federal prosecutor): “There is absolutely no wall between the Justice Department and the White House. Blanche literally does what Trump wants him to do.”

From a former friend and colleague (Mimi Rocah, former federal prosecutor): states she believes the candidate has “fundamentally changed” since his prior role as the President’s personal defense attorney.

From a former chief of staff, submitted in the candidate’s defense, published this week in the Wall Street Journal: praises the candidate’s discretion “when the cameras were off,” urging swift confirmation.

Reviewer note: the committee notes that all positive feedback in this file describes the candidate’s conduct in private, off-camera settings, while all critical feedback — the judge’s audit, the peer commentary, the Department’s own public statements — concerns his conduct on the record. A candidate whose defenders can only vouch for what happens when no one is watching is not, strictly, being defended.

SECTION 5: OUTSTANDING ITEMS FLAGGED BY THE REVIEW COMMITTEE (Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse, on behalf of Committee Democrats)

Unresolved oversight requests as of this review, more than a dozen, including: the firing of DOJ antitrust official Gail Slater; termination of unspecified grant programs; the candidate’s personal role in reviewing and releasing the Epstein files, including his own interview of Ghislaine Maxwell, amid reports that documents referencing the President were withheld despite a statute barring exactly that kind of omission for reasons of “embarrassment, reputational harm, or political sensitivity”; and the fund addressed, at length, above. Whitehouse’s formal recommendation: “No new Attorney General should be confirmed until the Department has provided meaningful responses to all outstanding oversight requests.”

Reviewer note: request denied by the committee majority, who scheduled the hearing anyway.

SECTION 6: COMMITTEE VOTE STATUS

Two votes remain formally undecided: Sen. Thom Tillis, who wants firmer assurances the fund is dead before voting yes, and Sen. John Cornyn, who says he will decide only after the hearing concludes. Sen. Ted Cruz, describing an earlier closed-door confrontation over the fund, called it “one of the roughest meetings I’ve seen in my entire time in the Senate.” Most outside observers still expect confirmation, on a narrow, party-line-adjacent vote, because the review process, whatever its findings, was never actually designed to produce a different outcome.

COMMITTEE RECOMMENDATION:

Pending. Findings substantial. Self-assessment and external audit in direct conflict. Candidate’s own defense of his record consists primarily of denying that the record means what it says. Promotion expected to proceed regardless, per longstanding company policy that performance reviews are, in this particular workplace, largely ceremonial.
