Stephen Gordon September 2026

MEETING MINUTES — RECORDED FOR INTERNAL USE ONLY

Location: American Airlines Center, Dallas, TX (breakout room, not the main stage — the main stage was booked). Fluorescent lighting. One working coffee urn.
Session: Emergency joint meeting, Wednesday/Thursday combined
Chair: A senior member, requesting anonymity, currently polling at "historically bad, second only to Nixon"
Attendance: Higher than the fundraisers. Several members left early to take donor calls.

OPENING READING: The Twelve Steps of Standing By Him

Recited in unison, with varying enthusiasm:

  1. We admitted we were powerless over the $5,000 idea, and that our approval ratings had become unmanageable.

  2. Came to believe that a Power greater than ourselves — specifically, a Power holding $10 million in Super PAC disbursements — could restore us to electability.

  3. Made a decision to turn our campaigns over to His care, understanding that His care might arrive as $10 million, or as a public comment about our tailoring, and that we would not get to choose which.

  4. Made a searching and fearless inventory of the checks He has promised us before. (Secretary’s note: this step took considerably longer than the others. See: DOGE dividend, 2025. Tariff refund, 2025. Progress: pending.)

  5. Admitted to Him, to ourselves, and to one persistent Fox News anchor the exact nature of our disagreements — quietly, in the hallway, never at the microphone.

  6. One member attempted Step 9 out of order — the amends step — by trying to explain, out loud, where the trillion dollars was actually coming from. He got as far as “well, the way I see it—” before a staffer’s phone lit up and he had to take the call. The room did not ask him to finish. Nobody has asked him to finish since.

  7. Discussed, briefly, whether honesty is still part of the program, given the events of Step 6. Tabled the discussion. The coffee, which had gone lukewarm an hour earlier, was not going to get warmer either.

  8. Agreed, without a formal vote, to let three different explanations of the $5,000 exist at the same time — a tax rebate, a middle-class benefit, a payment to every adult regardless of income — on the theory that reconciling them was a November problem.

  9. Noted, with some discomfort, that no one in the room could say with confidence whether Congress’s approval was actually required, and further noted that no one wanted to be the one to ask Him directly.

  10. Watched one member leave his seat entirely and take his sharing to a hotel lobby instead, and agreed that this, too, was a valid form of participation.

  11. Sought, through prayer, meditation, and a separately scheduled fundraising luncheon, to improve our conscious contact with the base, praying only for knowledge of His polling numbers and the power to distance ourselves from them gracefully.

  12. Having had a political awakening as the result of these steps, we tried to carry this message to swing voters, while privately agreeing this convention wasn’t really built to reach them.

CHECK-INS

The Secretary reminds members that what is shared in this meeting is confidential, unless it is also shared with three separate New York Times reporters, in which case the group has already lost that battle.

A senator from Ohio shared that he has achieved a healthy sense of balance: in a five-minute speech, he mentioned the sitting president exactly twice, and mentioned the previous president exactly twice. The group applauded this as a breakthrough in emotional even-handedness.

A former party chairman, now a Senate nominee, shared that he has found peace in not mentioning Him at all, preferring instead to spend his allotted stage time discussing his actual opponent. The group noted this is technically still Step 6, just performed in reverse.

A representative from New York, one of the more vulnerable members present, offered a rare and startling admission to the group: “The truth is that the president and I don’t always agree, and that’s OK.” Several members studied the laminate flooring. Someone’s chair creaked. The meeting continued as though nothing had happened.

A vice chairman of the Freedom Caucus raised his hand to share that he remains “$40 trillion in debt” and does not “know where the money comes from,” regarding the $5,000 proposal specifically, though members noted this concern could apply to several items on this week’s agenda.

A former White House aide, attending as a guest, offered the group some hard truths: that recent policy adjustments have begun to “blur the line between us and democratic socialists,” and that, in his clinical opinion, “it’s basically a redistribution of wealth.” The group thanked him for his honesty and reminded him this is a safe space, not a caucus meeting.

A senator from Texas shared conflicting feelings. On cable television, immediately following the proposal, he called the $5,000 a “real incentive” and tried to reframe it as “a tax rebate.” The group gently pointed out that the man who proposed it did not call it a tax rebate, has never called it a tax rebate, and was, in fact, standing about ten feet away at the time. The senator said he’d sit with that. (See Step 8.)

A newer member, still finding his voice, was asked about the same proposal on a different network and clarified that the money is not for people like the hosts asking him about it — “this is for the American middle class,” he said. Also see Step 8.

A former congressman from Michigan, currently running for Senate, did not check in verbally. He spent the session in the donor lobby of a nearby hotel, cycling through meetings with prospective contributors. The group agreed this was, in its own way, also a form of sharing.

One member, the nominee from Texas, did not share any doubts whatsoever. He used his time to praise the group’s founder as the man who has “delivered more for this country than anyone before.” The group noted, with some interest, that this member’s campaign had recently received $10 million from the founder’s own political operation, and agreed not to read too much into the timing.

NEW BUSINESS

The guest speaker for Thursday’s session — a member with his own political ambitions, reportedly for 2028 — requested an additional 95 minutes of speaking time, so as not to be “shown up” by last week’s share. The group approved the request unanimously, noting that competitive oversharing is a documented stage of recovery.

CLOSING

Recited together, hands loosely joined:

Grant us the serenity to accept the polling we cannot change, the courage to keep standing on this stage anyway, and the wisdom to know the difference between “I don’t always agree with him” and a re-election campaign.

Next meeting: Same room, next Thursday. Newcomers welcome. Please bring your own defense of the last two years — supplies, this week, were limited.
