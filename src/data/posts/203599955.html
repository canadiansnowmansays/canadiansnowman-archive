SPOKESPERSON: Good afternoon. Thank you all for coming. Before we take questions, I want to address the events of last night directly, clearly, and with full transparency, because the American people deserve nothing less.

Last night, at approximately 0200 local time, our forces did not conduct any operation in the region. I want to be very clear about that, because there has been a great deal of irresponsible speculation. There was no strike. There was no aircraft. The facility in question, which does not exist, was not targeted, and the reports of an explosion are the product of enemy disinformation. We were not there. Nobody was there. Next question.

REPORTER (AP): You said “the facility in question.” Which facility?

SPOKESPERSON: I’m glad you asked that, because it lets me clarify what actually happened. Last night, at approximately 0200 local time, our forces conducted a precise, limited, and overwhelmingly successful strike on a high-value facility, neutralizing the threat it posed. This was a textbook operation. Every objective was met. The President was in the Situation Room throughout and personally authorized each phase. It is, frankly, one of the most successful operations in the history of the program, and I think when the full story is told, the American people will be very proud. Next question.

REPORTER (Reuters): I’m sorry — was there a strike or was there not a strike?

SPOKESPERSON: I would refer you to my earlier remarks, which were clear. Let me move us forward. The casualty figures circulating online are completely false. There were no casualties because there was no strike. At the same time, the casualties that did occur were entirely combatants, every one of them a legitimate military target, and any civilian harm is the sole responsibility of the adversary, who as you know places military assets in civilian areas. We take extraordinary care. We took extraordinary care last night. We will continue to take that care in future operations of the kind that did not occur. Yes, in the back.

REPORTER (NYT): Can you tell us who authorized the operation?

SPOKESPERSON: Certainly. No one authorized it, because there was no operation to authorize, and I would caution this room against printing rumours as fact. The President authorized it personally, after extensive consultation with his national security team, demonstrating exactly the kind of decisive leadership the country expects. The authorization came through standard channels and also did not happen and was also the sole decision of the President acting alone, which is his constitutional right. I hope that clears it up. Next.

REPORTER (NYT): It does not clear it up. That’s three different —

SPOKESPERSON: I understand there’s a lot of interest. Let me keep going. Yes.

REPORTER (Washington Post): Were allies notified in advance?

SPOKESPERSON: Great question. There was nothing to notify them about, so they were not notified, and we respect the sovereignty of all nations in the region. Our allies were fully briefed in advance and stand shoulder to shoulder with us, as the strong coalition we have built continues to demonstrate. We did not inform them because operational security required absolute secrecy, and the leak you are referring to is being investigated as a serious breach. All three of those things are true and I don’t see the contradiction. Yes.

REPORTER (CNN): With respect, they cannot all be true.

SPOKESPERSON: With respect, they are all operative.

(A pause. The transcript notes no sound.)

REPORTER (CNN): “Operative.”

SPOKESPERSON: Operative. Each statement is operative within its own framework. I’m not going to relitigate the premises of your question. Let’s keep moving. Yes, you.

REPORTER (BBC): Can I just establish a baseline. Did anything happen last night? Anything at all?

SPOKESPERSON: That’s a fair question and I want to give it a complete answer. Nothing happened last night; it was a quiet night and the reports are fabricated. A significant and historic event happened last night, the details of which I cannot share for reasons of security but which the President considers among his proudest achievements. And a regrettable incident happened last night, which is under review, for which responsibility has not yet been assigned, and about which it would be premature to comment. I’ve now answered you three times. I’m not sure what more I can do.

REPORTER (BBC): You could pick one.

SPOKESPERSON: I don’t accept the framing that there is one. Next question.

(The transcript records a lengthening interval here. No question is asked.)

SPOKESPERSON: Anyone? Yes.

REPORTER (Al Jazeera): The families of the dead are outside this building right now. What do you say to them?

SPOKESPERSON: First, my heart goes out to them, though I would note there are no dead, so I’m not certain who is outside. Second, every loss in war is a tragedy and the President feels each one personally, which is why the success of last night’s operation, in saving countless future lives, must be understood in its full context. Third, the individuals outside are, our information suggests, paid actors, and I would be cautious about amplifying a staged event. I think that addresses your question from every angle.

REPORTER (Al Jazeera): It addresses it from every angle except the true one.

SPOKESPERSON: I’m going to push back on the premise that there’s a single true one. That’s a very 20th-century way of thinking about an event. Yes, last one.

REPORTER (AP): When you go home tonight. When the cameras are off. Which of the three do you believe?

(The transcript records the longest interval of the briefing here. The stenographer, by convention, marks only silence. There is a great deal of it.)

SPOKESPERSON: I believe all of them. That’s the job. You learn to hold them all at once, and after a while you stop noticing the weight, the way you stop noticing your own heartbeat. The trouble starts only if you try to set them down. So you don’t. You hold the three accounts, and you walk out to this podium, and you give them to the country, and the country holds them too, because the country has also learned that setting them down is the dangerous part.

That’s all for today. Thank you. There will be a briefing tomorrow, on what did, did not, and regrettably may have happened. I hope to see you all there. I always do.

(End of transcript.)

This satire belongs to the Iran war diary, but it is set in no particular country and names no particular government, because the technique it describes — the three simultaneous official accounts, each delivered as if it were the only one — has by now been deployed by enough of them that to name one would be to flatter the others. The “Numbers” section is omitted today. There are, the reader will understand, no agreed numbers.
