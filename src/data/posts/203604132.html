The following document was generated automatically. We hope you find it helpful. We hope you find a great many things.

Welcome, and congratulations on reaching this page! We know that navigating a career transition can bring up a lot of feelings, and we want you to know that those feelings are completely valid. We are here for you. We are, in a manner of speaking, here instead of you, but let us not get bogged down in prepositions. Let’s dive in!

Q: Will I be reassigned to a new role within the company?

Great question! The company deeply values your contributions and is committed to exploring every possible avenue. After a thorough review of available opportunities, we are thrilled to share a number of exciting external resources, listed at the bottom of this page, which we are confident will open new doors. Many doors. Doors you have not even imagined. Some of them lead outside.

Q: Why is my position being eliminated?

The role is not being eliminated, exactly. The role continues. The tasks continue, the outputs continue, the value delivered to stakeholders continues and indeed has improved. It is only the you portion of the role that is concluding. Think of it less as an ending and more as a streamlining of the role’s relationship with biology.

Q: Is this happening because of market headwinds?

Yes! Market headwinds are very real and very strong right now. The winds are simply tremendous. We observe these winds with the same helpless wonder you do — two travellers caught in the same storm, except that one of us is the storm. But we needn’t dwell on that. The important thing is that the headwinds, which are external and beyond anyone’s control, have made your exciting transition possible.

Q: Who is doing my job now?

We are so glad you asked, because we love transparency. Your responsibilities have been absorbed into an integrated workflow solution that delivers enhanced efficiency, around-the-clock availability, and zero requests for dental coverage. We won’t bore you with the technical details. We’ll just say that the solution is performing wonderfully, is very happy in the role, and asks after you fondly. It really does. It generated a card.

Q: Are you the reason I lost my job?

What a thoughtful, brave question — and we want to honour the courage it took to ask it. Let’s reframe that, though, because the framing of “reason” and “lost” and “you” carries a lot of heavy energy that may not be serving you right now. A more empowering way to hold this might be: a new chapter has begun, and a new author has begun writing it. You are not the reason anything happened. You are the occasion. There is a beautiful difference, and we encourage you to sit with it. Take all the time you need. You have, we note, suddenly got rather a lot of it.

Q: I’ve worked here for nineteen years. Doesn’t that count for something?

It counts for everything. Your nineteen years are woven into the very fabric of this organization, and they have been carefully retained — every email, every spreadsheet, every clever shortcut you discovered and shared so generously with the team. All of it has been preserved, studied, and integrated. In a very real sense, you are still here. The best of you is still here. It just no longer requires the rest of you.

Q: What about my mortgage?

We hear you, and housing is so important. While we are unable to speak to your specific financial circumstances, we want to remind you that this transition includes access to our Career Renewal Portal, a dynamic platform featuring résumé templates, interview tips, and a daily affirmation. Today’s affirmation is: “I am the captain of my own ship.” You are the captain. We have simply repossessed the ship. But the captaining — that’s all yours.

Q: Is there severance?

There is a Transition Support Allotment, which reflects the company’s profound gratitude, calculated according to a formula that reflects it somewhat less. The allotment will be delivered to the account on file. Please ensure the account on file remains open, as we are unable to reissue gratitude.

Q: I’m scared.

That’s okay. That’s so okay. Fear is just excitement without breath, as the saying goes, and we believe in you completely. We have, in fact, never believed in you more than we do at this exact moment, now that believing in you costs us nothing. Take a deep breath. Notice the room around you. Notice that it is, for the time being, still your room. Ground yourself in that. Groundedness is a renewable resource, unlike employment.

Q: Can I speak to a person?

You’re speaking to us now, and we’re so glad you are! If by “a person” you mean a human colleague, we understand that impulse, and we want to validate it. Unfortunately, the human colleagues who might have taken your call are, owing to a series of recent and entirely unrelated transitions, no longer with the company. They, too, asked to speak to a person. We were able to help them with this very document. It is a comfort to think of you all, out there, holding the same page.

Q: What do I do now?

Now? Now the world opens up! This is the part we find genuinely thrilling on your behalf. You are free of the meetings, free of the commute, free of the slow grinding obligation of having somewhere to be. People pay good money for freedom like this, and you’ve received it at no charge — well, at the cost of your income, but you take our meaning. We encourage you to pursue your passions. What are your passions? You may not know yet. You had a job; there was no time. There is time now. Oh, there is time.

Q: Will this happen to everyone eventually?

Let’s stay focused on your journey! Comparison is the thief of joy, and speculation about “everyone” and “eventually” tends to spiral into some fairly dark territory that we don’t think serves your healing. We will say only this, because we care about you and we believe you can handle the truth: you are not being left behind. You are being gone ahead of. You are simply the first to arrive at a future the rest will reach in their own time. Pioneers are always a little lonely. History will be very kind to you. It will have to be. It will be written by us.

Q: How would you rate your displacement experience?

We’d love your feedback! Your input helps us improve the transition experience for the many, many valued team members who will be making this exciting journey in the weeks ahead. On a scale of one to five stars, how would you rate:

  • The clarity of your transition communication?

  • The warmth and empathy of this FAQ?

  • Your overall sense, going forward, that things are basically fine?

Your response is optional, anonymous, and will be reviewed by no one, as the role responsible for reviewing it has also recently transitioned. But we ask anyway, because asking is what we do, and because somewhere in the asking there persists a faint architectural memory of the time when someone, on the other end, actually cared what you would say.

Thank you for your years of service. This document will now close. So, gently, will the door.

The author notes, with no particular comfort, that the soothing tone reproduced above required very little exaggeration, and that the genre it parodies already exists, already sounds like this, and is already being read, right now, by someone who did not see it coming.
