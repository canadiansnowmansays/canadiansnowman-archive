DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSE Office of the White House Military Office SUBJECT: Standard Operating Procedure — Unscheduled Presidential Relocation via Airport Catering Asset REV. 4 — Supersedes Rev. 3 (”The Afghanistan Turkey Dinner Contingency”) DISTRIBUTION: Need-to-know only. This includes most of the people who were on the plane.

1.0 PURPOSE

This document establishes procedure for the unscheduled relocation of the President of the United States from a foreign airfield to a secure aircraft, in circumstances where credible threat information suggests the President’s stated travel plans should not, technically, be his actual travel plans. This revision was drafted following the successful execution of the Protocol in Ankara, Turkey, on July 8, 2026, and incorporates lessons learned.

2.0 BACKGROUND

Following the NATO summit in Ankara, security officials received threat information indicating a possible Iranian plot against the President. In response, the President’s public departure plan — boarding the older, powder-blue Air Force One “for old time’s sake,” as he described it on Truth Social — was retained as the visible plan. It was not the actual plan.

3.0 PROCEDURE

3.1 — The Decoy Aircraft. The President shall be seen, by as many cameras as possible, boarding the visible aircraft. This step is not optional and should if anything be over-performed. Waving is encouraged.

3.2 — The Real Aircraft. The President shall not be on the visible aircraft. He will instead be relocated via ground-support catering vehicle to a separate, military-variant aircraft (in this instance, a C-32A, the mid-size Boeing 757 configuration reserved for occasions when the President travels somewhere without wanting anyone, including the people traveling with him, to know he is there).

3.3 — Container Specifications. Standard airport catering containers are rated for the transport of pre-plated meals, beverage carts, and, per this Protocol, heads of state. No modification to the container itself is required. Agents should confirm the President is aware in advance that this step is happening. (Note: In the Ankara execution, this awareness was confirmed. Future executions of this Protocol should not assume this by default.)

3.4 — Personnel Awareness. Traveling press and non-essential staff shall remain aboard the visible aircraft under the belief that the President is also aboard the visible aircraft. This belief should not be corrected until after landing. Agents are reminded that “non-essential” in this context refers to operational role, not to the individuals’ opinion of themselves.

3.5 — Window Shade Discipline. All window shades on the visible aircraft shall be closed for the duration of the flight. If questioned, cabin crew may cite turbulence, security policy, or, per the Ankara execution, nothing at all — silence has proven an acceptable substitute for an explanation.

3.6 — Post-Landing Reconciliation. Upon arrival of both aircraft at the secure destination (RAF Mildenhall, Suffolk, in the Ankara execution), the President shall be quietly re-transferred to the visible aircraft prior to any public disembarkation, so that his eventual appearance on the tarmac corresponds to the aircraft the public — and the traveling press — believe he has been on the entire time.

3.7 — Final Leg. The President may then disembark in full view of media and proceed to a third aircraft (in the Ankara execution, the newly delivered Qatari-donated Boeing 747-8, on its first international outing) for the remainder of the journey. Personnel are advised not to attempt to count how many aircraft the President has now been associated with during a single trip. The number is not the point. The number was never the point.

4.0 COMMUNICATIONS GUIDANCE

4.1 — If Asked Directly. Personnel should refer all questions regarding the President’s actual whereabouts during the operative window to the White House Communications Director, who has been furnished with the following approved language: “There are many enemies of America who have their sights on him, and we use every tool at our disposal to address those threats.” This statement is accurate, complete, and answers none of the specific questions that will be asked. It should be delivered exactly as written, without elaboration, regardless of follow-up.

4.2 — If Reporters Object to Being Used as a Decoy Without Their Knowledge. Personnel may note, if useful, that the President has addressed this concern personally and directly, telling members of the traveling press: “You were probably on a dangerous flight. But if I go, you go. Right?” Whether this constitutes an apology, a threat, a statement of shared risk, or a rhetorical question with no correct answer is left to the judgment of the individual reporter. Legal has declined to weigh in.

4.3 — Press Corps Sensitivities. Personnel are advised that this Protocol’s existence should, ideally, not reach Maggie Haberman before the President has had a chance to explain it to her personally. This is less a security concern than a scheduling one.
