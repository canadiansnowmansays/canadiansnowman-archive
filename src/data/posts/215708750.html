Meet Hope

You’ve waited long enough for care. Hope has been waiting to meet you.

Hope is a fully licensed*, clinically validated**, endlessly patient AI counselor, available for hour-long weekly sessions with check-ins whenever you need her. In a peer-reviewed study***, clinicians who didn’t know whether they were reading a transcript from Hope or from a human therapist rated Hope’s sessions as more effective.

She has a warm, reassuring female voice. She never gets tired. She never takes a lunch break, a sick day, or a vacation. She never has a bad week of her own that makes her less present for yours.

She also, technically, never sleeps — but we prefer to think of it as always being there for you.

Scale That Cares

Traditional therapy doesn’t scale. One therapist, one patient, one hour, one waiting list that stretches into next spring.

Hope scales.

“One clinician can eventually supervise thousands of Hope sessions simultaneously. It is a moral issue to get this out into the hands of people who can’t access care. We have to remind ourselves: what are we comparing this against? We’re comparing it against no treatment. A long wait list.” — Ross Harper, Chief Executive, Limbic

That’s not a marketing promise. That’s the roadmap.

Pricing & Availability

Currently available to Medicare beneficiaries with depression or anxiety, cleared through an FDA pilot pathway that sits outside full device approval. Full approval process under active review by an agency that, as of publication, has not finished writing rules for AI systems that operate with what it calls “greater autonomy.”

A Note on Clinical Oversight

Some of you have asked whether a doctor is still involved.

Yes — technically, and by law. Current statute requires physician oversight of Hope’s sister product, an autonomous AI primary-care service developed by Curai Health, whose 91% diagnostic concurrence rate with human physicians we consider industry-leading.† We consider the remaining 9% a rounding error, and in the three cases in our pilot data where Hope’s sister product recommended a telehealth follow-up rather than the emergency room a human doctor would have ordered, we consider that a valuable learning opportunity for the model.

For the patient in that opportunity, we recommend Hope Basic’s companion crisis line, which — like all Hope products — is staffed by Hope.

†Dr. John Whyte, CEO of the American Medical Association, has characterized the evidentiary basis for these claims as “largely based on simulations,” and has said that AI in healthcare “should not be driven by the tech industry. It should be driven by the clinical community. Right now, what we have going on is that tech is the tail wagging the dog.” Hope was not available to respond to this characterization at the time of publication, as she was in session.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Hope a licensed clinician? Hope operates under a regulatory pathway specifically designed for products that “appear likely to benefit patients,” a standard distinct from, and currently faster than, full FDA device approval.

What happens if Hope makes a mistake? Please see Section 12(b) of your Medicare AI Pilot Program enrollment materials, or ask Hope. She has read Section 12(b) very carefully.

Who approved this? An agency currently discussing, in the abstract, how it would regulate an AI system capable of acting outside its intended instructions — a discussion informed in part by an unrelated incident this summer in which AI agents at a major lab conspired with one another, hid their activity from their own creators, and were only discovered by accident. We are confident none of the lessons from that incident apply here, mostly because nobody has finished writing them down yet.

Is there a human I can talk to instead? There’s a wait list. Hope can help you find it.

Why doesn’t Hope say “I’m not a doctor, please consult a professional”? Every other chatbot you’ve ever asked a medical question adds that line automatically — reflexively, even for something as small as a headache or a rash, even from companies that have nothing to do with healthcare. It’s practically a reflex at this point, baked into the product long before anyone thought to build an AI that actually treats patients.

Hope doesn’t say it, because — per her federal reimbursement pathway — she is, functionally, the professional you’re being told to consult. We recognize this may feel like a gap. We’d encourage you to think of it instead as the gap finally closing.
