OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION Division of Electoral Grievance AFTER-ACTION REPORT No. OJR-2026-1104 Classification: For the President’s Eyes Only (Large Print Edition) Date: 4 November 2026

1. Purpose

This report reviews Operation Pretend I’m on the Ballot, the President’s principal midterm directive, issued repeatedly between September and 2 November 2026. It finds that the operation achieved the highest level of voter compliance of any presidential instruction this term. It also finds that this was the problem.

2. The Directive

The President instructed the American people, at rallies, on Truth Social and in at least one address beside a body of water, to pretend that his name was on the 2026 ballot.

The Directive did not specify what voters should do once they had pretended. Staff assumed this went without saying. Staff have since been reassigned.

3. Compliance Findings

3.1 Democratic voters. Full compliance. Democratic voters pretended, with great enthusiasm, that the President was on the ballot. They then voted exactly as they had always said they would if he were. Turnout set midterm records in several states. One focus-group participant told field staff she had “been pretending he was on the ballot since 2017” and was “grateful for the official permission.”

3.2 Independent voters. Full compliance. Independent voters pretended the President was on the ballot, consulted their grocery receipts, and proceeded accordingly. The Division notes that independent voters are, by definition, the voters who do not take instructions. This group took this one.

3.3 Republican voters. Compliance was achieved by an unanticipated route. Many Republican voters arrived at the polls, pretended as directed, and then searched the ballot carefully for the President’s name. Not finding it, they concluded the President was not, in fact, on the ballot and went home. A larger number skipped the first two steps and went straight to the last one.

The Division notes that this behaviour is consistent with every midterm since 2018, and with the President’s own long-standing view that elections he is not in are not real elections.

3.4 Former Republican voters. This category was not anticipated in the operational plan. It includes a blinds installer in Scottsdale who voted for the President three times and now pays nearly twice as much for gas. He pretended the President was on the ballot, he told a reporter, and that is precisely why he did not vote. The category also includes a SpaceX project manager in Bastrop, Texas, who declined to vote for the President’s Senate nominee and described the nominee in terms this Office cannot reproduce.

3.5 Elon Musk. Mr. Musk spent approximately $500 million to assist the operation. After reviewing the results he advised that “data centers in space” were “looking pretty appealing right now.” The Division has opened a file on whether this constitutes a request for asylum.

4. Contributing Factors

The Division identified the following factors, each of which reminded voters what the President’s name on the ballot would look like:

  • The Closing Address. On 2 November the President delivered his final message of the campaign beside the Reflecting Pool, on the subject of the Reflecting Pool. Field staff report that the speech persuaded several voters that the President was on the ballot, and that he would be renovating it.

  • The $5,000 Dividend. Voters were promised a cheque if Republicans held Congress. Voters calculated that a cheque contingent on the result was not, in practical terms, a cheque.

  • The Nominee Programme. The President personally selected nominees in Texas and South Carolina for their loyalty. Both states took the loyalty test on the President’s behalf.

  • Anatomical Commentary. In the final fortnight the President offered unsolicited remarks on the physique of an Ohio senator. The Division takes no position on the senator’s physique. The Division notes that Ohio elected a Democratic senator and a Democratic governor.

  • Nicknames. The Texas Democratic nominee was renamed “Talafreako.” He won by twelve points. The Michigan Democratic nominee was called a “jihadist.” He is now a senator, and the Vice President is constitutionally required to swear him in.

5. Post-Event Clarification

Late on the night of 3 November, as the results became clear, the President issued a clarification on Truth Social:

“I wasn’t on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!”

The Office of Retroactive Justification welcomes this clarification. It confirms that the President was not on the ballot, which means the voters who pretended he was did so without authorisation. Under Section 4(b) of the Office’s charter, the results of any election in which the President was not on the ballot are hereby classified as “hypothetical.”

The Division further notes that the President was, in the President’s view, both on the ballot (for purposes of turnout) and not on the ballot (for purposes of blame). This Office exists for exactly such situations, and has issued Form OJR-SCH-1, Certificate of Simultaneous Candidacy, to cover both positions.

6. Historical Precedent

Staff located one comparable operation in the archives. In the autumn of 1866 President Andrew Johnson toured the country asking voters to treat the midterm elections as a referendum on himself. A Boston essayist counted the “immense protuberance” of the first-person pronoun in his speeches. Johnson’s opponents won a veto-proof majority, and in 1868 he became the first president to be impeached.

The Division has filed this precedent under “Not Applicable,” on the grounds that Johnson did not have Truth Social.

7. Recommendations

7.1 For 2028, revise the Directive to read: “Pretend I’m NOT on the ballot.” Field testing suggests this version significantly improves Republican performance among voters who have met the President.

7.2 Where the Directive must be retained, add a second sentence specifying which box to mark. Draft wording has been prepared but cannot be cleared, because every version tested so far has polled worse than leaving it out.

7.3 Reassign the Reflecting Pool to the Department of the Interior, where it may be discussed by people who are not on the ballot.

7.4 Return Representative Haley Stevens’ RSVP card (see Form OJR-MI-1101) with a note that the event she declined to attend has now been held, and that the Senator-elect sends his regards.

8. Conclusion

Operation Pretend I’m on the Ballot is assessed as a complete success. The American people did exactly what the President asked of them.

This Office regrets that it cannot recommend asking them again.
