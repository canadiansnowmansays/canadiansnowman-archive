OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION

Applicant: Ken Paxton, Attorney General of Texas
Position sought: United States Senator, Texas
Referee: The President of the United States
Relationship to applicant: Endorser (complete and total)
Method of collection: Referee was not contacted. Remarks were transcribed from the referee’s own rallies, where he has been providing references to the applicant in public, unprompted, for several weeks.

Section 1: General Assessment

The Reference Desk thanks the referee for his candour. Most references must be pried loose. This one arrived in instalments, at volume, in front of crowds.

The referee describes the applicant as one of the best attorneys general in the country. On October 1 in Denton, he described him as “one of the most loyal people.” Loyalty is the quality the referee values above all others, and the Desk notes that it is the only quality in this reference to receive an unqualified mark.

Section 3: Comparative Endorsements

Form RJ-7 asks the referee to list any other person he endorsed on the same occasion, so the reader can calibrate.

On October 7 in San Antonio, at a rally held to help the applicant, the referee was introduced by a retired baseball pitcher. The referee announced that the pitcher would receive the Presidential Medal of Freedom. He also told a long story about a second baseball player. The applicant was invited onstage briefly.

Desk calibration: Pitcher, Medal of Freedom. Applicant, walk-on. The Desk notes, without comment, that the applicant’s Senate race is the one on the ballot.

Section 4: Mandatory Hazard Disclosure (Dragons)

Question 14 of Form RJ-7 reads: “Is the applicant at present being carried off by a dragon? If yes, describe the dragon.”

The Desk regrets to answer yes.

The creature first appeared in this file as a drag. That was the word a donor used in September, asking the applicant whether “he” was a bit of one. Like many things in Texas, it has grown. By October it was large enough to be classed as a dragon, and it has been formally entered in the Desk’s register as the Drag-On (Draco gravitas texanus).

Lineage. The Drag-On is not the first winged monster found living inside an American map. In March 1812 the Boston Gazette printed a Massachusetts voting district redrawn so crookedly under Governor Elbridge Gerry that an artist gave it claws, wings and a head and called it the Gerry-mander. (Gerry said his name with a hard G. History has not returned the favour.) The species has done well in Texas, which redrew its congressional map in 2025, mid-decade, at the referee’s urging, to find five more Republican seats. The Desk notes the precedent without drawing conclusions from it. The Gerry-mander held the legislature for Gerry’s party that spring. Gerry lost his own election. A gerrymander can be drawn around voters, but a Senate race covers the whole state, and nobody has yet found a way to redraw Texas.

Field description. The Drag-On does not breathe fire. It breathes diesel, at more than $6 a gallon. Its scales are individually labelled, and each label is something the applicant would prefer not to discuss. Scales identified so far:

  • The Convention. On September 24, at that private fundraiser, the applicant replied that the convention “dropped our numbers. Everybody’s numbers dropped. Right now, yeah, not good.”

  • The War. In a second recording published October 7, the applicant called the Iran war and the price of fuel “by far our biggest problem.” That same evening the referee, at the applicant’s own rally, campaigned on the war.

  • Zero. The number of debates the applicant has agreed to. The Dallas date of October 6 came and went with a stage and no Paxton.

  • Three Thousand. The applicant’s follower count on TikTok, where 70 percent of the conversation about his race concerns his opponent.

  • The Archive. An impeachment, an acquittal and a cell tower. These scales are older and have hardened.

Behaviour. The Drag-On is not hostile to the applicant. It is very fond of him. The Desk’s naturalists report that it holds him close, praises his loyalty, and flies steadily downward.

Containment. No containment is currently possible. The applicant’s party has chosen to feed it.

Section 5: The Applicant’s Own Statement

Form RJ-7 allows the applicant to respond to his reference. The Desk invited a response. The applicant’s campaign called the first recording manipulated nonsense and the second a non-story. The Desk has filed both responses under “Scales, additional.”

Section 6: Recommendation

The referee recommends the applicant without reservation, apart from the reservations listed in Sections 2, 3 and 4.

Early voting opens on October 19. Election Day is November 3. The Desk will update this reference if the referee provides further remarks, which, on current evidence, he will, at volume, about something else.
