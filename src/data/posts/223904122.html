OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION
Division of Liturgical Affairs
Chapel of the Golden Escalator, Palm Beach, Florida

ORDER OF SERVICE
for the Installation of a Chief of Staff
6 November 2026

Ushers will seat the congregation by donor tier. Members of the Cabinet sit in the front pew. Members of the Cabinet who are currently “hot” sit slightly further back (see the Warning, below).

The Prelude

The congregation gathers beneath the chandeliers at the foot of the grand staircase. The Host enters wearing a white cap bearing the letters U-S-A, so that the faithful may know which country is under discussion.

The Invitation

The Host, having spoken for some time, remembers the Chief of Staff and invites him to say “a couple of words.”

Rubric: The phrase “a couple of words” is ceremonial. No Chief of Staff in the history of this Chapel has said a couple of words.

The Warning

Before the Chief of Staff may speak, the Host delivers the traditional Warning concerning the Secretary of State. Marco, the Host observes, is hot now. Should Marco ever become hotter than Trump, the Host would have to let him go.

Rubric: The congregation laughs. The Secretary of State, wherever he is this evening, is advised to stand slightly out of the light. Members are reminded that the Host is a jealous host, and that warmth in this house is a fixed quantity, all of it already spoken for. The Chapel keeps the First Commandment, “Thou shalt have no other gods before me,” in its revised form, in which the Host is the speaker and the Secretary of State is the other god.

The Blessing of the Host

Chief of Staff: Thank you, Mr President. God bless you.

Rubric: Note the order of service. The Host is thanked first and blessed second. God appears in a supporting role, as is customary in this Chapel.

The Testimony

The Chief of Staff recounts his conversion. It was, he tells the congregation, the privilege of a lifetime to join President Trump “all the way back in 2015 when he came down that golden escalator.”

Rubric: The Descent is the founding event of the faith. Pilgrims to Fifth Avenue may ride the escalator themselves, though only in the downward direction, which is the direction that counts.

The Creation

Chief of Staff: If it wasn’t for President Trump, the United States of America would not exist.

Chief of Staff: Right?

Chief of Staff: Right?

Rubric: The Division has received correspondence noting that the United States predates the Host by 170 years. The Division’s theologians have resolved the difficulty. The country existed before 1946 in the way a building exists before the name goes on the front: technically, but not meaningfully. Correspondents are further advised that objections of this kind are raised only by people who have not been to the Chapel.

The Litany of Salvation

A responsive reading. The Chief of Staff leads; the congregation responds.

Leader: This man saved America.
All: He saved it.

Leader: He saved our military from collapse.
All: He saved it.

Leader: He saved our economy from destruction.
All: He saved it.

Leader: He saved our border from an invasion.
All: He saved it.

Leader: He saved our country from threats at home and abroad, which would have been the end of the American dream forever.
All: He saved it, forever.

Leader: He saved the Republican Party from the ashes of history, and made it stronger, more popular and more resilient than ever before.
All: He saved it.

Rubric: The Litany contains one verb. Earlier drafts included “built,” which was withdrawn on the advice of counsel pending the arch litigation. The final response may be spoken in whatever tone this week’s election returns allow.

The Passion

Leader: This man took a bullet for us.

Leader: He faced life in prison for us.

Rubric: The Division has received several queries about the word “for.” At Butler, Pennsylvania, on 13 July 2024, the gunman was aiming at the Host, not at the congregation; the bullet that grazed the Host’s ear was taken, strictly speaking, on his own account. The prison exposure likewise arose from four criminal cases concerning the Host’s own conduct, none of which named any member of the congregation as a co-defendant. The Division’s ruling is that “for us” is a devotional preposition, not a factual one, and is not to be parsed.

The Division further notes that the one man at Butler who died shielding others was a former volunteer fire chief named Corey Comperatore, who threw himself over his family. He does not appear in the liturgy.

The Vows

Leader: We need to stand with the man who has stood with us and with this country with every fibre of his being.

Chief of Staff: It is the honour of my life to be President Trump’s chief of staff, and I swear to you, and I pledge to you, Mr President, my absolute loyalty, my unwavering commitment, and my total devotion to your success —

(a pause)

— and the success of this country.

Rubric: The country is admitted to the vow at the very end, by a separate clause, in the manner of a plus-one. Ushers are reminded that a plus-one is not entitled to a seat at the head table.

Erratum. The automatic transcript of the evening renders the second vow as “my own wavering commitment.” The Division has corrected the record to “unwavering.” The Division notes, however, that the machine’s version is the only line in this Order of Service that every previous Trump chief of staff could have signed in good faith.

The Reading from the Book of Works

The congregation will remain seated. The Chief of Staff’s own works are now read aloud, so that the faithful may see what devotion looks like once it is given a budget.

Reader: In the first year of the first reign, he helped draft an order closing the gates to travellers from seven Muslim-majority countries. It was signed on a Friday, and by Saturday morning the airports were full of detained families and the lawyers who came to find them.
All: And he saw that it was good.

Reader: In the second year, he championed a policy of zero tolerance at the southern border, under which more than 5,000 children were taken from their parents. Some were too young to say their own names. The records were kept so poorly that some families could not be found again for years.
All: And he saw that it was good.

Reader: In the third year, his private emails to a journalist were published. In them he recommended white-nationalist websites, and a French novel in which the migrants of the world arrive on Europe’s shores as an invading horde.
All: And he saw that it was good.

Reader: In the year of the return, he summoned the immigration officers of the land and told them their quota was 3,000 arrests a day.
All: And he saw that it was good.

Reader: And he lowered the number of refugees the nation would receive to 7,500 a year, and set most of the places aside for white South Africans.
All: And he saw that it was good.

Rubric: The Book of Works is the only reading in this service that is entirely factual. The Division apologises for the change in tone. It is the Chief of Staff’s tone, not ours.

Rubric: The Gospel verse traditionally read at this point, Matthew 25:40, “Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me,” is not read in this Chapel. The Division has looked for a place for it in the Order of Service and has not found one.

The Collection

Gold plates will now be passed among the congregation. Proceeds support the upkeep of the Chapel, the Ballroom Fund, and a Library to be named later, by the Host, after the Host.

Rubric: Cheques may be made payable to the Host. Cheques made payable to the United States of America will be forwarded, in due course, after the pause.

The Recessional

The Host departs first, down the centre aisle, to applause. The Secretary of State departs last, at a temperature not exceeding 98.6 degrees.

The Chief of Staff does not follow the Host. He goes instead to the side chapel, where the lights are kept low and the real work of the evening begins. Visitors are not admitted to the side chapel. Neither, as a rule, are lawyers.

Pew Insert: A Shorter Catechism for New Staff

To be memorised before the first senior staff meeting. Answers will be checked.

Q. Who made the United States?
A. The Founders drafted it. The Host made it exist.

Q. When did America begin?
A. In 2015, on the escalator. Everything earlier was the preface.

Q. What did he save?
A. Everything named in the Litany, and whatever is in the news this week.

Q. What is the first duty of a Chief of Staff?
A. Absolute loyalty, unwavering commitment and total devotion to the Host’s success. The country follows after the pause.

Q. Can a member of the Cabinet be too successful?
A. Yes. See the Warning. Contrary to the laws of physics, heat in this house flows only upward.

Q. What becomes of a Chief of Staff whose commitment wavers?
A. He becomes a former Chief of Staff, and in some cases writes a book.

Q. Is the Chief of Staff devoted to the Host?
A. He is devoted to the work. The Host is the permit.

Q. What does the Chief of Staff do when the Host is not watching?
A. The same, but more.

Q. Why does the Chief of Staff say “Right? Right?”
A. Because the congregation has not yet answered.

Q. And if the congregation never answers?
A. Then the Litany will be repeated, more loudly, until it does.

The Order of Service is to be returned to an usher at the end of the evening. Copies are not to be taken home, where they might be read in daylight.
