THE COURT CIRCULAR Office of the Lord Chamberlain (a division of the Office of Retroactive Justification) Department of Follicular Succession LETTERS PATENT No. 2028-HA Date: 5 November 2026

1. Preamble

WHEREAS on 3 November 2026 a body styling itself “the electorate” did, without royal assent, remove both Houses of Parliament from His Majesty’s possession;

AND WHEREAS on 4 November His Majesty’s Vice-Regent did publish a manifesto of more than 600 words on the matter of who lost them;

AND WHEREAS His Majesty, asked who will succeed Him, replied, “Maybe he won’t run. I don’t know who’s going to run”;

NOW THEREFORE the Lord Chamberlain is commanded to settle, once and for all, the question of the Heir Apparent, and to do so before anyone suggests holding a primary.

2. Definitions

The Court reminds readers of the distinction preserved in all well-run monarchies.

An Heir Presumptive is next in line only until someone better comes along.

An Heir Apparent cannot be displaced by anyone.

A Hair Apparent is a claim so visible that it can be seen from Marine One at cruising altitude. In this realm, it is the only kind of claim the Sovereign has ever recognised.

3. On the Abolition of Voting as a Method of Succession

The Court notes with approval that the Vice-Regent has already settled the constitutional question. “Donald Trump will ultimately decide who’s going to be the Republican nominee,” he told a former press secretary last month, thereby removing the primary electorate from the process in a single sentence. The Court regards this as the most elegant piece of constitutional drafting since the Twenty-second Amendment, which it will come to presently.

Succession in this realm passes not by vote, nor strictly by blood, but by hair. The Court has accordingly drawn up the following standards.

4. The Royal Follicular Standards

Each claimant has been assessed against five criteria:

  1. Volume. The hair must occupy more space than the policy platform.

  2. The Sovereign Shade. A colour somewhere between gilt and marmalade, officially designated Pantone 1600.

  3. Wind resistance. The claimant must stand beneath the downdraft of Marine One for ninety seconds without the hair changing its position, its story or its party.

  4. Fealty. The claimant must be prepared to praise the Sovereign’s decisions, including the ones the claimant is currently blaming for the election.

  5. The Mirror Test. The claimant must hold two positions at once without visible strain. The Court’s reference standard was set by the Sovereign Himself on 4 November, when He agreed that the war had cost the party the election and that He “would have done nothing different.”

6. On the Vice-Regent’s Manifesto

The Court has examined the Vice-Regent’s post of 4 November with great care. It declared that “the reason we lost is the war,” and listed four things that would win back the realm’s lapsed subjects: end the war, focus on the cost of living, put America first, and change the relationship with Israel. Each of these, the Court observes, is a criticism of a decision taken by the Sovereign.

In an ordinary monarchy this would be treason. The Court finds, however, that the manifesto was shown to the Sovereign before publication and received positive feedback, and that the Sovereign subsequently described it as “largely true.” A denunciation of the King, pre-approved by the King, is therefore an act of loyalty. The Office of Retroactive Justification has filed it under “Fealty, Advanced.”

The Court further notes that the Crown Prince shared the manifesto within hours, with the inscription “Every word of this is 💯💯💯.” Under the customs of the realm this constitutes the Royal Seal, and the manifesto is now binding on everyone except the Sovereign.

The Court also notes the manifesto’s warning against “establishment politicians” who push “amnesty.” The Chancellor of Foreign Affairs is advised that no one in particular was meant, and that he should not take it personally, and that he should keep a copy.

7. The Sovereign’s Position

The Sovereign has advised the Court that He would “love to run again if I had the chance, but the language is very exacting.”

The language in question is the Twenty-second Amendment, which the Court classes as the realm’s Salic Law: an ancient and inconvenient rule limiting who may take the throne. The Court has referred it to the Office of Retroactive Justification, whose Division of Exacting Language reports that it has read the Amendment several times and that the language remains, regrettably, very exacting.

The Sovereign has further advised that a poll the other day showed He would beat everybody. The Court accepts this poll as authoritative, notwithstanding a larger poll conducted on 3 November in all fifty states.

8. Determination

Having weighed the claimants against the Royal Follicular Standards, the Lord Chamberlain determines as follows:

(a) There is no Heir Apparent. There is only a Hair Apparent, and it is already in residence.

(b) The Vice-Regent is confirmed as Heir Presumptive, on condition that he continue to blame the war for everything while agreeing that the war was correct.

(c) The Duke of Tallahassee is reminded that “extinction-level event” is language reserved for the Sovereign’s descriptions of His opponents.

(d) Succession by primary, caucus or general election remains abolished, the Vice-Regent having abolished it.

(e) The body styling itself “the electorate” is not recognised by this Court. The Lord Chamberlain notes, purely for the record, that it has now removed both Houses of Parliament, seven Senate seats, six of them in states His Majesty carried, five governorships, and Mr. Speaker Johnson, who has stepped aside to spend more time with his family.

The Court does not expect to hear from it again until 2028.
