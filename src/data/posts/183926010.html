Thirty-five years later, after Trump’s first year back in power, we watched that project fracture.

The Democratic Wager: What Year One Tested

Democracy makes an extraordinary claim: Free people, governing themselves through elections, rule of law, and constitutional constraints, can produce better outcomes than authoritarian systems.

For most of human history, this seemed absurd. Every successful empire was authoritarian. The American experiment changed that calculation. Then the postwar democratic expansion. By the 1990s, democracy seemed ascendant.

But the wager was always fragile. Democracy requires:

  • Institutional integrity: Courts, legislatures, bureaucracies functioning independently

  • Rule of law: Even the powerful face consequences

  • Free press: Information flows freely, power can be scrutinized

  • Electoral legitimacy: Losers accept results, winners transfer power peacefully

  • Constitutional constraints: Limits on power exist and are enforced

  • Shared reality: Citizens operate from common facts

  • Economic opportunity: Enough broadly shared prosperity that people believe the system works

When these elements erode, democracy collapses.

What we watched during Trump’s first year back:

Every element eroded over those twelve months. Institutions captured. Rule of law abandoned for the powerful. Press attacked as “enemy of the people.” Electoral legitimacy contested. Constitutional constraints ignored. Shared reality shattered. Economic opportunity concentrated upward. And the oversight mechanisms that sustain institutional integrity — the Inspectors General, the ethics offices, the administrative infrastructure — deliberately demolished.

The democratic wager failed over twelve months in its most powerful exemplar. And the world watched.

The counterfactual: Harris’s first year would have demonstrated that democratic institutions can resist authoritarian capture, that rule of law can survive stress, that free elections can remove failed leaders, that constitutional systems can self-correct. Not proving democracy is perfect — proving it’s resilient enough to survive challenges.

And we spent a year proving it’s not resilient enough.

The Authoritarian Alternative: What Year One Validated

Authoritarians make a competing claim: Democracy is messy, slow, weak. Strong leaders making decisions without democratic constraints can act decisively, implement policy efficiently, maintain stability, produce prosperity.

Even in America, democracy produces chaos, corruption, and authoritarian capture.

The counterfactual: Harris’s first year would have demonstrated that democratic institutions can resist capture, free elections can remove authoritarians, constitutional systems can self-correct.

Not proving democracy is perfect — proving it’s viable.

And we spent a year proving it’s not viable.

The Domino Theory Redux: What Twelve Months of Contagion Produced

The underlying insight of the domino theory was valid: Political systems influence each other. Success or failure in one nation affects calculations in others.

American influence on this cascade during year one:

When America elected Trump, every authoritarian felt vindicated. When Trump attempted a coup, every would-be autocrat took notes. When Trump faced no accountability throughout his first year back, every authoritarian leader learned: coups can be attempted without terminal consequences.

Brazil demonstrated the alternative in 2022 — Bolsonaro attempted Trump’s playbook and lost. Harris’s first year would have been the most powerful global demonstration that democratic resistance succeeds. Instead, Trump’s first year back proved authoritarian patience outlasts democratic resistance.

And we spent twelve months teaching that lesson to every authoritarian worldwide.
