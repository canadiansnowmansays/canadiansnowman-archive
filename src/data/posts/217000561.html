Talking Points Harmonization Ahead of Thursday’s State Visit DATE: Wednesday, 23 September 2026 DISTRIBUTION: All Podiums, All Toasts, All Staff Within Earshot of the President Between Now and Thursday Noon

1. PURPOSE. This memorandum exists to reconcile remarks delivered by the Principal on Tuesday, from the podium of the United Nations General Assembly, with the diplomatic posture required for Thursday’s state visit of President Xi Jinping of the People’s Republic of China. The Office notes, for the record, that it was given no advance warning that Tuesday’s remarks and Thursday’s summit would require reconciling, and would have appreciated one.

2. BACKGROUND. During an otherwise unrelated portion of Tuesday’s address concerning artificial intelligence — a section devoted primarily to the Principal’s preferred rebranding of the term “artificial intelligence” as “Super Intelligence,” or “S.I.” — the Principal stated, without prompting from any question, memo, or apparent operational necessity, that the United States is “leading now over China by a lot, and everyone else, and we’re going to keep it that way.” This remark was delivered to one hundred and ninety-three member states, several dozen accredited news organisations, and, per the Office’s best information, at least one staffer from the Chinese Mission to the United Nations who was almost certainly taking notes.

3. TALKING POINTS RETIRED EFFECTIVE IMMEDIATELY. The following formulations, while accurate transcriptions of remarks actually made, are no longer to be repeated, paraphrased, alluded to, or gestured toward in any setting where a member of the Chinese delegation might reasonably be present, which as of Thursday morning will include most rooms in Washington: — “Leading over China by a lot.” — Any restatement of the same sentiment using synonyms for “leading,” “China,” “a lot,” or all three. — The word “everyone” as used in the phrase “leading... over China by a lot, and everyone else,” which several staff have independently flagged as doing almost as much damage as the China reference itself.

4. APPROVED SUBSTITUTE LANGUAGE. The Office has prepared the following redlined draft of Thursday’s proposed toast, for review and, ideally, actual use:

“As I said just two days ago, we’re leading over China by a lot, and everyone else, and we’re going to keep it that way.” “The United States and China share a deep and abiding interest in the responsible advancement of emerging technologies, for the benefit of both our great peoples.”

“They’re not even close, frankly.” “We look forward to continued dialogue on matters of mutual technological interest.”

“I have a big decision to make on a lot of things, and I make good decisions, the best decisions, better than anyone.” “President Xi and I share a commitment to constructive engagement.”

The Office notes that the redlined version is eleven words longer and contains no verifiable claims whatsoever, which it considers, on balance, an improvement.

5. GUIDANCE SHOULD THE SUBJECT ARISE ANYWAY. Staff are reminded that the Principal cannot be pre-briefed out of an idea he considers to be going well. In the event Thursday’s remarks include any restatement of Tuesday’s posture — and the Office’s internal modelling puts this above a coin flip — the following fallback framing has been cleared: the comment was intended as a compliment to China’s own remarkable progress, on the theory that one cannot meaningfully be “ahead of” a country that is not, itself, moving very fast indeed. Staff are advised this framing has not been tested on an actual Chinese delegation and should be deployed only as a last resort, ideally by someone other than the Principal.

6. ITEMS THE PRINCIPAL SHOULD NOT BE REMINDED OF, ON THE THEORY THAT REMINDING MAKES IT WORSE. For internal awareness only; this section is not for briefing purposes: — That Thursday’s meeting was, per multiple press accounts, expected to “overshadow” the entire General Assembly session, a framing the Office considers no longer operative given Tuesday’s competing headlines. — That the phrase “we’re going to keep it that way” was delivered in the same address in which the Principal announced a rebranding campaign for an entire academic and industrial field, unilaterally, from the floor of an international body with no standing to approve or reject the rebrand. — That President Xi’s own remarks, whenever delivered, will be transcribed, translated, and circulated with the same care Tuesday’s remarks received, by an Office very much like this one, somewhere in Beijing.

7. SUMMARY. The Office recommends Thursday’s meeting proceed as scheduled. The Office further recommends that whoever drafts Friday’s readout begin drafting it now, in the conditional tense, with several versions prepared in advance depending on how many of Tuesday’s talking points make an unscheduled return appearance.

Distribution list attached. Please destroy after Thursday, or after the next unscheduled restatement, whichever comes first.
