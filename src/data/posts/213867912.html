The following is an original comedy bit. The premise — the United States as Canada’s overbearing new stepfather — is a well-worn one in stand-up, and it’s a good one, so this is a fresh routine built on it, with its own jokes, using this year’s actual headlines as the raw material.

TRANSCRIPT — “FAMILY MATTERS,” LIVE AT AN UNNAMED VENUE ON QUEEN STREET

COMEDIAN: So. My mom got a new boyfriend this year. Big guy. Loud. Owns a lot of buildings with his own name on them, which, if you’re keeping score at home, is never a great sign in a stepdad.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: First week he’s in the house, he redecorates. Doesn’t ask anybody. Just — new paint, new furniture, and then one day I come downstairs and he’s renamed the lake. We had a lake. Behind the house. Had a name for four hundred years. Guy’s been living with us a year and a half, he’s like, “this is Lake America now.” I said, “it’s touching water in two countries, my guy, you don’t get to just — “ He said “I already told the map app.” The map app already knew before I did. My own phone found out before I did.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: And you know what’s crazy? Half our neighbors’ phones went along with it. Not all of them. There’s one map app still holding out — bless them — and Mom’s boyfriend was furious, called them personally, and they basically told him, quote, “name it whatever you want at your leisure, sir, we’re keeping the old one.” And that company’s stock went up. Turns out “we didn’t cave” is a great marketing plan now. Who knew.

(laughter, applause)

COMEDIAN: But that’s not even the worst part. The worst part is the property line. Guy moves the fence. Doesn’t say anything, just — one morning there’s a new fence, and it’s further into our yard. And when I go ask him about it, he goes, “I’m not moving the fence, I’m just applying a tariff to your grass.” I go, “that’s not — that doesn’t make it not moving the fence.” He goes, “Section 338.” I said “what’s Section 338,” he goes “it’s the part where I can do this.”

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: And then, this is real, he starts itemizing things. He’s got a whole list. Dog muzzles. Christmas ornaments. Flags. FLAGS. He’s tariffing our flags. I said, “you’re taxing the thing we wave to show we’re happy to be your neighbors,” and he goes “should’ve thought of that before you had your own flag.”

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: And here’s the thing about my mom’s boyfriend, he’ll do this thing where he insults you and compliments you in the same sentence and you don’t figure out which one it was until you’re already in the car. He told my grandmother — my grandmother — that she used to be a real power player, “back in the day,” and now she’s basically nothing, and grandma said thank you. She said thank you. I said, “Grandma, that was an insult,” she goes, “he said I used to be powerful,” I said, “the sentence continued.”

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: He cut her allowance too, by the way. Grandma’s had the same milk money since before I was born — some very reasonable, boring arrangement, nobody’s getting rich off it — and he’s decided that’s “unfair,” that grandma’s milk money is somehow the reason his numbers don’t add up. Grandma’s eighty-one. She sells dairy to the co-op. That’s the whole scheme.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: And the friends he brings around now! Oh my god, the friends. He’s got this one buddy — invited him to the big family dinner, everybody’s there, and half the table’s like, “wait, THAT guy? He’s still doing the thing with the neighbor’s yard, the thing everyone agreed was bad?” And Mom’s boyfriend goes, “if I don’t let him at the table, how’s he ever gonna stop doing the yard thing,” and everybody just kind of looked at their plates. Meanwhile there’s another family friend — lovely woman, been coming to dinner for thirty years, lives two doors down — and he didn’t invite her this year. Just, not on the list. Nobody knows why. When someone asked he said something about her yard too, except her yard’s fine, everybody’s seen her yard, it’s a very normal yard.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: And then — this is the part that really got me — he brings some of his OWN business associates to the family dinner. Guys in suits. Introduces them to everybody. And when the neighbor kids ask if THEIR parents can bring people too, he goes, “no, this dinner’s for my associates specifically.” It’s HIS dinner, technically, he keeps reminding everyone, even though it’s supposed to be a dinner about how we’re all gonna get along.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: Dinner ends, by the way, and there’s no toast. No group photo even, half the family wouldn’t stand next to the guy with the yard problem, so they just — skipped the photo. Eighty years running that family’s had a group photo at the end of dinner. This year: nothing. Guy hosting the dinner gets up at the end and goes, “well, we couldn’t agree on the toast because someone at this table has a huge, unsustainable amount of leftovers they won’t share,” and everyone just kind of knew who he meant and nobody said anything.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: And the thing is — and I want to be fair here, because I think fairness is important — he’ll occasionally do something almost reasonable, and it throws the whole family off balance, because nobody knows what to do with it. Someone brought up the fence thing again recently and he goes, “you know, that probably should get sorted out between me and your mom directly, one of these days.” And everybody just went dead silent, because that’s the most normal sentence he’s said all year, and nobody trusts it.

(laughter)

COMEDIAN: Anyway. My mom says she’s happy. My mom says it’s complicated. My mom says a lot of things. But I’ll tell you this — the lake’s still called what it’s always been called, on our side of the fence. He can rename it on his phone all he wants. We know what the water’s called. We’ve known for four hundred years. He’s been here a year and a half.

(laughter, applause)

COMEDIAN: Thank you, you’ve been great, drive safe, and if anyone asks, it’s Lake Ontario.

(applause, blackout)

Author’s Note: Every specific bit in this routine is drawn directly from actual reporting this series has covered over the past year — the Section 338 tariff schedule, the Lake Ontario/”Lake America” renaming and MapQuest’s public refusal, the G20’s failed communiqué and boycotted photograph, South Africa’s exclusion from the summit, Russia’s invitation over European objections, and the American-executives-only access policy at the same gathering. The stepdad metaphor is borrowed, honestly, from a comedy tradition that’s used the “new stepdad” bit for family dynamics forever; everything hung on the frame here is original.
