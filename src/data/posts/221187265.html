Editor’s note: The filing below is invented. The facts it leans on are not. On Saturday, October 10, 2026, the National Park Service gave notice it would remove “up to 529” trees from East Potomac Golf Links and had felled at least 430 by nightfall, as NPR reported. Later counts put the total closer to 530. Judge Ana C. Reyes had required advance notice before any removal of more than ten trees. The Justice Department later moved to disqualify her for “histrionics” and asked for the hearing audio. On Sunday, October 18, the Government told the court the plan “is not final.” That same day President Trump posted a rendering of the finished course, and on Monday he announced that work would begin “IMMEDIATELY,” as the New York Times reported, while calling the judge “a crazed lunatic.” On October 26, the judge issued a preliminary injunction protecting every tree in the park. The trees have not commented. — S.G.

IN THE UNITED STATES DISTRICT COURT OF PUBLIC OPINION
FOR THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA (FAIRWAY DIVISION)

THE ROYAL LINKS AUTHORITY, acting by and through the Office of Retroactive Justification, Litigation Division,Movant,

v.

THE TREES OF EAST POTOMAC PARK STILL STANDING, their number disputed (the Government admitted to 430 felled; later counts approach the full 529; the trees’ own count is unavailable owing to the condition of their colleagues),Respondents.

Civil Action No. FORE-2026-0529

MOVANT’S EMERGENCY MOTION TO DISQUALIFY THE TREES, AND FOR RELEASE OF THE AUDIO

I. Introduction

The Crown brings this motion with the deepest regret and at the earliest available moment, which is to say after the work was done.

For more than a century, the Respondents have stood on federal land in plain view of the Washington Monument. They have done so without a permit, without a loyalty review, and in most cases without having been asked. They have shaded golfers of every party. They have watched administrations come and go. They have, in short, been present for everything, and the Crown submits that a witness who has seen everything cannot be impartial about anything.

The Crown therefore moves that the Respondents be disqualified from further participation in this matter, whether as parties, witnesses, scenery, or obstacles to a long iron.

II. Statement of Facts

  1. On Friday, October 9, 2026, the Government gave notice that it intended to remove up to 529 trees, in phases.

  2. On Saturday, October 10, 2026, the Government removed at least 430 trees, in one phase. The Crown regards the word “phases” as satisfied. A day has a morning and an afternoon.

  3. The removal took place on a federal holiday weekend. The Crown categorically rejects any suggestion that the timing was chosen. The timing chose itself.

  4. Golfers continued to play throughout. The Crown notes this as evidence that the removal was routine maintenance: nobody stops a round for routine maintenance.

  5. At an emergency hearing on Monday, October 12, held by videoconference because it was a holiday, one senior official joined from his car in a parking lot. The Crown considers this a demonstration of commitment. The Court appears to have considered it something else.

  6. The Court, displaying what the Department of Justice has described as histrionics, told Government counsel they had done their “absolute worst.” The Crown contests this finding. The Crown has not yet done its worst. The Crown has fourteen months.

  7. At the hearing of Monday, October 19, counsel for the Government advised the Court that “there is no plan to close the course.” On Wednesday, October 28, the Interior Department advised the Court that it was working to close the course on November 15, the President having twice directed it to. The Crown regards these statements as entirely consistent. On October 19 there was no plan. By October 28 there was a direction, which is not the same thing as a plan, and is considerably faster.

III. Argument

A. The Respondents Were Appointed by a Previous Administration

Not one of the Respondents was planted by the current President. Several were planted under administrations of the opposing party. A number of the cherries trace their lineage, spiritually if not botanically, to a 1912 arrangement with the Mayor of Tokyo, which the Crown can only describe as foreign influence in a federal landscape.

A judge appointed by President Biden may not, the Department contends, sit in judgment of the President’s golf course. A fortiori, an oak appointed by President Coolidge may not stand in its way.

B. The Respondents Have Displayed Foliar Impropriety

Since the commencement of this litigation, and particularly in the past ten days, the Respondents have been observed:

  • shedding leaves on Government property, in October, in what can only be described as an unorthodox and regrettable display of seasonal theatre;

  • rustling, at times at a near-shouting level, when the wind came off the Potomac;

  • standing in exactly the same place they stood before the litigation began, which the Crown interprets as stubbornness and the Court interprets as evidence.

A tree that cannot keep its composure in autumn cannot be trusted with a fairway.

C. The Respondents Have Questioned the Government’s Intelligence

The Court has been accused of unfairly questioning the intelligence of Government attorneys. The Crown wishes the record to reflect that the trees did it first. Each of the 430 departed Respondents left behind a stump bearing growth rings: a sworn, dated, year-by-year record of exactly how long it had been there and exactly what the Government knew. The Crown regards this as a selective quotation of history, and a weaponized one.

D. The Removal Restored Fidelity to the Original Design

The Superintendent has advised the Court that, over a century, different plantings were permitted on the course with no coherent adherence to the original design, and that the Park Service intends to stay true to East Potomac’s identity as a links course.

The Crown would gently remind the Court what a links course is. It is a course built on bare, windswept coastal ground. It does not have trees. It has never had trees. The trees, it follows, were never the course. They were the deviation. The Government did not cut down 430 trees. It corrected 430 errors in the historical record, and it did so in a single afternoon, which is more than can be said for the National Archives.

E. The Plan Is Not Final and Will Begin Immediately

The Respondents’ counsel has made much of an apparent tension in the Government’s position. On Sunday, October 18, the Government advised the Court in writing that no final plan exists and that the details remain subject to review. On the same Sunday, the President published a picture of the finished course and set a closing date of November 15. On Monday, he announced that work was scheduled to begin IMMEDIATELY.

The Crown sees no tension. The plan is not final. The plan is also beginning. These statements are reconciled by the doctrine of Retroactive Finality, under which an agency action becomes final at the moment it can no longer be undone. Applying the doctrine here, the felled trees are final. The standing ones are pending.

In her opinion of October 26, the Court observed that the Department was, in effect, asking it not to believe the President when he said a cabinet secretary had “developed” the plan. The Crown confirms that this is its position. The President is to be believed in all matters except those he announces.

F. Communications With the Trees Are Privileged

At the hearing of Monday, October 19, the Court sought to inquire into Government emails and meetings about the removal. The Crown asserts privilege over all such communications on the ground that, at some point, a lawyer was copied. The Crown further asserts privilege over its communications with the trees, on the ground that the trees were present when they took place. Asked who authorized the removal, the Crown responded that it did not know, but could find out.

The Crown has since found out. The removal was approved on Tuesday, October 6, at 5:39 p.m. The Court was notified on Friday, October 9, at 4:55 p.m., at the start of a holiday weekend. The Crown regards the intervening seventy-one hours as a reasonable period of quiet reflection, and the timing of the notice as a courtesy, so as not to disturb the Court’s long weekend with anything it could act on.

G. The Court Has Disclaimed Speaking for the Trees

In September, the Court stated on the record: “I am not the Lorax. I do not speak for the trees. I am a judge.” The Crown accepted this assurance in good faith and proceeded accordingly, by chainsaw.

The Crown now notes that the Court’s injunction of October 26 protects every tree in East Potomac Park, and respectfully asks the Court to explain, in no more than seventy pages, for whom exactly it was speaking.

IV. Request for Release of the Audio

The Department has asked that the audio of the Court’s hearing be released so that the public may hear the Court’s tone. The Crown joins that request and extends it.

The Crown moves for the release of all audio recordings made at East Potomac Golf Links on Saturday, October 10, 2026, between sunrise and sunset, with the following exception: any portion of the recording consisting of chainsaws, which the Crown regards as background noise and not probative of anyone’s temperament.

V. Relief Requested

The Crown respectfully requests that the Court:

  1. Disqualify the Respondents from further participation in this matter;

  2. Reclassify the 430 stumps as tee markers, effective nunc pro tunc to October 10;

  3. Declare that a federal judge is not in charge of golf course maintenance, the Crown having searched the Constitution and found no clause for it (the Crown also found no clause for the President being in charge of golf course maintenance, but has elected not to raise the point);

  4. Deem the ten business days’ written notice required by the Court’s injunction to have been given on Sunday, October 18, 2026, by Truth Social, and again on Monday, October 26, shortly before midnight, also by Truth Social; and

  5. Grant such further relief as the Court deems just, or, failing that, as the President has already announced.

CERTIFICATE OF SERVICE

The undersigned certifies that a copy of this motion was served upon each of the Respondents by being nailed to it. In the case of the 430 Respondents no longer standing, service was effected upon the stump.

Respectfully submitted,

/s/ Office of Retroactive Justification, Litigation Division

STOP PRESS

The real hearing on Monday, October 19, went much as the motion above imagines. As Roger Parloff’s hearing diary for Lawfare records, Judge Reyes asked the Justice Department’s lawyers to come to the podium and sign the Government’s filing. The Associate Attorney General told them not to, and none did. An Interior Department deputy chief of staff testified that the President’s social media post was the only place he had seen the November 15 closing date. The Government asserted privilege whenever the questions approached the Secretary of the Interior or the White House. “There is no universe,” the judge said, “where all of this just coincidentally happened at the same time.”

On Monday, October 26, she issued a 70-page preliminary injunction. It found the Government had likely broken the National Environmental Policy Act, barred it from cutting or damaging any tree in East Potomac Park, and required ten business days’ written notice before the course is closed or irreversibly altered, for as long as the case lasts. The ruling, she wrote, “does not end the round: it simply marks the ball.”

The President replied before midnight, as Forbes reported, calling the ruling unconstitutional and announcing that the course would close on November 15 regardless. The next day he added that the course, in its current form, is “extremely dangerous to play,” owing to “a constant barrage of people being hit with wayward shots,” as Golf.com reported. The Government filed its appeal the same Tuesday. On Wednesday, the Interior Department told the court it was working to close the course on November 15, as the President had twice directed.

On Friday, October 30, Judge Reyes appointed a special master. As the New York Times reported, the outside investigator will examine whether the holiday-weekend tree removal was “designed to evade judicial review,” whether the Justice Department falsely assured the court that nothing had been decided, and whether the Interior Department and the Park Service were truly kept in the dark or had been briefed all along. The appointment followed a standoff with Stanley Woodward Jr., the Associate Attorney General, over whether he had personally misled the court when he said there was no immediate plan to close the course. Mr. Woodward maintained that, even after the President’s posts, no plan had been made final. The judge stressed that she was making no finding of misconduct, though she had concluded earlier that one Interior official, Gregory Wischer, had probably withheld some of what he knew.

The Court’s order protecting the trees has no end date. The President’s closing date for the course is Sunday, November 15. The Crown’s position on the gap between those two dates is that the trees are a hazard, the golfers are a hazard, and the only thing on the course not presently endangering anyone is the plan, which does not exist. The Crown welcomes the special master, and will be pleased to make the plan available for interview.
