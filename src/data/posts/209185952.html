STORE CLOSING — FINAL HOURS — ACTING MANAGEMENT MUST CLEAR REMAINING INVENTORY BEFORE PERMANENT MANAGER ARRIVES

Attention, American public: the Office of the Director of National Intelligence is undergoing what its outgoing acting director calls “smart” and “careful” restructuring, and we would like you to know that everything — analysts, career staff, institutional memory, whatever the opposite of institutional memory is — must go, and go now, because the man doing the going is going too.

On Tuesday, hours before the Senate voted to confirm his own replacement, Acting Director Bill Pulte announced a fifth and, in his words, “near final” round of terminations: an additional 30 percent staff reduction from where the agency stood only weeks earlier. He called this good news. He posted it the way a car dealership posts a sign that says EVERY VEHICLE MUST GO, TODAY ONLY, MANAGEMENT HAS LOST ITS MIND — except in this case management had, in fact, lost its job, and was spending its last morning on the premises taking the shelving with it. By that evening, the Senate had confirmed Jay Clayton as the permanent director, 51 to 47, along party lines — the sale’s final bell, rung the same day the last markdown went up.

This is not Mr. Pulte’s only job, incidentally — he also runs the Federal Housing Finance Agency, which he will continue doing after his intelligence-community moonlighting concludes. One imagines the exit interview: “Before you go, Bill, anything else you’d like to reduce?” “Just the ceiling insulation, if nobody’s looking.”

A HISTORY OF THE MARKDOWN, FOR CONTEXT

This is not, credit where due, an isolated liquidation event. It is closer to a chain of stores, each new manager clearing more shelf space than the last. Tulsi Gabbard, in an earlier tenure, cut the agency’s size by roughly 40 percent before resigning in June — the “Founder’s Edition” clearance, if you like, the one that set the template. Mr. Pulte was brought in afterward specifically because, as the president reportedly noted, he would be “less shackled” to cut further, being only temporary himself — a species of honesty rare enough in Washington that it deserves its own plaque: here lies an administration that openly explained it wanted a manager with nothing to lose, because a manager with something to lose might hesitate before losing it for you.

By the time Jay Clayton — confirmed as permanent director on July 28th — walks through the door, he will be inheriting an agency roughly halved from where it stood a few months prior — coordinating the work of seventeen other intelligence agencies with, depending on whose baseline you use, several hundred fewer people to do the coordinating.

PRODUCT DISCLAIMERS

“Refocusing on national security, the law, and the statute” — a phrase that sounds, on first read, like a mission statement, and functions, on second read, as a synonym for “smaller.” No inventory of what specifically fell outside “national security, the law, and the statute” has been made public. The agency’s exact size remains classified, which means the public is being asked to trust the percentage without ever seeing the number it was a percentage of.

“The Intelligence Community must protect the American people, NOT the political whims of the bloated and corrupt elite class” — a sentence structured to make a staffing cut sound like a moral victory rather than a headcount. Whether the departing analysts were, in fact, protecting political whims or simply doing intelligence analysis is a distinction the sale flyer does not trouble itself with.

“5th and Near Final Round” — note the “near.” Even in the closing announcement, on the final morning, with the successor confirmed and waiting, the sale reserves the right to extend itself. Somewhere, a shelf is still not empty enough.

CUSTOMER TESTIMONIALS

Collected, in the spirit of full transparency, from the sale’s own promotional materials.

“Smartly, and carefully.” — Mr. Pulte, describing his own approach, in the same statement announcing a near-final round of terminations conducted in his last days on the job, with the agency’s total size still classified and the specific roles eliminated undisclosed. A shopper is generally allowed to describe their own bargain-hunting as smart. It is a stranger claim to make about firing several hundred people from an intelligence-coordination office without saying which several hundred, or coordinating what, afterward.

“Less shackled.” — The president, explaining in advance why he wanted a temporary director for this particular job: not because Mr. Pulte had special expertise in intelligence architecture, but because a man who knows he isn’t staying has less reason to worry about what he breaks on the way out. This is, in its way, a remarkably candid theory of management — hire for indifference to consequences, because indifference to consequences is what the deepest cuts require.

“Protect the American people, NOT the political whims of the bloated and corrupt elite class.” — A formulation that assumes its own conclusion. It does not identify which analysts, exactly, were serving whims rather than statutes; it simply asserts the distinction and lets the percentage do the arguing. A 30 percent reduction is a number. Whether it corresponds to genuine bloat or genuine capability is a question the sale flyer is not equipped to answer, because the sale flyer is not actually in the business of answering it.

THE FINE PRINT NOBODY ASKED FOR

There is an old commercial law about liquidation sales: the deepest discounts appear right before the doors close for good, because the person running the register that week has no stake in what the shelves look like once new management walks in. Mr. Pulte’s own dual role — simultaneously running the Federal Housing Finance Agency and moonlighting through the nation’s intelligence-coordination office — makes this dynamic unusually literal. He has, by his own account, another full-time job waiting on the other side of this one. The incentive to leave the shelves fuller for his successor was never obviously there in the first place.

This is worth remembering the next time an agency head is praised in public for how fast and how deep they were willing to cut on their way out the door. Depth and speed are not, by themselves, virtues. They are simply easier to achieve when you will not be the one standing behind the counter explaining the empty shelves to the next customer.

A WORD FOR THE NEW MANAGER

Mr. Clayton now inherits an office that has been aggressively “smartly and carefully” reduced by two consecutive temporary occupants, each of whom made a point of doing the reducing themselves rather than leaving the decision to whoever showed up permanently and had to live with the consequences. His own confirmation hearing did him no particular favors either — Democrats who had initially welcomed the pick soured on him after he declined to directly acknowledge the winner of the 2020 election, a fact Senate Intelligence Vice Chairman Mark Warner cited when he said he’d been “bitterly disappointed” by Clayton’s performance in front of the committee, even while extending, on confirmation, the traditional hope that the concerns would prove unfounded. This is, in its way, the ballroom problem in miniature: an institution reshaped before the person meant to answer for it ever gets a vote, so that by the time oversight arrives, there is considerably less left to oversee — and the person now arriving to oversee it walked in already trailing his own credibility questions.

The lights, presumably, are still on. Whether anyone remains inside to answer them is a separate inventory question, and the receipt, as always, has already been signed.

A CLOSING INVENTORY

Add it up, and the arithmetic gets uncomfortable fast. Gabbard: roughly 40 percent, on her way out in June. Pulte: an additional 30 percent on top of that, on his way out this week. Two acting or outgoing directors, each cutting deepest at the exact moment they had the least reason to care what the agency looked like afterward, handing the newly confirmed permanent director — one who will actually have to answer for the office’s performance going forward — an ODNI reduced by some compounding fraction of its original self, size still classified, roles eliminated still undisclosed.

The public is being asked to take it on faith that what’s left is leaner rather than merely smaller — that “refocused” and “gutted” happen, in this instance, to look identical from the outside. Perhaps they are the same thing. Perhaps they aren’t. Either way, the man who will have to live with the difference wasn’t the one holding the scissors.
