Sunday night at MetLife Stadium, Spain lifted the World Cup — and, for the second year running, had to do it with an uninvited guest still standing on the podium. This week’s satire comes in two parts, same story, two different case files: first, a nature-documentary account of the pattern itself; then, the formal rap sheet documenting exactly how often this stadium has seen this exact behavior before. Read separately or back to back — either way, the file stays open.

[A METAL DESK. A SINGLE OVERHEAD LAMP. A MANILA FOLDER SLAPS DOWN, STAMPED IN RED: “PATTERN OF CONDUCT — REVIEW.”]

SUBJECT: Donald J. Trump KNOWN ALIASES: POTUS, 47, “The Alpha,” Guest of Honor (uninvited) JURISDICTION: MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford, New Jersey PATTERN UNDER REVIEW: Unlawful loitering at trophy ceremonies not his own

INCIDENT NO. 1 DATE: July 13, 2025 LOCATION: MetLife Stadium COMPLAINANT: FIFA Club World Cup, Chelsea Football Club SUMMARY OF OFFENSE: Subject inserted himself onto the trophy podium following Chelsea’s 3-0 win over Paris Saint-Germain. Subject was observed presenting the trophy to team captain Reece James, then failing to exit the stage as choreographed. Subject remained in frame for the entire trophy lift. Three separate players gave statements afterward; all three, independently, described the same sequence — told he would present and leave, observed instead that he stayed. Full witness testimony is on file under separate cover (see companion case, “Wild Kingdom,” filed same jurisdiction).

RESPONDING OFFICER: G. Infantino, FIFA President, acting in an unofficial handler capacity. Subject was eventually maneuvered to the rear of the stage. No arrests made. No lessons, evidently, learned.

INCIDENT NO. 2 DATE: July 19, 2026 LOCATION: MetLife Stadium (same address as Incident No. 1 — subject appears to favor this location specifically) COMPLAINANT: FIFA World Cup Final, Spain National Team SUMMARY OF OFFENSE: Subject and Officer Infantino entered the field to a chorus of booing, measured by on-site instruments at approximately 84 decibels — six decibels above the stadium’s ambient crowd noise, meaning the disapproval was, by any reasonable evidentiary standard, audible and specific. Subject proceeded to distribute medals to the Spanish national team, then, in a maneuver consistent with Incident No. 1, remained on the podium for the trophy lift despite Officer Infantino’s repeated and increasingly visible attempts to relocate him. Officer Infantino was observed jogging — actual foot speed, not a brisk walk — across the podium in pursuit of compliance.

AGGRAVATING FACTOR: Subject had, weeks prior, at a separate proceeding involving NATO, described the victim nation as “a wasted cause,” stating he intended to “do any trade with Spain” no longer. Subject’s decision to personally hang gold medals around the necks of that same nation’s players is entered into the record as a probable contradiction, though subject has shown no history of being troubled by this category of inconsistency.

PRIOR RELATED CONDUCT, SAME EVENING: Subject was separately overheard boasting, at a reception two days earlier, about having influenced a disputed red-card decision against a U.S. player earlier in the tournament, citing his personal rapport with Officer Infantino as the mechanism. This claim is unverified and, per department policy, is noted but not treated as established fact — subject has a documented history of claiming credit regardless of verification.

VICTIM IMPACT STATEMENT: None obtained. Spain’s captain, Rodri, elected to lift the trophy regardless of subject’s continued presence on stage, a response investigators are characterizing as “the only available option” rather than tacit approval.

INCIDENT NO. 3 (EVIDENCE TAMPERING, BOTH DIRECTIONS) DATE: July 19-20, 2026 LOCATION: Same as Incident No. 2, aftermath SUMMARY OF OFFENSE: Following the events described in Incident No. 2, both FIFA’s official account and the victim nation’s football federation released celebratory images from which subject had been cropped or otherwise excluded, selecting frames captured only after subject had departed the stage. A broadcaster’s open microphone captured the intent in advance: “they can edit him out of that.” They did.

COUNTER-OFFENSE, SAME EVENING: Subject’s own office subsequently circulated a separate set of photographs in which subject appears prominently — in two instances, as the sole subject in focus — with the victorious team relegated to the background or omitted. Investigators note this represents an unusual escalation: not merely a refusal to leave the frame in person, but a retroactive dispute over the frame itself, conducted entirely after the fact, by both parties, in public.

INVESTIGATOR’S NOTE: This office has encountered subjects who dispute the facts. It has rarely encountered a case where both parties independently edited the same event into two irreconcilable photographic records, neither of which is false, exactly, so much as selective. File both under “Exhibit A” and “Exhibit A, Alternate Version.”

PATTERN ANALYSIS

Three incidents on file, the third arguably stranger than the first two combined. Same stadium. Same handler. Same refusal to exit on cue, followed, in the most recent case, by a dispute over the historical record itself. Same booing, rising each time rather than diminishing, which investigators note is the opposite of what usually deters repeat conduct. In each case, the trophy in question belonged to someone else, the ceremony had a script, and the subject declined to follow it in the specific and consistent way of someone who has never once been made to feel that a room's schedule applied to him — and who, this time, was apparently unwilling to let the room's photographers have the final word either. Investigators reviewing this file are directed to the companion case, "Wild Kingdom," for a fuller behavioral account; this file concerns itself only with the pattern, not the psychology behind it.

RECOMMENDATION: File remains open. Given the recurrence at the identical venue, investigators recommend flagging MetLife Stadium itself for enhanced monitoring during any future trophy ceremony. Subject is not considered a flight risk. Subject is, if anything, the opposite — a stay risk, chronic, with no indication the pattern will not continue at the next available trophy, whichever sport happens to be holding one.

CASE STATUS: Ongoing. Some things, per the file, do not need re-investigating so much as re-confirming.
