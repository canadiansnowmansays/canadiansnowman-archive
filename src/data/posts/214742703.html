FROM: Office of Nomenclature and Legacy Affairs TO: All Fifty (50) States, Five (5) Territories, and the District Formerly Known as Columbia RE: Timeline for Transition from “United States of America” to “Trumpland” CLASSIFICATION: Extremely Enchanting

A Note on Sourcing

The Office wishes to state, before proceeding, that everything in Stages One through Three below is not satire. The Gulf of Mexico really was renamed by executive order. Lake Ontario really was renamed by executive order, on August 27, tied explicitly to the collapsed Canada trade deal. And New Mexico really did appear on the President’s own Truth Social account this month, “Mexico” struck through in red marker, captioned as a state improved by having its name replaced with the word “America.” The image, this Office notes with some professional embarrassment, was originally posted five days earlier by an unrelated account as a joke about where this logic obviously ends. The President and the White House reposted it anyway, credited to no one, laughing, apparently, at a punchline written about him rather than by him.

Only what follows from here — Trumpland, the Drumpf ban, the currency, the crane — remains, for now, satire. The Office would like this distinction on the record, mostly because the distinction is getting harder to maintain.

Background

The rebranding programme has proceeded in careful, escalating stages, each one softening the public for the next:

Stage One: The Gulf of Mexico was renamed the Gulf of America by executive order, on paper, with minimal fanfare and maximal cartography. Mapmakers grumbled. The Gulf, being a body of water, did not.

Stage Two: Lake Ontario — a body of water this Office notes is shared with a neighbouring country that did not consent to, request, or particularly appreciate the gesture, and roughly a third of which by surface area remains, geographically, theirs — was renamed Lake America by executive order. Senator Martin Heinrich has since published a short list of names he intends to keep using regardless, of which Lake Ontario is one of exactly three.

Stage Three: New Mexico. Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham noted, not unreasonably, that the state’s name predates the country currently trying to change it by roughly two hundred and fifty years, and suggested the whole exercise was timed to distract from a war in Iran. Deb Haaland, the Democratic nominee for governor, was more concise: not for sale. The Office notes these objections were filed swiftly, in full sentences, by people whose actual jobs this affects — which the meme, regrettably, did not anticipate.

Stage Four: The wrecking ball. New Mexico’s decades-old “Land of Enchantment” welcome signage was removed by crane, in broad daylight, and replaced with signage reading “Welcome to New America,” to the visible delight of several thousand social media accounts with egg avatars, several of whom appeared unaware that the original meme they were celebrating had been a joke at their expense to begin with.

Having proven the concept works on water, then on a state that objected loudly and was ignored anyway, the Office has determined it is ready to scale nationally.

Proposed Timeline

Phase One (Complete): Rename large bodies of water. Low cost, no wrecking ball required, mild diplomatic incident optional.

Phase Two (Complete): Float a state renaming via social media, credit unclear, legal mechanism nonexistent.

Phase Three (Complete): Demolish the physical signage anyway, mechanism or no mechanism. Replace with signs featuring nothing that could be mistaken for a vegetable.

Phase Four (Current): Rename the country itself. Leading candidates, in order of internal polling:

  1. Trumpland — front-runner. Evokes theme park. Evokes ownership. Evokes a gift shop.

  2. New America — already field-tested in the desert; audience-approved; unfortunately also the name of at least four defunct think tanks and one 1970s prog-rock album.

  3. The United States of Trump — rejected on the grounds that it sounds like a hostile takeover, which several cabinet members noted was “kind of the point, no?”

Phase Five (Pending Legal Review): Correct spelling. The Office wishes to state, for the permanent record, in eighteen-point bold, underlined twice, laminated and mounted at every future ribbon-cutting: it is TRUMP, not DRUMPF. The ancestral Bavarian spelling has been retired. Anyone caught issuing signage, currency, coinage, commemorative plates, or skywriting reading “Drumpfland” will be reassigned to the New Mexico wrecking-ball crew, indefinitely, without chili peppers.

Timing Considerations

The Office notes with some enthusiasm that this initiative arrives conveniently ahead of the November midterms, a scheduling coincidence the Office insists is entirely unrelated to any electoral calculation whatsoever, the same way the Grand Canyon is unrelated to erosion.

Cost estimates for replacing fifty state welcome signs, several thousand highway markers, all federal letterhead, the currency, the passports, Mount Rushmore’s Wikipedia infobox, and one (1) very large sign somewhere near Palm Beach have not yet been finalized, but early figures suggest the number will be large, round, and paid for by someone who is not currently in this memo.

Anticipated Objections

The Office is aware that renaming a 250-year-old constitutional republic after one living person, mid-term, using a wrecking ball as the visual metaphor, may strike some observers as symbolically on the nose. The Office would like to clarify that this is not a metaphor. This is simply what the sign says now.

A Note on New Mexico Specifically

The Office would be remiss not to flag, for internal purposes only, a small tactical concern regarding the pilot state that the officials quoted above did not spell out for us directly, presumably out of professional courtesy.

New Mexico is roughly half Hispanic, home to nineteen Pueblo nations, and — not for nothing — a state Republicans have spent the better part of two decades trying, and largely failing, to win statewide. Removing four centuries of a place’s name by crane, on video, weeks before a midterm election, in a state where persuading exactly the voters most likely to find this insulting is not obviously a winning play. Early focus-group data (three men in a truck, filmed for content) suggests enthusiasm. Actual New Mexican reaction has been somewhat harder to source, mostly because the calls are not being returned.

The Office recommends, at minimum, that Nevada be considered for the next pilot instead. Nevada has no chili peppers to defend and, more importantly, no memory longer than a casino’s.

Next Steps

Awaiting sign-off from the relevant authority, who has indicated a strong personal preference for gold lettering, a minimum fourteen-point font on his own name, and a groundbreaking ceremony that involves, once again, a crane.
