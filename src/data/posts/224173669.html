“Power concedes nothing without a demand. It never did and it never will.” (Frederick Douglass, 1857)

This series began on November 5, 2025, the day after California voted to answer a Texas gerrymander with one of its own. It was meant to be about maps. It did not stay there.

What followed was a sustained attempt to decide who votes, whose vote counts and who gets to say so: the demand for every state’s voter rolls, the seizure of Fulton County’s ballots, the fall of the Voting Rights Act in Callais, the order on mail ballots, the Justice Department’s monitors at the polls. Much of it failed. This series is largely about why: judges, clerks, state officials and, on November 3, 2026, the voters themselves.

Every essay is listed below in order, with a one-line description. New parts are added as they are posted.
