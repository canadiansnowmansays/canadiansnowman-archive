Satire. The words attributed to Donald Trump, Pope Leo XIV and Pete Hegseth are real. The Family, its minutes, its Commission and its fight card are not.

Part One: Minutes of a Sit-Down

Convened: Saturday, October 31, 2026, the back room, Palm Beach
Chair: The Don
Present: The Consigliere (minutes); Pete, Capo of the Department of War; two associates from Communications
Reserved but empty: One chair, place card reading “Louis”

Item 1. The Disrespect.

The Consigliere read into the record the Don’s statement of April 12, 2026, in which the Don called the parish Don “WEAK on Crime, and terrible for Foreign Policy,” and added, in front of reporters, that he was “not a big fan.”

The Consigliere then read into the record the parish Don’s reply. There was none. The parish Don got on a plane to Algeria the next day for eleven days in Africa.

The Don asked what kind of man does not answer back. The Consigliere said, in his experience, a man who is planning something. The room agreed this was concerning.

The Don noted for the record that he had also said the parish Don would not be in the Vatican if the Don were not in the White House. The Consigliere confirmed the parish Don had not thanked him for this either.

Item 2. The Problem.

The Consigliere summarized developments since April. On October 13, the parish Don had surgery to remove a cancerous growth and the lower lobe of a lung. He announced the diagnosis himself. He took a telephone call from Vladimir Putin in his recovery room. He was out of hospital in under a week. On Friday, November 6, he departs for Argentina, Peru and Uruguay: more than a dozen flights and 19,000 miles.

The Don observed that he also takes Putin’s calls and has never needed to be in hospital to do it. The Consigliere wrote this down.

The Consigliere was then asked to find a comparable itinerary in the Don’s own diary. The Consigliere asked for more time.

Item 3. The Chair.

The Don asked why there was an empty chair. The Consigliere explained that it had been reserved for Louis Prevost, the parish Don’s elder brother, whom the Don had publicly said he liked “much better,” on the grounds that Louis is “all MAGA.” The Consigliere noted that Louis had not been consulted about joining the Family’s corner, and that, in the Consigliere’s long experience, a man’s brother is a poor place to look for a cornerman.

The chair remained empty for the duration.

Item 4. The Prayer.

The Capo proposed opening the bout with the prayer he recited at the Pentagon in March, asking God to “break the teeth of the ungodly.” The Consigliere noted that the line comes from Psalm 3, that the parish Don has presumably read the other hundred and forty-nine, and that the Family might wish to avoid a scripture contest against a man who does them for a living.

The Capo said he would pray louder.

Resolved: That the question of toughness be settled in the ring. That the Family promote the bout, run the weigh-in, appoint the judges and score the rounds. That a fight card be drawn up forthwith.

Moved: The Don. Seconded: The Don. Carried.

Brother vs. Brother. Cancelled. The Family billed Louis Prevost as “all MAGA.” Louis was not consulted. Promoters are reminded that endorsing a man’s sibling is not the same as owning his corner.

Part Three: The Weigh-In

The weigh-in was held in the Gold Corner’s ballroom, under the Gold Corner’s chandeliers, on the Gold Corner’s scale.

The Gold Corner arrived on time, weighed in, and described the number as “the best number anyone has ever seen on a scale.” He then took questions for fifty minutes, none of them about the bout.

The White Corner sent apologies. He was in a pre-trip briefing. When the Family’s representative asked whether the parish Don understood that missing a weigh-in meant forfeiting the title, the Vatican’s reply, in full, was that the trip to South America would go ahead as planned.

The Family’s representative asked whether this was a threat. He was told it was a schedule.

The Consigliere notes for the record a moment from the previous year, when Queen Rania of Jordan asked the parish Don, out of genuine concern, whether it was safe to go to Lebanon. The parish Don replied, “Well, we’re going.” He went. The Consigliere has started to suspect this is simply how the man answers questions.

Part Four: The Judges’ Scorecards

The Family appointed three judges. All three scored every round to the Gold Corner. The Commission, an independent body the Family forgot to dissolve, reviewed the scorecards and wishes to put two findings on the record.

First, the Commission credits the Gold Corner with one undisputed round: July 13, 2024, in Butler, Pennsylvania, when he was shot, got back up bloodied, and raised his fist. That round was real, and the Commission scores it his. The Family has asked that this single round be scored as all twelve. The Commission declined.

Second, the Commission notes that the White Corner’s record consists almost entirely of rounds nobody watched: a typhoid ward in northern Peru, a parish where the Shining Path was leaving bodies in the street, a compound in Jos, Nigeria, in November 2001, two months after hundreds died in sectarian fighting there, where he flew in to open his order’s first African province. He did not post about any of it. For forty years, he mostly did not mention it.

The Commission observes that the Family’s idea of toughness is measured by what a man is willing to break, and the parish Don’s by what he is willing to carry. The two are not scored on the same card.

Part Five: Ruling of the Commission

The bout will not take place.

The first bell is scheduled for Friday, November 6, 2026. At that hour, the White Corner will be at the airport, short one lung lobe, with nineteen thousand miles of flights ahead of him, on his way back to the continent where a young friar once caught typhoid and decided to stay.

The Gold Corner has already drafted his declaration of victory by forfeit for Truth Social.

Ruling: No contest. One fighter will be leaving the building. The other will never enter the ring, because he has somewhere to be.

Appeal: The Family has filed an appeal in advance. Grounds: the opponent is expected to decline to hit back, which the Family considers a form of cheating.

The Consigliere has been asked to find a precedent. The Consigliere has asked for more time.
