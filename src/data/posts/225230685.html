Editor’s note: The following onboarding packet was recovered at 10:25 a.m. Eastern on Monday, November 16, 2026, one minute after The New York Times sent its breaking-news alert announcing that Robert Pattinson will be the next James Bond, ending a seven-year casting search that ran longer than most papal conclaves and many marriages. It was found face down in a printer tray at Amazon MGM Studios, wedged between a Prime Video ad-tier pricing memo and a box of complimentary cufflinks. It is reproduced here in full, minus the pages that self-destructed, which, we are told, is a Mission: Impossible thing and a sore point in the building.

A note on scheduling: Satire in this series normally keeps to Sundays, alongside church, brunch and the weekly post-mortem on the Leafs. Today it does not. Some news can wait six days. This is not that news. Summits can idle, wars can pause for the commercial break and the bond market (the other one) can fend for itself. Empires have risen and fallen without settling who would play 007 next, and after seven years of suspense ours very nearly did. Holding the news of the most important casting decision of the decade until Sunday would have been a dereliction of duty serious enough to earn a summons to M's office. The regular Sunday Satire will appear as usual. Consider this an emergency broadcast.

AMAZON MGM STUDIOS: DOUBLE-O SECTION New Associate Onboarding Packet: Agent 007 (Seventh Generation) Classification: For Your Eyes Only (and Alexa’s)

Section 1: Welcome to His Majesty’s Secret Service

Dear Mr. Pattinson,

Congratulations, and welcome aboard. On behalf of everyone at Amazon MGM Studios, M, Q, Miss Moneypenny, and the customer-obsession team that now owns all three of them, we are delighted you have accepted the role of James Bond.

You join a distinguished line. Sean Connery established the brand. George Lazenby lasted one film and has been answering questions about it ever since. Roger Moore raised a single eyebrow for twelve years and called it acting, and nobody argued. Timothy Dalton brooded before brooding was fashionable. Pierce Brosnan restored the shine. Daniel Craig spent five films, from Casino Royale in 2006 to No Time to Die in 2021, proving that Bond could bleed, grieve and, ultimately, die, a precedent our legal team has asked us to describe as “a one-time, limited-edition event.”

We note that you once told an interviewer you would rather take the role in about twenty years. You have arrived roughly thirteen years ahead of schedule, which makes you the first thing in the history of this company to beat its own delivery estimate.

The sections below set out what is expected of you. Please read them carefully. This packet will not self-destruct. That is a Paramount property, and we have been asked to stop bringing it up.

Section 2: A Note on the Unsuccessful Candidates

You were not the only man screen-tested in a dinner jacket this autumn. As a courtesy, and because several of them will be at the premiere, please familiarise yourself with the following.

Nicholas Hoult tested with distinction. This is the second time he has lost a role to you, having previously come within a cowl’s width of playing Batman. HR asks that you not raise this at parties. HR further notes that he currently plays Lex Luthor, and he has therefore been transferred, at no extra charge, to Villain Development, which is preparing a memo of its own.

Jack Lowden was considered highly qualified, having already spent several seasons as a disgraced spy in Slow Horses. The panel concluded that he has seen so much of the inside of MI5 that MI6 would feel like a sideways step, and that Slough House would never sign the transfer form.

Callum Turner had the height, the jaw and the stillness, and arrived with a pop-star fiancée who could have handled the theme song in-house. Finance wept when he was passed over. The panel’s only concern was that he already looked so much like Bond that audiences would assume he was a returning character.

Josh O’Connor has, in a sense, already served His Majesty, having played him in his younger days in The Crown. The panel felt that an actor who has worn the crown could not credibly take orders from M, and would almost certainly correct her posture.

Finally, we extend our thanks to the gentlemen who have been “about to be named the next Bond” in tabloid headlines more or less continuously since Daniel Craig announced his departure in 2019, among them Idris Elba, Tom Hardy, Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Regé-Jean Page, Jacob Elordi and Theo James. Between them, they have been cast as 007 more often by bookmakers than by any studio on Earth. Each has been offered honorary Double-O status, a commemorative cufflink and lifetime membership of the George Lazenby Society, which meets once and then never again.

You will also be relieved to hear that the screen test no longer includes the scene Henry Cavill once described from his own audition, in which candidates re-enact Sean Connery stepping out of a bathroom in nothing but a towel. Under current policy, the towel is a Prime Wardrobe item and must be returned within thirty days.

Section 3: The Name, the Number and the Introduction

Your introduction remains “Bond. James Bond.” Please do not deliver it in the gravel-and-whisper register you used as Bruce Wayne. In testing, audiences heard “Bond” and assumed you were confessing to something.

Equally, please avoid the full Edward Cullen pause: the long, haunted, centuries-deep stare before the second “Bond.” On the ad-supported tier, our systems will detect the silence and insert a thirty-second spot for Prime Day.

Your Double-O number is non-negotiable. We tested alternatives. “Agent 0-Prime” scored well with shareholders and badly with humans. “Agent 1-Click” was flagged by Legal as an admission of something.

Section 4: Your Prior Employment

Our background check reveals a remarkable résumé for a man about to be issued a Walther. In a single career you have been a Hogwarts champion who died in a graveyard, a vampire who sparkles in sunlight, a billionaire who dresses as a bat, and a secret agent who lives his life in reverse. You are also about to appear in Dune: Part Three, which, we are told, functioned as a rather expensive audition for the director who will now be shouting “action” at you on the Bond set.

Human Resources has raised the following concerns.

The sparkle clause. Bond has always worked in daylight: Monte Carlo, the Bahamas, the ski slopes of The Spy Who Loved Me. You are contractually forbidden from glittering on camera. All daytime exteriors will be shot as night-for-day, and the next film’s internal working title has been changed from Bond 26 to The Living Daylights (Please Avoid Them).

The cape. You already have one. Please return it to Warner Bros. at the end of your shift. Bond does not wear a cape. Bond wears a dinner jacket that fits so well it has its own agent.

The Tenet problem. Bond films traditionally run forwards. If at any point you find yourself un-shooting a henchman, step out of the turnstile and contact Q.

Dual-identity fatigue. You have spent years playing a man who pretends to be a playboy to hide his secret life as a vigilante. You are now asked to play a man who actually is a playboy, with a secret life as a government assassin. We recognise that this is essentially the same job with a different employee handbook. Think of it as a lateral move with better cocktails.

Section 5: Equipment and Gadgets

Q Branch has been fully integrated into Amazon Devices. Please familiarise yourself with your standard kit.

The Aston Martin DB5, now available through Subscribe & Save. Ejector seat sold separately. The machine guns behind the headlights have been replaced with doorbell cameras, which will record the villain fleeing and share the footage with local police without troubling anyone for a warrant.

The wristwatch, which formerly contained a laser, a garrotte and a Geiger counter, now contains Alexa. It will not cut through steel restraints, but it will tell you the temperature of the shark tank you are being lowered into and offer to reorder fish food.

The exploding pen has been discontinued following a customer review titled “One Star. Exploded.”

The Walther PPK is now available in Kindle Edition. Customers who bought this item also bought one silencer, one dinner jacket and the complete Ian Fleming box set, which nobody has finished.

Q has asked us to pass along a personal note: “Pay attention, 007. And for heaven’s sake, sign for the package this time.”

Section 6: The Martini Policy

Shaken, not stirred, remains the official position. However, following your years in Forks, Washington, Catering has asked whether you might prefer something “a little darker, slightly warmer, and served in a goblet.” The answer is no. You will drink the vodka martini. If the scene requires anything red, it will be supplied by the villain, as tradition dictates. Catering also wishes to state, for the record, that the Heineken incident of 2012 will not be repeated.

In keeping with corporate synergy, all martinis will now arrive by drone. Please expect your drink to be shaken, not stirred, then shaken again on the doorstep, and on at least one occasion left with a neighbour.

Section 7: Villains, Henchmen and Customer Service

The traditional villain monologue, in which the antagonist explains his entire scheme while the hero is strapped to something, will be retained but reframed as a customer-service call. Blofeld will place you on hold. The laser will advance one inch every time the hold music loops.

“Do you expect me to talk?” remains your line. The villain’s reply has been updated: “No, Mr. Bond. I expect you to rate this interaction on a scale of one to five.”

SPECTRE has been rebranded as a third-party Marketplace seller with a 4.2-star rating, mostly for prompt delivery of doomsday devices, though one reviewer complained the volcano lair arrived in two separate boxes. Blofeld’s white cat now has an influencer storefront and a better engagement rate than you.

Jaws has been reassigned as a fulfilment-centre picker who must hit his hourly rate while biting through cable-car wires. Oddjob’s lethal bowler hat will now ship in recyclable packaging, which has made it considerably less aerodynamic and Oddjob considerably more bitter.

Section 8: The Opening Credits

The gun-barrel sequence will be retained. Your walk across the frame must be completed within the standard productivity window; any time off task will be logged and discussed at your next one-to-one.

Music has shortlisted the following working titles for the theme song: “Bezos Are Forever,” “The World Is Not Enough (Free Shipping Over $35),” “Tomorrow Never Dies (But Your Subscription Auto-Renews),” “Live and Let Prime,” and “You Only Live Twice (Thirty-Day Return Window).” The finalist will be selected by an algorithm that has listened to every previous Bond theme and concluded that the answer is always more brass.

Section 9: Workplace Conduct

Bond’s historical approach to the office would, under current policy, generate more complaints than the fulfilment centres. Miss Moneypenny has been promoted to Director of People Operations and has already opened your file.

The traditional flirtation by the hat stand has been converted into an annual compliance module. Please complete On His Majesty’s Secret Service: Respectful Workplace Training by Friday. A quiz follows. You cannot charm the quiz. Several of your predecessors tried.

The women of the franchise, referred to for six decades by a term HR will no longer print, are now “Bond colleagues.” Several have been informed that, where the story warrants, they may have the last word, the last shot and the Aston Martin.

Section 10: Dying

As you know, your immediate predecessor died at the end of his tenure. We would gently note that you are the first Bond to arrive with a pre-existing record of dying. Cedric Diggory: graveyard, 2005. Edward Cullen: technically deceased since 1918. Bruce Wayne: presumed dead in several continuities and, we gather, sulking about it in most of the others. We note, too, that David Heyman, one of your two producers on this film, also produced the one in which you were killed in a graveyard. He assures us this is not a pattern.

Legal therefore requires a reminder that resurrection allowances are strictly governed by the franchise agreement. You may Live Twice. You may Die Another Day. You may not invoke Never Say Never Again, which was made by someone else and which we do not discuss at the dinner table. No Time to Die has been used up.

Section 11: Performance Review

Your performance will be assessed against our Leadership Principles, adapted for espionage.

Customer Obsession. The customer is the Crown. Also the shareholders. Mostly the shareholders.

Bias for Action. Shoot first. Write the six-page memo later.

Frugality. You may destroy one Aston Martin per film. Additional Aston Martins require written approval from M and from Finance, in that order, though Finance does not believe in the order.

Disagree and Commit. When M orders you to stand down, disagree, then commit, wholeheartedly, to disobeying her for the remaining two hours and twelve minutes.

Dive Deep. Primarily into the Thames.

Earn Trust. Of everyone except the person who just handed you a drink.

Welcome to the Double-O Section, Mr. Pattinson. Your lanyard is in the Aston Martin. The Aston Martin is in a locker. The locker code is 007. Of course it is.

The packet ends there, save for a yellow sticky note on the final page in what appears to be Miss Moneypenny’s handwriting: “He’ll do. He looks as if he’s been brooding since 1962.”

On this side of the Atlantic, the news landed the way most British news does: a few hours late, slightly chilled and politely received. We are told the world is not enough. But with free shipping, it comes surprisingly close.
