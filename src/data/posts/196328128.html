MEMORANDUM

TO: The Honorable Speaker of the House of Representatives; The President Pro Tempore of the United States Senate

FROM: Office of White House Counsel

DATE: May 1, 2026

RE: In the Matter of the Terminated Hostilities (Whichever Ones Those Were)

CLASSIFICATION: Legally Unambiguous

PRECEDENCE: Urgent (Standard)

I. Executive Summary

The hostilities have terminated.

This memorandum exists to confirm that fact, to provide the relevant legal authority for that determination, and to address, in a spirit of complete transparency, certain ancillary questions that members of Congress, the press corps, and allied governments have raised regarding which hostilities, specifically, have been terminated, and where.

This Office wishes to state at the outset that these are not the kinds of questions that typically require a memorandum. In normal circumstances, the termination of hostilities is self-evident. This Office acknowledges that circumstances have been, in certain narrow technical respects, somewhat less than normal. We are nonetheless confident that the hostilities — the correct ones — have terminated, and that this memorandum will lay that matter conclusively to rest.

We thank Congress for its patience and remind it that it has a recess to get back to.

II. Legal Authority

The War Powers Resolution of 1973 — a statute passed by a Democratic Congress over a presidential veto, during a period of what historians have characterized as “significant executive overreach,” which the reader is invited to find ironic — provides that the President must obtain congressional authorization for the use of military force within sixty days of the commencement of hostilities, or within ninety days if an extension is sought.

The current administration does not seek an extension.

The current administration does not acknowledge that the sixty-day clock has run.

The current administration’s position, as articulated by the Secretary of Defense under sworn congressional testimony, is that a ceasefire “pauses or stops” the sixty-day clock. The Secretary did not identify the location of the pause button in the relevant statutory text, nor did he indicate who had pressed it, nor when. These are, this Office submits, implementation details.

Senator Schiff has argued that “ceasing to use some forces while using others does not somehow stop the clock.” This Office notes that Senator Schiff is a Democrat, and declines further comment.

The broader constitutional question — whether the War Powers Resolution is itself constitutional — has been disputed by every president since Nixon, which this administration takes as dispositive authority, since the only thing more compelling than one president ignoring a law is forty-five of them doing so in sequence.

We are therefore operating under what this Office has designated Executive Time (Conflict Edition), a variant of standard executive time in which the clock runs when it is helpful and pauses when it is not.

III. The Question of Which Hostilities

Several members of Congress, and at least three allied governments, have inquired as to which hostilities, precisely, this memorandum concerns.

This Office appreciates the question and wishes to answer it directly.

The terminated hostilities are the ones that began on February 28, 2026, in the theatre that was the subject of the President’s letter to Speaker Johnson and President Pro Tempore Grassley, dated May 1, 2026, which this memorandum supplements and clarifies.

This Office acknowledges that the President, at a press availability on April 29, 2026, immediately following a telephone call in which both active conflicts were discussed, provided a status update on one of them while appearing to describe the other.¹ Specifically, the President reported that the opposing force’s navy had been destroyed — approximately 159 ships, now underwater — and that their air force had been decimated, their missiles reduced by roughly 82 percent, and their drone manufacturing capacity largely eliminated.

These figures are accurate. They are the figures from the Pentagon’s Iran battle damage assessment, compiled over six weeks of briefings following Operation Epic Fury. They describe Iran.

The President was asked about Ukraine.

This Office wishes to make clear that this discrepancy does not affect the legal validity of the termination. A war does not become un-terminated simply because its statistics were momentarily assigned to the wrong country. The underlying facts remain the underlying facts, wherever the President believes they occurred.

This Office further notes that Ukraine’s navy has not been destroyed. Ukraine does not have 159 ships. Ukraine’s navy was effectively scuttled in 2014 and has since operated primarily through sea drones and vessels of a size that does not lend itself to dramatic accounting. Any suggestion that the President described Ukraine’s military situation with precision is, charitably, aspirational.

¹ This retrieval discrepancy is consistent with the cognitive demands placed upon any Commander in Chief simultaneously managing two active military theatres following a ninety-minute call with a foreign head of state. This Office notes that the call was described by the Kremlin as “friendly and businesslike,” which we present without editorial comment, because we value our employment.

IV. The Strait

On April 17, 2026, the President posted on Truth Social: “IRAN HAS JUST ANNOUNCED THAT THE STRAIT OF IRAN IS FULLY OPEN AND READY FOR FULL PASSAGE.”

He meant to write Strait of Hormuz.

He accidentally wrote the truth.

The Strait of Hormuz is, in every operational, strategic, and economic sense that currently matters, Iran’s strait. Iran closed it. Iran controls access to it. The United States Navy is blockading it on Iran’s behalf, in reverse. Commercial transit has been reduced to a trickle. The global oil market has registered its opinion. “Strait of Iran” is, assessed against present reality, the more accurate designation — more precise than the name on the maps, more honest than anything the administration has said about the waterway since February 28.

The President arrived at this formulation by accident, in a Truth Social post, in capital letters, at an unspecified hour.

This Office wishes to note that this constitutes the administration’s most geographically accurate public statement of the conflict to date.

It was a typo.²

² This Office has reviewed the President’s Truth Social post and confirms that “Strait of Iran” does not appear in any official U.S. government cartographic database. We note, however, that cartographic databases do not command naval blockades. The President, for the moment, does.

V. The Ships

One hundred and fifty-nine ships are underwater.³

They are in the correct body of water.

This Office will confirm which body of water in a subsequent addendum, pending clarification from the National Security Council as to which conflict this memorandum is primarily addressing.

³ A supplementary memorandum regarding the ships — their registry, flag status, and the theatre in which they were engaged — is being prepared and will be circulated once interdepartmental consensus has been reached on the relevant geography.

VI. The Currency

The President noted, during the April 29 press availability, that the opposing nation’s currency is “valueless” and that the currency “doesn’t have a name.”

Both currencies — the Iranian rial and the Ukrainian hryvnia — have names.⁴

The rial has been the official currency of Iran since 1932. It appears on every Iranian banknote, in every international financial transaction involving Iran, and in every news article about the Iranian economy published in the past ninety-four years.

The hryvnia has been the official currency of Ukraine since 1996. It appears on every Ukrainian banknote, in every international financial transaction involving Ukraine, and in the word “hryvnia,” which is a word that exists and has existed for thirty years.

The President’s assertion that the relevant currency lacks a name is, this Office acknowledges, factually incorrect. However, the President’s broader point — that the currency has been significantly devalued — is, with respect to the rial, substantially accurate. This Office therefore characterizes the statement as directionally valid, which is the standard we have adopted for this administration’s public statements in matters of geopolitical finance.

As to which country’s currency was under discussion: this Office declines to speculate.

⁴ Inquiries regarding the specific name of either currency should be directed to the Department of Treasury, which has been notified and is, as of this writing, formulating a response.

VII. The Putin Dimension

This Office notes, as a matter of factual record requiring no further elaboration, that on April 29, 2026, the President of the United States emerged from a private ninety-minute telephone call with Vladimir Putin — whose country is Iran’s largest strategic partner, whose military has coordinated with the Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps, and whose drone supply chain runs through Tehran — and immediately:

a) Recited the Iranian military’s battle damage assessment as though it described Ukraine;

b) Characterized those who opposed Russia’s invasion of Ukraine as “some people” who had “made it difficult” for Putin “to make a deal”; and

c) Appeared to receive, and tentatively accept, a policy offer from the head of state of the country whose ally he is currently blockading.

This Office makes no assessment of whether this sequence of events represents a foreign policy achievement. We note only that the Kremlin described the call as “friendly and businesslike,” that Putin offered to “be of help” with Iran, and that the President responded warmly to a man who has, by conservative estimate, gamed the previous ninety days of diplomacy with the methodical patience of someone who has correctly assessed his counterpart’s ability to track two simultaneous conflicts.

We present these facts in the order in which they occurred. We invite the reader to arrange them into whatever narrative seems most comforting.

VIII. Conclusion

The hostilities have terminated.

The War Powers clock has paused, stopped, or been rendered constitutionally inapplicable, depending on which member of the administration you ask and on which day.

The ships are underwater. The missiles are 82 percent gone. The factories are mostly gone. The currency — whatever it is called — is valueless. The strait is Iran’s. These are the correct facts about the correct country, and this Office stands behind them in all respects except the geographical ones, which remain under review.

Congress has returned to its recess. The Navy maintains its blockade. The Strait of Hormuz — or, per the President, the Strait of Iran, which is the more accurate formulation and which this Office is not yet prepared to adopt officially — remains closed. Ships continue to be fired upon. Peace talks have been scheduled, postponed, relocated, and tentatively rescheduled in a different country.

The war is over.

This Office is confident that, in time, we will also determine which one.

This memorandum was prepared by the Office of White House Counsel and reflects the legal position of the administration as of the date of issuance. It should not be construed as a diagnosis, a declaration of war, a concession of error, or an admission that two active military conflicts are, in any meaningful cognitive sense, distinguishable from one another at the level of senior executive retrieval. All figures are accurate as applied to the correct country. The correct country will be identified in a forthcoming supplement.

For questions regarding currency nomenclature, please contact Treasury.

For questions regarding ship locations, please contact Navy.

For questions regarding which war has terminated, please contact whoever picks up.
