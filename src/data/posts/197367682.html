PREAMBLE

The President of the United States of America, Donald J. Trump, and the General Secretary of the Communist Party of China and President of the People’s Republic of China, Xi Jinping, met in Beijing on May 14 and 15, 2026, for a summit described by both sides as historic, significant, and productive, terms which, in the context of American-Chinese diplomacy, mean that both parties left the building.

The two leaders met at the Great Hall of the People, toured the Temple of Heaven, attended a state banquet, and held bilateral discussions spanning trade, technology, Taiwan, the Iran war, rare earth minerals, artificial intelligence, and, according to sources familiar with the discussions, soybeans.

The two leaders wish to confirm that the discussions were historic.

The two leaders wish to note that the soybeans were also discussed.

I. ON THE GENERAL STATE OF RELATIONS

The two sides agreed that the relationship between the United States and the People’s Republic of China is the most important bilateral relationship in the world.

The two sides further agreed that this relationship has been, in recent years, complicated.¹

The two sides agreed to stabilize the relationship, which both described as their primary objective, their secondary objective, and, upon reflection, probably also their tertiary objective, given that every other objective on the agenda would require a stable relationship to accomplish and neither party wished to discuss what would happen if it wasn’t.

¹ The United States side noted that tariffs had reached 140% before being partially reversed. The Chinese side noted that rare earth export restrictions had been deployed twice, on both occasions causing the United States to fold. Neither side included these observations in the formal communiqué. Both sides are aware of them.

II. ON TRADE

The two sides reached agreement on the following:

Boeing aircraft. China will purchase Boeing aircraft. The number of aircraft, the timeline for delivery, and the financing arrangements will be confirmed in a subsequent working-level discussion. The aircraft are expected to be real.

American soybeans. China will purchase American soybeans. This commitment builds on the Busan Summit soybean purchase commitment of October 2025, which China is described as “on track” to fulfil, which represents progress over the 2017 commitments, most of which were not on track to be fulfilled because they were not commitments so much as memoranda of understanding with West Virginia.

The Board of Trade. The two sides announced the establishment of a bilateral Board of Trade, which will convene regularly to discuss trade matters. The Board of Trade is a new institution. It has no staff, no budget, and no fixed meeting schedule. It is, at this time, primarily a name.²

The two sides expressed confidence that the Board of Trade would produce results.

The two sides have expressed similar confidence about previous bilateral institutions, some of which also produced results, and others of which produced subsequent press releases confirming that discussions were ongoing.

² Analysts noted that the last major deliverable from a Trump-Xi bilateral trade announcement was the $250 billion in deals announced during the 2017 Beijing visit, including the $83.7 billion West Virginia memorandum of understanding, which exceeded West Virginia’s entire GDP and never materialized. Both sides are aware of this. Neither side mentioned it. West Virginia remains.

III. ON TAIWAN

The two sides discussed Taiwan.

The nature of these discussions is not fully reflected in this communiqué.

The American side wishes to confirm that its Taiwan policy has not changed.

The Chinese side wishes to confirm that it heard what it heard.

Taiwan, which was not represented at the summit, wishes to confirm that it was watching very carefully and would like clarification on approximately seventeen specific phrases used in the joint statement, several of which sound superficially similar to previous formulations but contain small word-level adjustments that, in the context of cross-strait relations, represent either minor diplomatic variance or the most significant shift in American Taiwan policy in four decades, depending on whom you ask.

The American side has noted that it cannot speak to every interpretation of every phrase.

Taiwan has noted that it can.

Taiwan is currently asking.³

³ General Secretary Xi’s meeting with KMT opposition leader Cheng Li-wun the previous month, at which Xi stated that “China will never tolerate independence for Taiwan,” was also not mentioned in the communiqué. Analysts suggest it did not need to be.

IV. ON IRAN

The Iran situation is, both sides agreed, complex.

The United States is currently blockading the Strait of Hormuz, through which passes approximately twenty percent of the world’s oil supply, including a significant portion of China’s crude imports, which the United States Navy is intercepting on China’s behalf, in reverse, which is to say: not on China’s behalf.

China is Iran’s largest crude buyer. China has ordered its companies not to comply with American sanctions on Iranian oil. China recently hosted Iran’s Foreign Minister for a visit in which China positioned itself as having “already weighed in with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz,” which Iran has not done.

The United States Navy is still at the Strait.

The two sides agreed that this situation is complex.⁴

China expressed its view that the Iran conflict should be resolved through dialogue. The United States expressed its view that the Iran conflict has been terminated, which is the legal position, if not the operational one. Both sides agreed that resolution of the conflict would benefit global stability, oil markets, and the atmospherics of future Trump-Xi summits.

Neither side resolved it.

The summit proceeded anyway, which analysts described as proof that both leaders “want the optics of stability even if its foundations are shaky,” a sentence that could apply, with minor edits, to most of what was agreed this week.

⁴ The Russian President is scheduled to visit Beijing next week, immediately after Trump’s departure. The scheduling was described by the Kremlin as coincidental. It was described by the White House as not a concern. It was described by analysts as “the most legible calendar entry in modern geopolitics.” Putin, whose country is Iran’s largest strategic partner, whose military has coordinated with the IRGC, and whose phone calls with the American President have previously resulted in the American President describing the wrong war to the press corps, will arrive in Beijing to meet Xi Jinping approximately seventy-two hours after Trump boards Air Force One. Both sides declined to comment on what will be discussed. Both sides are aware of what will be discussed. The soybeans will not be on that agenda.

V. ON ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

The two sides agreed to continue discussions about artificial intelligence.

The two sides agreed, as they had at previous summits, not to connect AI to nuclear command and control, which remains the lowest possible bar for bilateral AI governance and which both sides cleared without difficulty.

A working-level AI dialogue will be established. It will build on the working-level AI dialogue established at the Biden-Xi summits of 2023 and 2024.

Both of those dialogues are ongoing.

This one will also be ongoing.

The two sides expressed optimism about the ongoing dialogues.⁵

⁵ For context, the Asimov Gap — the distance between what AI systems can do and what democratic institutions have enacted to govern them — currently spans approximately the distance between Beijing and Washington. Both capitals are aware of this. Neither capital’s delegations discussed it at the summit, possibly because closing the gap would require governing something, and governing things was not on the agenda.

VI. ON RARE EARTH MINERALS

The two sides agreed to expand China’s supply of rare earth elements to the United States, building on commitments made at the Busan Summit.

The United States noted that China’s suspension of rare earth exports in April and October 2025 had caused significant disruption to American manufacturing, defence supply chains, and the production of items that it would prefer not to enumerate in a joint communiqué.

China noted that it had, on both occasions, resumed exports after the United States made adjustments.

The United States did not describe these adjustments as capitulation.

China did not describe them as leverage.

Both sides described the rare earth agreement as a win.⁶

⁶ The CFR noted before the summit that Xi “successfully beat back Trump’s unprecedented trade escalation” twice using rare earths as a “break glass” tool, and that “when Xi threatened to restrict those flows, Trump folded rather than credibly threaten escalation.” This analysis was published two days before the summit. It was read by many people. It was not included in the communiqué.

VII. ON THE VISIT ITSELF

The President visited the Temple of Heaven.

The President attended a state banquet.

The President did not receive a private dinner in the Forbidden City, as he did in 2017, which analysts noted as a signal of the relative positioning of the two leaders, though both sides declined to characterize it as such.

The President was accompanied by a delegation of American CEOs, including representatives of Boeing and Mastercard, whose presence signalled that the visit was primarily commercial in nature, which is to say: the President brought business partners to a geopolitical summit, which is either a novel approach to diplomacy or a very familiar one, depending on how one reads the Trump Organization’s pre-visit activities in Beijing.⁷

⁷ Eric Trump and his wife were reported to have visited Beijing in advance of the summit. The Trump Organization’s activities in China were not discussed in the formal bilateral sessions, or if they were, they were not included in the communiqué.

VIII. CONCLUSION

The summit was historic.

The relationship is stable, or more stable, or at least no less stable than it was before, which in the current environment is the operational definition of stability.

Boeing will sell aircraft. China will buy soybeans. The Board of Trade will convene. The AI dialogue will continue. Taiwan will seek clarification. Russia will arrive next week.

The Strait of Hormuz is still closed.

The soybeans were confirmed.

Both leaders expressed satisfaction with the outcomes.

The two sides agreed that future meetings would be beneficial.

The two sides agreed to hold them.

This communiqué was prepared by the Office of Bilateral Outcomes and reflects the consensus positions of both delegations. It should not be construed as a resolution of the Iran conflict, a clarification of Taiwan policy, a commitment to deliver any specific quantity of Boeing aircraft, a guarantee that the Board of Trade will have staff, or an indication that the $83.7 billion West Virginia memorandum of understanding from 2017 has been revisited.

West Virginia was not mentioned.

West Virginia persists.
