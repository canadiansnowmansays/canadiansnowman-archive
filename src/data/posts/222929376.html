OFFICIAL VOTERS’ GUIDE · District of Columbia General Election · Tuesday, 3 November 2026

Prepared by the Office of Ballot Measures Subject to Further Review. Please read carefully. Congress won’t.

Preface

On Tuesday, 3 November 2026, voters in Washington will decide Initiative 86, which would ban the force-feeding of ducks and geese to fatten their livers, along with the sale of foie gras in the District. As Hana Kiros reported for The Atlantic on 31 October 2026, it has become the most contentious question on the capital’s ballot, and the argument has long since stopped being about ducks.

This guide concerns a companion measure, Initiative 86½. It does not ban force-feeding in the District of Columbia. It accepts that force-feeding already takes place there daily, on an industrial scale, in buildings with marble floors. It proposes only to change what goes down the tube.

Section 1: Ballot Text

Shall the District of Columbia require that every elected official working within its boundaries, including those who merely work there and fly home on Thursdays, be fitted with a feeding tube for the purpose of enlarging their attention to constituents beyond its present size, and be fed, twice daily, the unfiltered letters, voicemails, emails and town-hall questions of the people they represent?

Section 2: Summary by the Office

Current practice. Members of Congress are already gavaged. The feeding is carried out by donors, political action committees and lobbyists, usually at receptions with name tags, and the diet is rich, steady and remarkably uniform from member to member. Nobody has ever complained about the tube. Several have complained when it was briefly removed.

What the measure changes. The tube is redirected. Constituent correspondence would be transcribed in full and administered without summary, including the letters written entirely in capitals and the ones that begin, “I have voted for you for thirty-one years, but.”

Medical note. In ducks, gavage swells the liver to as much as ten times its normal size over roughly two weeks, after which the bird can barely move. The Office notes that in legislators the organ affected is the sense of mandate, which reaches ten times its normal size without assistance, and that Congress achieved near-total immobility unaided some years ago.

Section 3: Fiscal Impact Statement

No new tubing is required. The existing tubes, installed over several decades at considerable expense to the donor class, are in excellent working order. Redirection only.

Estimated cost: one afternoon, one plumber, and the formal objection of a K Street trade association, which has already booked the room for its press conference.

Estimated revenue: none. Estimated savings: considerable, should any of the letters be read.

Section 4: Statement of the Proponents

Submitted by Constituents for Being Heard.

Initiative 86 has a mascot. Olivia is a four-and-a-half-pound duck from Maryland, bought on eBay as an egg by an owner who assumed he would hatch female. He did not, and kept the name anyway, which is more flexibility than most incumbents show on anything.

Initiative 86½ also has a mascot. Gerald is a retired schoolteacher from Ward 7. He was not bought on eBay. He was registered as a lobbyist through a clerical error, which is how he finally got a meeting.

Like Olivia, Gerald has been turned away from a public building. Unlike Olivia, nobody running for mayor has offered him a high five.

We are not asking for anything cruel. We are asking that each Member digest one letter, all the way down, without a staffer chewing it first.

Section 5: Statement of the Opponents

Submitted by the Incumbents’ Gavage Legal Defence Fund.

Our feeding is ethical. Our barns are open, by appointment, to anyone bearing a cheque. Our diet is overwhelmingly green. We have personally visited our own feeding operations, and we can confirm that we barely notice the tube.

We also wish to warn the voters of the District about outside money. Initiative 86½ is backed by people from outside the Beltway, namely the voters.

Footnote from the Office: The Office has searched the record and confirms that this is the first recorded objection by a sitting Member of Congress to money from outside the Member’s own state. The Office further notes that the Opponents’ concern is not without precedent. The Atlantic found that nearly 90 per cent of the funding for the six citizen initiatives to reach the District’s ballot since 2014 came from outside the District. Everyone in Washington, it seems, is fed from somewhere else.

Finally, we submit that this measure is far too small an issue to waste anyone’s time on.

Footnote from the Office: The same argument is being made about the duck.

Section 6: The Constituent Tasting Menu

In protest at Initiative 86, a Capitol Hill restaurant introduced a four-course foie gras tasting menu, featuring a Waldorf salad on a bed of “foie gras snow” and a foie gras Twinkie with foie gras ice cream. In the same spirit of civic gastronomy, the Office offers the following, available nightly to any Member who asks. None has.

  • Amuse-bouche. A single postcard from a Ward 8 resident about a broken streetlight, served at room temperature, as it has been since 2019.

  • First course. One town-hall question, unscreened, on a bed of hostile follow-up.

  • Second course. A reduction of 212 voicemails about the same bill, simmered until they become a single sentence the Member cannot misinterpret.

  • Main. Waldorf constituency: farmers, nurses and laid-off federal workers, tossed together and dressed in the Member’s own campaign promises.

  • Dessert. The Polling Twinkie, with approval-rating ice cream. It keeps indefinitely. Nobody has ever finished one.

At a table near the kitchen, a man who identified himself only as an administration staffer told the Office he was an enthusiast of anything that is forced down somebody else’s throat. He then ordered the Twinkie and asked for the ballot to be sent back.

Section 7: The Clause at the Bottom

Residents of the District of Columbia pay federal taxes and send no voting member to Congress. Their delegate may speak on the floor of the House but may not vote on the laws that pass there.

Under the Home Rule Act of 1973, whatever the District enacts is sent to Congress for review, and Congress may overturn it, as it did in 2023 with the District’s rewritten criminal code. Initiative 86½, if approved by the voters, will therefore take effect only once it has been reviewed by the very legislators it proposes to feed.

The Office’s forecast: Congress will decline, citing a full stomach.

A Closing Note from the Office

Voters may support both measures on the same ballot. Nearly everyone the press has consulted, including the foie gras fans, expects Initiative 86 to pass. The Office regrets that it cannot make the same forecast for Initiative 86½.

So on Tuesday, the people of the District will decide what may be done to a duck’s liver. What may be done to the people of the District remains, as it has for two hundred and twenty-five years, a matter for legislators they did not elect.

The ducks may yet get out of the tube. The voters will go on feeding their representatives the one thing those representatives were built to digest, and it will not be their mail.

Satire, built on Hana Kiros’s report for The Atlantic, “Foie Gras Is Tearing D.C. Apart” (31 October 2026). The duck, the tasting menu and the funding figures are real; the Office, Gerald and Initiative 86½ are not.
