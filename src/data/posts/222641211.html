Sunday Satire. Everything the Assignee is said to have done below was reported by Ashley Parker and Michael Scherer in The Atlantic on October 29, 2026. That includes the FDR pep talk, the redistricting, the Srpska weekend, the ringtone, the nicknames, the Isaiah post and the Metallica tickets. The Instrument, the Office and every clause in it are invented.

INSTRUMENT OF EXTREME OWNERSHIP Midterm Cycle 2026 — Executed Prior to Results, as Is Customary

This Instrument is entered into on October 29, 2026, before the votes are counted. That is the last moment at which its terms can still be described as voluntary. The parties are:

THE PRINCIPAL, who makes the decisions; and

THE ASSIGNEE, Mr. James Blair, formerly of the West Wing and latterly of the private sector, who makes the decisions look like a plan.

RECITALS

WHEREAS the Principal stated publicly in January that the party that wins the presidency does not win the midterms. By that statement he established that any loss was foreseeable by him and that any win would be unforeseeable genius;

WHEREAS the Assignee did, in February, assemble the Cabinet beneath a green awning at the Capitol Hill Club and invoke the year 1934. In that year Franklin D. Roosevelt expanded his majorities by telling voters that things were getting better. The Principal has since improved on the technique by telling voters that things are already golden;

WHEREAS eleven days after that meeting the Principal commenced a war with Iran. The Assignee’s slide deck did not contain this event, there being no slide for it;

WHEREAS gasoline thereafter rose by more than half and diesel by more than seven-tenths, while the share of Americans worried about buying groceries reached nearly three in four. Sales of canned luncheon meat set records, which the Office declines to characterise as a leading indicator;

WHEREAS Texas, Kansas and Iowa were formerly described as “safe” and are now described as “fine,” a word the Office’s Lexicon Unit defines as “not”;

WHEREAS political directors, like field-goal kickers, are sent onto the field to attempt a kick whose distance they did not choose, and are remembered only for the result;

WHEREAS the Assignee has publicly professed the doctrine of Extreme Ownership, under which a leader accepts complete responsibility and blames no one else;

AND WHEREAS the Principal has, by unbroken practice across five decades, professed the complementary doctrine of Extreme Ownership of Credit, under which the same rule applies in reverse;

NOW, THEREFORE, the parties agree that these two doctrines fit together like a lock and a key, and that it would be a waste not to use them.

ARTICLE 1 — DEFINITIONS

1.1 “Outcome” means the result of the November 3, 2026 election, whatever it turns out to be.

1.2 “Victory” means any Outcome in which the Republican Party retains either chamber, any seat, or the ability to describe the night as tremendous.

1.3 “Setback” means any other Outcome. The word “defeat” shall not appear in this Instrument or in any other document of the Office, which has no record of one.

1.4 “Strategy” means everything the Assignee proposed that worked.

1.5 “Execution” means everything the Assignee proposed that did not work.

1.6 “Vision” means everything the Principal did, including the things nobody proposed.

ARTICLE 2 — ALLOCATION

2.1 In the event of Victory, all of the credit shall vest in the Principal immediately and without further act, to be announced at a rally.

2.2 In the event of Setback, all of the responsibility shall vest in the Assignee, who volunteered for it in writing (see Article 6).

2.3 Partial Outcomes shall be divided on the same principle. If the House is lost while the Senate is held, the Senate goes to the Principal and the House goes to the Assignee. Texas goes to whichever of them is standing nearer the microphone when the race is called.

2.4 The parties acknowledge that this allocation is not symmetrical. The Assignee acknowledges that asymmetry is what Extreme Ownership means, and that he said so first.

ARTICLE 4 — NOMENCLATURE

4.1 The Assignee’s honorific shall track his standing according to the following schedule:

  • Before the Principal got to know him: Wet Blanket (on the Principal’s own later recollection, delivered on the Chief of Staff’s patio)

  • On joining: Little James (to distinguish him from Big James, Mr. Braid, since departed)

  • On delivery of the 2024 result: Brilliant James

  • On Victory: Brilliant James (retained)

  • On Setback: One of Many Consultants

  • On Setback, after one news cycle: James Who?

4.2 The Assignee’s telephone presently plays “Hail to the Chief” when the Principal calls. On Setback, the Assignee is advised to replace it with something by Metallica. The Office’s music desk suggests “For Whom the Bell Tolls” and notes that the title alone will do.

ARTICLE 5 — DISAMBIGUATION RIDER

5.1 The Assignee is not Stephen Miller.

5.2 The Office receives frequent enquiries on this point. Callers cite the shorn head, the pressed lips, the drill-instructor intensity, and the habit of irritating colleagues who prefer staff to behave like staff. Above all they cite the shared distinction of being the two men in the building who have overcome the Principal’s reported preference for a full head of hair.

5.3 For the purposes of this Instrument the two are distinguished as follows. Mr. Miller decides what the government should believe. Mr. Blair decides which districts should believe it. Mr. Miller’s position does not depend on an election, whereas Mr. Blair’s position is the election.

5.4 In the event of Setback, the Office expects these enquiries to stop, because only one of the two men will still be in the photographs.

ARTICLE 6 — VOLUNTEER CLAUSE

6.1 Two weeks before the election, the Assignee answered his critics online with the reply the prophet Isaiah gave when the Lord asked whom He should send: “Here am I; send me.”

6.2 The Office has reviewed the rest of the passage. When Isaiah asked how long his errand would last, the answer was until the cities lay in ruins, the houses stood empty and the land was desolate. The Office notes that the Assignee quoted the volunteering and left out the job description, which is consistent with industry practice.

6.3 The post constitutes written acceptance of Article 2.2.

ARTICLE 7 — CONSIDERATION

7.1 The Assignee receives no payment from the Principal’s political operation. He declined it because of the Principal’s well-known concern about advisers billing him too much.

7.2 Instead, the Assignee may take outside clients, on the condition that any fee the Principal finds out about is donated to the Principal’s library.

7.3 The consideration for this Instrument is therefore the privilege of the role. The Office observes that this is also how the Principal’s building contractors were traditionally paid.

ARTICLE 8 — FORCE MAJEURE

8.1 Neither party shall be liable for events beyond its control. For the Principal, that means everything. For the Assignee, it means nothing.

8.2 The following are specifically excluded from force majeure:

  • (a) wars the Principal starts;

  • (b) prices caused by wars the Principal starts; and

  • (c) remarks at rallies about the golden age arriving faster than expected.

ARTICLE 9 — VENUE

9.1 Disputes under this Instrument shall be heard in Las Vegas, at the Sphere, during the Metallica residency for which the Assignee bought tickets long ago.

9.2 The Office approves the venue. Las Vegas is a city where everyone understands the odds, chooses a strategy accordingly, and still learns that the house wins. In this arrangement, the house is white.

ARTICLE 10 — SURVIVAL

10.1 This Instrument survives the Outcome. The Assignee may not.

10.2 Notwithstanding 10.1, the Assignee has assured colleagues of three things. A Democratic Speaker would last one term, new maps are coming next year, and the plan was right all along. The Office records this as Extreme Ownership of the next Outcome, executed in advance, and has opened Instrument No. 2028-1107 for countersignature.
