CLASSIFICATION: Internal / Not for Release FROM: Office of Digital Strategy, Creative Services Desk TO: Director of Communications RE: Approval status — “Ring Content,” Batch 4 (Manufacturing, Energy, Border) DATE: 13 September 2026

Per your request, the Creative Services Desk has reviewed the four completed videos in the “Ring Content” batch prior to their release on official channels. Below is our assessment, organised by asset, followed by three outstanding concerns the Desk cannot resolve without further guidance.

Asset 4-A (”Manufacturing”)

The President is depicted standing on a rock outcropping above a valley of glowing green factories, one arm extended, palm open, the word AMERICAN MANUFACTURING rendered in the same green as the light pouring from his hand. Runtime 0:27. Caption reads “What the fuck is wrong with them,” which — per the account notes — was not written by this office and appears to have been appended by the reposting account, not the original poster. We flag this only because the caption is now indexed alongside official content in search results, and the Desk was not consulted on tone.

Internally, this asset tested well. Focus group notes (Appendix C) describe the visual as “powerful,” “cool,” and, in one instance, “like if God ran a factory.” No participant asked what specifically was being manufactured, in what facility, by whom, or on what timeline. The Desk considers this a feature of the format rather than a gap in it.

Asset 4-B (”Energy”)

Similar composition. Green lightning in place of factories. No caption controversy to report.

Asset 4-C (”Border”)

Withheld from this batch pending legal review — see Concern 2 below.

Asset 4-D (”The Ring”)

A close, static portrait: the President’s hand raised toward camera, palm out, a single green ring glowing on the finger. Caption: “What the fuck is wrong with them,” again appended downstream, again not ours. Runtime 0:27. This is, functionally, our best-performing single frame across the batch. Engagement figures attached separately (Appendix D) exceed every other asset produced by this office in the current cycle, including the National Guard deployment explainer and the tariff-schedule infographic.

The Desk would like to note, for the record, that this asset is doing the numbers it’s doing specifically because it resembles something else. We should be honest about that internally even if we can’t be honest about it publicly.

Asset 4-E (”Threshold”) — unreleased, held for separate review

This asset was not part of the original four-piece batch and does not yet have Legal sign-off; it is included here because the Desk cannot make sense of the other four without it. Where 4-A through 4-D show the President generating something outward from a single point of contact, 4-E inverts the composition: he is not the source of the light, he is standing at the seam of it, and what comes through is not a factory or an energy field but a column of identically dressed figures, in formation, walking toward him out of the glow. Creative’s storyboard notes describe this as “the origin story” for the other four assets. The Desk has not been told what it is the origin story of.

OUTSTANDING CONCERNS

1. The likeness question.

Every asset in this batch draws — visually, unmistakably — on the Green Lantern character owned by Warner Bros. Discovery. The ring, the glow, the raised palm, the colour: these are not incidental choices. Legal has been asked twice to weigh in and has twice replied that “government use of a color and a shape is unlikely to constitute actionable infringement,” which is true and also not the question anyone was asking. The question is whether it is wise, as a matter of institutional dignity, for the executive branch’s official communications apparatus to be producing content whose entire visual grammar is borrowed from a comic book character built around a magic ring that grants its bearer whatever their imagination can construct — provided they have sufficient willpower and are not currently experiencing fear.

The Desk does not have a position on whether that comparison was intended. The Desk notes only that it was not prevented, and that several members of the focus group volunteered the comparison unprompted, three of them approvingly.

2. Asset 4-C.

The border asset was pulled from this batch after Legal flagged that the ring-glow effect, when composited over the wall footage per the original storyboard, produced an image “difficult to distinguish from a hallucinated deportation figure” in three of five test renders. Creative has been asked to revise. We are told revisions are “close.”

3. The underlying question nobody on this desk is positioned to answer.

We are producing content in which the President of the United States is depicted as a being who generates matter — factories, energy, presumably eventually policy outcomes — through concentrated will alone, channelled through a glowing object on his hand. This is not a metaphor this office invented. It is the literal content of the asset. Four times now, across four subjects, we have reached for the same image: a man alone in a landscape, arm extended, reality reorganising itself around the gesture.

The Desk raises this not as a creative note but as an observational one. Somewhere between the first ring video and the fourth, this stopped being a stylistic choice and became, functionally, the house style. We would like guidance on whether that was the intent, because if it was not, it has happened anyway, and it is now the thing our office is known for producing.

RECOMMENDATION

Approve 4-A and 4-B for release as scheduled. Hold 4-C pending revision. Release 4-D on a delay, paired with a follow-up asset the Desk has not yet been asked to make but assumes is coming, given the trajectory: something, eventually, will have to explain what the ring is for. At time of writing, the ring has generated a factory, an energy field, and — pending revision — a border enforcement action. It has not yet generated an explanation. As for 4-E: the Desk recommends it stay in the vault indefinitely. Not because it is worse than the others. Because it is more honest about what the others are doing, and honesty was never the deliverable.

We remain, as always, prepared to produce whatever is asked of us. We would simply like it minuted, somewhere, that we asked the question first.

— Creative Services Desk, Office of Digital Strategy
