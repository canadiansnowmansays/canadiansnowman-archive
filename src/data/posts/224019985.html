1. Purpose

On 3 November 2026 the American people delivered a decisive verdict. The Division has reviewed that verdict and declines it.

From 3 January 2027 both chambers of Congress will be in the hands of the Dumocrats. They will send the Don bills. This procedure sets out how those bills are to be received, refused, and then refused again by way of a second refusal that refuses the first.

Staff are reminded that the Division’s correct title is the Division of Denial. The earlier name, the Department of De Nile, was retired when the Don was briefed that it is a river in Egypt. The Don has stated that he is not in De Nile, has never been in De Nile, and that it is a very overrated river, some people are saying the worst.

5. Procedure on Receipt of a Bill

For training purposes the Division has used H.R. 4827, the Daycare, Dental and Diesel Deduction Act, which is popular, costs little and is therefore intolerable. The text as received refers throughout to the House of Representaitves and to a “Government Mourged Housing Initiative.” Nobody in the Division can say what that is.

Step 1. The bill arrives on the Resolute Desk. An aide reads aloud the title. The Don asks whether it has a ballroom in it.

Step 2. On learning that it does not, the Don vetoes the bill with the large black marker, in a signature visible from low orbit.

Step 3. A reporter asks the Don why he vetoed daycare.

Step 4. Mr De Nier is brought forward. The Don states that he did not veto daycare, that nobody loves daycare more than he does, that he has been to daycare, that it was beautiful, and that the marble came from a quarry whose owner liked him.

Step 5. Mr De Veto, hearing the denial, mounts the crate and brings down the Stamp. The file now records that the veto did not occur.

Step 6. All present say “Now envision chandeliers” and leave the room.

6. Note from Counsel (Do Not Circulate)

The Division’s sole remaining lawyer has raised a difficulty.

Article I, Section 7 of the Constitution provides that a bill the President does not return to Congress within ten days, Sundays excepted, “shall be a Law, in like Manner as if he had signed it.” Counsel observes that a veto requires the President to send the bill back with his objections. A president who says he never vetoed anything has, on his own account, sent nothing back. A clerk who then stamps the file to show that no veto took place has removed the only evidence that he did.

Counsel’s conclusion is that the procedure as written enacts the entire Dumocrat legislative programme, one denial at a time, without any need for a two-thirds majority. Daycare, for the avoidance of doubt, is now the law of the land. So, Counsel regrets, is the Government Mourged Housing Initiative.

Counsel further observes that the opposition need never hold a hearing. It need only send a bill, wait for the veto, and have someone ask the Don about it on the South Lawn.

The Don has been briefed on this finding. He denies it.

Counsel has been reassigned to the Reflecting Pool.
