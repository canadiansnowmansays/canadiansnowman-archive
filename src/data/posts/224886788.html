CUSTOMER CARE: TROUBLE TICKET

Ticket No.: FAA-20261113-0001
Customer: Federal Aviation Administration
Account holder since: 1958
Service plan: Copper Classic™ (introduced 1987, never upgraded)
Reported issue: “Nothing is flying.”
Priority: Normal

Tier 1: Front-Line Support

Agent followed Script 4B (“Customer Reports No Service”).

Agent: Thank you for calling. Before we begin, can you confirm the airspace is plugged in?

Customer: It’s the sky.

Agent: I understand your frustration. Can you see the router from where you’re sitting?

Customer: Which one? There are several hundred. Some of them are older than the controllers.

Agent: Is there a blinking amber light?

Customer: There is a blinking amber light over Kansas.

Agent: That means the router is thinking. Please unplug it, wait thirty seconds and plug it back in.

Customer: It’s in a locked room in a building from the Eisenhower administration. The man with the key retired.

Agent: Is there a newer man?

Customer: We’re hiring.

Agent’s note: Customer has not performed the basic troubleshooting steps. Advised customer that 80 per cent of outages are fixed by restarting the equipment. Customer advised agent that restarting the equipment would involve restarting the United States. Agent further advised that priority callers, such as football teams travelling by charter, may reach out to the FAA directly. Customer advised agent that it is the FAA. Escalated.

Tier 2: Technical Support

Technician’s note: Asked customer to check whether their equipment is running the latest firmware. Customer asked whether the floppy disk counts.

It does not.

Ran a remote diagnostic. Results:

  • Traffic demand: 45,000 flights a day

  • Traffic the router was designed for: about four, plus a Zeppelin

  • Router status: Overloaded and philosophical

  • Router’s last reported message: “I’ve been doing this since the Reagan administration and nobody has ever said thank you.”

Technician’s assessment: No fault found on our side. The customer’s equipment is working exactly as well as equipment from 1987 works.

Escalated to Retention, because the customer used the word “cancel” twice.

Tier 3: Customer Retention

The following offer was sent to the customer’s address on file.

A SPECIAL UPGRADE OFFER FOR A VALUED CUSTOMER

Dear Federal Aviation Administration,

Our records show you’ve been with us for 68 years, and for 39 of them you’ve been on our Copper Classic™ plan. Thank you for your loyalty. Very few customers are this loyal. Most of the others are dead.

We’d like to invite you to upgrade to FIBRE+ for Airspace, with:

  • Symmetrical speeds up to “noticeably faster than now”

  • A free modem (installation not included; please be home between 8 a.m. and the next fiscal year)

  • Our exclusive Backup Fibre add-on, now with repair windows of just thirteen hours. In September your own Administrator called a break on our backup line “massive.” We prefer to call it “a learning opportunity.”

As with all our plans, FIBRE+ is billed one month in advance, so you’ll be paying for October’s airspace in September and for the outage before it happens. Customers tell us this gives them something to look forward to.

Offer not valid during outages. Airspace sold separately. Speeds may vary depending on time of day, weather, appropriations, and whether anyone has found the key.

Retention agent’s note: Customer declined the offer, citing “Congress.” Escalated to Network Operations.

Tier 4: Network Operations

Engineer’s note: Since the customer can’t fix the problem, we issued a notice telling everyone about the problem. This is standard practice in our industry and in theirs.

NOTAM 26/1113 A) ALL OF IT

B) 2611130000 C) UFN

E) AIRSPACE U/S. ROUTER NEEDS A MOMENT. PILOTS ADVISED NOT TO STARE AT IT. DO NOT ASK IF IT IS DONE YET. IT IS NOT DONE YET.

For readers outside aviation: a NOTAM is a “Notice to Airmen,” the official warnings pilots read before they fly. For a few years it was a “Notice to Air Missions,” until this administration changed it back. That is the most recent upgrade the system has received. “U/S” means unserviceable. “UFN” means “until further notice,” which in this case means until the router finishes thinking.

Engineer’s second note: Some pilots reported that they couldn’t read the NOTAM because the NOTAM system also runs through the router.

Tier 5: Escalated to the Office of the President

This is the tier where complaints go when every other tier has failed and somebody has written to a newspaper. Note: Customers contacted by this office have been known to assume the call is coming from 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue. Agents are reminded to clarify. In this instance, regrettably, it was.

Office of the President’s note: Ticket received. Responsibility has been assigned.

Assigned to: The previous administration.

Status: Pending a press conference to announce new routers that will be the most advanced in the history of routers, delivered in two weeks. Customer advised that “two weeks” is a unit of time used by this office and does not correspond to a number of days.

Customer was further advised by the Secretary of Transportation that the system is “going to continue to fail.” This office has logged the statement as a service guarantee.

Escalation officer’s personal note: In my experience, a complaint that reaches this office was usually fixable at Tier 1, by someone who had the key.

Linked Ticket: TOR-20261114-0002

Opened by a residential customer in Toronto the morning after the outage. Linked by the system as “similar fault.”

Customer: A retired gentleman with 2.5-gigabit fibre, symmetrical, up and down.

Reported issue: The modem sits nine feet from the television, and he still waits for the television to come on.

Diagnosis: Customer’s connection could carry the entire North Atlantic flight schedule. Customer’s television is still waking up and finding its apps, like a teenager on a Saturday.

Resolution: Customer advised that every link in the chain has to be fast: the line, the modem, the cabling and the device. The slowest part sets the speed for all of it. You can bring fibre right to the door, but if the box at the end of it is old and tired, you have bought a very expensive way to wait.

System’s note: Same resolution applied to Ticket FAA-20261113-0001.
