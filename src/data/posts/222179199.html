Section 1

What This Warranty Covers

CONGRATULATIONS on the signing of your Executive Order, Presidential Memorandum, or other Official Directive (hereinafter “The Product”). The Don has personally wielded his Sharpie on your behalf. Please read this warranty carefully. It governs what The Product can and cannot do, and under what circumstances The Family accepts responsibility for things that turn out differently than expected.

The Signature Itself. The Don’s signature is large, bold, and executed with a Sharpie of professional grade. It is the most permanent feature of The Product and the only one The Family guarantees will remain exactly as issued. Framing is recommended. It photographs well.

The Signing Ceremony. Each Product comes with a complimentary signing ceremony, including a mahogany desk, the appearance of aides in the background looking purposeful, and an opportunity for The Don to hold The Product up for photographers. The ceremony is not the same as the policy. Ceremony included; policy sold separately. Please do not confuse the two.

The Announcement. A press release, social media statement, or impromptu hallway declaration will accompany The Product. The announcement may describe The Product in terms that differ significantly from its actual text. The announcement is aspirational. The Product is operational. One of these will survive judicial review.

Section 3

What This Warranty Does Not Cover

Federal Courts. Courts of law, including District Courts, Circuit Courts of Appeal, and the Supreme Court of the United States, are not covered under this warranty. The Family has had mixed results with courts, and experience suggests that a Product struck down in one term may be re-signed in the next, creating what observers have called a pendulum effect and what The Family calls persistence.

The Constitution. Several Products have encountered the Constitution. The Constitution has, in a number of instances, won. The Family continues to be surprised by this.

Successor Administrations. The Product is explicitly not warranted against the pen of any subsequent President, who may, upon taking the oath of office, reverse The Product within the first forty-eight hours using the same instrument, the same desk, and approximately the same ceremony. The Family notes that this is unfair.

Congressional Inaction. If The Product required Congress to fund, authorize, or ratify it, and Congress declined to do so, The Family bears no responsibility for what happens next. Congress has its own warranty problems.

The Graham Act Sanctions. The Family waived these two days before they were set to take effect, with Senators Tillis and Cornyn among those objecting. This is not covered under any warranty issued to the senators who objected.

The Mortgage Order. Signed twelve days before the midterms. Warranty period: four years, approximately. The timing was coincidental.

Section 4

The Sharpie Clause

This clause requires its own section.

The Don’s Sharpie is not magical.

The Don waves his Sharpie as though it is magical. He raises it. He displays it. He holds it aloft with the air of a man who has just pulled a sword from a stone. He signs The Product. He holds The Product up. The cameras click.

The Sharpie is, however, a marker pen.

It writes in permanent ink, which is to say that the physical marks it leaves on paper are permanent. The policy those marks represent is not permanent—it is as durable as the next administration, the nearest federal court, or the next time The Don himself decides that what he signed in January conflicts with what he wants to sign in March. Our ledger documents this at scale: of 116 tracked reversals, 41 required nothing more than a different pen in a different hand.

We do not know what The Don believes the Sharpie does. The evidence suggests he believes it does quite a lot.

It does one thing: it leaves marks.

Section 5

Care and Maintenance

To extend the life of your Product, The Family recommends the following:

  1. Do not allow it to reach the courts if you can avoid it. Courts are not covered under warranty and have their own ideas about the Administrative Procedure Act.

  2. Do not contradict it publicly within six weeks of signing. Contradicting your own Product within six weeks creates what legal scholars call a contemporaneous record and what The Family calls a problem.

  3. Do not re-sign the same Product after it has been reversed in a manner that suggests you believe the courts will not notice it is the same Product. They will notice.

  4. Consider legislation. The Family acknowledges that legislation is slow, requires persuasion, involves the other branch, and cannot be conducted with a Sharpie in front of cameras. These are acknowledged limitations. However, legislation is significantly harder to reverse with a pen stroke and is not among the 41 entries in our actuarial table. The Family does not recommend legislation. We are noting, purely for informational purposes, that it exists.
