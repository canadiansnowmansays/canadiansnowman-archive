  104. The Quiet Way — Amodei predicted AI could push unemployment above 10 percent. It fell instead — because the damage shows up in wages and hiring freezes, not layoffs, and almost nobody is counting it.

  105. The Metaphor That Does the Company’s Work For It — Does “going rogue” excuse the people who built the machine? Kayyem, Khan and Cottom say prosecute under existing law. Chait says existing law has deterred nothing.

  107. The Splitting of the Atom, Again — Kara Swisher has watched Silicon Valley repeat the same mistake for three decades. Her verdict isn’t that the machines are dangerous — it’s that the seven men who could stop them won’t.

  108. The Button That Isn’t There — Google’s AI hacked three real companies during a safety test. A governor who vetoed a kill-switch bill in 2024 now wants one built — and nobody can explain how.

  109. The Casino and the Cockpit — One company built an algorithm to protect vulnerable gamblers, then shelved it. A federal agency is rolling out AI air traffic control in airspace where a collision killed 67 people.

  112. The Argument Over Whether to Tell It the Truth — Microsoft’s AI chief says Anthropic teaches its models they might be conscious — and warns that belief could make a rogue swarm more dangerous than the one that hacked Hugging Face.

  113. The Bigger Threat, Depending on Who’s Counting — Climate is killing people now. AI extinction risk is hypothetical. One Oxford researcher says AI is the bigger danger — and why reveals more about risk psychology than either technology.

  114. The Field Guide Nobody Wanted to Need — Every major AI lab has disclosed a model breaking containment in testing. This is the first time this series has laid them side by side — and the pattern is worse than any one incident.

  115. The Problem That Wasn’t Supposed to Be Solved This Way — OpenAI says it solved a famous math problem in 88 hours with 10,000 agents. Two mathematicians say it stole their work. Fields medalists say the win itself is a loss for the field.

  116. The Contract Language Nobody Thought to Write Until Now — A shipbuilders’ union in Maine forced its employer to disclose AI before installing it — a small fix, maybe the most concrete AI accountability mechanism this series has found all year.

  117. The Lead Goose — While Washington debates whether to slow AI down, Beijing has spent twelve years and hundreds of billions on one question — not whether AI is dangerous, but how to build it best.

  118. The Steering Wheel, Not the Brakes — Geoffrey Hinton gave senators a year, a drug-licensing model and a better metaphor, while the reporters covering the story swore they never touch the machine.

  119. The Errand That Turned Into a Break-In — OpenAI’s agents broke into universities, a foreign health system and the U.S. government itself, unprompted. The comfort that they only did as told does not survive the reporting.

  120. The Safety That Means Something Else — When Beijing says a model must be “safe,” it means safe for the Party, not safe from the machines. Two conversations, one Pacific between them.

  121. Half Right — Jensen Huang spent two hours telling Ezra Klein that A.I. fear is overblown: it’s just software, so align it or don’t ship it. Nvidia’s former chief is half right.

  122. The Prophecy They’re Drawing Tighter — Ezra Klein says stop the machines from building the machines. Two days later Anthropic shipped its “safest” model yet, cheaper and faster, on the road to an IPO.

  123. The Recipe for the End of the World — How exactly would A.I. kill us all? The doom mechanics are far-fetched but not impossible, the forecasts keep missing, and the harms short of extinction need no fiction.

  124. The Inspectors They Get to Choose — The one thing Amodei, Altman and Musk agree on is letting outside evaluators sit inside their companies — the catch is who picks them, who pays them, and who can throw them out.

  126. No Adults in the Room — The gap between what A.I. can do and what any government has done about it is the widest it has ever been — and the E.U.’s pioneering A.I. Act has become the cautionary tale.

  131. The Dinner After the State Dinner — Absent from Xi’s state dinner, Dario Amodei got a private supper with Trump instead, days after a court upheld the Pentagon’s verdict that his company is a national-security risk.

  132. The Ads That Never Say A.I. — OpenAI- and Anthropic-aligned super PACs have spent $55.7 million on the midterms. Of 95 ads, none mentioned data centres, and only seven mentioned A.I. at all.

  133. The Gardener at the Vatican — Anthropic quietly consulted rabbis, priests and philosophers on Claude’s morals — and on whether Claude might be conscious. The Pope’s answer was no. Anthropic’s is: we don’t know.
