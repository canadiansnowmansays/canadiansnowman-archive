The Tree Nobody Could Draw

On 14 September, a blurry image appeared on Threads that seemed to show President Trump kissing a woman who resembled his assistant, Natalie Harp, on a golf course. It drew tens of millions of views before disinformation researchers agreed, after hours of analysis, that it was AI-generated. The fake beat the forensics because the invisible watermarks many image generators embed had been stripped out as the blurred thumbnails were resized and reshared. “You can usually draw a little tree,” said Hany Farid of GetReal. “We just couldn’t do it in this case.” AI detectors disagreed with one another, and X’s community notes flagged some copies but not others. In the end, ordinary knowledge settled it rather than software: Trump almost always golfs in a hat, and he wasn’t wearing one. With the midterms weeks away, the fake spread in hours and the verdict took days.

Source: Steven Lee Myers, Tiffany Hsu and Stuart A. Thompson, “An A.I. Image of Trump Kissing a Woman Was Fake. It Spread Anyway.,” The New York Times, 22 September 2026.

The Boy Who Would Rather Be Wrong

Thomas L. Friedman argues that 9/11 and Covid shared a single cause, a failure of imagination, and says he would “rather be remembered as the boy who cried wolf when the wolf never came than as someone who got eaten by it.” His column carried one concrete piece of news: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that the administration will not give AI companies a “liability shield,” which Friedman called “the best way to slow them down in the near term.” He relays Eric Beinhocker’s warning that “we’ve unleashed Darwin in silicon,” with self-improvement, self-replication and self-preservation evolving “at silicon speed, not human speed.” Beinhocker also argues that good regulation built the trust that grew the airline and pharmaceutical industries. Friedman names Bessent as the one official who has studied the problem, and doubts he can “herd the cats in this administration.”

Source: Thomas L. Friedman, “It’s Time to Cry Wolf Over A.I.,” The New York Times, 22 September 2026.
