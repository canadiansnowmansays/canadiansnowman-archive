THE WHITE HOUSE COOKBOOK Lame Duck Edition, Winter 2026–2029 Recipe No. 47 of 47

LAME DUCK À L’ORANGE

(Canard Boiteux à l’Orange)

Serves: 340 million, whether they ordered it or not Preparation time: Ten years Cooking time: One evening (November 3, 2026) Resting time: November 4, 2026 to January 20, 2029 Difficulty: The duck will tell you it was very easy, the easiest anyone has ever seen Kitchen: The State Ballroom (under construction; please mind the dust)

A Word from the Kitchen

Every great cuisine has its signature dish. The French have canard à l’orange. Americans produce, every so often, a lame duck. On the evening of November 3, 2026, the two traditions met in a single pan.

The French name repays a moment’s study. A lame duck in French is a canard boiteux, a limping duck. Canard is also French slang for a false story, a hoax, a tale that doesn’t hold up. So the dish before you is three things at once: it limps, it is orange, and it is made up. Few recipes are so economical.

The bird was declared done when voters handed both chambers of Congress to the other kitchen. The House went first. The Senate followed, with seven Republican seats changing hands, in North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Kansas, Iowa and Maine. Kansas elected its first Democratic senator since 1932, which in culinary terms is like discovering that a restaurant has been serving the same soup since Hoover and has finally changed the menu.

Ingredients

  • 1 duck, term-limited, approximately 80 years of age, height as certified by the Division of Vertical Affairs (see Certificate No. 2026-0603)

  • 1 cup orange glaze, self-applied daily, reduced over ten years to a hard lacquer

  • 54 Senate seats, held by the other kitchen

  • 1 House majority, likewise

  • 1 red cap bearing the word VETO (the duck supplies its own)

  • 1 rubber stamp, described by the duck as “nice”

  • 1 bill, very large, attached to the front of the duck

  • 1 stack of bills, Democratic, to be returned unsigned

  • Flattery, Cabinet-pressed, as much as the duck can absorb (there is no upper limit)

  • Gas prices, to taste (taste: bitter)

  • 1 vice-president, in sprig form, for garnish (may wilt)

  • 1 ballroom, optional, not yet finished

Method

1. Select your duck. Premium lame ducks are rare. This one was raised in a gilded pen and fed for a decade on a single diet, making it a natural foie gras: the liver of a bird force-fed nothing but Fox. Note that the feed has lately soured. On election night, as the first polls closed, the duck complained that Fox’s ratings were down because the network was “AGAINST me.” A duck that turns on its own trough is a duck nearing doneness.

2. Glaze. Apply orange. You will find the duck has already done this itself, unevenly, and will resist any suggestion that it has been glazed at all.

3. Place in the oven on November 3. The duck will insist it is not in the oven. It will tell you that if it had been on the ballot, the kitchen would have won in a landslide. The kitchen notes, for the record, that the duck had told voters to pretend it was on the ballot. They did.

4. Baste at intervals. At each basting the duck will say something different. First it is “a little bit surprised.” Then it congratulates the other kitchen on a job well done. Then it promises to send back everything they cook with “a nice stamp.” Then it blames the other ducks in the pan, the Republican candidates, for spoiling the dish. All of these statements are served together. The duck contains multitudes, most of them contradictory.

5. Check for doneness. Conservative commentator Erick Erickson, a food critic of long standing on the right, warned a year ago that lame duck status would come even faster now. It came on schedule. The Speaker of the House, for his part, has explained that the kitchen was defeated by more than a century of midterm history. This is the first recorded case of a chef blaming the oven for being an oven.

6. Rest. A lame duck must rest for 26 months before it can be carved. During this period it does not go quiet. Unlike other poultry, the lame duck quacks louder the longer it is cooked.

7. Stamp. Present each incoming bill to the duck on a silver salver. The duck will stamp it. The duck has also posted a picture of its own cap with VETO written across it, so that no one is left in suspense. Place a small brass plaque on the Resolute Desk reading THE DUCK STOPS HERE. Nothing actually stops. It is a lovely plaque all the same.

8. Serve. In the new ballroom, once it is finished. The duck chose the ballroom’s construction ceremony, on the day after the election, as the moment to field questions about the defeat. It talked about the building materials first. Priorities are a matter of taste.

Chef’s Notes

On the administration. Purists call this period a maladministration. The kitchen prefers Mallardministration, which is more accurate and fits better on the menu.

On scandal. You may notice that no sauce, however acidic, sticks to this bird. Scandal runs off it like water off a duck’s back. The kitchen suspects the glaze contains Teflon.

On housekeeping. Before January 20, 2029, the duck will be getting its ducks in a row. In this kitchen that phrase refers to the pardon queue, alphabetised.

On January. A new Congress arrives on January 3, 2027, and with it the gavels. Committees chaired by the other kitchen may now wish to invite the duck’s Cabinet to explain the ingredients. A bird that spent two years at the head of the table may find itself a sitting duck.

Wine Pairing

Château Veto 2026, a red, the exact colour of a familiar cap. Nose of grievance, with notes of Diet Coke and burnt toast. Palate: thin. Finish: very long, lasting precisely 26 months. Decant before serving. Do not, under any circumstances, ask the sommelier what happened in Kansas.

Variations

Peeking Duck. The same bird, prepared so that it peeks over the garden wall at the 2028 ballot to check whether anyone has left a line open for a third term. Serve with a constitutional amendment on the side. The Twenty-Second has been on the side since 1951.

Duck Confit. A duck preserved in its own fat. See also: the Cabinet.

Duck Soup. A classic from 1933, in which the leader of Freedonia, Rufus T. Firefly, leads his nation into a war out of wounded pride. Modern versions substitute Iran for Sylvania. Asked after the election whether the war had been worth the political cost, the duck said yes. It did not say who was paying.

Duck, Duck, Goose-Step. From the children’s menu. Now prepared under the supervision of the new Chief of Staff.

Quackery. A side salad from the Department of Health and Human Services, dressed in the natural era’s new dyes, which are made from bugs. Pairs well with nothing.

The Ugly Duckling. A side dish, see garnish. The vice-president waits at the edge of the pond, studying his reflection, quite certain that in 2028 he will turn out to be a swan. Other ducks in the pond have their own views.

Troubleshooting

My duck says it is a swan. All lame ducks say this. Do not argue. Simply count the seats.

My duck says it won. The duck said on election night that it accepted the results. It then said the results were somebody else’s fault. Both statements are part of the recipe. Neither changes the seat count.

My duck says the war was worth it. Ask the duck what it cost the kitchen. Then ask Iowa, Ohio and Texas, three states the duck carried three times, which sent the other kitchen’s senators to Washington anyway.

Can I return the duck? Not before January 20, 2029. The kitchen does not offer refunds. An early return is a different recipe altogether, requiring two-thirds of the Senate, which is 67 seats. The other kitchen has 54. The duck knows how to count to 67, even if it struggles with other numbers.

My duck is threatening to veto everything. “Mr. Veto,” as the duck has styled itself, has explained that after a couple of months of vetoes the other side always comes around to make a deal. The kitchen notes that the other side now holds the agenda, the subpoenas and the calendar, and that a duck which can only say no is still, technically, a duck that can only say no.

Is the duck safe to eat? No one has ever eaten this duck. The duck eats you.

To Serve

The Senate’s Democratic leader has promised the public, in five words, that relief is on the way. The kitchen cannot speak to relief. It can only report, in the language of the dish, the verdict every French cook knows.

Le canard est cuit.

The duck is cooked. In French, il est cuit also means he’s finished. It’s an old idiom, and it works on both counts.

Bon appétit.

Satire. The election results and the President’s quoted remarks come from election coverage of November 3–4, 2026 by NPR, NBC News, ABC News and Reuters/AFP. The ballroom is real. The duck is a metaphor. The glaze is disputed.
