FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

FROM: Mattel, Inc., Office of Corporate Communications TO: The American Public; The International Press Corps; Whom It May Concern DATE: May 4, 2026 RE: Clarification of UNO® Game Rules as They Pertain to Recent Social Media Activity

Mattel, Inc. wishes to address, in a spirit of constructive corporate citizenship, a post published on the social media platform X on May 4, 2026, by the account of the President of the United States, depicting the President holding a hand of UNO® Wild cards beneath the caption “I HAVE ALL THE CARDS.”

We appreciate the President’s enthusiasm for the UNO® brand.

We have some concerns.

I. The Fundamental Rules Clarification

In UNO®, the objective of the game is to be the first player to have no cards remaining in your hand.

We wish to be direct: holding all the cards is not winning. Holding all the cards is losing. The winner of UNO® is the player who successfully gets rid of every card they were dealt, declares “UNO” when one card remains,¹ and plays their final card to end the round.

A player in possession of a full hand of Wild cards — however powerful those cards may appear — has not won the game. They have not ended the game. They are, by the conventions of UNO® as played in more than eighty countries, in a difficult position that is made no less difficult by confidence.

We understand this may require a moment to absorb.

¹ Failure to call “UNO” when holding a single card, if caught by another player before the next turn begins, results in a mandatory Draw Two penalty. Mattel notes this rule exists and applies universally, without regard for the cardholder’s approval ratings, executive orders, or prior assertions that the rules are “very unfair.”

II. The Wild Card: Capabilities and Limitations

The UNO® Wild card is among the most powerful cards in the deck. It allows the holder to declare any colour they choose, regardless of what colour is currently in play.² It cannot, however:

  • End a war.

  • Identify which war is currently in progress.

  • Determine the name of a foreign currency.

  • Locate the Strait of Hormuz on a map, or any strait the holder has recently renamed.

  • Pause a constitutional clock.

  • Compel the opposing players to stop drawing cards.

These limitations are printed, implicitly, in the spirit of the rules. We regret that the packaging did not make them more explicit.

The Wild Draw Four — the most powerful card in the standard UNO® deck — carries an additional constraint that Mattel considers important to restate at this time: it may only be legally played when the cardholder has no other playable card in hand. Playing a Wild Draw Four when other options exist is, under official UNO® rules, a challengeable offence.³

We present this information without editorial comment.

² The current colour in play, as of press time, remains unclear. Mattel’s geopolitical colour-tracking division has noted readings consistent with both desert sand and Eastern European wheat field, depending on which theatre the active player believes themselves to be in.

³ If challenged and found to have played illegally, the cardholder must draw four cards themselves. Mattel acknowledges this enforcement mechanism lacks the institutional infrastructure of, say, the War Powers Resolution, but notes that both are being ignored on roughly the same schedule.

III. “I Have All The Cards”: A Brief Strategic Assessment

Mattel’s internal analytics team has reviewed the claim “I HAVE ALL THE CARDS” against current game conditions and offers the following assessment:

Having all the Wild cards means every move you make will be arbitrary. There is no suit to follow. There is no colour on the table. There is no logical constraint on what you play next, which sounds like freedom and functions, in practice, like chaos dressed as strategy.

Your opponents are still drawing cards. They are still taking their turns. They know their hands. They are, to varying degrees, waiting.

Having all the Wild cards means you have deferred every decision — every commitment to a colour, a direction, a consistent rule — in favour of maximum flexibility. This is an approach with a distinguished record in both card games and geopolitics. The record is not distinguished in the way the holder typically intends.⁴

In forty-seven years of UNO® production, Mattel has never observed a player win by holding cards indefinitely while announcing they hold cards.

⁴ Mattel acknowledges that one of the current opposing players recently described a ninety-minute strategy session as “friendly and businesslike,” which, in UNO® terms, is what an experienced player says immediately before playing a Draw Four on you. We flag this without further elaboration, as our legal department has advised us that geopolitical commentary falls outside our core competency. Card game commentary, however, does not.

IV. The Question of Which Game Is Being Played

Mattel wishes to raise, respectfully, the possibility that the game currently in progress may not be UNO®.

UNO® is a game with defined rules, a fixed deck of 112 cards, and a winner who is determined by the elimination of cards from a single player’s hand. It is designed for two to ten players, ages seven and up, and typically concludes within thirty minutes.

The game currently in progress appears to involve two active theatres of military engagement, a naval blockade of a waterway the active player has renamed, a constitutional clock that has been paused by an as-yet-unlocated button, and a foreign head of state who described his most recent ninety-minute session as “businesslike” and then offered to “be of help.”

These are not standard UNO® mechanics.⁵

Mattel produces, in addition to UNO®, a range of other games that may be more appropriate to current conditions: RISK®, for players managing multiple simultaneous territorial conflicts; Battleship®, for commanders who would benefit from knowing the location of their naval assets before announcing their status; Scrabble®, for players who would benefit from a structured relationship with words and their meanings; and Clue®, for situations in which the identity of the responsible party, the location, and the instrument all require independent determination.

We understand how well you already play Monopoly.

⁵ Mattel’s Special Editions division has explored a “Wild Draw Four Hundred Billion” variant for advanced players. Development has been paused pending a review of what “advanced” currently means.

V. Conclusion

Mattel, Inc. is proud that UNO® has, since 1971, brought joy, competition, and occasional family tension to players in more than eighty countries.

We are proud that the President of the United States has chosen to engage with our intellectual property on the world stage.

We ask only that the engagement reflect an understanding of the rules — specifically, that the goal of UNO® is to finish with nothing in your hand, that Wild cards are a means and not an end, and that “I have all the cards” is, technically, a description of a player who has not yet begun to win.

The game continues.

Other players are taking their turns.

We recommend playing something soon.⁶
