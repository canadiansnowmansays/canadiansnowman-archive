JUNE 14, 2026 — UFC FREEDOM 250 South Lawn, The White House (formerly a lawn)

The celebration of America’s 250th birthday begins, as the Founders clearly intended, on June 14th — which is Flag Day, and also, entirely coincidentally, the President’s 80th birthday.

The centerpiece of the evening: an octagon-shaped cage erected on the South Lawn, featuring six championship bouts between men who will punch each other in the head while 4,000 guests watch from ringside and up to 85,000 additional citizens observe on massive screens at the Ellipse. Fighters will walk to the Octagon from the Oval Office, passing portraits of Lincoln, FDR, and Eisenhower. The replacement of the White House lawn afterward is expected to cost $700,000, which historians note is approximately what it cost to run the entire Continental Army for a month in 1776. This is called progress.

The President has described this event as one that “will never happen again.” He means this as praise.

WHAT IS NOT ON THE PROGRAM

(A Supplementary Schedule of Events That Were Available But Not Booked)

The following events were considered for inclusion in America 250. They were not selected. We present them here as a public service.

A READING OF THE DECLARATION OF INDEPENDENCE Duration: Approximately 1,320 words. Free.

The document being celebrated — adopted July 4, 1776, by the Second Continental Congress — contains the line “all men are created equal.” It also contains a lengthy list of grievances against a king who “has erected a multitude of New Offices, and sent hither swarms of Officers to harrass our people.” Historians consider it foundational. It has never been read aloud at any America 250 event. It remains available.

A CONCERT BY ARTISTS WHO WERE NOT TOLD THE EVENT WAS PARTISAN Duration: Several hours. Would have been lovely.

Martina McBride, a country singer of considerable distinction, said she was “assured this was a nonpartisan event that was meant to celebrate ALL 50 states.” Young MC, whose 1989 hit “Bust a Move” is objectively more fun than a MAGA rally, wrote that “the artists were never told about any political involvement with the event.” The Commodores, who wrote “Brick House” and “Easy,” had prepared sets. Bret Michaels, who has survived a brain hemorrhage, a tornado, and a stage set falling on his head at the Tony Awards, drew the line at Freedom 250.

These artists are available. They simply needed the event to be what it said it was.

A CEREMONY AT THE LINCOLN MEMORIAL Duration: 30 minutes. Cost: A podium and some chairs.

The Lincoln Memorial, completed in 1922, houses a 19-foot marble statue of the president who preserved the Union, issued the Emancipation Proclamation, and delivered the Gettysburg Address in 272 words. It is 1.3 miles from the White House. It has hosted some of the most significant public moments in American history. It was not the venue chosen for the central event of America’s 250th birthday. The UFC weigh-ins were held there instead.

We are not making this up.

A SYMPOSIUM: TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY YEARS OF AMERICAN DEMOCRACY Duration: One day. Cost: Modest. Intellectual return: Considerable.

The Smithsonian Institution, the Library of Congress, the National Archives, and approximately forty universities were prepared to host forums, exhibitions, and public conversations about the meaning of 250 years of self-governance — the amendments, the failures, the expansions of rights, the ongoing argument about what “all men are created equal” actually requires of us. The National Portrait Gallery has been running an “American Presidents” exhibition since January. The National Archives holds the actual Declaration of Independence, which you can see for free.

None of this made the main program.

A MESSAGE FROM A FOREIGN ALLY Duration: Brief. Cost: A phone call.

Several allied nations — Canada, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Japan — were available to offer congratulations on America’s 250th anniversary. Given that the United States has spent the past eighteen months threatening Canada with annexation, imposing tariffs on the European Union, and launching regime-change wars without consulting NATO, the messages might have been somewhat carefully worded. But they would have been offered. The gesture would have meant something.

No such message has been solicited for the program.

REFLECTION: ON THE OCCASION OF 250 YEARS Duration: As long as it takes.

In 1852, on the 76th anniversary of American independence, Frederick Douglass delivered an address in Rochester, New York, that began with this observation: “The eye of the reformer is met with angry flashes, portending disastrous times.” He lamented the nation’s failures while expressing faith in its capacity for renewal. He did not suggest canceling the event and replacing it with a rally. He did not describe the other speakers as Third Rate. He showed up and said the hard true things, because that is what the occasion demanded.

His speech is in the public domain. It is available for inclusion. It has not been scheduled.
