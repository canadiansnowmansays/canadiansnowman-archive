A note before the form: Kimberly Guilfoyle resigned as US ambassador to Greece on October 13, 2026, after the White House asked her to. The direct quotations below are real, as reported by Tyler Pager in the New York Times on October 13, 2026. The $100,000 credit card story is the account of a donor, Eric Deters, first given to the Wall Street Journal; Ms. Guilfoyle calls the accusations false. The Office of Retroactive Justification, its forms, timestamps, valuations and footnotes are invented. The Office regrets nothing, which is in its charter.

OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION Division of Personnel Separations — Diplomatic Branch

FORM ORJ-DS-1 (Rev. October 2026): Ambassadorial Exit Interview and Offboarding Checklist

This form must be completed before the departing official’s embassy badge is deactivated, or shortly after, or at any point at which the Office is asked whether it was completed.

SECTION 1: EMPLOYEE INFORMATION

Name: Kimberly Guilfoyle

Post: Ambassador Extraordinary and Plenipotentiary, Hellenic Republic

Distinction: One of the first ambassadors nominated in the President’s second term. The Office notes she was also among the first to be processed by this form, which suggests a certain symmetry.

Date of separation: October 13, 2026

Type of separation (check one):

  • ☐ Voluntary

  • ☐ Involuntary

  • ☒ Voluntary, Upon Request

Note 1: “Voluntary, Upon Request” was added to this form in March 2026 to accommodate departures that the White House wishes to describe as the employee’s idea. It is now the most frequently checked box on the form. “Involuntary” has not been checked since the box was introduced, and the Office is reviewing whether to retire it.

SECTION 2: RELATIONSHIP TO THE PRESIDENT

Departing officials whose appointment involved proximity to the President’s family must have this field verified. The field has been revised several times. The full revision history is reproduced below, as required.

Note 2: Revision 7 introduces the words “I guess.” The Office’s Linguistics Desk advises that “I guess” is the President’s highest available register of uncertainty, previously deployed only for crowd sizes he had not seen and books he had not read. Its use here indicates the relationship has been reclassified from Family to Rumour.

Note 3: Revision 7 also introduces “a long time ago.” The engagement was announced in 2020. The Office’s Calendar Desk confirms this is six years. The Office’s Political Calendar Desk confirms that in the President’s service, six years is the Bronze Age.

SECTION 3: REASON FOR DEPARTURE

Official statement (White House Press Secretary): The White House asked the Ambassador to resign, described her as a wonderful asset to the team, and wished her well.

Note 4: The statement was delivered by the President’s new press secretary, Katie Zacharia, whose own onboarding paperwork is on the next desk. The Office has placed the two files a respectful distance apart.

Supervisor’s comments: The Ambassador was doing a good job, but the President “didn’t like what I read.”

Note 5: The Office flags the phrase “what I read.” The President’s direct supervisor confirms he read something. This is recorded here as an independent development of some significance and has been forwarded to the Division of Unexpected Events.

Supervisor’s location at time of comment: On the South Lawn, departing for Alaska.

Note 6: The Office has observed that the President’s statements about former associates are most often given while boarding an aircraft. The Office’s working theory is that rotor noise improves memory loss.

SECTION 4: ACCOMPLISHMENTS AT POST

To be completed by the departing official.

Note 7: The Ambassador told the New York Times in 2025 that her active social life demonstrated her commitment to the job. The Office has accepted this standard and created a new performance metric for all diplomatic posts: Canapés per Bilateral Meeting. Embassy Athens leads the Foreign Service by a wide margin. Embassy Ulaanbaatar has requested a recount.

SECTION 5: OUTSTANDING BALANCES AND RETURN OF PROPERTY

Personal charge card balance, July 2025:

This item is not a government obligation and appears on this form only because it appeared in the newspapers.

  • According to the donor, Eric Deters, the then-nominee asked him to wire $100,000 to pay off her American Express card, just before her Senate confirmation hearing in July 2025.

  • According to messages Mr. Deters provided to the New York Times, she offered in return to be his “ride or die in Trump world.”

  • According to the departing official, these are false accusations that will not be allowed to define her decades of public service.

The Office takes no position, which is its position on most things.

Note 8: The Office’s Valuations Desk was asked to assess the market value of the consideration offered: one (1) unit of “ride or die in Trump world.” The Desk reports that the value of this instrument fluctuates sharply and is pegged directly to the holder’s current entry in Section 2. Between 2020 and May 2026, it traded at a premium. At 11:59 a.m. on October 13, 2026, it was delisted.

Note 9: The Valuations Desk adds that “ride or die” is a two-party contract, and that in the Trump world it has historically been the other party who rides.

SECTION 6: HOST NATION FEEDBACK

Departing ambassadors are evaluated in part on host-country sentiment.

Summary of local feedback, as reported: A retired Athenian, speaking to the New York Times, said that sending someone with so little tact or diplomacy to Greece showed how low her country ranked among the President’s priorities.

Office response: The Office wishes to reassure the Hellenic Republic that it should not take this personally. The administration’s ranking of allies has no floor. Greece is in good company there, alongside Canada, Denmark, the European Union generally, and, as of October 13, 2026, the Ambassador herself.

Note 10: The Hellenic Republic has asked the Office to remember that Greece gave the world the word irony, from the ancient eironeia, meaning feigned ignorance. The Office has reviewed Section 2, Revision 7, and confirms that the word remains in active use. It thanks Greece for its continued contribution.

SECTION 7: PRIOR EMPLOYMENT AND BACKGROUND VERIFICATION

Note 11: In 2004, Harper’s Bazaar predicted that the Ambassador’s first marriage would be among the most glamorous political unions since Jack and Jackie. The couple divorced in 2006. The ex-husband is now Governor of California and widely expected to seek the presidency. The Office declines to speculate on which of the departing official’s former households currently has the better long-term prospects. It notes only that one of them now describes her as a long time ago.

Note 12: Under “Previous experience with Form ORJ-DS-1 or any equivalent separation process,” the departing official checked “Yes.” The Office thanks her for her familiarity with the paperwork and her efficiency in completing it.

SECTION 8: EMPLOYEE’S CLOSING STATEMENT

In her own words, as posted to social media on October 13, 2026, and summarised by the Office where permitted.

The departing official states that her time as ambassador is coming to an end. She states that she will “vigorously defend my name, my reputation, and my integrity.” She states that, although her ambassadorship is ending, her commitment to the President, the America First movement and the country she loves will never end.

Supervisor’s countersignature: ____

Unavailable. En route to Alaska.

Note 13: The Office has reviewed the closing statement against Section 2. The departing official has pledged a commitment that will never end to a party who, on the same day, placed the relationship “a long time ago.” The Office’s Records Desk has no standard category for this arrangement and has filed it under Unrequited, pending the creation of a dedicated section.

Please answer all questions. Responses are anonymous, except to everyone.

Note 14: The Office has noted the response. The Office would add, gently, that in this administration, accusations rarely define anyone. What tends to define people is Section 2.

SECTION 10: STANDING ADVISORY TO ALL POLITICAL APPOINTEES

Issued with every completed Form ORJ-DS-1.

The Office reminds all serving appointees of the following, which it has now had occasion to restate after the departures of a former Attorney General (the honour of a lifetime), a former Secretary of Homeland Security (a promotion to Special Envoy) and a former ambassador to Greece (someone the President’s son dated, I guess):

  1. Family status is revocable. It is held at the pleasure of the President and may be withdrawn verbally, on a lawn, without notice.

  2. Proximity is a lease, not a title. All leases expire. Most expire before the tenant notices.

  3. Loyalty is a one-way street. Appointees are reminded to check which direction they are travelling on it.

  4. Your entry in Section 2 will be written by someone else. Appointees are encouraged to keep their own records. The Office will not.

  5. The aircraft is always leaving. Appointees should not expect a countersignature.
