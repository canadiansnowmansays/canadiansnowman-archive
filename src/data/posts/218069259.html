Sunday Satire. The following Office Action is invented. Everything it objects to is real: on 24 September 2026, as Newsweek reported, DTTM Operations LLC, the Trump Organization’s trademark-holding company, applied to register TRUMP TV, three days after the White House launched a government channel by that name.

UNITED STATES PATENT AND TRADEMARK OFFICE Office Action (Official Letter) About Applicant’s Trademark Application

U.S. APPLICATION SERIAL NO.: 5012-TV (satirical) MARK: TRUMP TV (standard characters) APPLICANT: DTTM Operations LLC FILING BASIS: Intent to use CLASSES: Four, at a filing fee of $1,400 EXAMINING ATTORNEY: Margaret Plainly, Law Office 250 ISSUE DATE: 29 September 2026

Summary of Issues That Applicant Must Address

  1. Ownership of the mark

  2. Consent of a living individual

  3. Likelihood of confusion

  4. Identification of services

  5. Strength of the mark

The examining attorney has searched the Office’s records and the evidence of record, and has also watched some of the programming, which was not required and which she would like noted.

1. Ownership of the Mark

An application must be filed by the owner of the mark. The applicant here is DTTM Operations LLC, a private company.

The record shows, however, that the mark is already in use. On 21 September 2026, the White House launched a channel called “TRUMP TV: The Essentials Station,” carrying presidential speeches, appearances and administration content. The applicant filed three days later, at, according to trademark attorney Josh Gerben’s account of the filing, 11:20 in the morning.

The applicant must therefore clarify the following:

  • Is the applicant the United States of America?

  • If not, why is the United States of America using the applicant’s mark?

  • If so, why does the United States of America have an LLC?

Mr Gerben has observed that, should this application register, the White House would likely need a licensing agreement with the Trump Organization to keep using the name of its own channel. He believes this may be a first in American history. The examining attorney has checked, and she believes it too.

The examining attorney offers no view on what licensing terms the President should negotiate with himself. She notes only that it would be the easiest negotiation of his career, and that he would still describe it as the toughest.

2. Consent of a Living Individual

Section 2(c) of the Trademark Act bars registration of a mark that identifies a particular living individual without that individual’s written consent.

Written consent has been received. The examining attorney accepts it.

For the record, the consent was granted by the applicant’s principal, to the applicant, for a mark already in use by a government channel that exists to broadcast the consenting party, and which the consenting party runs. The Office has no form for this. The examining attorney used the ordinary one and wrote “see above” in three of the boxes.

3. Likelihood of Confusion

Registration is refused under Section 2(d) where a mark is likely to cause confusion as to the source of the services.

The applicant is no stranger to this refusal.

  • In October 2016, as Fortune reported at the time, a Manhattan public-health physician named Mark Grabowsky filed for TRUMP TV after watching the third presidential debate. He called it a whim and said he had no plans to launch a network. He was, as far as this Office can determine, the only applicant for that mark to have been completely candid about his intentions.

  • In August 2022, as Axios reported, the Office initially refused the applicant’s related TRUTH SOCIAL application because two other companies were already using the name.

The present confusion is of a different kind. The question is not whether consumers will mistake TRUMP TV for some other company’s channel. It is whether they can tell a government broadcast from a private product with the same name, the same host and the same content.

The examining attorney has viewed both. She could not tell them apart either.

4. Identification of Services

The applicant identifies, among other services, “news programming.”

The record shows that in the days before the channel’s launch, the White House barred reporters from CNN, MS NOW and Politico from its grounds, and that a federal judge, Timothy Kelly, issued a temporary restraining order blocking the ban for fourteen days.

The Office has no objection to a channel that carries only the President. It does ask the applicant to consider whether “news” is the most accurate description of a service built, in part, by removing the news. The examining attorney suggests the following amendment:

Class 41: Entertainment services, namely, presidential programming featuring the President, in the field of the President.

This wording is acceptable and can be entered by examiner’s amendment if the applicant agrees by telephone. Please call during business hours. Please do not call to explain the ratings.

5. Strength of the Mark

Here the examining attorney must raise a matter the Office does not usually encounter.

A trademark lives in the mouths of the public. A famous mark is protected more broadly than an obscure one, and fame is proved by evidence that people are saying the name.

The evidence of record suggests they are saying it less.

A Wall Street Journal analysis of 147 political podcasts, some 70,000 hours of programming, found that mentions of the President fell about 70 per cent from their 2024 election peak. As IBTimes summarised the Journal’s findings, the rate dropped from roughly forty mentions an hour to about eleven by 20 September 2026. Mentions fell roughly a further 30 per cent after the war with Iran began in late February. Over the same period, the most-mentioned Democrat who is not a former president was James Talarico, who is running for the Senate in Texas.

The Journal cautioned that a mention is not an endorsement. The examining attorney accepts this. She notes that the reverse is also true.

This is, the Office believes, the first application in its history in which the mark’s most loyal promoters have been measured declining to promote it in the same month the mark was filed. The applicant is putting its name on the channel at the moment its own ambassadors are quietly taking the name off the microphone.

Mr Gerben reports that the channel peaked at around 8,000 viewers. The examining attorney accepts this as some evidence of use. She observes that it is roughly the crowd at a decent junior hockey game, and that the hockey game also had a concession stand.

Response Guidelines

The applicant must respond within three months of the issue date of this Office Action. The applicant may submit:

  • evidence that the applicant, and not the United States, owns the channel;

  • evidence that the United States, and not the applicant, is paying for it;

  • a specimen showing the mark in current use by the applicant’s supporters, such as a recent podcast episode in which the name is said aloud, at least once, on purpose.

Failure to file a timely response will result in the application being deemed abandoned. The examining attorney recognises that abandonment has lately become a familiar concept to the applicant, most notably from the direction of his own supporters, and she offers no further comment on it.

/Margaret Plainly/ Examining Attorney Law Office 250 United States Patent and Trademark Office (satirical)

This Office Action is nonfinal. Nothing about the applicant ever is.
