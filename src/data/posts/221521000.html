A note before the handbook. The handbook, its Division and its demerit points are invented. The quotations and events are real, from Peter Baker’s White House Memo in the New York Times on October 21, 2026, “In Trump’s World of International Diplomacy, Insults Are a One-Way Street,” and the reporting around it. Baker supplied the metaphor; I have only added the road signs. — S.G.

OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION Division of Diplomatic Traffic

THE OFFICIAL DRIVER’S HANDBOOK One-Way Street: International Edition Revised October 2026. Supersedes all previous editions, alliances and treaties.

Introduction

Welcome, Foreign Leader.

You have been issued this handbook because you share a road with the Principal. The rules are simple, and we are told they were summed up best by the New York Times: “He may trash you. But woe unto you if you whack back.”

Read carefully. There is a test.

Part 1: Road Signs

Yield to autocrats at all intersections.

ONE WAY Insults travel in one direction only. Drivers who are insulted must continue in the direction of travel and must not reply.

YIELD Yield to autocrats at all intersections. Approved drivers currently include the President of Russia, the President of China and the Supreme Leader of North Korea, all of whom may proceed without signalling.

NO U-TURNS U-turns are prohibited for all drivers except the Principal, who may sign a sanctions law in September and exempt Russia from it in October without leaving his lane.

DO NOT ENTER The Oval Office, when the Principal is shouting. Prior drivers will recall February 2025.

SPEED LIMIT 100
Posted for years and never enforced. Enforced from October 14, 2026, for Ukrainian drones only. This allows the Principal’s office to state, accurately, that “nothing has changed.”

SLOW: CHILDREN Sign currently not enforced on the Ukrainian section of the route.

Part 2: Right of Way

2.1 The Principal has the right of way at all times, in all lanes, including the oncoming one.

2.2 A driver whose country has been invaded must yield to the driver who invaded it, where the Principal has expressed a preference. He has: on October 18, he wrote that he did not want to “do anything that will drive, foolishly, Russia away from the bargaining table.”

2.3 Where two vehicles meet, and one is firing ballistic missiles at the other’s power plants, the Principal will assess which of them is the obstacle to traffic. On October 20 he assessed it: “Zelensky has to want to settle this war. And if he doesn’t, I think it’s going to be a very bad thing.”

2.4 Russia, the Division notes, “has been fine.”

Part 3: Sounding the Horn

3.1 The horn may be sounded by the Principal only.

3.2 Approved uses of the horn this month have included: calling it “disgraceful” for Ukraine to strike Russian oil refineries; instructing Ukraine to “damn well stop”; and recommending that a fellow democracy “get a new president.”

3.3 These are classified as courtesy signals and carry no penalty.

Part 4: The Demerit Point System

Points are assessed on the driver who responds.

Demerit points are assessed on the driver who responds, not the driver who started it.

A driver who accumulates 15 points will be required to attend remedial driving school. President Zelensky reached 19.

Part 5: Remedial Driving School (Oslo Campus)

Instructor attended by video. Still had views.

The Division is pleased to report that President Zelensky completed his remedial course at the Government Guest House in Oslo on Tuesday, October 20.

Instructors: - Steve Witkoff, Special Envoy (in person). - Jared Kushner (by video link; the instructor was unwell and could not attend in person, but still had views).

Observers: - Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre of Norway. - President Alexander Stubb of Finland.

Both are strong supporters of Ukraine who also work very hard, in the Times’s phrase, “not to rile him unnecessarily.” The Division regards this as the definition of a defensive driver.

Course content: The instructors explained, according to a person informed about the session, that the student’s conduct “had infuriated the president.” They also explained at length that the Principal’s recent turn toward Moscow had nothing to do with the midterm elections, which are two weeks away.

Student’s response, at one point: “I never said that.”

Instructor’s note: Answer recorded. Points not refunded.

Examination: The student passed the oral examination, held before journalists afterwards, with a model answer: “We are thankful to the American side.” He described the session as “very constructive.” The instructor hugged him for the cameras.

Post-course assessment, from an American close to the talks: a “frank but respectful” exchange that allowed everyone “to clear the air.” The student’s public criticism, the American added, may serve his short-term goals but is “ultimately counterproductive.”

Comments from the observers: Prime Minister Støre: “There’s nothing to gain from digging into what was said yesterday or a week ago.” President Stubb: “Today was a success and tomorrow is a new day and hopefully we don’t do any backtracking.”

The Division would like to commend President Stubb, who understood the handbook better than anyone in the room. Backtracking on a one-way street is prohibited. Except for the Principal. See Part 1, No U-Turns.

Part 6: Frequently Asked Questions

Q. If the Principal insults me, may I reply? A. Yes. Please see Part 4 for the cost.

Q. Why is the Principal so hard on Ukraine in particular? A. Records show a long-standing grievance. Early in his first term, advisers persuaded the Principal that Kyiv had meddled in the 2016 election against him, an assertion American intelligence agencies have called Russian disinformation. In 2019 he asked the Ukrainian president for an investigation into Joe Biden and was impeached for it. The Division files this under Old Fender-Benders.

Q. Has any other driver hit back and kept his licence?

Class: Polite.

A. The Prime Minister of Canada. His licence is suspended but he continues to drive, very politely, in the other lane. The Division has been unable to determine why this works.

Q. Who was driving the car that caused all this? A. In the past fortnight, the Principal’s administration struck a deal to buy Russian diesel, lifted some sanctions and exempted Russia from others, curbed some intelligence and satellite help for Kyiv, delayed interceptor deliveries, and worked to broker a sale of Russian oil assets to people with ties to the Principal’s circle. The Division has checked the registration. Please see Part 2.1.

Q. What happens next? A. The instructor left Oslo for St. Petersburg, where he thanked President Putin for releasing an American prisoner and called him “a humanitarian.” The President of Russia was not asked to attend driving school. He holds a diplomatic licence, a different class of vehicle, which permits travel in every lane and never expires.

A different class of vehicle.

Note from the Examiner

The road test ends with a question the handbook cannot answer. In Oslo, between the thank-yous, the student said this:

“We are losing people, and because Russia attacks, we’re losing people and time, and it’s a pity because our children don’t have real life, childhood.”

No points were assessed. There is no column for it.
