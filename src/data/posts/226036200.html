FAMILY PROCUREMENT DIVISION ### Supplier Code of Conduct, Amendment No. 1 #### Effective immediately (Friday, 20 November 2026). Retroactive to whenever we say.

TO: All contractors, subcontractors, sub-subcontractors, vendors, suppliers, architects, engineers, landscape designers, caterers and anyone else currently being paid by the Family, or hoping to be

FROM: The Office of the Consigliere

RE: Outside conversations

Dear Valued Partner,

Thank you for your continued loyalty. As you know, the Family has always believed in a close working relationship with the businesses that build things for it, feed it, insure it and, in a growing number of cases, donate to its ballroom.

It has come to our attention that certain individuals on Capitol Hill, who will be taking over the neighbourhood on 3 January, have begun sending letters. Some of you have received one already. More of you will. We want to be clear about how this works going forward.

As reported in The New York Times on 21 November, the Don has signed an order. The paperwork calls it an executive order. We call it the Vendor Agreement. Its main provision is reproduced below for your convenience. Please read it carefully, then destroy nothing, because the Committee has asked you not to, and the Committee will want to know if you did.

SECTION 1. WHO WE ARE TALKING TO

This agreement applies to anyone holding a federal contract, a federal subcontract or a federal anything.

If you are unsure whether you hold a federal contract, ask yourself one question: does money arrive from Washington? If so, congratulations. You are Family now.

This includes, but is not limited to:

  • Defence contractors (welcome back, it has been at least a week)

  • Technology companies with government cloud agreements

  • The architect, construction firm and engineering firm working on the ballroom

  • The engineering firm the Park Service quietly hired in September, for $389,355, to begin work on the East Potomac golf course

  • Golf course designers, generally

  • Any donor to the ballroom who also holds a federal contract, which according to the watchdogs is more than half of them

That last group should take particular note. More than half of the ballroom’s publicly identified donors won new or expanded federal contracts worth more than $50 billion in the six months after they gave. We do not say this to embarrass anyone. We say it so you understand that you have already made one contribution to the Family, and that this agreement simply asks you to make a second one, which costs nothing. Silence.

SECTION 2. THE CLAUSE

2.1 Before responding to any request, letter, notice, phone call, subpoena, raised eyebrow or polite enquiry from the Committee, the Vendor shall first coordinate its response with the Family.

2.2 The Vendor shall withhold any information the Family deems confidential.

2.3 The Family deems everything confidential.

2.4 Failure to comply may result in suspension or termination of the Vendor’s contract. Nobody is threatening anybody. We are just saying it may result.

Clauses 2.1, 2.2 and 2.4 are paraphrased from the actual order. Clause 2.3 is implied.

SECTION 3. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q: Isn’t this illegal?

Representative Jamie Raskin, who will soon chair the House Judiciary Committee, called it “blatantly unlawful.” Senator Maggie Hassan, who will soon chair the Senate Homeland Security Committee, said it “does not override” your obligation to comply with Congress. We would gently point out that neither of them signs your cheques.

Q: But the Committee has subpoena power.

So it says. The Family has termination for convenience. A subpoena takes years in court. A cancelled task order takes an afternoon.

Q: What counts as “coordinating”?

You tell us what the Committee asked. We tell you what you remember. Most vendors find, after a short conversation with the Family, that they remember a great deal less than they thought.

Q: What if the Committee only wants to know how I won the contract?

That is precisely the kind of question the Family considers confidential.

Q: What if I won it fairly?

Then you have nothing to worry about, and no reason to talk.

Q: Representative Garcia sent me a letter asking me to preserve my documents. Is that a threat?

The Don has described letters like it as “a criminal act” and has asked the Justice Department to look into them. The Family’s lawyer has said oversight “may cross the line into blatant criminality,” though he has not said how. Asking a man to keep his filing cabinet is apparently organised crime. Telling a man he will lose his business if he opens it is procurement policy. We hope this clears things up.

Q: Can my employees talk to the Committee on their own?

Technically, federal law protects contractor employees who tell Congress about fraud, waste or lawbreaking on a federal contract. The Family has great respect for the law. The Family would simply like to know which employees those are.

SECTION 4. A NOTE ON THE PERSONAL PET PROJECTS

The Family wishes to reassure vendors that this agreement has nothing whatsoever to do with the ballroom.

It also has nothing to do with the golf course on Hains Point, a public park Congress created in 1897 for the “recreation and pleasure of the people,” which the Family has kindly volunteered to make more pleasurable for fewer people. The non-profit that held a 50-year lease to run it has been relieved of that burden. The Park Service awarded the first contracts without bothering Congress with an announcement. The rubble from the East Wing has been stored on site, in a three-storey mound, for safekeeping.

None of this is why the order was signed in November, seven weeks before the people asking about it get their gavels. The timing is a coincidence. The Family is a great believer in coincidence. Coincidences are very hard to subpoena.

SECTION 5. WHAT HAPPENED LAST TIME

Some of our longer-serving vendors will remember 2019, when a House committee subpoenaed the Don’s accountants. On that occasion the Don had to sue his own accounting firm, in his own name, and go all the way to the Supreme Court. It took three years. It was undignified. People talked.

The Family has learned from this. We no longer ask a judge. We simply remind the accountant who pays him.

The Family notes, for the record, that it learned this approach from watching how other institutions were brought into line over the past two years: the universities, the law firms, the broadcasters. All of them discovered that the Family’s displeasure did not need a court ruling to be expensive. Most of them came to an arrangement. We expect our vendors to be at least as sensible as a university.

SECTION 6. APPROVED RESPONSES

For vendors who are nonetheless dragged in front of the Committee, the Family has prepared the following approved answers. Please memorise them. Do not write them down. Writing things down is how all of this started.

  • “I would have to check with the White House.”

  • “That information may be subject to executive privilege.”

  • “I’m not sure I’m the right person to ask.”

  • “I don’t recall.”

  • “I don’t recall recalling.”

  • “The ballroom is a gift to the American people.”

  • “Golf is good for the economy.”

Vendors are reminded that the Public Citizen co-president Lisa Gilbert has already described the order as “a naked ploy to try and protect the perpetrators of this corruption.” Please do not repeat this phrase under oath, even by accident.

SECTION 7. THE FINE PRINT

7.1 This agreement does not apply to the Committee, which is not a party to it and has not been asked.

7.2 This agreement cannot repeal a statute. The Family regrets this and is looking into it.

7.3 Any vendor whose contract is terminated in the next two years should understand that the termination was for convenience, and had nothing to do with any testimony, which in any case never happened, and which the vendor does not recall.

7.4 The Committee may, at its discretion, examine the timing of every terminated contract, put the Family’s own order into evidence as Exhibit A, and ask each vendor under oath what the Family told it to leave out. The Family considers this unlikely. The Family has considered a great many things unlikely this month, including losing the House popular vote by double digits.

7.5 The general statute of limitations for federal crimes is five years. The Family is aware of this. The Family prefers not to discuss it.

CLOSING

The Family thanks you for your understanding and looks forward to many more years of mutually beneficial partnership, or at least two.

Please countersign below and return to the Office of the Consigliere. Do not send a copy to the Committee.

They will be asking for one anyway.
