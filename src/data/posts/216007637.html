PROPERTY OVERVIEW

Welcome to Washington’s most anticipated address — a 90,000-square-foot state ballroom rising where the East Wing used to be, offering unrivalled proximity to the Oval Office, the Rose Garden, and, for the time being, a fair amount of exposed rebar. Seating up to 650 guests, more than triple the capacity of the East Room, The People’s Ballroom is being marketed as “the finest facility of its kind anywhere in the U.S.A.,” a claim that will become independently verifiable once the facility exists.

Designed by McCrery Architects, built by Clark Construction, engineered by AECOM — this is not some fly-by-night operation. This is a $200–400 million commitment to the proposition that America, too, can have a very large room. As the developer himself put it in a Truth Social post issued the day after touring a competitor’s very large room in Beijing: “China has a Ballroom, and so should the U.S.A.!” You heard the man. Reserve today.

CURRENT OCCUPANCY STATUS

Honest disclosure, as required by pre-construction listing standards: the unit is not yet move-in ready. The East Wing that formerly occupied this address — home to the First Lady’s offices since 1902 — has been demolished. Several lawsuits regarding the sequencing of that demolition relative to standard historic-preservation review remain outstanding and are, per our legal team, “not something prospective buyers need to think about right now.” One (1) construction-related injury has been logged this month at an adjacent property (North Portico, currently undergoing separate restoration); the affected worker has been transported and is not expected to affect your closing date.

Anticipated opening: September 2028. This is, coincidentally, very close to the end of the current administration’s second term, a timing detail our marketing department assures us is purely a function of construction logistics and not, as some cynics have suggested, an elegant excuse to still be talking about the ballroom in year four rather than presenting a finished one.

A builder at heart, with — in the words of his own Chief of Staff — “an extraordinary eye for detail.” Sunday remarks characterized the previous condition of the 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue property as “a dump” prior to the current renovation programme, a characterization some may find surprising given the property’s status as a National Historic Landmark since 1960, and which the listing agent will neither confirm nor dispute, since arguing with the developer has not historically gone well for anyone involved in this transaction.

AGENT’S NOTE

Some prospective buyers have asked whether it makes sense to reserve a unit in a building that will not open for two more years, has already produced litigation over how it was started, and is being financed in part by donors whose names will be permanently engraved into a federal building before the building itself has walls. We understand the hesitation. But consider the alternative: hosting a Chinese head of state for a landmark state dinner in whatever existing, non-construction-zone space happens to be available on September 24, 2026 — a far less exciting proposition, and frankly, not much of a listing at all.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q: Will the ballroom be ready in time for the Xi Jinping state visit? A: No. The state dinner on September 24, 2026 will be held in existing White House spaces. Consider this less a delay and more an extended opportunity to admire the construction fencing.

Q: What happens to the lawsuits over the East Wing demolition? A: They continue, entirely independently of this listing, on their own separate and much less glamorous timeline.

Q: Is the $1 billion Secret Service funding request related to the ballroom? A: Approximately $400 million of it is earmarked for ballroom-adjacent security and visitor screening. The remaining $600 million is a separate matter this listing is not authorized to discuss.

Q: Why does China get to keep its Great Hall and we have to build a new one? A: China’s Great Hall was completed in 1959. Ours is projected for 2028. We are, as the developer noted, “ahead of schedule” — schedule undefined.
