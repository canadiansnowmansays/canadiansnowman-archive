COVER NOTE, NOVEMBER 2026

This file came back out of the archive this week. A junior officer had been reading about a chip company, a leather jacket and a quiz in The Atlantic, and asked aloud whether anything like this had ever happened before. The archivist did not answer. He went downstairs and came back with a box.

SECURITY SERVICE: PERSONAL FILE REF: PF/1961/HONEY CLASSIFICATION: SECRET (downgraded 2026 to MERELY EMBARRASSING)

Section 1: Subject Particulars

Name: The Rt Hon. Harry Honeypotter, MP

Office: Secretary of State for War

Married to: A celebrated actress of stage and screen. Of the two of them, she is the one who can act.

Access: Everything. Troop dispositions, weapons programmes, the Cabinet, and the good table at the club.

Distinguishing features: Charm. A firm handshake. An unshakeable belief that rules are written for other people, chiefly people who went to other schools.

Assessed security risk: None.

Basis of assessment: He went to the right school.

Section 2: Positive Vetting Interview (1960)

Conducted in a comfortable office in Mayfair. Sherry was served.

Q: Are there any secrets you haven’t told us?

A: Old boy, I am the secrets.

Q: Do you have any contact with foreign nationals?

A: Only socially.

Q: What would you do if a foreign power tried to compromise you?

A: Notice.

Q: What frightens you?

A: Parliamentary questions. And the fish course at the Savoy.

Assessor’s note: Passed with distinction. The assessor was at school with him and can confirm he was a capital fellow even then.

Section 3: Incident Report: The Swimming Pool

Location: A country estate on the Thames, guest of a viscount.

Date: A warm weekend, July 1961.

Persons present:

  • SUBJECT, in evening clothes, strolling.

  • Miss K., 19, a showgirl, in the pool.

  • Dr S. W., a society osteopath who knows everybody, introduces everybody, and has straightened the spines of half the Cabinet without improving the posture of any of them.

  • Captain Yevgeny “Eugene” I., listed as naval attaché at the Soviet Embassy. His duties as naval attaché are understood to involve very little navy.

Sequence of events:

  1. Subject met Miss K. at the poolside.

  2. Captain I. drove Miss K. back to London.

  3. Subject obtained Miss K.’s telephone number.

  4. Two men with entirely different employers were now ringing the same flat.

Officer’s assessment: Two men, one telephone number, no plan. This Service was aware. This Service did nothing, on the grounds that doing something would have required telling a Minister of the Crown that he was being an idiot, and nobody wanted to be the one to tell him.

Section 4: Intelligence Yield Assessment

It was alleged afterwards that someone once suggested Miss K. might ask the Minister when the Americans meant to put nuclear warheads in West Germany.

She did not ask.

Even if she had, the Minister was not a man who answered questions. He was a man who told people things, and later told the House the opposite.

Conclusion: The honeypot produced no honey. The bees stung everyone anyway.

Section 5: Statement to the Chamber of Commons, March 1963

Rumours were going round Westminster. The Subject rose in the House and told Members that there had been “no impropriety whatsoever.” He added that he would sue anyone who repeated the allegations outside the Chamber, where the law of libel applies.

Ten weeks later, on 5 June 1963, he admitted that he had lied and resigned.

Analyst’s note: The Chamber of Commons is the one room in the kingdom where a Minister may not lie. It is also the room where he is most tempted to.

Second analyst’s note: He did not resign for spying. Nobody ever proved there was any spying. He resigned for misleading the House, which in 1963 was considered the worse offence.

Archivist’s note (added later): Keep this paragraph for future generations, who may find the idea charmingly quaint.

Section 6: Disposition of Persons

SUBJECT: Resigned. Spent the following four decades volunteering in the East End of London, starting with the lavatories. Later appointed CBE. A decent second act, everyone agrees, and paid for in a currency very few public men now carry: shame.

CAPTAIN I.: Recalled to Moscow in January 1963, before anyone thought to ask him anything.

DR S. W.: Prosecuted. Died before the verdict.

MISS K.: Convicted of perjury in a related case and imprisoned. Spent the rest of her life being asked about one summer.

THE PRIME MINISTER: Resigned that October, citing ill health. His party lost the next election.

Pattern noted for the file: The two men with access to secrets walked away. The two people with none paid the bill.

Section 7: Cross-Reference, November 2026

Appended by the Archivist.

On the matter raised by the junior officer, the Archivist sets out the following comparisons:

  • 1961: The honeypot was a Soviet captain with a car and a cover job.

  • 2026: We are told the honeypot is alleged, the asset is alleged, and the only thing nobody disputes is the jacket.

  • 1961: The secrets were warhead delivery dates, kept in a Minister’s head.

  • 2026: The secrets are etched into silicon and shipped by the container-load, some of them legally. The licences come from the Commerce Department, which is also the department responsible for stopping them.

  • 1961: A Minister lied to the House and was out of office within days of admitting it.

  • 2026: The questions have been referred to a hearing, the hearing to a date, and the date to December.

  • 1961: MI5 knew and said nothing, because the man in question went to the right school.

  • 2026: The Archivist is advised that the right school is now called a donor list.
