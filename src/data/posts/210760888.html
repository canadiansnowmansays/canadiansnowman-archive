THE WHITE HOUSE Office of Communications CORRECTIONS AND CLARIFICATIONS — ISSUED THIS WEEK

The following statements were made by the President over the preceding several weeks regarding the condition of the Reflecting Pool renovation. In the interest of accuracy, each is corrected below.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT: “The algae is vandalism. Radical Left lunatics, probably paid, dumping chemicals in there to make us look bad.”

CORRECTION: The algae is algae. It grows in standing water, particularly water sitting in a pool whose lining was not properly sealed during renovation. No radicals, paid or otherwise, were involved in its cultivation. We regret any confusion caused by the President’s earlier, more exciting explanation.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT: “The peeling — you saw it, everybody saw it — that’s sabotage. Someone got in there at night and peeled it. We’re looking at the footage.”

CORRECTION: The footage, once reviewed, showed no one peeling anything at night. The lining is peeling on its own, during the day, for free, without assistance from saboteurs. The President has now conceded there was “some contractor error” involved. We are not aware of a version of this sentence that was harder for anyone to extract than that one.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT: “Whoever did this to the Pool, we will find them, and it will not be pretty for them.”

CORRECTION: No “them” has been found, on account of there being no “them” to find. The responsible party, per the President’s own revised account, was the renovation itself. The Office of Communications can confirm that “it will not be pretty for them” remains available for use against a different party at a future date, pending developments.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT: “This is one of the most beautiful projects ever created by anybody, and people are trying to tear it down with lies about the water.”

CORRECTION: The water was, in fact, discolored, which is not a lie so much as a color. The project’s beauty is a matter of ongoing public debate and is not something this office is in a position to correct, confirm, or comment on further, as it falls outside the category of verifiable fact.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT (via Truth Social): “FAKE NEWS is saying the Reflecting Pool has ‘issues.’ It has ZERO issues. It is PERFECT. The only issue is the people reporting on it.”

CORRECTION: The Reflecting Pool has, per the President’s subsequent statement, at least one issue: “some contractor error.” This office recognizes that ZERO and “at least one” are different numbers and apologizes for any mathematical inconvenience this creates for readers attempting to reconcile the two statements, which were made twelve days apart.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT: “The National Park Service is incompetent, always has been, run by people who hate this country and want to see it fail.”

CORRECTION: The National Park Service is the agency that, per its own Section 106 review, flagged the very landmarks now discoloring and peeling — before construction began, in writing, to the people who proceeded anyway. This office regrets describing as incompetent the one party in this sequence of events that appears, on the available paper trail, to have been right.

ORIGINAL STATEMENT: “We’re going to get to the bottom of who’s responsible, and when we do, it won’t be good for them, believe me.”

CORRECTION: We have, in fact, gotten to the bottom of who’s responsible. It is the contractor. The President said so himself, in five words, after several weeks of saying otherwise in considerably more than five words. This office notes that “it won’t be good for them” has not, as of this printing, been followed by any specific action against the contractor, whose identity and invoice remain, as far as this office is aware, entirely intact.

A NOTE ON THE NATURE OF THIS CORRECTION

Readers may reasonably ask why an administration that spent several weeks insisting a landmark’s decay was the work of vandals, saboteurs, and Fake News would suddenly concede, without apparent prompting, that the actual cause was “some contractor error.” This office does not have insight into that decision and is not in a position to speculate as to motive. We can confirm only that the water is still green, the lining is still peeling, the vandals were never found because they were never real, and the contractor — unlike the vandals — has an actual name, an actual invoice, and an actual phone number that someone at the National Park Service is, as of this week, presumably now dialing.

We would also note, for the sake of completeness, that this is not the first correction this office has issued regarding a National Mall renovation this year, nor is it likely to be the last. A pattern is not an accusation. It is, however, a pattern, and this office is beginning to run out of synonyms for the word “regret.”

On the matter of “some contractor error” specifically: this office wishes to clarify that this phrase does not constitute an apology, an admission of fault, or an acknowledgment of error on the President’s part, and should not be read as such by anyone, anywhere, under any circumstances. The President has not apologized for anything since roughly the Ford administration, and this office sees no reason to suggest, even by implication, that a five-word concession about a contractor represents a change in that policy. If anything, “some contractor error” is best understood as a temporary reassignment of blame to a party who was not available to hold a press conference about it, which is, historically, as close as this administration comes to the word “sorry.”

For readers keeping a running tally, the sequence to date reads roughly as follows: a ballroom that required no congressional approval, until a federal appeals court found that it did. An arch that will obstruct the view from Lincoln to Arlington, which the National Park Service flagged and was overruled on. A Reflecting Pool that was vandalized, until it wasn’t. And an Executive Office Building currently being painted a color its own granite was never designed to hold, which this office has not yet been asked to correct, but has begun drafting language for regardless, on the theory that preparation is its own form of institutional memory.

Bronze statues nearby have also been painted gold. This office has received no complaints about that one yet, which we mention only because it seems, historically speaking, to be a matter of time, and because this office would like the record to reflect that it saw it coming.

THE WHITE HOUSE OFFICE OF COMMUNICATIONS regrets any earlier confidence expressed on this matter and looks forward to not correcting it again.
