Sunday Satire — Spiral-bound, 26 pages, laminated in 24-karat gold. A former president said the White House looks like “Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory.” The current one spent twelve minutes on Monday insisting it doesn’t. Management has decided to settle the matter by printing the menu.

A Note from the Proprietor

Welcome to the finest establishment in Washington. Nobody loved this building like I do. Nobody. The previous managers let the windows go, the tiles go, the columns go — it was a disaster, frankly a disgrace. Tile from a box. I don’t want to say Home Depot. And we made it all beautiful.

Some people, very lousy people, have said this place looks like a chain restaurant. A chain. They said “just gold everywhere,” like that’s a bad thing. They said I should be on HGTV. I would be the greatest HGTV host in history, everybody says so, but I’m too busy fixing up the White House. We are not a chain. We are a single location with a ballroom, a helipad, a patio where a rose garden used to be, and very little time spent on any of it. Very little. Hardly any.

I do it because it relaxes me. Isn’t that a nice thing to do?

— The Proprietor

Before You Order

  • Portions are enormous. This is by design. Nothing in this establishment has ever been served at a size appropriate to the occasion.

  • The menu is 26 pages long because every time the kitchen finishes a project, the Proprietor commissions another one. Page 27 is under construction. So is page 28. Page 14 was demolished to make room for the ballroom.

  • Please be patient with service. The back of the house is currently at war with Iran, and staff have been asked not to discuss it with diners.

  • Market prices apply to fuel, groceries, and anything else a guest might actually need. Market prices do not apply to gold leaf, which is free, because you already paid for it.

Celebrity Sighting

Spotted at our sister location downtown on Monday, October 26, 2026: the Secretary of the Treasury, who lunched at an actual Cheesecake Factory to prove the former president was the elitist in this story, and advised the Martha’s Vineyard set to give it a try.

Management thanks the Secretary for his loyalty and notes that this is the first recorded instance of a Treasury Secretary defending the honour of a cheesecake while gas prices sat at uncomfortable highs. History will decide which crisis he chose wisely.

Management also thanks the cable news network that called the former manager an out-of-touch elitist for mocking a cheesecake restaurant. The man building the 90,000-square-foot ballroom remains, as ever, a man of the people.

Tonight’s Entertainment

ROAST BATTLE ON THE SOUTH LAWN — LIVE

Proposed by a late-night host, who said there was only one way to settle this and promised to phone the Roastmaster General first thing in the morning.

Opening Act: A veteran satirist will remind the house that the Cheesecake Factory is not, in fact, a factory, and that no cheesecake jobs were ever shipped to China. He will also ask why, of everything in the former manager’s comparison, the Proprietor took offence at the cheesecake and not the Saddam Hussein.

The Challenger: The former manager, fresh from a rally in Detroit, said to be in excellent form. Patrons are advised he is now booked out as a stand-up comic. His one-hour special is streaming elsewhere, and he will not be gilding anything.

The Champion: The Proprietor, known to the trade as Rumple Thinskin. Confirmation pending. He has asked to see the ballroom first, then asked to hold the event in the ballroom, then asked whether the ballroom could be finished by tomorrow.

Seating: 650 chairs, 999 guests, one microphone. The microphone is gold.

House Rules: Contestants may say anything about each other’s records, hairlines and buildings. Rebuttals may run no longer than six minutes per remark. (Exemption granted to the Champion, who requires twelve.)
