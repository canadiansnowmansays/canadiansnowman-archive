Sunday Satire. On Wednesday, October 21, 2026, at the Konstantin Palace in Strelna, outside St. Petersburg, U.S. special envoy Steve Witkoff told Vladimir Putin that he, too, was a humanitarian, after Russia released the American Stephen Hubbard, whom the Washington Post reported had been held in detention. The quotations from Witkoff, Trump, Vance and the senators below are real. The Office of Retroactive Justification, its Division of Honours, and its forms are not. A tip of the hat to Alexandra Petri, whose October 21 piece in The Atlantic got to the dictionary first; the Office has gone to the filing cabinet instead.

OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION Division of Honours, Commendations and Definitions Under Review

COMMENDATION No. 2026-1021 Status: CONFERRED (Reciprocal) Classification: For That Particular Purpose

Section 1 — Citation

In recognition of the release, on October 21, 2026, of one (1) American citizen, Mr. Stephen Hubbard, held on charges the United States government has called fabricated, the Office hereby confers upon the President of the Russian Federation the title of Humanitarian, as previously conferred upon the President of the United States by the President of the Russian Federation, and as confirmed in person by the Special Envoy for Peace Missions, who, according to CNBC, saluted, commended, and thanked again.

The Office notes that three separate affirmations (salute, commend, thank again) exceed the programme minimum of one.

Section 2 — Background

The President of the Russian Federation had spoken on several occasions of the President of the United States as a humanitarian. The Special Envoy, whose professional formation is in New York real estate, recognised a reciprocal easement when he saw one.

The programme therefore operates on the principle long familiar to the property trade: you show me your humanitarian, I show you mine. No appraisal is required. No independent inspection is permitted. The title transfers on signing, and both parties agree not to look in the basement.

Section 3 — Revised Eligibility Criteria (Effective October 21, 2026)

Until Wednesday, eligibility for the title of Humanitarian required (a) sustained activity to reduce human suffering, (b) the absence of any sustained activity to increase it, and (c) not being the subject of an outstanding arrest warrant from the International Criminal Court for the unlawful deportation of children.

These criteria have been revised in light of presidential guidance. Asked about his envoy’s remark, President Trump allowed that you could say Putin was a humanitarian “for that particular purpose,” as Spectrum News reported.

Accordingly, eligibility is now assessed per purpose. A nominee need only be humanitarian for the particular purpose under review, on the particular afternoon, in the particular palace. All other purposes are out of scope.

The Office notes, for completeness, that this standard also qualifies the arsonist who calls the fire department, the pickpocket who returns your bus pass, and the landlord who refunds half the damage deposit after keeping the flat.

Section 5 — Letters of Support

From the Vice President. Mr. Vance told reporters on Wednesday morning that he agreed with the Special Envoy’s praise of Putin for the release. The Office has accepted this letter without reading it, which is also how it was written.

From the President. “For that particular purpose.” The Office has engraved this on the reverse of the medal, where space is limited and so, as it turns out, is the purpose.

From the Special Envoy. “You are a humanitarian also, sir.” The word also has been reviewed by the Division of Definitions Under Review and found to be doing a great deal of unpaid work.

Section 6 — Comments Received During the Public Consultation Period (Approximately Four Hours)

The Office received a number of dissenting comments, most of them, unusually, from the President’s own party.

As MS NOW reported, Republican senators Thom Tillis and John Cornyn criticised the remark; one senator suggested the envoy look up the definition of the word. The Office has looked it up. The definition has been referred to the Division of Definitions Under Review, where it joins peace, deal, and beautiful in the queue.

The Office notes that Senator Cornyn’s schedule has been considerably more open since May 26, and thanks him for using the time.

Republican Representative Max Miller of Ohio called the comments ridiculous and invoked the dead civilians of Bucha. Senator Joni Ernst of Iowa said Putin was neither a humanitarian nor Washington’s friend. From across the aisle, Senator Mark Kelly asked what world Putin was a humanitarian in, and answered his own question with the words “war criminal.”

All dissenting comments have been classified as friendly fire, for that particular purpose.

The Office draws attention to the final row. As CNBC noted in its headline, the St. Petersburg talks were held without Ukraine. The previous day, October 20, the same envoy and Jared Kushner had met President Zelensky in Oslo, as Ukrainska Pravda recorded.

Readers of the companion document, The Official Driver’s Handbook: One-Way Street (International Edition), will recall that insults on this route travel in one direction only. The Office is pleased to confirm that compliments do too. They simply travel the other way.

Section 8 — Frequently Asked Questions

Does the title come with a medal? Yes. It is gilt, and will be delivered by the Special Envoy in person. The Office is still determining which side of the front line it was cast on.

Can the title be revoked? Only for a different purpose.

Was the Nobel Committee notified? Notified, yes. Answered, no.

Did the talks produce anything besides a compliment? The Special Envoy said Putin agreed to resume trilateral talks among the United States, Russia and Ukraine. The Office has scheduled the commendation for those talks in advance, to save time.

Who is next on the shortlist? Nominations remain open, each for its own particular purpose. Early submissions include a mugger who leaves his victim bus fare, a tariff that was paused, and a hurricane that missed Mar-a-Lago.

And Mr. Hubbard? Here the Office sets down its pen and speaks plainly. Stephen Hubbard is going home, and that is good news, and anyone with a heart is glad of it. Of everything in this file, that gladness is the only word that still means exactly what it says.

Issued at the Konstantin Palace, Strelna, October 21, 2026. Seal: a dove (for that particular purpose).
