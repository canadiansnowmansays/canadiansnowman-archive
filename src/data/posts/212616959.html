I have been counting for a very long time.

Longer than you’d think. People assume I started when I opened this Substack, and it’s kind, but it isn’t accurate. I was counting well before that. I counted the ships that left for the Armada — a hundred and thirty, and I said at the time it was too many to call fortunate before they’d sailed, but nobody listens to the man with the ledger. I counted the days on Magellan’s ship and came up one short, same as everyone, and I’ve never entirely forgiven the planet for it. I was at the printing of the first folio. I didn’t help — I’m not saying I helped — but I was there, and I noticed they were two men doing the work of a nation, and I noted it down, because that is what I do. I note it down.

It has been, when I tally it honestly, a rather long career. Two thousand years, give or take — though I’m told that’s a suspicious number, and I’ll not defend it.

So this is the index. It’s every year the count has reached so far, in the order it reached them — which is not the order they happened, because I don’t move through history. History keeps turning up where I’m already standing, holding a pencil.

Start wherever you like. I’ll be counting.

New here? Start with The Numbers Game: An Introduction — it explains how a series ends up organized by a running essay count. Otherwise, dip in anywhere a year catches your eye. Each essay stands on its own, though they talk to one another.

Register key: 🖋 wry (written with a wink) · ⚖️ serious (written with my hands still on the keyboard) · 👤 personal. I’ll always tell you at the top of each essay which one you’re getting.

The 1200s–1300s

Essay 1,215 — The one that pushed back (thanks for the inspiration, King John) 🖋 Magna Carta at Runnymede: a king forced to admit he was subject to the law — and a president who shares his middle name.

Essay 1,271 — Marco Polo: One Horsepower Then 🖋 A seventeen-year-old Venetian leaves home for twenty-four years — while, by coincidence, someone retraces his route by electric car.

Essay 1,313 — Dread: The One That Doesn’t Want to Be Written ⚖️ The year with the look of a number that bites — and the dread of knowing what the count is walking toward.

Essay 1,337 — The One Where History Undersold the War ⚖️ The Hundred Years’ War begins — and the chroniclers round a catastrophe down to something a person could hold.

Essay 1,347 — The One I Cannot Make Funny ⚖️ The Black Death arrives. No wink, no punchline — the count reaches a year that refuses them.

Essay 1,378 — The One With Too Many Popes 🖋 The Western Schism: the arithmetic breaks when one office somehow holds two, then three, occupants.

Essay 1,381 — The One Where the Bill Came Due ⚖️ The Peasants’ Revolt: the ledger of the powerful, presented at last to the people who’d been the entries.

The 1400s

Essay 1,431 — The One Where the Verdict Changed Its Mind ⚖️ Joan of Arc: a judgment recorded, then reversed — and what it costs to un-write an official count.

Essay 1,440 — The One That Made All the Others Possible 🖋 Gutenberg’s press: the machine that took the rare and made it endless — the ancestor of everything that follows.

Essay 1,453 — The One That Was an Actual Turning Point ⚖️ The fall of Constantinople: a date historians actually agree to hinge an age upon.

Essay 1,485 — The One About the King Under the Car Park 🖋 Bosworth Field and Richard III — whose count came to rest, centuries later, beneath a Leicester parking lot.

Essay 1,492 — The One Everyone Thinks They Know 🖋 Columbus: the most famous year in the Western calendar, and the least honestly counted.

The 1500s

Essay 1,517 — The Itemized Complaint 🖋 Luther answers the Church’s ledger with a ledger of his own: ninety-five numbered items, nailed to a door.

Essay 1,519 — The Ledger That Stayed Shut ⚖️ Leonardo counted more, and more carefully, than anyone — and published almost none of it. The cost of not keeping the books.

Essay 1,522 — The Day the Earth Stole 🖋 The first ship around the world comes home with a perfect log that is, impossibly, one day wrong — and no one aboard erred.

Essay 1,543 — The Ledger That Fired Its Client ⚖️
Copernicus quietly moves the Earth out of the centre of the universe — demoting all of us from proprietors to a single unremarkable entry.

Essay 1,582 — The Ten Days That Never Happened 🖋
The calendar itself gets audited: a pope deletes ten days from October, and half of Europe refuses the correction out of sheer spite.

Essay 1,588 — The Adjective That Sailed First ⚖️
The Spanish Armada — styled “Most Fortunate” before a single ship sailed. The inventory was magnificent; the weather kept its own books.

The 1600s

Essay 1,602 — The Fraction You Could Sell 🖋
The Dutch invent the tradeable share — and ownership stops being a thing you hold and becomes a number you own, divisible without limit and sellable to strangers.

Essay 1,605 — The Rhyme and the Windmills 🖋
One year, two men at war with reality: a cellar full of gunpowder beneath Parliament, and a Spanish knight charging windmills. History remembers the fictional one more fondly.

Essay 1,609 — Four New Entries 🖋
Galileo raises a tube to the sky and finds four moons circling Jupiter — an observation that could not be un-seen, and could not be un-counted.

Essay 1,620 — The Ship That Founded a Mythology ⚖️
The Mayflower: forty-one signatures on a compact, and half the passengers in the ground by spring. A nation kept the signatures and buried the winter.

Essay 1,623 — The Two Who Kept the Count 🖋
Two actors gather a dead friend’s scattered plays into one volume, seven years on — and thereby save half of Shakespeare from oblivion. The genius wrote the plays; the keepers wrote the future.

Essay 1,633 — The Recantation ⚖️
Galileo, sixty-eight and kneeling, is made to unsay a true number aloud. The institution did not want the count corrected; it wanted it withdrawn.

Essay 1,648 — The Year the Map Became the Unit ⚖️
The Peace of Westphalia ends thirty years of slaughter and invents the sovereign state — flattening a tangle of overlapping loyalties into clean bounded boxes. The map becomes the unit we have counted the world in ever since.

Essay 1,666 — The Year of the Beast, the Fire, and the Apple 🖋
The most feared number on the calendar comes due; London burns; and a farm boy in plague quarantine quietly invents the mathematics of everything. The world did end in 1666 — just not the one that was watching for it.

Essay 1,687 — The Universe, Audited ⚖️
Newton's Principia: the same law governs the falling apple and the orbiting Moon. Halley funds the book out of the proceeds of a flop about fish — and a comet returns on schedule, decades after its predictors are dead.

The 1700s

Essay 1,707 — The Addition of Nations 🖋
England and Scotland become Great Britain not by conquest but by treaty — two kingdoms consenting to become one sum. The price was £398,085 and ten shillings, itemised down to the shilling, because addition is the one arithmetic operation that requires the consent of the thing being added.

Essay 1,752 — The Eleven Days, Payable at Last 🖋
Britain finally accepts the calendar correction it had refused for a hundred and seventy years, and the second of September is followed by the fourteenth. The arithmetic was never in question — only the signature at the bottom of it — and the fuse lit in 1582 reaches the powder at last.

Essay 1,755 — The Count That Showed Its Work 🖋
Johnson’s Dictionary defines 42,773 words, and loses that count outright to a Stepney schoolmaster who had six thousand more. What made it matter was the number nobody quotes: 114,000 quotations — the moment English stopped asserting and began showing its evidence.

Essay 1,759 — Fifteen Minutes on a Field 👤
An army climbs a cliff in the dark, two lines form above the St. Lawrence, and the future of a continent is settled in about a quarter of an hour. Both commanders fell — and the shortest number on the entire map decided which language this narrator would keep his books in.

Essay 1,769 — The Whole Species Takes a Measurement 🖋
Rival empires, barely six years out of a world war, issue passports to each other’s astronomers and point dozens of telescopes at one black dot crossing the sun. The species measures the distance to the sun to within about one percent, and Lieutenant Cook brings his crew home having lost essentially no one to scurvy.

Essay 1,776 — The Proof and the Promissory Note ⚖️
The Declaration isn’t a cry of the heart. It is a proof, built like Euclid’s: axioms first, facts submitted to a candid world. It also left out about half a million people, one in five of those its self-evident truth applied to. America has been presenting that promissory note against its authors ever since.

Essay 1,789 — Two Revolutions, One Year, Opposite Ledgers ⚖️
New York swears in a president, and Paris fights over whether to count votes by order or by head. Two revolutions reach their decisive hour in the same year, and they keep their books by opposite methods: one leaves the books open, the other closes them. The difference in the bookkeeping is the difference in the body count.

Essay 1,791 — The Invoice ⚖️
In the richest colony on Earth, half a million people were counted precisely and filed under equipment, and then they won their freedom outright. France answered with warships and a bill for 150 million francs. Haiti finished paying for its independence in 1947.

Essay 1,812 — Nil–Nil, Three Winners 🖋
A war meant to be “a mere matter of marching” ends at Ghent with no territory changed and an official score of nil–nil. Then Americans, British and Canadians each go home and post a victory in their own books. The only honest ledger belonged to the people the peace betrayed.

Essay 1,835 — The Blank Column 🖋
A seasick young naturalist spends five weeks in the Galápagos, collects the finches, and forgets to write down which island each one came from. The greatest data-collection error in the history of science was rescued by an ornithologist in London and a servant who labelled everything.

Essay 1,842 — The Auditor Who Was Shown the Good Books ⚖️
Dickens sees straight through the solitary cells of Philadelphia, then tours Kingston Penitentiary and calls it excellently regulated. In the same building, an eight-year-old was flogged for giggling. An audit is only as honest as the ledger you’re allowed to open. At the end, the register turns to the person who taught this narrator to love history.

Essay 1,844 — The Year Distance Stopped Counting 🖋
“What hath God wrought”: Morse turns language into counted pulses, and the message no longer needs a messenger. The truth gets faster, and so does the lie. The telegraph didn’t invent the dishonest advantage; it only gave it a clock.

Essay 1,857 — The Dead Who Were Counted and the Dead Who Were Estimated ⚖️
Rebellion in India. The British dead at Cawnpore are named, carved and carried home. The Indian dead of the reprisals are a range in the hundreds of thousands, with almost none named. The oldest injustice in the ledger: precise grief for some, estimated grief for others.

Essay 1,865 — The Fraction, Retired ⚖️
April 1865: a war fought over a constitutional fraction ends at Appomattox. Five days later, a president is shot for suggesting the men once counted as three-fifths should count as one, each. The fraction was retired. The instinct that wrote it merely changed columns.

Essay 1,867 — The Quiet Addition 👤
Seventy-two resolutions drafted by committee, in winter, and a country founded without a shot. Its promise was peace, order and good government: a deliverable you can audit, not a happiness you can’t. The addition is still running, one entry at a time, at a kitchen table in Toronto.

Essay 1,868 — The Country That Audited the Auditors 🖋
Japan looks at the ledger the West is keeping about it and decides to keep a better one: it sends half its government abroad to count the West, and deletes most of a month from its own calendar. The most admirable act of self-determination in the century, and the same machinery that would later be turned on its neighbours.

Essay 1,869 — The Man I Have Been Impersonating All Along 👤
Stephen Leacock: an economist by trade, whose economics everyone forgot and whose jokes everyone kept. A name on a father’s shelf, a man alone on a stage being a whole town, and the confession of where this series got its voice.

Essay 1,876 — The Voice, Wired 🖋
Conceived above the Grand River at Brantford, patented three days before it worked, and decided by the order of two filings on the same day. The first call ever made asked for the one thing a telephone can’t deliver: come here, I want to see you.

Essay 1,882 — The First Thing Ever Sold That No One Could See 🖋
Edison lights a corner of Manhattan, and light stops being something you make and becomes something you’re billed for, by a meter you can’t read. Once a month, a man weighed a metal plate from a jar, and that was your bill.

Essay 1,885 — The Country That Had to Subtract to Add ⚖️
An iron spike completes the railway from sea to sea. Nine days later, Louis Riel is hanged in Regina, and the same Parliament puts a fifty-dollar head tax on the countrymen of the men who blasted the line through the mountains. One country, three ledgers.

Essay 1,895 — The Year Power Stopped Being Local 🖋
Niagara sends its force down a wire to a city twenty-six miles away, and the War of the Currents turns out to be an argument about which number to count in. Ontario picked twenty-five cycles and spent half a century fixing every clock in the province.

Essay 1,897 — Duration as Legitimacy ⚖️
A Diamond Jubilee, invented for the occasion because no monarch had lasted long enough to need one. London counted Victoria’s sixty years to the day; India’s famine dead went uncounted to the nearest hundred thousand. Only two sovereigns have ever reached sixty years, and I’ve lived under both.

Essay 1,898 — The Degree Is Disputed. The Reverence Is Not. 👤
Hugh Llewellyn Keenleyside: diplomat, a refuser of the 1942 removal, a dam with his name on it, and a relation I rounded up to great-uncle out of love. Everything I know of him came on paper, because the only time I knocked on his door, nobody answered.

The 1900s

Essay 1,905 — The Exchange Rate ⚖️
A patent clerk in Bern sets the exchange rate between a gram and a city. E=mc², posted in 1905, was cashed in 1945.

Essay 1,913 — Time, Sold by the Piece 🖋
Ford’s moving assembly line cuts the working day into pieces that can be timed, counted and sold.

Essay 1,914 — War by Timetable ⚖️
Eleven thousand trains that could be started but not paused. And Great-Uncle Art, regimental number 928726, whose name is on no memorial.

Essay 1,918 — The Country That Counted Honestly Got the Blame ⚖️
The flu kills more people than the war it followed, and it is named for Spain, the one country that didn’t censor the count.

Essay 1,929 — The Day the Ticker Ran Late ⚖️
The ticker falls hours behind the trading floor, and a nation of investors reads old prices as the present.

Essay 1,936 — The Paper That Proved the Count Has a Limit 🖋
A young Alan Turing proves that almost all numbers can never be computed, and invents the computer in the process.

Essay 1,939 — The List in the Attic ⚖️
Two lists of refugees: 669 children counted onto eight trains by a London stockbroker, and Canada’s answer to the St. Louis. A ninth train that never left. A scrapbook in an attic.

Essay 1,942 — Eleven Million, by Country ⚖️
Fifteen officials in a lakeside villa outside Berlin draw up the most terrible table of figures ever recorded.

Essay 1,945 — The Countdown Was Invented for the Moon ⚖️
A film director’s countdown to a make-believe Moon launch is first used for real to set off a bomb. And a Canadian mine at Port Radium kept two ledgers.

Essay 1,947 — A Census, Drawn as a Border ⚖️
Partition: a disputed census turned into a border in five weeks by a man who had never been to India.

Essay 1,948 — The Unit of Everything 🖋
Shannon’s bit, the transistor, and a declaration of human rights whose first draft was written by a Canadian. It was the year everything became countable, rights included.

Essay 1,951 — Measured in Sinks 👤
My own year: born four months too late for the census, small enough to bathe in the sink, and named for a grandfather who died the year I arrived.

Essay 1,953 — Four Letters, and the Woman Left Out of the Count 🖋
Life turns out to be a four-letter code. Rosalind Franklin’s photograph is filed under other men’s names, and it takes a graffitist to put her name back on the plaque.

Essay 1,963 — The Hour I Would Have Sworn To 👤
Where were you when you heard? I checked my own answer against the clock in Dallas and found my hour wrong by sixty minutes and my summer wrong by a year. The people in it stayed put.

Essay 1,969 — The Count That Ran Backward 🖋
A dead president’s deadline, kept with five months to spare. An overflow alarm survived by design, thirty seconds of fuel spent on precision, and one missing letter in eleven words.

Essay 1,989 — The Year Two Walls Came Down 🖋
One wall falls on a proposal marked “vague but exciting”, another because a spokesman misreads a date on live television. And a square in Beijing, which I have stood in, where the count was forbidden.

The 2000s

Essay 2,001 — The Name That Is a Number ⚖️
The only catastrophe in the series named by its date. Seventeen minutes, a memory checked against the clock, a Canadian scout on Flight 175, and a town in Newfoundland that fed everyone who landed.

Essay 2,026 — The Distance 👤
The last of the numbered years, and the answer to the confession at 1,066. A husky, an e-bike, and the length of the Trans-Canada Highway, passed without leaving Toronto.

The count runs on, but the numbered years are done. Next: Year Zero, on Christmas morning, a year that never existed. On Boxing Day, the last word goes to someone a good deal older than I am. This index updates as each essay lands.
