CanadianSnowmanSays | Stephen’s Sunday Satire

A 600-ton steel arch has risen on the South Lawn of the White House as a venue for the upcoming Ultimate Fighting Championship event.Credit...Allison Robbert for The New York Times

I want to begin by wishing the President of the United States a very happy 80th birthday.

June 14th. Flag Day. A day that has commemorated the adoption of the Stars and Stripes since 1777, which is, if my arithmetic is correct, approximately 169 years before the President himself arrived. This coincidence has not been lost on the President. It has, in fact, been exploited to its fullest possible extent.

This week, the National Park Service quietly updated its schedule of free admission days. June 14th — Trump’s birthday and Flag Day — has been added. Martin Luther King Jr. Day has been removed. Juneteenth has been removed. Both are federally recognized holidays. Neither, apparently, clears the bar for free park access. The President’s birthday does. One is asked to absorb this information calmly and move on.

I note, in passing, that June is also Pride Month. The administration that removed Juneteenth from the park admission schedule has also spent considerable energy removing LGBTQ protections, scrubbing rainbow flags from federal buildings, and reversing hard-won recognitions. The juxtapositions of June 2026 are, to put it mildly, instructive.

Meanwhile, in Congress, Representative Claudia Tenney of New York has introduced the “Trump’s Birthday and Flag Day Holiday Establishment Act,” which would permanently codify June 14th as a federal holiday, recognizing the President as, and I am quoting directly, “the founder of America’s Golden Age.” Just as George Washington’s birthday is a federal holiday, Tenney argues, so too should Trump’s be — the implication being that Washington and Trump occupy roughly equivalent positions in the American story, a comparison that Washington, were he available for comment, might find surprising.

But the birthday festivities do not end there. Because no 80th birthday is complete without a cage fight, a 600-ton steel arch, a lawsuit filed by a Vietnam veteran, and a stock purchase.

Construction crews have been at work on the South Lawn of the White House, building a venue for a UFC fight to be held on the President’s birthday. The venue includes a 600-ton steel arch, which now dominates the skyline of what was, until recently, the most recognizable lawn in American democracy. The Rose Garden will, presumably, be watching. The Oval Office will be within earshot of whatever sounds a UFC fight produces, which are not, traditionally, the sounds of statecraft. The Easter Egg Roll is held on this lawn. Marine One departs from this lawn, carrying presidents to and from the machinery of government. Press access to those departures has been closed since construction began. The octagon takes priority.

The fighters, according to court documents filed this week, are expected to make their pre-fight walkouts from the Oval Office. The people’s office — the room where Lincoln signed the Emancipation Proclamation, where Roosevelt negotiated the Treaty of Portsmouth, where Kennedy managed the Cuban Missile Crisis — will serve as a green room for mixed martial artists on June 14th. The Lincoln Memorial, meanwhile, is scheduled to host the ceremonial weigh-ins. Abraham Lincoln, who presided over the bloodiest conflict in American history and was assassinated while trying to bind the nation’s wounds, will watch men step on scales in his shadow.

This is not satire. This is the schedule.

One sees no particular irony in any of this and moves on.

A federal lawsuit was filed by a political organizer and a Vietnam veteran, represented by the Public Integrity Project, arguing that the transformation of the South Lawn was never authorized by Congress and that the event constitutes an extraordinary use of government space to benefit both Dana White, the chief executive of UFC, and the President himself. The lawsuit cites premium tickets to the event selling for more than $1 million. It also notes that in March, while actively promoting the event, the President purchased between $15,000 and $50,000 worth of stock in TKO Holding Group, the parent company of UFC.

The President, in other words, has used the White House grounds — public property — to stage a private for-profit sporting event run by his personal ally, while holding a financial stake in the promotion company, having removed Juneteenth from the free park schedule to make room for it. The case has been assigned to Judge Amit P. Mehta, an Obama appointee in Federal District Court in Washington. The universe, as always, has a sense of humour.

This is not, it should be noted, an impulsive gesture. Trump has sat cageside at UFC matches with the regularity of a season ticket holder. Dana White has described the relationship simply: “Everyone has their thing, and Donald Trump’s thing is the UFC.” It is June. It is Pride Month. And the President of the United States is spending his birthday watching men in tight shorts grapple on the ground, apply submission holds to each other’s limbs, and press each other against cage walls while he watches from the front row. The same administration that has systematically dismantled LGBTQ recognition has arranged for its birthday celebration to consist of sweating, grappling men in an octagon on the South Lawn. I raise this not to be uncharitable — the sport is legitimate, the athleticism genuine — but simply to observe that the cultural politics of the moment are producing a tableau of considerable richness.

And here is where the birthday celebrations ascend to a plane of self-regard that deserves its own moment of architectural reflection.

Because the 600-ton steel arch on the South Lawn is not the only arch the President is building.

Across the Potomac, at Memorial Circle near Arlington National Cemetery, the Trump administration is simultaneously pursuing a 250-foot triumphal arch — a permanent monument inspired by the Arc de Triomphe in Paris, featuring a Lady Liberty figure holding a torch aloft, flanked by two gilded eagles, with the Pledge of Allegiance inscribed in gold lettering on both sides. The four lions originally planned to guard its base have been removed following public objection, though one suspects they will be missed.

Vietnam War veterans have sued to stop it, arguing it would obstruct the sightline between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington House — a sightline deliberately designed after the Civil War to symbolize national reunification. A military veteran on the National Capital Planning Commission objected that “what’s required here, if anything, is a solemn memorial rather than a monument.” The commission has requested more information, including how the 250-foot structure might affect air navigation. This is not a question that typically arises during memorial planning, and suggests the arch has reached a scale that concerns people beyond the aesthetically sensitive.

Trump has asserted he does not need congressional approval for the arch because he is building it on federal land. This is the same logic he has applied to the South Lawn UFC venue. The President has discovered that federal land is very useful when you own the government.

He has also, in a video posted from the Oval Office, mused about the UFC arena’s future. He noted that the Eiffel Tower was originally meant to be temporary but was never taken down. “We’re building something in front of the White House that’s quite attractive to a lot of people,” he said. “Maybe we’ll never ever take it down.” The man simultaneously inspired by the Arc de Triomphe and the Eiffel Tower — both Parisian — is building a UFC cage he would like to keep forever and a triumphal arch he is comparing to France’s greatest monuments, on the grounds of a democracy he is systematically refashioning in his own image.

Paris, for the record, took two years and three hundred workers to build the Eiffel Tower. The Arc de Triomphe took thirty years and was commissioned by Napoleon, which is a comparison the White House communications team may wish to consider before deploying it further.

On April 11th, while Trump sat cageside at UFC 327 in Miami, grinning and waving to the crowd, Vice President JD Vance was in Islamabad announcing that twenty-one hours of peace talks had failed to produce a ceasefire with Iran. The President was at the fights. The Vice President was delivering news of a failed negotiation in a war that has killed more than 1,200 Iranians. “Everyone has their thing.”

Compare, if you will — and the President himself invites comparison, having posted a side-by-side photograph this week of “Hussein Obama” versus “Trump,” using what appears to be the same photograph twice, identical cloud formations, identical water ripple patterns, identical everything — the image of Barack Obama, who will turn 65 this August, running half-marathons, kite-surfing, shooting hoops, photographed everywhere he goes in the kind of physical condition that suggests a man who has not spent the last decade moving primarily between a golf cart and a fast food counter.

The photograph was meant to show the improvement Trump has brought to Washington. The problem is that it is the same photograph. The Washington Monument looked the same in both. The world noticed. The President did not.

He is already planning next year’s celebration. His 80th birthday will also feature a UFC event — bigger, more elaborate, already confirmed. The founder of America’s Golden Age, turning 80, with professional fighters walking out of his Oval Office, a 600-ton steel arch on his lawn that he would like to keep forever, and a 250-foot triumphal arch across the river that Vietnam veterans are suing to prevent.

From Canada, where we mark our birthdays with cake and a modest gathering of people who actually like us, this is a great deal to absorb in a single week.

But I am determined to be gracious. Happy birthday, Mr. President. The flag is flying — it always is on June 14th, and has been since 1777, long before you arrived to share the date. The parks are free today, at least for those whose holidays haven’t been quietly removed from the schedule to make room.

It is Pride Month. It is your birthday. Two men are going to hit each other on your lawn and you are going to love every minute of it. Lincoln is hosting the weigh-ins. The Eiffel Tower endures. The Arc de Triomphe endures. Napoleon, at least, waited until he had won a few wars.

Many happy returns.
