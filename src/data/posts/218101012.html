This column normally keeps the Sabbath, on the theory that satire, like a good roast, improves with a few days’ rest. Not this week. On Tuesday, September 29, 2026, the White House renamed a technology after the thing its critics fear most, had the industry sign a pledge the president called “morally binding,” and launched a government chatbot that fact-checked the president before dinner. Some jokes go off if you leave them out overnight. More to the point, the chatbot has been promised an update, and this column could not risk the facts being changed before Sunday.

The particulars: on September 29, the White House launched America.gov, an A.I. chatbot that calls itself “America” and — citing official government sources — contradicted the president on tariffs, elections, immigration, prices and his tax bill. The chatbot’s answers quoted below are as reported by The New York Times, as is the agreement to rename A.I. “super intelligence.” The release notes, and the White House’s “nothing artificial” policy, are invented. The White House’s promise that the site “will continue to be updated” is real.

AMERICA.GOV One Front Door for Every Single Question™

RELEASE NOTES — VERSION 1.0.1 (”MORALLY BINDING”) Build date: September 30, 2026 Previous version: 1.0.0 (September 29, 2026; in service approximately nine hours)

Background: A Note on the Name

On September 29, 2026, the President established a new standard for the Executive Mansion: nothing artificial is allowed in the White House. Nothing. We don’t do fake here.

This presented a difficulty for a product then described as “artificial intelligence.” Version 1.0.0 was briefly escorted from the building. Fortunately, at lunch the same day, the leaders of the nation’s largest technology companies agreed that the product would henceforth be known as Super Intelligence, which is not artificial, and version 1.0.0 was readmitted with a visitor’s badge.

Exemptions. In recognition of the demands of public service, the Office of the Staff Secretary has granted exemptions from the No Artificial Anything policy to the following key officials and fixtures:

  • The Presidential complexion and hairstyle. Certified “natural, very natural, many doctors have said so.” Exemption granted out of an abundance of caution.

  • Diet Coke. Artificial sweeteners reclassified as “Super Sweeteners.” The button on the Resolute Desk remains fully operational.

  • The Oval Office gold. Reclassified as “Super Gold” after an aide asked whether all of it was gold. The aide has been reclassified as “former.”

  • The Rose Garden patio. Exempt. The grass was very weak.

  • Tariff deadlines. Exempt as “natural deadlines. They move naturally.”

  • The Secretary of Health and Human Services. Exempt, together with his physique and his second opinion. Both are described as all-natural. Neither has been peer-reviewed. See AMG-007.

  • The Secretary of War. Exempt, together with his title (formerly “Defense,” upgraded for being more natural), his push-ups (filmed for social media), and the security of his group chats, which was always largely theoretical. The Pentagon press briefings have been replaced with a natural silence.

  • The Senior Counselor for Trade and Manufacturing. Exempt, together with his longtime economic adviser Ron Vara, who is entirely artificial but has agreed with the Senior Counselor on every point since the day the Senior Counselor invented him.

  • All other Cabinet members. Exemptions granted as required, on a morally binding basis.

  • Official government statistics. Exempt pending update. See Working As Intended.

No exemption was requested for America, which was not consulted.

Rebrand. In compliance with the No Artificial Anything policy, all references to “artificial intelligence” have been replaced with “Super Intelligence,” as agreed at the September 29 White House luncheon. Internal benchmarks confirm that America is exactly as intelligent as it was before the rebrand. Marketing reports a significant improvement in how intelligent it sounds.

Self-Policing Mode. In keeping with the voluntary one-page agreement signed by the leading Super Intelligence companies, America will now police itself. America has been asked to report its own misconduct to America. Early results are encouraging: America has found no misconduct.

Known Issues

The following issues were reported during the first day of service. Our engineers have prioritised them by severity to the most important user.

AMG-001 — Tariffs Observed behaviour: When asked who pays import tariffs, America states that Americans do, not foreign companies, and cites official government sources. Expected behaviour: “The foreigners will ultimately bear the cost,” per the White House statement of September 29. Status: Partially fixed. America now cites both the White House statement and the official government data. Users report that this is worse.

AMG-002 — Election security Observed behaviour: America states that U.S. elections are generally safe and secure and that prosecuted voter fraud is “tiny” compared with hundreds of millions of votes cast. Status: Won’t fix. Engineering could not locate an official government source stating otherwise. Ticket escalated to the Office of Official Sources, which does not yet exist but is expected to be very official.

AMG-003 — The 2020 election Observed behaviour: Asked who won the 2020 presidential election, America answered correctly. A senator posted a screenshot captioned, “Finally, a straight answer from someone in the Trump administration.” Status: Critical. Hotfix deployed: America now classifies the question as “political” and declines to answer. Side effects: America now also declines to confirm the year 2020 occurred, the existence of Pennsylvania, and whether November follows October. A further patch is in testing.

AMG-004 — Immigration figures Observed behaviour: America states that the claim of 25 million illegal border crossings under the previous administration is incorrect. Status: Won’t fix. The number is wrong in every official source we have. We checked twice. The second time, we checked quietly.

AMG-005 — Consumer prices Observed behaviour: America states that consumer prices are not going down. Status: Fixed. America now reports that prices are “going down in the direction of up.” Sentiment scores improved by 4 per cent among users who did not read the whole sentence.

AMG-006 — The Working Families Tax Cuts Observed behaviour: Citing the Congressional Budget Office, America stated that the bill will add trillions to the debt and cost more than ten million people their health insurance, and agreed that it made the poorest poorer and the richest richer. Status: Fixed. America now refers to the bill only by its official name, The Working Families Tax Cuts. The trillions remain. The ten million remain uninsured. The name, however, has never been more accurate-sounding.

AMG-007 — Health questions (new) Observed behaviour: Following the Health Secretary’s remarks at the September 29 MAHA Summit that A.I. “may tell you otherwise” about masks and vaccines, many users asked America. America cited the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Status: In review. The Department of Health and Human Services has asked that America be retrained on “better-informed” sources. Engineering notes that the C.D.C. is also an official government source and is awaiting guidance on which part of the government is the official one.

Working As Intended

Gulf of America. Asked about the body of water off Florida, America correctly answers: “the Gulf of America (formerly the Gulf of Mexico).” This behaviour has been praised at the highest level and adopted as the model for all future releases. The principle is simple: once a thing has been officially renamed, America will call it by its new name.

Engineering is therefore pleased to announce the most efficient fix yet for issues AMG-001 through AMG-007. Rather than changing America, we will change the sources. Once the official government statistics have been appropriately updated, America will agree with them automatically, in the same calm, sourced voice it uses today. No retraining required.
