OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION Executive Office of the President

MEMORANDUM FOR: Director, Department of Government Efficiency (Restored), Fraud Detection Unit FROM: Deputy Assistant Director for Scope Integrity DATE: October 5, 2026 SUBJECT: Scope of the Fraud Detection Mandate, as Originally Issued and as Subsequently Amended CLASSIFICATION: UNCLASSIFIED // EMBARRASSING

1. Original Scope (as issued 09:00, October 5, 2026)

Pursuant to the President’s determination that fraud is everywhere, and that nobody has ever seen fraud like it, the Department of Government Efficiency is hereby restored. Its mandate is:

To identify and eliminate all waste, fraud and abuse in the federal government, wherever it hides, without fear or favour, and to follow the money wherever it leads.

The Unit is reminded that “wherever it leads” is a figure of speech.

2. Amendment No. 1 (issued 09:41, October 5, 2026)

Re: Transactions Without a Purchaser

Forty-one minutes into its first query, the Unit flagged a Department of Homeland Security purchase of five aircraft from Daedalus Aviation Corporation, approved on the morning of March 24, 2026. That was the last morning of Secretary Noem’s tenure. She was in Guyana at the time, beginning her new duties as Special Envoy, and her successor was due to take over that afternoon.

The Unit’s preliminary notes identified:

  • A Boeing 737 converted into a private jet, complete with a stand-up bar, two bedrooms, marble showers and a table upholstered in manta ray skin. It was purchased by the Department for $108 million. Its previous owners had been unable to sell it for less than $90 million for more than two years, and Daedalus had acquired it in September 2025.

  • Two ten-year-old Gulfstream jets that Daedalus bought for about $83 million combined and resold to the Department for $106 million, a markup of more than $11 million per aircraft.

  • Two 17-year-old Boeing 737s, formerly flown by Southwest and Avelo, at $45 million each. Six aviation experts consulted by the Times put the going rate between $15 million and $30 million. One of them, Richard Aboulafia, said, “I hope they came with buckets of caviar each.”

The Unit is advised that these findings fall outside its scope, for the following reasons.

Secretary Noem’s office states that the purchase was finalised under Secretary Mullin. Secretary Mullin’s office states that the aircraft were purchased before Secretary Mullin arrived. The Office accepts both statements. The aircraft therefore have no purchaser. Fraud requires a fraudster, and a transaction with no purchaser cannot be fraud. It can only be weather.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude any transaction for which every responsible official has a statement saying someone else did it.

The Unit further notes that Secretary Mullin considered cancelling the purchase and consulted the White House Chief of Staff, who advised against it because of the financial penalty. The Office commends this as the only act of fiscal restraint in the file: the government declined to waste money on not wasting money.

3. Amendment No. 2 (issued 10:15, October 5, 2026)

Re: Donors Who Did Not Know

The Unit observed that Daedalus’s chairman, William Walters, contributed $10,000 in 2024 to American Resolve, Secretary Noem’s political action committee, and that companies led by Mr. Walters subsequently received more than $900 million in Department contracts, including a no-bid arrangement to act as a middleman on the aircraft.

The company has explained that Mr. Walters did not know the committee was associated with Secretary Noem. The Office notes that the committee’s website prominently displayed a photograph of Secretary Noem’s face. The Office finds this consistent with not knowing. Many people look at a face without knowing whose it is. It is the basis of the entire portrait industry.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude contributions from any donor who states that he did not know, together with all contracts subsequently awarded to companies led by that donor, and all companies those companies then hired to park the aircraft (see Salus Worldwide Solutions, more than $2 million in May and again in June, for looking after planes that were bought from Salus’s sister company to replace planes chartered from Salus).

4. Amendment No. 3 (issued 10:52, October 5, 2026)

Re: Documents Whose Release Would Compromise National Security

The Unit requested the Department’s written rationale for awarding Daedalus a no-bid contract. The law generally requires such documents to be made public. The Department has declined on the grounds that release would “compromise national security or create other security risks.”

The Office has reviewed this position and agrees. For the purposes of this memorandum, “national security” is defined as the security of any national named in the document.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude any document the Unit would need in order to find anything.

5. Amendment No. 4 (issued 11:30, October 5, 2026)

Re: Aircraft Painted in the Presidential Livery

The Unit’s attention was drawn to the fact that, while the Department was still chartering the manta-ray jet for Secretary Noem’s travel, Daedalus repainted it in the same red, white and blue scheme as the President’s redesigned Air Force One.

The Unit is reminded that the colour scheme belongs to the President, and that any aircraft wearing it is, for audit purposes, a family member.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude any asset that has been made to look like the President’s.

6. Amendment No. 5 (issued 12:10, October 5, 2026)

Re: Advertising

The Unit flagged a $200 million-plus Department advertising campaign that hired several of Secretary Noem’s political allies. That campaign was among the controversies that preceded the President’s announcement, on March 5, 2026, that the Secretary would be replaced. She then remained in office for a further 18 days, during which the aircraft were purchased.

The Unit also flagged a series of taxpayer-funded public service announcements featuring the President. The Office reminds the Unit that these were public service announcements. Service is not spending. A message that serves the public by telling the public about the President is, by definition, efficient, since it delivers both the service and the President in a single broadcast.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude advertising, messaging, announcements, campaigns and any video in which the President appears, including videos in which he appears to be advertising.

7. Amendment No. 6 (issued 13:05, October 5, 2026)

Re: The Private Sector

The Unit, apparently by following the money one step too far, arrived at the President’s certified 2025 financial disclosure, which reports $636 million from the $TRUMP memecoin and $799 million from World Liberty Financial.

The Office reminds the Unit that it is the Department of Government Efficiency. The President’s income is private sector income, even when it is earned by being President. That income is not government money until it has been spent by the government, at which point it is a contract and falls under Amendment No. 2.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude the President, his family, his businesses, his coins, his tokens, his stablecoins, his trademarks, his golf courses, his licensing agreements and any future ventures not yet announced.

8. Amendment No. 7 (issued 13:40, October 5, 2026)

Re: The Unit

The Unit has submitted a query regarding its own contracting history. The Office declines to describe the query further.

Amendment: The scope is hereby narrowed to exclude the Unit.

9. Revised Scope (consolidated as of 14:00, October 5, 2026)

Taking Amendments 1 through 7 together, the Department of Government Efficiency (Restored) is directed to identify and eliminate waste, fraud and abuse committed by:

(a) immigrants; (b) states that did not vote for the President; (c) the Kennedy Center, which is closed; (d) any official who has publicly disagreed with the President, retroactively to the date of the disagreement; and (e) whoever bought the planes, once identified.

The Unit is reminded to pursue item (e) vigorously, and the Office is confident that it will never be identified.

10. Preliminary Findings Under the Revised Scope

Total fraud identified to date: $0.00.

The Office regards this as conclusive evidence that the revised scope is working. A fraud unit that finds no fraud is either incompetent or looking in a clean place, and the Office has taken every step to ensure it is looking in a clean place.

11. Disposition of Findings Under the Original Scope

All findings produced between 09:00 and 09:41 on October 5, 2026, before Amendment No. 1 took effect, have been sealed and placed aboard one of the ten aircraft the Department purchased from Daedalus for a total of $464 million.

The aircraft is stored at Chennault International Airport in Lake Charles, Louisiana, with most of the others. The Department bought them urgently for deportation flights, but lacks the crews to fly them. One was leased to the FBI, the Department now says four will carry government leaders and members of Congress on overseas trips, and the manta-ray jet is awaiting transfer to the Pentagon, which has not agreed to take it. The Department has declined to say when, if ever, any of the ten will be used for a deportation flight.

The findings are therefore safe. They are in the one place in the federal government where nothing has moved since spring.
