Welcome back to the Sunday round-up of the week’s Substack writing: long reads on politics, detours into history, a closer look at AI, science and technology, and the odd satire when events left me no choice.

The ground covered is broad; the point of view is not. It is Canadian, written from just outside the room, anchored in the record, and dry in the way Stephen Leacock was dry, on the understanding that the person in the doorway usually has the better view.

American politics is the main event: its institutions, its rot, its surprising talent for recovery, and the consequences for everyone living in its long shadow, which is nearly everyone. Around it sit the other series, on AI governance and energy, foreign affairs, Canada’s trade fight, space, consumer advocacy, and the long history of democracies under pressure. Some essays investigate. Some explain. The satire carries a label, for the avoidance of doubt.

New subscribers, welcome. Start wherever you came in, then go exploring. The voice doesn’t change from one series to the next.

Ukraine: The Country That Would Not Be Traded is a new series I should have started long ago. It tells the story of Russia’s war from the Ukrainian side, beginning with this month’s crisis over the Trump–Russia diesel deal. The three foundation essays went up on Wednesday, Thursday and Friday. From next week the series settles into a weekly update, posted on Fridays.

The Numbers Game had its biggest week yet, thirteen essays running from Confederation to the First World War, and a few of them are personal. Essay 1867 marks the year Canada became a country, and tells that story through the people who share my house today. Essay 1869 is about the man I have been impersonating all along: Stephen Leacock, born that year. Essay 1898 belongs to my great-uncle, Hugh Keenleyside, the diplomat who later ran the UN’s Technical Assistance Administration. My mother loved to tell how Hugh travelled with King George on the royal tour across Canada, and how, years later, he sat beside the Queen and Prince Philip at a dinner party and was proud to tell them about that trip.

Alberta votes in its referendum tomorrow, Monday, October 19th. The Neverendum Story will pick up the result, whichever way it goes. Somewhere in a Tim Hortons in Red Deer, someone is already saying, “Again? Must be Tuesday.” How fitting my 1867 essay landed the week between the Quebec vote and the Alberta vote.

The US midterms are just over two weeks away, on Tuesday, November 3rd. He Loves War: The Hegseth Files passes the halfway mark and finishes on October 28th, while The Household Ledger, When Democracy Fights Back, Let’s Talk Texas and Loyal Opposition keep building toward the vote.
