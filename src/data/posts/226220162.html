Editor’s note: This casting memo was recovered on Monday, November 23, 2026, seven days after Amazon MGM Studios named Robert Pattinson the next James Bond, and two days after BBC News asked its readers the question the studio would rather it hadn’t: who will play his leading lady? It was found clipped to the back of last week’s onboarding packet and the writers’-room memo on villains, and it appears to be the third and final document in the set. One reader under the BBC’s post observed that Bond has been modernised but the marketing department is still auditioning women as accessories. The memo below suggests the casting department read that comment, agreed with it, and then went further than anyone asked.

AMAZON MGM STUDIOS: CASTING DEPARTMENT Bond 26: Principal Female Role Shortlist and Recommendation Classification: For Your Eyes Only (Compliance has asked us to add “and Commerce’s”)

Section 1: The Brief

The franchise retired the phrase “Bond girl” some years ago, at roughly the same pace it retired the ashtray and the jet pack. Legal has since suggested “Bond woman,” HR has suggested “Principal Female Role,” and Marketing, which has not read any of the memos, put “Bond girl” back on the BBC’s graphic before lunch.

Whatever we call her, the director, Denis Villeneuve, and the screenwriter, Steven Knight, have given us a brief. The role requires:

  • a mysterious past that cannot be fully verified;

  • fluency in at least one offshore jurisdiction;

  • a working knowledge of advanced technology the West would prefer the other side not have;

  • the ability to walk into any room on three continents and leave with the most powerful man in it on her phone; and

  • the ability, when the credits roll, to be gone.

The department notes that in sixty-four years only a handful of women have met even two of these. Most were written out after one film. One was painted gold. We intend to do better.

Section 2: The Shortlist, as Supplied by the Press

The newspapers have kindly done the first round for us, free of charge. In fairness to every name below, all are talented, all are in demand, and none of them asked to be in this memo.

Margaret Qualley. The bookmakers’ early favourite. The department has been told that in Britain the bookmakers cast the Bond films and the studio merely confirms their choices, and on the evidence of the past seven years we see no reason to argue.

Zendaya. She has already worked with both Mr. Pattinson and Mr. Villeneuve. In Hollywood this is called chemistry. In Washington it would be called a conflict of interest, and somebody would be briefed about it twice.

Florence Pugh. She has already played a Russian-trained assassin for Marvel. Legal wants to know whether the training transfers, or whether Disney keeps it.

Sydney Sweeney. Her name is reportedly on almost everyone’s lips. Wardrobe has been asked not to raise the subject of jeans.

Ana de Armas. She stole about ten minutes of No Time to Die in 2021 and was never invited back. The franchise calls this tradition. Everyone else calls it a mistake.

Hunter Schafer, Saoirse Ronan, Anya Taylor-Joy and Maya Jama. All appear in the BBC’s graphic, reproduced above, in evening wear, in a straight line, waiting. The department regards that image as Exhibit A for the reader comment in the editor’s note and has pinned it to the wall as a warning.

All of these candidates would be excellent. All of them, however, would be acting. Which brings us to the submission that arrived on Friday, unsolicited, in a plain envelope, with no return address and a Singapore postmark that has since been cancelled.

Section 3: The Unsolicited Submission

Name: Huang Le, known professionally as Alice Huang. Casting wishes to stress, as The New York Times stressed before us, that she is not related to Jensen Huang. The department has had enough trouble with confused surnames since the Dan Sullivan business in Alaska.

Prior screen experience: Her online résumé says she spent four years as a television reporter. Nobody has been able to verify this, which makes her the only applicant in the history of the franchise whose acting credits are themselves classified.

Training: Private banking at HSBC, then CITIC Securities, the state-backed Chinese investment bank. Bond’s leading ladies have traditionally been marine biologists, nuclear physicists or, on one regrettable occasion, a woman called Christmas. None of them could have read a balance sheet.

First known location: A “sports and leisure club” company registered in 2010 inside Beihu No. 9, a private golf club in Beijing popular with officials, where the buffet reportedly ran to foie gras, hairy crabs and Häagen-Dazs. Villain Development points out that Goldfinger merely cheated at golf. This candidate incorporated at one.

Stunt work: By late 2023 the company she ran, Megaspeed, belonged on paper to a Singapore shell whose listed director nobody could locate. Its Malaysian buyer’s registered address turned out to be a dilapidated storefront outside Kuala Lumpur with a construction company’s sign out front. Its real office sat on an upper floor of a shopping mall in Johor, between the snack shops and the beauty parlours, behind reflective glass and fingerprint scanners. Production Design has asked whether it can simply rent the set. It is better than anything we have built since Ken Adam left.

The audition: In June 2024, after a trade show in Taipei, she stood in a bar overlooking the city just after midnight and told a crowd of executives, “I bet you guys I can get Jensen here.” According to one of them, the chief executive of the most valuable chip company on Earth then walked in and did a whiskey shot with the group. Most candidates read their lines opposite a casting assistant. This one summoned the leading man.

Box office: Megaspeed went on to buy roughly $4.8 billion worth of Nvidia chips. By the department’s rough count, that is more than the combined production budgets of every official Bond film ever made, and she managed it without a single product-placement deal. Heineken has asked for her agent’s number.

Exit: Singapore questioned her, released her for insufficient evidence on the customs matter, issued her a stern warning and barred her from re-entering. She is the first leading lady in franchise history to be written out of a country rather than a sequel.

Legal insert, which Legal insists be printed in full: United States officials believe Ms. Huang was spying for China. They have not made their evidence public. China calls the allegation a smear. Nothing in this memo should be read as a finding of fact by Amazon MGM Studios, which has trouble enough finding a release date. Wardrobe has been instructed to sew the word “allegedly” into every costume.

Section 4: Why She Tops the List

For six decades, as our reader observed, this franchise has auditioned women as accessories. The department went through Ms. Huang’s file three times looking for the accessory and eventually found it. It was Jensen Huang.

Consider the evidence. He was summoned to a bar on a bet. He was photographed beside her at dinner in Singapore in December 2023, just as her company’s restructuring was completed. Four days after the ownership changed, Nvidia named Megaspeed a cloud partner. He turned up in her social media at a golf course and over wine, where, according to an executive who saw the photographs, a picture with Jensen was something people showed off. He wore the jacket in every frame. He did not, at any point, appear to be the one running the operation.

Nor was he the only man in the story who ended up as set dressing. Nvidia’s compliance team examined Megaspeed and found that, on paper, it had no Chinese shareholders, which was true, because the rules tested only the paper. The Commerce Department investigated the company for chip smuggling, and it is still unclear whether its investigators knew the rest of Washington suspected her of spying. The President of the United States was briefed about the matter and then briefed again, and the administration went on to let Nvidia sell chips to China in exchange for a 25 per cent cut.

Bond’s leading ladies have traditionally needed rescuing. On the evidence of this file, it is the United States government that needs rescuing.

Section 5: Obstacles

The department is not naive. Several difficulties remain.

Availability. The candidate is barred from Singapore, is unlikely to accept a chemistry read in Washington, and has not answered our emails. Casting points out that she has never answered anyone’s emails, which is precisely the quality we are looking for.

Non-disclosure agreement. Business Affairs has asked whether she will sign one. Business Affairs has been reminded that she has never disclosed anything in her life and is unlikely to start for us.

The leading man. Mr. Pattinson has expressed concern about sharing the screen with someone who has already been the subject of more real counter-intelligence briefings than he has had script meetings. We have reassured him that he survived the Twilight fandom, and nothing in counter-intelligence compares.

Mr. Huang. We understand he has since returned to Nvidia as chief executive, after a disappearance so brief that our Villain Development colleagues have filed it under stage magic rather than espionage. He is not being considered for any role in this production. Villain Development wishes it to be noted that he did not ask.

Section 6: Recommendation

The department recommends that the role be offered to Ms. Huang, or failing her, to whichever actress can most convincingly play someone who never needed the part.

Character name: Agent H20, after the chip Nvidia designed to sell to China within the American rules. Legal prefers “Ms. Blackwell,” after Nvidia’s newer chip, on the grounds that it already sounds like a Bond woman and nobody has yet been briefed about it.

Working title: For Your Chips Only. The runners-up were Chips Are Forever, The Spy Who Bet Me She Could Get Jensen, and Tomorrow Never Discloses.

Theme song: Still open. Music has been told that “Nobody Does It Better” is taken and that, on current evidence, it would be a little on the nose.

Attached: A yellow sticky note in Q’s handwriting, found stuck to the front of the file:

She won’t need gadgets. She already has a cloud partnership. — Q

Satire. The Alice Huang and Megaspeed details are drawn from The New York Times’s reporting; the casting memo, its departments and Q are invented. U.S. officials have not made public their evidence that Ms. Huang spied for China, and China denies it.
