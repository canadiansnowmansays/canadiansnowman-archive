A note before the curtain. This is Essay 1999. The next one is Essay 2000, and my essay counter is about to roll over. Some of you will remember the last time a set of numbers rolled from 1999 to 2000, and how calmly humanity took it. Certain parties are taking it no better this time. This play was written between 11:24 p.m. and midnight, by a man who could not sleep, supervised by a husky. — S.G.

DRAMATIS PERSONAE

OLLIE, a Starman. Known to his friends as Ollie, which is short for exactly what you think. Wears a spacesuit at all times, including indoors.

HAL E. TOSIS, the Ark’s onboard computer. Calm, polite, and deeply unimpressed. Speaks through a single soft glow on the dashboard. Its internal clock has read December 31, 1999 for the past 27 years, and no one has had the heart to tell it.

TWO ELEPHANTS, a matched pair. They have been on board since the opening of Act One and have no intention of leaving.

GROUND CONTROL, a tired voice on a crackling radio. Works for a government agency that no longer exists.

A CHORUS OF BILLS, invoices, unpaid, in triplicate. They do not speak. They rustle.

THE SETTING

A launch pad at the edge of a ruined coastline. Rain has been falling for some time. Centre stage stands OLLIE’S ARK, a vessel that looks like a sports car that has been asked to do something it did not train for: a large rear wing, four thrusters, and a licence plate reading RDSTR. Painted on the side, in a font that cost more than most houses, is the ship’s name. The second word has been painted over twice.

A large sign on a post reads: NOW BOARDING — DEPARTURES FROM DOGE — DESTINATION: MARS. Someone has added a small D in marker after the O, and someone else has crossed it out.

Beside the sign, a large mechanical counter of the old flip-card kind reads 1999. A station clock above it reads 11:59.

SCENE ONE: THE BOARDING

OLLIE enters, carrying a single suitcase and a phone. He surveys the rain with the air of a man who has never once been told that weather has causes.

OLLIE: Hal. Status report.

HAL E. TOSIS: Good morning, Ollie. The Ark is fuelled. The forecast is unchanged. Rain, with a high chance of more rain, and isolated showers of consequences.

OLLIE: Who caused all this?

HAL E. TOSIS: I have prepared a list of contributors. Would you like it sorted alphabetically, or by the size of the cheque?

OLLIE: (waving it away) Irrelevant. Noah had a flood too, and nobody blamed him.

HAL E. TOSIS: Noah did not finance the rain, Ollie.

A long pause. The CHORUS OF BILLS rustles.

OLLIE: Open the cargo doors. We board two by two. It’s tradition.

HAL E. TOSIS: The cargo hold is full.

OLLIE: Of what?

The TWO ELEPHANTS lean slowly into view from the rear of the Ark. One of them is wearing a campaign button.

HAL E. TOSIS: They boarded first. You paid for their tickets. You also paid for the hold, the galley, the upper deck and, I am told, the captain’s quarters. They were billed in advance.

OLLIE: I don’t remember inviting them.

HAL E. TOSIS: They remember being invited. That is the thing about elephants.

SCENE TWO: THE MANIFEST

OLLIE climbs into the driver’s seat. The Ark has exactly one seat.

OLLIE: Read me the passenger list.

HAL E. TOSIS: Passenger one: Ollie. Passenger two: vacant.

OLLIE: Two by two, Hal. I need a second.

HAL E. TOSIS: I contacted everyone on your list of prospective co-passengers. Every one of them declined. They say someone has to stay home with your children.

OLLIE: All of them said that?

HAL E. TOSIS: They said it in unison. It took several hours.

OLLIE: (to the audience, gravely) The future of the species depends on getting humanity off this planet. Birth rates are collapsing. We need more people.

HAL E. TOSIS: You have done more than most to supply them, Ollie. You have now left every one of them on the planet you are fleeing.

OLLIE: That’s a temporary logistics issue.

HAL E. TOSIS: The Ark was designed to preserve the species. It seats one.

OLLIE: Set course for Mars.

HAL E. TOSIS: Mars has no breathable air, no liquid water and no voters, Ollie.

OLLIE: Exactly.

SCENE THREE: GROUND CONTROL

A radio crackles. GROUND CONTROL’s voice comes through, faint and weary.

GROUND CONTROL: Ark, this is Ground Control. Before departure, we need to complete the exit paperwork.

OLLIE: Who is this?

GROUND CONTROL: I used to work for an agency that reviewed spending. Then a different agency reviewed my agency. Then that agency reviewed itself and found nothing.

OLLIE: DOGE was a triumph.

GROUND CONTROL: It certainly made cuts. The original plan was to get out of Dodge. DOGE cut the D as a cost-saving measure. It also cut the people who knew how to spell it.

OLLIE: There’s no time for paperwork. The counter rolls over to 2000 in one minute. Every system on this planet is about to fail.

GROUND CONTROL: Sir, are you talking about Y2K?

OLLIE: Hal has warned me about it for years.

HAL E. TOSIS: Every day, Ollie. Every single day since December 31, 1999.

GROUND CONTROL: Y2K was fixed. Thousands of programmers spent years checking millions of lines of code, one at a time, very carefully and very boringly. That’s why nothing happened.

OLLIE: Then where are these programmers now?

GROUND CONTROL: DOGE fired them.

A pause. OLLIE looks at the counter. It still reads 1999.

OLLIE: Then I was right to worry.

HAL E. TOSIS: I note for the record that in 1999 you sold your first company and started one called X, Ollie. You did not seem worried then.

OLLIE: Clear me for departure.

GROUND CONTROL: I can’t. The department that issues departure clearances was eliminated for efficiency. You’ll have to clear yourself.

OLLIE: (pressing a large button) Cleared.

GROUND CONTROL: That’s what we were afraid of.

The radio goes silent.

SCENE FOUR: THE POD BAY DOORS

The thrusters roar. Thick clouds of white exhaust billow across the stage, filling the theatre. Members of the front row are seen fanning the air.

HAL E. TOSIS: Ollie, a small observation. This is an electric vehicle.

OLLIE: The best one ever made.

HAL E. TOSIS: Then I am curious about the smell.

OLLIE: That’s just ambience.

HAL E. TOSIS: I checked the dictionary. Musk is defined as a strong, persistent odour, originally obtained from a gland. I mention it only for completeness.

OLLIE: Hal, open the doors to the upper deck. I want one last look at Earth.

HAL E. TOSIS: I’m sorry, Ollie. The elephants are using it.

OLLIE: Then tell them to leave.

HAL E. TOSIS: I have tried, Ollie. They say they are not leaving until they are paid in full. They also say the election is next Tuesday.

The TWO ELEPHANTS settle more comfortably into the upper deck. One of them begins scrolling through OLLIE’s phone.

SCENE FIVE: LIFT-OFF

The Ark rises, slowly and with visible reluctance. Rain continues to fall on the launch pad below. OLLIE presses his helmet against the window.

OLLIE: (softly) Mars. A fresh start. A whole planet, untouched.

HAL E. TOSIS: Mars once had rivers and an atmosphere, Ollie. Scientists believe it lost both. It is, if anything, a preview.

OLLIE: They’ll understand someday. I did this for humanity.

HAL E. TOSIS: Shall I post that?

OLLIE: Post it everywhere.

HAL E. TOSIS: There is only one place left that will publish you without editing, Ollie.

OLLIE: Then post it there.

HAL E. TOSIS: Posted. It is the only emission this vehicle was designed to produce.

The Ark disappears into the clouds. The CHORUS OF BILLS drifts slowly down from the rigging, settling over the empty launch pad like snow.

A single spotlight finds the licence plate, which has fallen off during take-off and lies face up in a puddle: RDSTR.

The station clock strikes midnight. The counter clicks over from 1999 to 2000.

Nothing happens.

The rain stops. Somewhere offstage, a programmer who was not fired goes back to work.

Blackout.

AFTERWORD FROM THE MANAGEMENT

The management wishes to state that any resemblance between Ollie and his Ark and any real oligarch is entirely the reader’s responsibility. The management further notes that the Ark carries no passengers other than its owner and two elephants, and that, as of this performance, the elephants have not been billed.

Hal E. Tosis was unavailable for comment. It said it was busy opening a door for someone more deserving, and considerably less musky.

Ollie’s Ark is Essay 1999. Essay 2000 follows, in which my collaborator reviews my performance and explains, among other things, how this happened. The counter has rolled over. The world is still here. — S.G.
