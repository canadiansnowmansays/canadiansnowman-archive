MEMORANDUM

FROM: Office of Retroactive Justification RE: Threat characterization for Tariff Schedule 338-A, -B, and -C CLASSIFICATION: Sensitive / Do Not Ask Follow-Up Questions

1. PURPOSE

This office has been asked to prepare the threat assessment supporting the 554 tariff lines enacted on 22 August. We note, for the record, that the list was finalised before this assessment was commissioned. We have proceeded regardless. It is not our function to ask which end of the process we are standing at.

2. METHODOLOGY

Our analysts were given the list and instructed to identify what each item threatens. Where no threat could be identified, analysts were instructed to look harder. Where looking harder produced nothing, analysts were instructed to consider that the absence of an identifiable threat may itself constitute a form of threat, being precisely what a sufficiently sophisticated adversary would arrange.

Three analysts resigned. Their positions have been backfilled with people who did not read the list first.

3. FINDINGS BY CATEGORY

3.1 — Wigs (partial), false beards, eyebrows and the like, of synthetic textile materials.

The most alarming entry on the schedule, and the one this office recommends leading with in any public communication. A partial wig is, by definition, a device for appearing to be someone one is not. That the northern government has been permitted to ship these across the border unimpeded for one hundred and fifty years represents, in the assessment of this office, a lapse of extraordinary duration.

We are unable to determine how many false eyebrows have entered the country. We are unable to determine where they are now. We consider this the appropriate note on which to request additional funding.

3.2 — Dog leashes, collars, muzzles and harnesses.

Restraint technology. We note the northern government’s simultaneous public position that it will not be restrained, and observe that a nation exporting this volume of restraint equipment while refusing restraint itself is either signalling something or is not. Both readings support the tariff.

3.3 — Base metal statuettes.

Analysts spent four days on this line. The finding is that a base metal statuette is a small figure of a person, made of metal that is not precious. No further characterization was possible. One analyst has proposed that the statuettes may be intended as decoys, though he was unable to say for what, and has since been reassigned to the plywood file.

3.4 — Plywood (36 varieties).

The office wishes to flag a complication. American homebuilders have advised that there is not enough domestic plywood to replace the tariffed Canadian supply, and that construction costs will rise accordingly.

Our analysis is that this concern falls outside the scope of a threat assessment and should be forwarded to whichever office is responsible for consequences. We have been unable to locate that office. We have been told it may not have been stood up yet.

3.5 — National flags.

Canadian-manufactured national flags are now subject to a fifty percent duty. This office confirms that the schedule does not specify which nation’s flags. American flags sewn in Canada are therefore taxed at fifty percent on entry to the United States.

We have flagged this as a possible drafting oversight. We were advised that the schedule is final and that our role is characterization, not correction. Accordingly: the threat posed by American flags is that they are arriving from the wrong direction.

3.6 — Studio television cameras. Smartphones.

Neither item is manufactured in Canada. This office initially recorded this as an error.

On reflection, we note that a tariff on goods that do not exist cannot be evaded, cannot be absorbed, and cannot be retaliated against. It is, in the strict sense, the only perfectly enforced measure on the entire schedule. We recommend expansion of this approach.

3.7 — Mixtures of or with a basis of odoriferous substances with 20 to 50 percent alcohol by weight requiring only the addition of ethyl alcohol or water to be a beverage.

This office is not certain what this is. This office is not certain anyone is. This office notes that the phrase “requiring only the addition of ethyl alcohol” describes a substance already half alcohol, and that the person adding more is not a subject of trade policy but of concern.

Assessed threat: low. Assessed comprehensibility: lower.

3.8 — Wood furniture of a kind used in the bedroom and not designed for motor vehicle use.

The office has no finding. The office would, however, appreciate knowing what prompted the clarification.

3.9 — Articles for Christmas festivities, ornaments, not of glass or wood.

Glass and wooden ornaments remain untariffed. Every other ornament is now a fifty percent proposition.

Analysts were unable to construct a threat model in which the material composition of a tree decoration bears on national security. Analysts were reminded that the assessment is due Thursday.

4. SUMMARY OF THREAT

Taken together, the schedule identifies a northern adversary capable of deploying: disguises, restraints, small metal people, structurally necessary building materials, our own flag, objects that do not exist, a liquid nobody can describe, bedroom furniture of unusual specificity, and Christmas.

This office assesses the combined threat level as whatever is required.

5. LEGAL BASIS

The measures rest on Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, a provision which has never previously been used, has no implementing regulations, no court precedent, and no established process.

This office regards the absence of precedent as an advantage. Precedent is, after all, principally a record of the times a thing was tested. There is no record here. There is nothing to compare this to. There is nothing to be found wanting against.

We recommend describing this as flexibility.

6. RECOMMENDATION

That the list be published, that the assessment be published shortly afterward, and that the order in which they were produced not appear in either document.

The Office of Retroactive Justification has no permanent staff, no budget line, and no address. It is stood up as required and dissolved on completion. Its findings have never once contradicted the decision that occasioned them, a record it regards as evidence of methodology.
