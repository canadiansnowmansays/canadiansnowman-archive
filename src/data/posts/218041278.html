AD NAUSEAM™

(trumpaganda hydrochloride) — 30- and 60-second broadcast spots

Available without prescription on selected television channels, whether or not you asked for it.

PAID FOR BY THE U.S. GOVERNMENT

Read this leaflet carefully before watching. Then watch it again. And again. That is, in a sense, the point.

1. WHAT IS AD NAUSEAM?

Ad Nauseam is a public service announcement. The White House has been very clear about this. Its stated purpose is to remind Americans to love their country and to understand what makes it worth defending — at home, at the border, and abroad.

The active ingredient is the President of the United States.

Inactive ingredients include: a black-and-white hallway; a long, meaningful stare into the lens; archival footage of a speech at Mount Rushmore; the words AMERICA WILL NEVER BE A COMMUNIST COUNTRY, superimposed in capitals for most of the running time; a selection of policy boasts including “Largest tax cuts in history” (see Section 6, Side Effects); and a song by the musician JMSN titled “Love Me,” whose licensing status is best described as aspirational.

Ad Nauseam does not contain the country, except as a backdrop.

2. A NOTE ON THE NAME

Ad nauseam is Latin for “to the point of sickness.” It describes something repeated so often that the listener becomes physically unwell.

Some patients believe the phrase means “forever and ever.” It does not. “Forever and ever” is a separate formulation, Ad Infinitum, which remains under active development by the manufacturer but is not approved for use beyond January 20, 2029, under the Twenty-Second Amendment. Patients who see Ad Infinitum advertised should report it immediately to someone. (See Section 9 for why “someone” is doing a great deal of work in that sentence.)

3. HOW SUPPLIED

At least $443,000 has been spent airing a single spot, according to the Associated Press. Additional spots are in rotation. The full dosage has not been disclosed.

Patients may wonder why the manufacturer did not fund the product from its own reserves. As Senator Thom Tillis of North Carolina has observed, the President’s political operation held roughly $416 million as of August 31. The manufacturer’s position is that this money is for politics, whereas Ad Nauseam is not politics. The manufacturer’s second position is that you are paying for it.

4. WHO SHOULD WATCH AD NAUSEAM?

Everyone.

Important clinical note: A White House official has confirmed to NBC News that the ads are not political because the President is not on the ballot. The President has separately instructed his supporters, on Truth Social on the morning of September 28, 2026, to pretend that he is.

Patients are advised to hold both statements to be true simultaneously. Patients unable to do so may be experiencing an early symptom (see Section 6).

5. DOSAGE AND ADMINISTRATION

Watch until symptoms appear. Then continue.

Call to action: None.

Ad Nauseam contains no call to action. This absence is the legally active component of the product. Under federal law, a message without an explicit instruction to vote is not campaign advocacy, no matter how closely it resembles a late-cycle campaign spot. Viewers who, after the fourteenth exposure, feel a spontaneous urge to support Republican candidates on November 3 should understand that this urge originated entirely within themselves.

6. POSSIBLE SIDE EFFECTS

Very common (more than 1 in 10 viewers):

  • Déjà vu. Patients may feel they have seen Ad Nauseam before. They have. The flagship spot is a revised version of an advertisement produced by the President’s campaign in the closing weeks of the 2024 race, now reissued in a publicly funded edition.

  • Involuntary eye-rolling at the words “Largest tax cuts in history,” which The Atlantic’s David A. Graham has called flatly false, and at “reigniting American manufacturing,” which is a matter of opinion held principally by the active ingredient.

Common (1 in 100 viewers):

  • A persistent sensation that the President is the state. This is not a hallucination. It is a policy.

  • Hearing the President promise, in the recycled 2024 footage, to expel the warmongers from government, and then glancing at the evening news. The manufacturer regrets not trimming this line and has asked editing to be more careful with future prophecies.

Uncommon:

  • Flashbacks to Budapest. Senator Tillis reported that the use of taxpayer money in this manner reminded him of Viktor Orbán, whom Hungarian voters removed from office earlier this year. Patients experiencing Hungarian flashbacks are advised that the Hungarian cure, while effective, is only administered at the ballot box.

Rare but serious:

  • Senatorial confusion. Senator John Kennedy of Louisiana, a Republican, was observed on CBS’s Face the Nation on September 27 stating that there “oughta be a rule” against spending public money to promote oneself — before correcting himself mid-sentence to note that there probably is one. There is. See Section 9. It did not help.

7. CONTRAINDICATIONS

Do not watch Ad Nauseam if you are allergic to:

  • Sun Kings

  • Gold leaf

  • The word “Trump” appearing on things that did not previously have it

Also from the makers of Ad Nauseam: the Trump Gold Card, a 47-day ICE course numbered in honour of the President, his portrait in your passport, and Trump TV, a White House streaming app — which may be the only product on this list you can switch off.

8. COMPARABLE PRODUCTS

The manufacturer points to precedent: an anti-drug announcement aired under George W. Bush, and a vaccination campaign under Joe Biden. Clinical reviewers note two minor formulation differences. Neither prior product starred the sitting President. And neither prior product featured him staring down a corridor, in black and white, to inform the viewer that this is the final battle with you at his side.

For reference, a genuine civic public service announcement has three standard properties: it does not star the sitting President, it does not recite the governing party’s campaign slogans, and it does not begin airing five weeks before a national election. Ad Nauseam is formulated without any of the three.

The anti-drug announcement asked Americans not to take drugs. Ad Nauseam asks nothing. It simply is, the way weather is.

10. CLINICAL TRIAL RESULTS

In extended real-world testing across the American population, Ad Nauseam has been shown to reliably induce nausea.

It has not been shown to induce love.

Researchers note, as the economist Paul Krugman and others have pointed out, that the President’s personal unpopularity is already weighing on Republican candidates. Increasing the dosage of the active ingredient in the weeks before a midterm election is therefore, in the considered clinical view of this leaflet, a bold protocol.

Patients may broadcast a song called “Love Me” as many times as the budget allows. Love, unlike airtime, cannot be requisitioned.
