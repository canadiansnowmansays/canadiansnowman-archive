A brief note on sourcing: on the night of September 8, 2026, President Trump signed five proclamations banning the import of Canadian beer, wine, cider, whisky, vodka, and other spirits, effective September 29 — replacing, not adding to, the existing 50 percent tariff on those goods. The signing came the same day Canada’s own retaliatory tariffs took effect, and the same week a Dallas convention absorbed most of the news cycle. The liquidation flyer below is not real. The countdown is.

ATTENTION VALUED CUSTOMERS

Due to a Presidential Proclamation and not, as some signage in this store previously suggested, a change in ownership, the Canadian Aisle will be closing permanently in 20 days. All remaining inventory is priced to move. Store credit will not be issued. Please, for your own sake, do not ask the cashier why.

CLEARANCE — WHILE SUPPLIES LAST

A NOTE FROM MANAGEMENT ON HOW WE GOT HERE

Long-time customers may recall that in February and March of 2025, our friends north of the border did this exact thing to us first — Ontario’s provincial retailer pulled every American bottle off its own shelves and stopped restocking, officially as tariff retaliation. British Columbia followed within the week, its premier adding that many British Columbians simply no longer wanted to see American products on the shelf at all. Management would like it on record that we found this rude at the time. Management would also like it on record that we have now done the identical thing back, three weeks from taking effect, and are describing it as retaliation for discrimination rather than as the copying it resembles. This is not a contradiction. This is retail.

TESTIMONIAL FROM A CONCERNED STAKEHOLDER

“We do not wish to see Canadian distillers face similar hardship.” — The Distilled Spirits Council of the United States (DISCUS), speaking this week about a ban issued on its own government’s letterhead, on its own industry’s behalf, that it did not ask for and does not want, after its own exports to Canada already fell more than 70 percent the last time this happened.

Store response: Noted. Shelf remains scheduled for removal regardless.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Is this the same as a tariff? No. A tariff is a price. This is an absence.

Can I stock up before the 29th? Yes. This flyer takes no position on how many bottles constitute “enough,” and neither, evidently, did the Proclamation.

Will Canadian motorcycles be affected too? Larger-capacity motorcycles and mopeds are banned outright alongside the alcohol, effective the same September 29 date — we just don’t carry those here. Animal hides are a separate matter, added to the existing tariff list effective September 15, taxed rather than banned. The store map has been updated twice this week and may be updated again before you finish reading this sentence.

Why didn’t I hear about any of this until the shelf was already half-empty? The signing took place Tuesday night, alongside four other proclamations, during a week largely occupied by a two-day convention in Dallas. Management cannot explain national news priorities. Management can only confirm the shelf.

Hasn’t this happened before? Once, yes — in the other direction. During Prohibition, Hiram Walker’s Canadian Club later marketed itself as the best-selling smuggled whisky in the United States, while Samuel Bronfman built a wholesale supply network to American bootleggers years before his name was legally sold south of the border. By 1935, Washington was pursuing Canadian distillers for roughly $60 million in unpaid excise tax on spirits that had crossed regardless. Management declines to speculate on whether the loading dock remembers the route.

CLOSING NOTICE

This liquidation is final. There will be no reopening under a different name, no rebrand, no “Canadian-Inspired” store-brand substitute quietly filling the gap by October. When liquids are disappearing, they do not come back disguised as something else.

Twenty days remain. Act accordingly.
