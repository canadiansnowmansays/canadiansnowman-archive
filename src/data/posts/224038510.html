Last Tuesday morning I posted a fight programme with an empty chair at centre canvas, a striped shirt over the back and a ballot on the seat. By Tuesday night the chair was empty no longer. The referee, as the rules of that cage have always said, turned out to be the voter, and the voter had plenty to say.

So this week’s round-up is the one the last several months were building toward: the morning after, and the week after that. Politics leads, as it has to, followed by war, the courts, history, technology, and a satire shelf so crowded it needed a second bookcase.

If you’ve just found your way here, welcome. I write from Toronto, close enough to the American election to feel the floor shake and far enough to keep my balance. The satire carries a label. Everything else carries a source.

Several series that spent the autumn asking questions got their answers on 3 November. Let’s Talk Texas closes with Ken Paxton’s “Come and Take It” tour, and Texas voters taking him at his word. Super PACman ends where the money did: the machine kept it and lost the game. Loyal Opposition files its epilogue, the audit returned by the voters themselves, though not for all the reasons the audit expected. And The American Contradiction, after 250 years of exclusion and inclusion, gets a new chapter it could not have written in advance: the country Trump spent two years trying to cure of being woke elected its first Muslim senator, its first openly gay male senator and its first Black woman governor. America woke up.

The week after an election is when the binders come out. The Pendulum and the Ratchet opened the White House’s “Wednesday binder”, a plan begun in April to keep the ratchet turning whatever Congress the voters sent, and then counted the ten reversals that were stopped outright. When Democracy Fights Back watched the prosecutors at the polling-place door and asked what their notes become now. And in Tehran and Washington, The Iran Crisis follows a letter and a target list drafted for the same week.

Two series promised for after the vote have begun. The Rise and Fall of Trumpism opened with its introduction, The Man and the Movement, and returns weekly. The Conditional Women started with The Exits: four women gone from the cabinet in eleven weeks, each replaced by a man. The rest of the slate, FIFA Fraud, The Third Rail and The Endless Frontier, will follow in its own time.

The satire kept pace with the news, and then some: ten Sunday Satires, two from The Enforcers, and a liturgy from The Hypocrisy of Donald Trump for Stephen Miller’s installation as Chief of Staff. Fight Night opened the week. By Wednesday the Office of Retroactive Justification was filing an after-action report on why voters followed the president’s instructions so precisely, and before the week was out the Don of the Family had been booked against the Don of the parish. Some weeks the forms fill themselves in.

A reminder on Your Phone Bill and Mine: the CRTC hearing is November 30 in Gatineau, three weeks from tomorrow, and I’ll provide a summary in December.

That’s the lead. The full week follows.
