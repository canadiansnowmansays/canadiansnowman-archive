The Republican Party is committed to a diverse, inclusive coalition of Americans — provided they meet certain eligibility criteria. This orientation package has been prepared by the Office of Approved Identities, a division of the Republican National Committee, and distributed on the occasion of Halloween 2026, when Americans across this great nation opened their doors to scary figures demanding things. Please read all sections carefully before submitting your candidacy application. Candy not included.

SECTION ONE: WELCOME!

Congratulations on your interest in becoming a Republican candidate.

You have chosen to join the most dynamic, forward-looking, and diverse political movement in American history — a movement that has welcomed businesspeople, veterans, doctors, immigrants, and reality television personalities into its ranks without regard for background, creed, or the number of times they have declared bankruptcy.

We are the Party of Lincoln. We are the Party of Reagan. We are, increasingly, the Party of People Named Vivek, which tells you something about how far we have come.

The Republican Party does not see colour. The Republican Party does not see religion. The Republican Party sees potential — the potential to win seats, move poll numbers, and attack the opposing candidate’s family in a thirty-second spot that costs four million dollars and causes even Nikki Haley to call you “a bad guy” on social media.

That is the beauty of the American dream. That is the beauty of this Party.

Please proceed to Section Two.

SECTION TWO: THE DIVERSITY DIVIDEND

You may have noticed that the Republican Party has, in recent election cycles, welcomed candidates from a wider range of ethnic and cultural backgrounds than at any previous point in its history.

This is not an accident. This is strategy.

Our research indicates that a diverse candidate pool provides the following operational advantages:

  • Inoculation against accusations of bigotry (”We can’t be racist — our candidate’s name has fourteen letters and none of them are traditional”)

  • Access to donor communities previously unreachable by candidates named Todd

  • The ability to attack the other party’s diverse candidates from a position of plausible deniability

  • A ready supply of compelling personal narratives involving immigrant parents, the American dream, and bootstraps

The Party is proud of its record in this regard. We have fielded candidates of South Asian, Cuban, Puerto Rican, and Nigerian heritage. We are, as the Vice President has noted, a “big tent.”

What is inside the tent is covered in Section Three. Please do not skip ahead.

SECTION TWO-B: A SEASONAL NOTE (HALLOWEEN EDITION)

You may have noticed that this orientation package arrives the morning after Halloween — the one night of the year when Americans open their doors to strange figures in frightening costumes who demand things from them and then disappear into the dark.

We at the Republican Party consider this excellent preparation for the midterm elections.

The parallels are instructive. On Halloween, a small child dressed as a vampire appears on your doorstep. You give him candy. He does not say thank you. He moves on to the next house. You are not sure, afterward, exactly what just happened or why you feel vaguely unsettled.

This is called voter outreach.

On Halloween, someone always goes too far. A haunted house that traumatises rather than entertains. A neighbour who hands out toothbrushes. A campaign advertisement featuring a candidate’s estranged mother, aired at a cost of four million dollars, causing members of the candidate’s own party to call it “revolting” and “very, very gross” while the Vice President announces he is “disgusted” — by the people who said it was revolting.

Every neighbourhood has one.

The key difference between Halloween and an election, of course, is that on Halloween the monsters leave at nine o’clock. In an election, some of them end up in the Senate.

Which brings us to Section Three.

SECTION THREE: WHAT IS INSIDE THE TENT

The tent is large. The tent is welcoming. The tent has rules.

These rules are not written down anywhere official, which is why we are writing them down here, in an orientation package that we trust you will treat as confidential.

Rule 1: You are welcome in this Party provided that you are willing to attack other people who look like you.

This is the foundational principle of Republican diversity. A candidate of South Asian heritage who is willing to air an advertisement featuring the estranged mother of his Democratic opponent — a mother who has not spoken to her daughter in years, who has aired this estrangement in a paid political advertisement, and who has done so in a race the candidate is currently losing — that candidate is exactly the kind of diverse Republican we are looking for.

We do not require you to win. We require you to commit.

Rule 2: The Party will defend you against your critics, and then criticize your critics more harshly than it criticized you.

When members of our own party call your advertisement “revolting,” “very, very gross,” and “a bad move,” you may feel briefly alone. You are not alone. The Vice President of the United States has publicly announced that he is “a little bit disgusted” — not by your advertisement, but by the Republicans who complained about it. He would not have run the ad himself, he notes. But he is more bothered by the people bothered by it.

This is called Party unity. Please familiarise yourself with its mechanics.

Rule 3: The tent is large, but it has a zip.

The zip is on the inside. It is controlled by the President of the United States.

The Party welcomes immigrants whose parents came to America from South Asia and who have made their fortune in biotechnology and are willing to spend four million dollars of it on an advertisement exploiting a candidate’s estranged family.

The Party does not welcome immigrants whose parents came to America from Egypt and who became a doctor and then a public health official and are now running for the Senate as a Democrat.

The difference, for those keeping score, is not the immigration. It is not the heritage. It is not even the name, though the name is apparently very relevant.

And also, apparently, the name.

SECTION FIVE: A PERSONAL NOTE FROM THE DIVERSITY OUTREACH DIVISION

We want to be clear: the Republican Party is not anti-immigrant. The First Lady is an immigrant. The Vice President’s wife is of South Asian descent. Several of our most prominent voices were born outside this country or are the children of people who were.

We are also not anti-Muslim. We have Muslim supporters. We are told they exist.

What we are is selective. A diverse candidate who attacks on command, who spends their own money, who does not flinch when Nikki Haley calls them a bad person on social media — that candidate belongs in this tent.

A diverse candidate who runs as a Democrat in a swing state, who leads in the polls, who responds to being called a terrorist by running an ad about his faith — that candidate belongs in Appendix J.

The tent is large.

The zip is controlled by the man in the gilded tower.

Welcome aboard. Leave your costume at the door — or don’t, because frankly at this point it’s getting difficult to tell the difference.
