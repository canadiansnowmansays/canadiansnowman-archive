THERAPIST: Thank you both for coming in. I understand this is a — how did you put it in the intake form — “trial separation that got a little long.”

DONALD: Seven years. But who’s counting.

KIM: I am counting. I have people who count for me.

DONALD: See, this is what I mean. He’s got a whole department. I’ve got Stephen Miller and a guy who used to do my Twitter. There’s an imbalance of resources here and I think it’s worth noting for the record.

THERAPIST: Let’s not start with grievances. Donald, you reached out first. Why now, after seven years?

DONALD: I was going through old photos. You do that sometimes, right? Late at night, you’re scrolling, and you land on 2019, and you think — we had something. Singapore. Hanoi. The DMZ. Nobody’s had a DMZ like that since.

KIM: He reposted the Singapore photo. On the weekend. No caption for two days, then “great guy, tremendous instincts, we’ll be talking soon.”

THERAPIST: And how did that land for you?

KIM: Honestly? I have testing to do. But I looked at my calendar.

DONALD: See, that’s — that’s a yes. In his culture that’s basically a ring.

THERAPIST: Let’s talk about what happened at the end. Hanoi, 2019. You’ve both described it differently in outside interviews.

DONALD: He wanted everything lifted and gave me nothing back. I walked. Best deal I ever didn’t make.

KIM: He walked out of a summit I built an entire train for. Do you understand what that train cost. I don’t take a plane. I took a train for four days so we could sit in a room for two hours and he stood up.

THERAPIST: Donald, did you know about the train?

DONALD: I heard about the train. Everyone talks about the train. I don’t love that the train gets more sympathy than I do in this room.

THERAPIST: It sounds like there’s some unresolved resentment around transportation and effort.

KIM: There is always unresolved resentment around effort with him. He wants the summit, the photo, the Nobel talk, and none of the part where you sit at a table for eleven hours discussing enrichment percentages.

DONALD: Because that part’s boring! I’m a big-picture guy. I did big picture with you. Nobody had done big picture with you before me. Obama wouldn’t even look at you.

KIM: This is true. I will give him this. Nobody had looked at me before him.

THERAPIST: I want to pause on that, because I think that’s actually the heart of it. Kim, you’re describing something that sounds like you felt seen.

KIM: I felt seen. And then I felt returned, like a gift he decided he did not want after opening it in front of everyone.

DONALD: That’s a little dramatic.

KIM: I built a summit train.

DONALD: Okay, the train, I get it, the train was a lot, I said that.

THERAPIST: Let’s talk about the years apart. Kim, what did you do during the separation?

KIM: I tested. Missiles, mostly. Some satellites. A submarine-launched device in 2021 that got very little coverage, which frankly hurt.

THERAPIST: Was that about him?

KIM: Everything is a little bit about him. You send something up, you wonder if he’s watching. He was president again by then, watching everything, commenting on everything. He commented on a hurricane’s “size and strength” for eleven minutes. I thought, surely, one test, one small paragraph.

DONALD: I did notice the tests. I want that on the record. I said “he knows I like him” to reporters. That was a message.

KIM: That is not a message. That is a compliment shaped like an insult shaped like a compliment. I do not have the analysts for that shape.

THERAPIST: Donald, what did you do during the separation?

DONALD: I had other things going on. Impeachments. An election. A different election. A verdict. Some legal stuff, you probably read about it, everybody did. I thought about him sometimes. Around the holidays mostly. He sends a nice letter.

KIM: I do send a nice letter.

DONALD: Beautiful letters. Tremendous letters. I told people that in 2018 and I’ll say it again now — his letters are like love letters. I’m not embarrassed to say it.

THERAPIST: Kim, hearing that — how does it feel?

KIM: It feels like being told your quarterly report was “kind of a page-turner.” I would like, once, for someone in this arrangement to acknowledge the amount of state apparatus behind a single letter.

THERAPIST: Let’s talk about now. Donald, you’ve said you expect to meet again this year. What’s different this time?

DONALD: I’ve grown. I’ve had a lot of therapy — not this kind, business therapy, deal therapy. I know what I want now. I want the meeting, I want the cameras, and this time I want to actually get something, because last time I feel like I gave him legitimacy and got a nice train story out of it.

KIM: You got a summit that let you claim, domestically, that you alone could do what no president had managed. You got quite a lot.

DONALD: I got a photo op and a headache from Bolton.

KIM: Bolton is gone now. I checked.

DONALD: Bolton is gone, that’s true, that’s a positive development for both of us honestly.

THERAPIST: There was a joke recently — I believe on American television — about the two of you potentially coordinating a couples costume for Halloween.

KIM: I heard about this.

DONALD: I heard the joke. I didn’t love it. I don’t think it’s disrespectful, exactly, but I don’t love being compared to a chip.

KIM: I would be guacamole. I looked into it. Guacamole is a serving suggestion, not a costume.

THERAPIST: Would either of you actually consider it? A joint appearance, symbolically, before the formal meeting?

DONALD: Depends what he wants to wear. I’m not doing a couples thing where I’m the punchline half. I did that with a running mate once, never again.

KIM: I would want something that photographs with dignity. Last time the belly-bump comparison was — not something my office appreciated.

DONALD: People love a belly bump joke. That’s just how it is now. You’ve got to have a thicker skin for this stage of the relationship.

THERAPIST: I want to ask a harder question. Denuclearization was the word that ended things last time. Is it still on the table?

KIM: It was never really on the table. It was on the itinerary. There’s a difference.

DONALD: See, this is the honesty I like. This is why I keep coming back. Everybody else in my life tells me what I want to hear. He tells me the itinerary was fake and somehow it still feels like a compliment.

KIM: It is not intended as a compliment.

DONALD: I take it as one. That’s on me, probably. My therapist — the deal one — says I do that.

THERAPIST: We’re almost out of time. I’d like each of you to say one thing you’d want the other to know going into this next meeting.

KIM: I would want him to know that I do not need the meeting the way he needs the meeting. I have testing to do either way. But I will come to the table, because a table with him photographs better than a table without him, and that is worth something to my program.

DONALD: I’d want him to know — and I mean this — nobody else does the summit like we do the summit. I’ve stood across from a lot of people at a lot of tables. Nobody else gets the walk-and-talk right like he does. That’s real. Whatever else, that part was real.

THERAPIST: That’s a good place to end. Same time in November?

DONALD: Depends on the costume.

KIM: I am not doing the chip.
