Effective: Tuesday, November 24, 2026, 11:00 a.m., Oval Office (closed press)

NOTICE OF CONSOLIDATION

For efficiency, the Office has merged the Medal of Freedom Nominations Desk with the Referrals, Indictments and Security-Detail Withdrawal Desk.

A review found that the two desks were processing the same people in opposite directions. The Medal Desk kept receiving files on people the Referrals Desk had just finished charging, and the Referrals Desk kept receiving files on people the Medal Desk had already decorated. At one point both desks were holding files on the same eighty-five-year-old immunologist, one to honour him and one to have him charged.

Under the new arrangement, a single form serves both purposes. One routing question, in Part B, sends each file to the right schedule. Staff report that the question takes about four seconds to answer and has never once needed a second opinion.

PART A — STATUTORY CRITERIA

Under Executive Order 11085 (1963), the Medal goes to people who have made “an especially meritorious contribution” to the security or national interests of the United States, to world peace, or to cultural or other significant public or private endeavours.

The Office has revised its working reading of this language as follows:

  • Especially meritorious: read as especially loyal

  • Security of the United States: read as security of the President

  • National interests: read as the President’s interests, which are the same thing (see Memorandum ORJ-0001, L’État, C’est Moi)

  • World peace: read as peace and quiet at Mar-a-Lago

  • Significant public endeavours: read as press conferences held in landscaping company car parks

The original 1963 text stays on file for historical interest, next to the Office’s copy of the Constitution.

PART B — THE ROUTING QUESTION

B-1. Has the nominee, at any point in their career, said no to the President?

☐ NO → Go to Schedule 1 (Medal, Pardon, or Both). ☐ YES → Go to Schedule 2 (Referral, Subpoena, Revocation of Security Detail, Removal of Portrait).

Guidance: Do not take into account whether the nominee was right. Rightness is not a field on this form. An earlier draft had a “Was the nominee right?” box, but it was found to send too many files to the wrong schedule and was taken out in testing.

Nominee: Rudolph W. Giuliani, 82 Honour conferred: Presidential Medal of Freedom Citation (as issued by the White House): “A lifetime of service to the American people.”

1(a). Career summary. As U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York in the 1980s, the nominee prosecuted the heads of the Five Families. The Office accepts this as a sincere public service, and also notes the irony: the man who once put the Commission on trial now belongs to the club the Commission would have recognised on sight.

1(b). 1(e). Ceremony arrangements. Closed to the press. The Office reminds staff that Schedule 1 ceremonies are always closed to the press, and Schedule 2 proceedings are always open to the press and, where possible, leaked to it beforehand.

ANNEX A — CONFLICTING HONOURS (FOR INFORMATION ONLY)

Under the previous administration, Ruby Freeman and Shaye Moss, the two Georgia election workers whom the Schedule 1 worked example was found liable for defaming, received the Presidential Citizens Medal.

The Office has examined whether one Medal of Freedom outranks two Citizens Medals. Its finding is that it does not matter, because the Office does not keep score on the people who were telling the truth. It only keeps score on the people who were told no.

ANNEX B — THE RECIPROCITY TEST

Staff in doubt about a file may apply the Reciprocity Test. Swap the nominee’s name with any name from the other schedule and read the file again.

Example: “Anthony Fauci, disbarred for repeated false statements, pardoned and awarded the Medal of Freedom for a lifetime of service.” / “Rudolph Giuliani, fifty years of public health service, referred for prosecution at 85.”

If the swapped file looks absurd, the original routing was correct. If the swapped file looks like the country you thought you lived in, please report to the Division of Re-Education on the fourth floor, where there is coffee.
