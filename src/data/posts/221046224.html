The Office of Retroactive Justification certifies where RSV came from, for the third time. The dates, the chimpanzees and the 1931 lung tissue are real; the certificate is not.

UNITED STATES DEPARTMENT OF HEALTH AND HUMAN SERVICES
Office of Retroactive Justification — Division of Pathogen Provenance

CERTIFICATE OF ORIGIN
(Amended. Supersedes HHS-RSV-2021-0001 and HHS-RSV-2023-0002. Does not supersede reality, which has been notified separately.)

Section 1: Description of Goods

Commodity: Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)

Common name: “The sniffles”

Declared use: Seasonal infection of approximately every human being before age two

Quantity: One (1) virus; billions of copies

Declared value: Biggest killer of kids in this country today (see Section 6, Valuation Dispute)

Harmonised Tariff Code: Unclassifiable; the Department of Commerce has been asked to invent one

Section 2: Declared Country of Origin

The Exporter declares that the goods described above originated in a laboratory.

The Office notes that the Exporter has made this declaration on several occasions, naming a different laboratory, mechanism or species each time. In keeping with Departmental policy that no declaration is ever withdrawn, only joined by another, all declarations are reproduced below and remain simultaneously in force.

Reconciliation note: Version 0003 states that RSV did not spread through vaccines. Version 0001 states that it did. The Office has reviewed both and confirms that each is correct. Readers who perceive a contradiction are reminded that the polio-vaccine theory was the entire reason for Version 0001, and that its quiet abandonment by the press office does not constitute a retraction, because nobody at the Department has read Version 0001 since it was published.

Section 3: Species Reconciliation Annex

Certain evidence submitted in support of Version 0001 refers to the Walter Reed chimpanzees as “monkeys.” Critics have noted that:

(a) chimpanzees are apes, not monkeys; (b) the kidney cells used to manufacture early polio vaccines came from monkeys, not chimpanzees; and (c) the theory therefore requires a chimpanzee virus to have travelled into a monkey, and from the monkey into a vaccine, with no account of how.

To resolve this, the Office hereby adopts the following definition for the purposes of this Certificate and all future Certificates:

Primate (n.): Any animal that is not a human being and that the Secretary would like to have been involved.

Under this definition, chimpanzees, rhesus macaques, gibbons, lemurs and Curious George are equivalent, and the objection is withdrawn.

The Office further notes that the author of the original evidence has stated that she merely posed a question, and that any reader who interpreted the question as an answer is misrepresenting her. The Secretary, who cited the question as an answer in his book, is excepted from this finding on grounds of seniority.

Section 4: Supporting Documentation Received

The following documents were received in support of the Exporter’s declarations:

  • One (1) book.

  • One (1) article from the Exporter’s former charity, written by a woman now employed as the Exporter’s senior adviser.

  • One (1) podcast.

  • Two (2) television appearances on the same day, which agree with each other in no particular.

The Office has examined these documents and finds them to be in order, in the sense that they are all in a pile.

Section 5: Objections Received and Their Disposition

During the comment period, the Office received a number of objections asserting that RSV has circulated in human beings for centuries and that the chimpanzees at Walter Reed caught it from us. Each objection has been reviewed and disposed of as follows.

Objector: Baltimore children, aged 4, tested 1957.
Objection: Roughly 80% of us already had antibodies to the virus that supposedly first reached a human in February 1956.
Disposition: Dismissed. Four-year-olds are notoriously unreliable witnesses and frequently claim to have had things they have not had.

Objector: Inmates, federal prison, Ohio, 1957.
Objection: We had the antibodies too, and we have not been anywhere near a chimpanzee.
Disposition: Dismissed. The Office does not accept testimony from persons who were, by definition, not getting out much.

Objector: A child who died in 1931, by way of preserved lung tissue re-examined at Vanderbilt University in 2007.
Objection: I had RSV twenty-four years before the chimps did.
Disposition: Dismissed. The objector has submitted no evidence of being alive, and the Office cannot accept filings from the deceased, except on voter rolls.

Objector: Author of an 1819 medical treatise.
Objection: I described an “acute suffocative catarrh” in infants that matches the disease exactly.
Disposition: Dismissed as hearsay. The treatise predates the laboratory and therefore cannot have come from it, which proves only how far back the cover-up goes.

Objector: Genetic analysis of the virus itself.
Objection: My ancestors have been circulating for hundreds of years.
Disposition: Referred to the Division of Things Viruses Would Say.

Objector: Larry Anderson, professor emeritus, Emory University, decades of RSV research.
Objection: (laughter, sustained)
Disposition: Noted. The Office has asked Professor Anderson to submit his objection in writing once he is able to.

Section 6: Valuation Dispute

The Exporter has declared the goods to be “the biggest killer of kids in this country today.”

Federal mortality data indicate that cancer kills more American children, and that RSV has historically been estimated to kill between 100 and 300 American children under five each year. The Office is satisfied that every one of those deaths is a tragedy and that a substantial number are preventable with the maternal vaccine and the infant antibody shots currently recommended, in principle, by this Department.

The Exporter was offered two national television appearances on August 2, 2026, in which to mention these products during the season when they matter. The Office has reviewed both broadcasts and can confirm that the products were not mentioned, the time having been fully allocated to Section 2.

Valuation is accordingly adjusted from “biggest killer” to “a serious winter illness that we have tools for,” a description the Office acknowledges lacks the necessary zip for broadcast.

Section 7: Certification

I hereby certify that the goods described above originated in a laboratory, a chimpanzee, a vaccine, a monkey, a lab worker and the general public, in that order and also in no order, and that the information in this Certificate is true and correct as of the time of reading, subject to amendment at the time of the next interview.

Appendix A: Disposition of One (1) Box of Chocolates and One (1) Bottle of Scotch

In the course of preparing this Certificate, the Office became aware of an outstanding liability dating from the 1950s.

Philip Roane, then a technician at Johns Hopkins, performed a substantial portion of the laboratory work that identified RSV in Baltimore’s children. He was not credited as an author on the 1957 paper. He was compensated by the lead author with a box of chocolates and a bottle of Scotch.

The Office has reviewed this arrangement and finds that it represents, by a considerable margin, the most accurate and proportionate transaction in the entire history of the RSV file. Mr. Roane did the work, told the truth, received modest confectionery, and in 2025 — nearly seventy years later — was finally listed as a co-author on a published history of the virus’s discovery. He is now 98 and teaches microbiology at Howard University.

The Office recommends no further action in respect of Mr. Roane, other than that someone at this Department read his paper.

The Office regrets that no equivalent liability can be identified on the Exporter’s side, as the Exporter has not done any of the work.
