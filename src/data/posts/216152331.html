If you’ve noticed your Sunday letter going quiet lately, it’s not because I stopped writing — it’s because it stopped arriving. A few weeks ago, Gmail slapped one of these newsletters with its “this might be dangerous” banner. I brushed it off as an algorithm with a flair for melodrama. I was wrong to brush it off and should have listened to my friend Sam. What actually happened, best I can tell, is that my personal Gmail account — sending one heavily-linked, mass-BCC’d email every week to the same seventy-some addresses — increasingly looks, to spam filters, exactly like the thing spam filters exist to catch. The warning banner wasn’t the problem. It was the first symptom of a reputation problem that’s only gotten worse since, to the point where the last several Sunday recaps left my Sent folder and, as far as I can tell, arrived nowhere at all. No bounce, no error, just silence — which is a genuinely bad way to find out your Sundays have been shouting into a void.

So: going forward, the recap comes to you the way the rest of this newsletter already does — as a Substack post, sent through Substack’s own mail system, not hand-BCC’d from Gmail. Nothing changes on your end. You’re already subscribed; you’ll keep getting one calm email a week, same as always. The only thing that’s different is the plumbing behind it, which is now built by people whose entire job is making sure emails arrive, rather than a retired guy and his dog improvising with a BCC field.

I also don’t want the last few weeks’ essays to just disappear into that silence, so I’ll be re-sending them properly, in order, starting with this one — until we’re caught back up to now.

— Stephen and Dior
