THE WHITE HOUSE Office of Retroactive Justification For Immediate Release — October 9, 2026

STATEMENT ON THE NORWEGIAN ERROR

The Office of Retroactive Justification has completed its review of this morning’s announcement from Oslo and issues the following findings.

Finding 1. At 11:00 a.m. Oslo time, a committee of five Norwegians, none of whom has ever built a hotel, announced that the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize had been awarded to a person other than the President. The Office has classified this as a clerical error.

Finding 2. The President already possesses a Nobel Peace Prize medal. It was received in January 2026, in a ceremony in the Oval Office that the Office considers more dignified than the one in Oslo, because it had better lighting and was attended by the President. The medal hangs in a gold frame, which is bigger than the frames used in Norway.

Finding 3. The Norwegian Nobel Institute has stated that a prize “cannot be transferred.” The Office notes that property is transferred in New York every day, sometimes several times, occasionally on the same afternoon. The Office does not recognize any category of gold that cannot change hands.

Finding 4. The Committee’s statement refers to a global shift toward “power politics.” The Office has determined that this is a reference to the President’s politics, which are not power politics but beautiful politics, and the phrase is under review by counsel.

The President has met many judges and has found most of them to be unfair, particularly the ones in The Hague, which is why his administration sanctioned several of them. Finding 8. The President has ended eight wars. A full list is attached as Enclosure B. The Committee did not request the list, which the Office regards as a failure of due diligence.

Finding 9. The prize carries an award of 12 million Swedish kronor, approximately $1.2 million. The President has generously offered to accept it on those terms (see Enclosure C).

Finding 10. The Office recommends that the error be corrected by Friday, October 16, 2026, and has prepared the following letter for that purpose.

STOP PRESS — Addendum, Posted late Friday morning….

Everything in this addendum actually happened.

The Office of Retroactive Justification regrets to report that events have overtaken this document.

The President’s official response to the Nobel announcement did not take the form of a letter. As Forbes reported on October 9, it took the form of a reshare: a post on Truth Social, written by someone else, calling the laureate an “unabashed hater of America and Israel.” The Office notes that this saved considerable drafting time.

The day before, the President had told reporters that if he did not win, it would be a “great discredit” to the committee. The Office has filed this under Finding 1.

Within hours of the announcement, CTV News and The Hill reported, the administration imposed sweeping sanctions on the International Criminal Court itself, the court where the laureate once served as a judge, cutting it off from banking, insurance and software. The Secretary of State explained the policy in a video statement: “Either the ICC will end its threats, or we will end the ICC.”

The Office has accordingly withdrawn Enclosure C. It is no longer necessary to invoice the laureate. The court where she once sat has been invoiced instead.
