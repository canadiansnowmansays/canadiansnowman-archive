IN THE COURT OF GLOBAL RESERVE CURRENCIES Family Division

IN RE THE MARRIAGE OF: THE WORLD FINANCIAL SYSTEM, Petitioner — and — THE UNITED STATES DOLLAR, Respondent

Case No. 89-PCT-GFX-1976 (matter previously dismissed on identical grounds; refiled)

PETITION FOR DISSOLUTION OF MARRIAGE

I. THE PARTIES

Petitioner, THE WORLD FINANCIAL SYSTEM (hereinafter “the World”), is a sprawling, multilingual, chronically overcommitted entity comprising some 190 sovereign nations, several thousand central bankers, and one exhausted Belgian settlement system that would very much like everyone to stop yelling. The World is represented in this matter by the newly formed firm of Euro, Renminbi & Sovereign Gold, LLP.

Respondent, THE UNITED STATES DOLLAR (hereinafter “the Dollar”), is a currency of advancing age, considerable inherited wealth, and a spouse — one PRESIDENT DONALD J. TRUMP, joined here as a Third Party Defendant for reasons that will become self-evident — whose conduct forms the substantial basis of this Petition.

II. BACKGROUND OF THE MARRIAGE

The parties were wed in 1944 at a resort in Bretton Woods, New Hampshire, in a ceremony best remembered for its punctuality, its gold standard, and the fact that everyone else in the wedding party agreed to peg themselves to the groom. It was, by any account, a marriage of convenience that somehow became a marriage of genuine devotion. The World gave the Dollar 89 percent of its foreign exchange transactions. The Dollar gave the World the comforting sense that at least one thing in the international system was boringly, reliably solvent.

There was a rough patch in 1971, when the groom, without consulting counsel, abandoned the gold standard entirely and closed what the parties’ historians refer to as “the Gold Window” — at the time considered so alarming that a distinguished couples’ therapist at M.I.T., one Dr. Charles Kindleberger, pronounced the marriage over in 1976 and filed the very complaint this Court now revisits. Dr. Kindleberger was, the record will show, premature. The parties reconciled. The groom’s family fired an uncle named Arthur Burns for enabling bad behaviour, hired a blunt disciplinarian named Paul Volcker instead, endured a decade of extremely high interest rates as a form of couples’ counselling, and by the mid-1980s the marriage was, if anything, stronger than before.

The World would also note, for the record, a 1974 side arrangement in which the Dollar’s family sent an emissary named William Simon to Saudi Arabia to secure the loyalty of a wealthy uncle, King Faisal, by trading continued arms shipments for a promise that oil would be priced exclusively in Dollars. The World does not raise this to relitigate the prenup. The World raises it to establish that this family has always understood exactly what it takes to keep a currency’s friends close, and is choosing, this time, not to bother.

III. GROUNDS FOR DISSOLUTION

The World alleges irreconcilable differences, particularized as follows:

A. Pattern of Threats Against the Household’s Only Independent Adult. The Dollar’s spouse has made repeated, public, and escalating efforts to remove or intimidate Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, whose entire function in this household was to be the one person not currently under his direct influence. The World notes that in 190-odd years of prior reserve-currency marriages — the British pound, the Dutch guilder — this has never once been a symptom of a healthy union. It has always, without exception, been the beginning of the end.

B. Unilateral Cancellation of Joint Commitments. The spouse has cancelled contracts he personally dislikes, including wind energy projects entered into in good faith by prior tenants of the household, on grounds that appear to be aesthetic rather than contractual. The World, which is now expected to hold this household’s paper as collateral, finds this concerning in the manner of a spouse who redecorates the shared apartment by throwing furniture out the window while the lease is still active.

C. Selective Prosecution of Former Household Members. The spouse has directed the household’s law enforcement apparatus at personal and political rivals with a specificity that the World’s attorneys describe, in their filing, as “not what due process customarily looks like.” A currency’s global standing has historically depended on the perception that the rule of law in its home country applies evenly. The World regrets to inform the Court that this perception has been “materially impaired.”

D. Contempt for the In-Laws. The spouse has treated longstanding alliance partners — NATO, South Korea, the general community of nations who have historically agreed to hold large piles of Dollars as a show of goodwill — with a mixture of tariffs, insults, and open skepticism as to whether the mutual defence commitments underlying those piles of Dollars still apply. The World notes that Japan currently holds $1.12 trillion in reserves it did not, strictly speaking, need to hold, and South Korea holds $135 billion of the same, and that both parties are now understood to be “rethinking their exposure,” which is a nice way of saying they are quietly seeing a divorce attorney too.

E. Refusal to Attend Couples’ Counselling, Generally. The Petitioner notes that every prior crisis in this marriage — 1971, the stagflation years, several subsequent Fed showdowns — was resolved because the Dollar’s household ultimately deferred to an independent institution and an independent judiciary. The Petitioner alleges that this arrangement is, as of the date of filing, no longer operative, and that the household’s response to being asked about it has been to suggest the question is itself disloyal.

IV. NEW RELATIONSHIPS OF THE PETITIONER (Disclosed in the Interest of Candor)

The World discloses, as it is obligated to under the doctrine of clean hands, that it has recently been spending time with other suitors. It has been seen at financial conferences with the Euro, who has been undergoing what friends describe as “a whole Capital Markets Union self-improvement journey” specifically designed to make the Euro easier to hold at scale. It has also exchanged numbers with the Renminbi, who has been aggressively expanding a payments system called CIPS that friends describe as “trying quite hard, bless it.” The World wishes to state clearly that neither relationship is serious yet. Both, however, are further along than they were eighteen months ago, and the World would ask the Court to note the pace of the courtship as probative of the underlying marital breakdown rather than its cause.

V. RESPONDENT’S ANTICIPATED DEFENSE

The World anticipates that the Dollar’s spouse will argue, in his own filing, that no viable alternative to the Dollar exists; that the Euro is encumbered by nineteen finance ministers who cannot agree on a shared toaster, let alone a shared bond market; and that the Renminbi is not fully convertible and is therefore, in the language of family law, “not currently in a position to offer stable housing.” The World does not dispute any of this. The World simply notes that a spouse’s continued residence in the marital home has historically depended on more than the other party’s difficulty finding a new apartment.

VI. PRAYER FOR RELIEF

WHEREFORE, Petitioner respectfully requests that this Court:

  1. Grant dissolution of the marriage on grounds of irreconcilable differences, effective upon a date to be determined by bond yields;

  2. Order equitable division of reserve assets, with due consideration to the fact that 60 percent of everything is still, awkwardly, in the Dollar’s name;

  3. Decline to award spousal support, on the theory that a currency which prints its own alimony payments is not, strictly, in need of any;

  4. Enter a restraining order preventing the Third Party Defendant from firing any further independent witnesses to this marriage prior to final judgment; and

  5. Grant such other relief as the Court deems just, prompt, and — given the Petitioner’s fifty years of documented patience on this exact question — extremely overdue.

Respectfully submitted,

THE WORLD FINANCIAL SYSTEM by its counsel, Euro, Renminbi & Sovereign Gold, LLP cc: the ghost of Charles Kindleberger, who would like the record to show he was only fifty years early

A note from the Court scribe: His Majesty’s counsel has filed a response indicating the Petition is “fake news, very unfair, tremendous currency, everybody says so,” and has moved to have the presiding judge fired. The motion is, as is customary in this Court, still pending.
