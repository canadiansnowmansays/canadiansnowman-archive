Sunday Satire. Katie Zacharia was named White House press secretary on October 9, 2026, held one briefing on October 13, and was out on October 16. Everything quoted from her, from the president and from Sean Hannity’s interview is real, drawn from People, Fox News, Hawaii News Now and Jonathan Chait’s October 16 piece in the Atlantic. The packet, the Office and its forms are invented. A companion to “Diplomatic Offboarding: Exit Interview, Embassy Athens,” posted earlier this morning.

SECTION 1: WELCOME LETTER

Dear New Press Secretary,

Congratulations, and welcome to the most visible job in the building after the President’s own!

You join a proud tradition. George W. Bush and Barack Obama got through seven press secretaries between them in sixteen years. We aim to beat that record with a single president, and with your help we are well on our way.

To save paper, this packet serves as both your onboarding and your offboarding materials. Experience shows that new press secretaries rarely have time to read two packets. Please keep it somewhere handy. You will need it again sooner than you think.

Warmest regards, Office of Retroactive Justification Division of Personnel Continuity

SECTION 2: QUALIFICATIONS VERIFIED

The President personally reviewed your file. His findings, in his own words: “She has an unblemished record of support, and has been a loyal and brilliant supporter, right from the beginning. She is, likewise, a devoted fan and member of MAGA!”

Our verification team has confirmed the following:

Note to file: The President’s statement used the word “unblemished” once. The word is now retired from this Office’s vocabulary until further notice.

SECTION 3: YOUR ONBOARDING SCHEDULE

Allotted preparation time before your first briefing: “less than maybe eight hours,” by your own estimate. The Office considers this generous. The President, after all, prepares for nothing, and he is his own best spokesperson.

SECTION 4: APPROVED TALKING POINTS

Please memorise the following. Pauses for breath are permitted but will be noticed.

  • On foreign affairs: The President “had a historic one-day war, and now we are, have a booming economy, and a great relationship” with Venezuela. The Office reminds you to finish this sentence before reaching the word “Venezuela,” as a pause in the middle invites reporters to wonder whether you are unsure which country it was.

  • On energy: Nuclear power is “something President Trump is delivering on, just like he delivers on every promise.” Follow it with the $54 billion figure for the Alaska LNG pipeline. If you hear yourself begin “it, we will be, it, we will be,” stop, breathe and go straight to the number. The number is the point.

  • On your own role: “My goal is to be able to communicate that in complete tandem with the president, as he is his own best spokesperson.” This is the most accurate sentence in the packet. Senior staff agreed with it so completely that they acted on it within seventy-two hours.

SECTION 5: BRIEFING ROOM PROCEDURES

5.1 Length. The standard briefing is fourteen minutes. Eight of those should be reserved for an opening statement on the President’s accomplishments.

5.2 Questions. The approved maximum is three. Outlets have been preselected for your comfort: Breitbart, the Daily Mail and Fox News.

5.3 Making news. You may make one item of news per briefing. We suggest announcing the departure of a woman. You will be pleased to learn that one is usually available.

5.4 Ending the briefing. You may end a briefing at any time by referring to Marine One. If a reporter asks how you will balance your family and your work, end it immediately. We do not ask the men that question, and you should not have to answer it either.

5.5 Identification. The President is the President. Your husband is your husband. Under pressure, new hires sometimes mix the two up. The Office recommends a small card in your jacket pocket.

SECTION 6: FRIENDLY VENUES

The following outlets are classified as friendly:

  • Fox News

  • Newsmax

  • Hannity

Advisory: Friendly venues stay friendly only while you remain an asset. Once a press secretary becomes a liability, Hannity may be reclassified without notice from “soft landing” to “interrogation room.”

If you are interrogated, the Office suggests avoiding the defence you used on his programme: “I really stepped up to the plate with very little prep time… I feel like I did my very best, Sean, to deliver for the American people that day.” It is true, and it is sympathetic. It is also an admission that the White House sent you out unprepared, and the White House does not admit things.

SECTION 7: CREDENTIALS

You have described yourself as an attorney. You hold a law degree, which is a fine thing to hold.

When a reporter on the South Lawn asked which state’s bar exam you had passed, you replied: “When I moved to China.”

The Office has searched the records of all fifty states, the District of Columbia and the People’s Republic of China. We have not located your admission. In the meantime, please list your profession on forms as “Press Secretary.” This title is guaranteed accurate until Friday.

SECTION 8: PERFORMANCE REVIEW

Your performance has been reviewed twice.

Review 1 (Tuesday, October 13): A White House release called your debut a “commanding inaugural press briefing.” Communications director Steven Cheung offered high praise. The President said you did “very good.” Rating: Exceeds expectations.

Review 2 (Friday, October 16): You are no longer press secretary. Rating: See Review 1.

The Office sees no contradiction here. Both reviews were correct on the day they were issued. That is the standard to which all statements in this building are held.

SECTION 9: OFFBOARDING (BEGINS HERE)

Welcome to the second half of your packet!

Please return the following items:

  • Your White House badge

  • Your briefing book (lightly used, as you received it the same morning)

  • The small card from Section 5.5

Please do not return:

  • Your law degree. That is yours to keep. The bar admission remains a matter between you and the People’s Republic of China.

Exit statement. Departing women in this administration traditionally say that the job was “the honor of a lifetime.” Ambassador Guilfoyle used it on her way out of Athens, and your predecessor used a version of it on her way out of this building. Your lifetime in the role ran from Friday to Friday, so a shorter phrase is acceptable: “the honor of a week.”

SECTION 10: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q. Was I set up to fail? A. The Office prefers “set up to brief.” The two are frequently confused.

Q. Would more preparation have helped? A. We will never know. That is the advantage of providing none.

Q. My predecessor lasted nineteen months. Why did I last seven days? A. Your predecessor left to be with her children, which is a reason the President accepts from women. You left because the President had seen enough, which is the reason he accepts from himself.

Q. Are men in this administration held to the same standard? A. The Secretary of War shared attack plans in a group chat that included a journalist. He kept his job. You gave a halting fourteen-minute briefing. You were given this packet. The Office notes that both outcomes were efficient.

Q. Who replaces me? A. Applications are open. The essential requirement remains an unblemished record of support. Prior podium experience is not required. Being a man is not required either, though the Office’s records show it does tend to lengthen a tenure.
