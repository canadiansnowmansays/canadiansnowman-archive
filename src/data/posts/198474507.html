The Duffy family, ready for the ultimate American road trip — fully funded by the companies Dad regulates. Nine kids, one ethics memo, zero disclosed donors.

The Transportation Department is pleased to announce The Great American Road Trip, a new YouTube series in which Transportation Secretary Sean P. Duffy, his wife Rachel Campos-Duffy (Fox News anchor, reality television veteran, and the only person in the family whose media career predates her husband’s cabinet appointment), and their nine children travel the United States in vehicles provided by companies regulated by Sean Duffy’s department, staying in hotels funded by companies regulated by Sean Duffy’s department, filling up at gas stations owned by companies regulated by Sean Duffy’s department, and flying on airlines regulated by Sean Duffy’s department.

The series is funded by a nonprofit that is not required to disclose its donors but whose donors include Toyota, Boeing, United Airlines, and Shell. It is part of the Transportation Department’s official efforts to mark America’s 250th anniversary, because nothing says “independence” like a cabinet secretary’s family vacation underwritten by his own regulated industries.

The department’s ethics lawyers have cleared the project. A memorandum of agreement states that the nonprofit will receive no preferential treatment from the department on regulatory matters. The memo does not extend to the nonprofit’s corporate donors, who are not signatories, which is a bit like a referee promising not to fix the game while the people paying the referee’s salary sit in the owner’s box making no promises at all.

Premiering June 2026. Here is what’s in store.

EPISODE 1: “Engine Trouble” The Duffys Visit a Boeing Factory (Inspection Not Included)

Inside Boeing’s Everett plant. “This is where the fuselage panels are joined together,” says Secretary Duffy, standing in front of planes his department is supposed to regulate. Gift shop not pictured.

The adventure begins at Boeing’s Everett, Washington assembly plant, where Secretary Duffy shows the children how a 787 Dreamliner is built. “This is where the fuselage panels are joined together,” he explains, standing in front of a production line that the FAA — an agency housed in his department — is responsible for inspecting.

Young Patrick, age 7, asks why a door fell off a plane last year. Rachel redirects with a fun fact about turbulence. The Boeing tour guide, who has been briefed, smoothly transitions to the gift shop.

The family receives complimentary Boeing model airplanes. The ethics memo is not mentioned.

Runtime: 22 minutes. Regulatory conflicts: 3. Gift shop purchases underwritten by: the nonprofit.

EPISODE 2: “Fill ‘Er Up!” Shell Station Surprise — Why Is Gas So Cheap Here?

Fill ’er up! The Duffys at a Shell station. Gas is somehow affordable today. Nothing suspicious about a family learning the supply chain from the companies Daddy regulates.

The Duffys stop at a Shell station outside Bakersfield, California, where gas is $5.89 a gallon for everyone except, presumably, the family whose road trip is funded by Shell.

Secretary Duffy explains the supply chain to the children using the gas pump as a visual aid. “This fuel comes from refineries that meet strict environmental and transportation safety standards,” he says, not mentioning that his department oversees the pipelines that deliver crude to those refineries and that Shell has pending regulatory matters before his agency.

Rachel films a TikTok of the children washing the windshield. It goes viral. Commenters are divided between those who find it charming and those who find it an ethics violation. Both groups are correct.

Runtime: 18 minutes. Gallons of gas purchased with nonprofit funds: 22. Pending Shell regulatory matters: undisclosed.

EPISODE 3: “Up, Up, and Away!” The Duffys Fly United (First Class, Obviously)

First-class United cabin. Nine tickets, classified as an “official gift” to the Department of Transportation. The ethics memo definitely covers this.

The family boards a United Airlines flight from Chicago O’Hare to Denver, filmed in partnership with United, which is a major donor to the nonprofit funding the series, and which operates under safety regulations enforced by the FAA, which reports to Secretary Duffy.

The children are delighted by the first-class cabin. Rachel explains to viewers that the family always flies first class when traveling for official department business, which this is, according to the ethics memo, which classified the entire production as a gift to the department.

A gift. Nine first-class tickets. For a YouTube series. About a road trip. On an airline regulated by the passenger’s department. As a gift.

Secretary Duffy visits the cockpit and speaks with the pilots about air traffic control staffing shortages — the same shortages his department has been struggling to address since 67 people died in a midair collision near Reagan National Airport in 2025. The FAA recently revised down its estimates of how many controllers are needed, arguing it could close gaps with “more efficient scheduling.” The air traffic controllers’ union called the study “flawed.”

This segment is cut from the final episode.

Runtime: 24 minutes. Edited runtime: 16 minutes. Segments cut for tone: 1.

EPISODE 4: “Truck Stop!” Rachel Explains to the Children Why Daddy’s Ethics Lawyer Says This Is Fine

“Daddy, is it okay that the companies you regulate are paying for our vacation?”
“It’s not a vacation, it’s an official 250th anniversary initiative.” Water slides: 4. Ethics memos read: 0.

Midway through Kansas, nine-year-old Maria Duffy asks the question the audience has been thinking since Episode 1: “Daddy, is it okay that the companies you’re in charge of are paying for our vacation?”

Secretary Duffy, to his credit, does not flinch. “It’s not a vacation, sweetheart,” he says. “It’s an official Department of Transportation initiative to celebrate America’s 250th birthday.”

“But we’re at a water park,” Maria observes.

Rachel intervenes with a monologue about how government service is a sacrifice, how the Duffy family gives so much to the country, and how this trip is really about showing Americans the beauty of their nation’s transportation infrastructure, which just happens to include the Schlitterbahn water park in Kansas City, Kansas.

The ethics memo is shown on screen for approximately 1.3 seconds. Viewers who pause the frame note that it covers expenses for Secretary Duffy but does not explain how expenses for Rachel Campos-Duffy and nine children constitute “official department business.”

Nobody pauses the frame. The water slides look fantastic.

Runtime: 26 minutes. Water slides: 4. Ethics memos shown on screen: 1. Ethics memos read by anyone: 0.

EPISODE 5: “The Open Road” In Which the Duffys Drive a Toyota Through States That May Lose Highway Funding

The Duffys cruising in a complimentary Toyota through states that might lose highway funding. “Look, a cow!”

The family drives a complimentary Toyota Highlander through Iowa and Nebraska, states that have not yet complied with the Transportation Department’s directive to stop issuing trucking licenses to immigrants for terms longer than their federal work permits.

Secretary Duffy explains the policy to the children. “Some states aren’t following the rules,” he says, “so Daddy’s department might have to withhold their highway funding.”

The Duffys drive these highways in a Toyota — a company that donated to the nonprofit funding the trip — through states that may lose federal money because of a policy implemented by the man sitting in the Toyota.

Thirteen-year-old Jack asks if withholding highway funds might make the roads worse. “That’s a great question, buddy,” Duffy says. “Look, a cow!”

Runtime: 20 minutes. Cows spotted: 7. Constitutional questions from teenagers successfully redirected: 1.

EPISODE 6: “Daddy’s Busy” Secretary Duffy Takes a Work Call While the Family Visits the Grand Canyon

The family at the Grand Canyon. Majestic views. Secretary Duffy steps away for an FAA call about a near-miss. Background music conveniently gets louder.

The family reaches the Grand Canyon. It is majestic. The children are awed. Rachel films everything. Secretary Duffy steps away to take a call from the FAA about a near-miss incident at Phoenix Sky Harbor.

The children peer over the rim. Rachel explains the geological history. Secretary Duffy, on the phone, can be heard saying “how close?” and “was anyone—” before Rachel turns up the background music.

The episode is dedicated to the 250th anniversary of the United States. No mention is made of the 67 people who died in the 2025 midair collision, the understaffed control towers, or the flawed study that redefined “fully staffed” to mean “whatever we currently have.”

The Grand Canyon is beautiful, though. Full marks for cinematography.

Runtime: 28 minutes. Near-miss incidents during filming: 1. Mentioned in episode: 0.

SEASON FINALE: “Home” The Duffys Return to Washington, Having Learned Nothing

The family pulls into the driveway of their Washington home, tanned, happy, and in possession of enough b-roll to fill a Senate confirmation hearing. The children have learned about America. Rachel has content for six months of Fox segments. Secretary Duffy has demonstrated that the Transportation Department celebrates national milestones by accepting gifts from the companies it regulates.

In the final scene, the family gathers at the dinner table. Secretary Duffy says grace. He thanks God, he thanks America, and he thanks the nonprofit — which is not required to disclose its donors — for making the trip possible.

The ethics memo, we are told, covers everything.

Young Maria, who asked the only honest question in the entire series, is noticeably quiet.

Runtime: 32 minutes. Total series runtime: 186 minutes. Regulatory conflicts identified by the audience: countless. Regulatory conflicts identified by the ethics office: 0.

THE GREAT AMERICAN ROAD TRIP premieres June 2026 on YouTube. A government watchdog has asked the department’s inspector general to investigate. So far, Republicans have not joined the pile-on.
