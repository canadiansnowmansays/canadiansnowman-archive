The following notice did not happen. No such surcharge exists, no ship has ever been delayed at the Welland Canal over what its papers called the lake at the other end of it, and the Corporation’s actual spokespeople have said so, on the record, more than once. This is satire, filed in response to satire — the online rumor that Canada was blockading “Lake America”-bound vessels, which several thousand people mistook for a genuine Fox News report despite no such report existing. If the joke escaped its quotation marks once already this week, it can withstand a second trip.

ST. LAWRENCE SEAWAY MANAGEMENT CORPORATION NOTICE TO SHIPPING NO. 2026-451 Subject: Supplementary Schedule of Nomenclature-Compliance Surcharges, Welland Canal Section Effective: Immediately upon your ship’s arrival at Lock 1, regardless of when you read this

  1. General. Pursuant to no statutory authority whatsoever, and in a spirit of purely voluntary bureaucratic enthusiasm, the Corporation hereby introduces a Schedule of Nomenclature-Compliance Surcharges applicable to all vessels transiting the Welland Canal whose manifests, bills of lading, or captain’s personal correspondence refer to the body of water at the canal’s northern terminus by any name other than the one it has carried for four hundred years.

  2. Base Toll. The base toll remains unchanged and continues to be assessed in strict accordance with the binational Tariff of Tolls, a document the Corporation is contractually obligated to remind you is, in fact, binational, jointly published, and not something either government gets to unilaterally reinterpret over a weekend, however satisfying that might be for morale.

  3. Schedule of Surcharges.
    a. Tier One — Simple Error. A vessel whose manifest lists the destination as “Lake America” through apparent oversight, autocorrect, or an overly patriotic clerk, shall be assessed a surcharge of $40, payable in either currency, and shall receive a complimentary laminated card bearing the correct name, its Wendat etymology, and a small drawing of a beaver looking unimpressed.

    b. Tier Two — Persistent Error. A vessel whose captain, upon being issued the laminated card described in Tier One, insists to the lockmaster that “everybody knows what I mean,” shall be assessed a surcharge of $400 and one (1) mandatory ten-minute conversation with a Parks Canada interpretive guide on the subject of pre-Confederation hydronymy, delivered at a pace calibrated to make the original toll seem, by comparison, a great bargain.

    c. Tier Three — Documented Defiance. A vessel whose owner has publicly stated, on any social media platform, an intention to use the term “Lake America” specifically because Canadian officials asked them not to, shall be assessed a surcharge of $4,000, to be donated in full to the Ontario Federation of Anglers and Hunters for the purpose of stocking the lake in question with fish that will, upon capture, be tagged exclusively in French.

    d. Tier Four — Executive Order in Hand. A vessel whose captain physically produces a printed copy of the executive order and attempts to hand it to the lockmaster shall be assessed no additional surcharge, waved through with unusual speed, and quietly flagged for a customs inspection so thorough that the ship will make its connection in Cleveland roughly around the time the lake finishes freezing over for the season.

  4. A Note on Legal Authority. The Corporation wishes to be transparent that none of the foregoing has any basis in the actual, binding, seventy-year-old treaty framework governing this waterway, which both governments have observed continuously through wars, recessions, and at least one previous attempt by a sitting American president to rename a body of water he does not fully own. The Corporation notes, purely for the historical record, that the last time someone tried to weaponize the Welland Canal’s geography for political leverage it was the Lake Carriers’ Association of Buffalo, in 1892, accusing Canada of discriminatory tolls against the port of Ogdensburg. That dispute was resolved through diplomatic correspondence. This one, the Corporation suspects, will be resolved through screenshots.

  5. Frequently Asked Questions. Q: Is this real? A: See paragraph 4, and also the actual statement issued this week by an actual spokesperson, who said, and we are quoting this because it is funnier than anything we could write: “Seaway operations are continuing normally and have done so throughout the weekend.” Q: If it isn’t real, why publish it? A: Because twenty-three thousand people believed the fake version, and the fake version did not even have the decency to invent a fee schedule. We felt the least we could do, having been dragged into a controversy over a name we did not choose to litigate, was litigate it properly, with tiers. Q: What should I actually call the lake? A: Lake Ontario. It has been Lake Ontario since roughly 1614, four decades before Newton discovered gravity and four hundred years before anyone discovered you could rename a body of water by pointing at a whiteboard in the Oval Office. The name will remain Lake Ontario on this side of the border regardless of what the whiteboard says, in the same spirit that the Corporation intends to remain a not-for-profit binational canal operator regardless of what anyone posts about it on X.

  6. Closing. The Corporation reminds all mariners that the actual toll schedule, the actual treaty, and the actual eight locks lifting actual ships three hundred and twenty-six and a half feet over the Niagara Escarpment will continue operating exactly as they have for nearly a century, indifferent to naming disputes, executive orders, and satire alike — which is, on reflection, rather the point of infrastructure.

Issued without irony by an office that specializes in it.
