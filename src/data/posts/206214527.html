There is a particular kind of tell in politics, and it isn’t subtle once you notice it: the loudest attack on a thing is often the clearest confession of what someone else once represented to you, and couldn’t. Anna Freud called it reaction formation in 1936 — the defense mechanism by which an uncomfortable impulse gets converted into its opposite and aimed outward, with extra conviction, at whoever embodies the thing you can’t sit with. She was writing about children and neurotics. She was not, as far as I can tell, writing about the current Secretary of Transportation. And yet.

On July 7, 2026, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced that his department would redirect $1.73 billion in Biden-era infrastructure grants away from what he called “DEI bike lanes,” toward roads and bridges instead. The phrase ran on X, then got laundered into respectability by The Daily Wire, which framed it as Duffy finally fixing “what Americans actually use” after Pete Buttigieg wasted a billion dollars making cities pedal-friendly.

Two things are worth sitting with before we even get to Duffy himself.

First: what actually got cut. The grants in question, per The New Republic, funded a transit center in North Carolina, bridge replacements in New Mexico, street extensions in New Hampshire, traffic lights and crosswalks in Missouri — and, yes, bike lanes in Seattle. Calling this a “DEI bike lane program” is the rhetorical equivalent of calling a grocery bill “the ice cream expenditure” because there was a pint of Häagen-Dazs somewhere in the cart.

Second: the “DEI” label traces back to Buttigieg’s own 2021 language, when he said the grants would “improve infrastructure, strengthen supply chains, make us safer, advance equity, and combat climate change.” The word “equity” appeared once, in a list of five goals. That single word is now doing the work of retroactively recasting an entire infrastructure program as a racial-spoils operation. Even the government’s own Federal Highway Administration disagrees with the framing it’s being used to gut — its official position is that bike lanes reduce bicyclist-driver conflict and make roads safer for everyone, DEI or no DEI.

Now to the part of this story that the DEI framing is designed to make you forget to ask about.

Pete Buttigieg is a cyclist. Pete Buttigieg is gay. Pete Buttigieg spent four years as the right’s designated punching bag for both facts simultaneously — “Mayor Pete rides a bike to the office” was never really a critique of transit policy, it was a way of saying he’s soft, he’s other, he doesn’t belong in a hard-hat department without saying any of that directly. The bike became a proxy. It always does.

Sean Duffy did not have to pick bike lanes as his signature cut. He had bridges, ports, aviation — an entire department’s worth of less loaded targets. He picked the one thing most closely associated, in the right’s own iconography, with his predecessor’s identity. That’s not an accident of messaging. That’s reaching for the exact lever that was already primed to work, because someone built the mechanism years before Duffy ever showed up to pull it.

Which is where reaction formation earns its keep as a frame, rather than a diagnosis. I’m not putting Duffy on a couch — I have no idea what he privately thinks about anything, and neither does anyone else writing about this. What I can say is that the politics of reaction formation don’t require access to anyone’s interior life. The mechanism works at the level of coalition, not psyche: you consolidate your own standing with an audience by attacking, with visible enthusiasm, whatever your predecessor was mocked for representing. Duffy doesn’t need to feel anything about bikes or about Buttigieg’s sexuality. He just needs to know which target reliably gets a cheer from The Daily Wire‘s comment section, and reach for it. The reaction is the same shape whether it’s felt or performed. That’s the uncomfortable part — it works either way.

It also fits neatly into the résumé. This is the same Sean Duffy who went from reality television to Congress to a several-month stint atop NASA to Secretary of Transportation, a career arc that treats competence as optional so long as the branding holds. “America is fortunate to have a Builder in the White House who knows America is only as great as our infrastructure,” he told The Daily Wire — a sentence that sounds like it was generated by an algorithm trained exclusively on hard-hat photo ops. The “Builder” framing isn’t incidental either. It’s the same masculine-competence branding exercise that spent four years being contrasted, implicitly, against a Secretary who was mocked for being cerebral, administrative, and — the unspoken third item — married to a man.

American families, as it happens, will lose actual safety infrastructure over this. Bike lanes reduce collisions. Crosswalks and traffic lights in Missouri were bundled into the same “DEI” basket and are now in funding limbo along with everything else Duffy decided to lump in with the culture-war target. The cruelty isn’t the point, to borrow a phrase — the point is the applause line, and the cruelty is just what falls out of it.

Anna Freud would have recognized the shape of this immediately. The rest of us just have to watch it happen at the scale of a $1.73 billion line item.
