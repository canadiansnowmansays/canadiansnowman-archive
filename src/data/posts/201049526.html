CanadianSnowmanSays | Sunday Edition

The photograph is instructive.

This week, the official Truth Social account of the President of the United States posted a side-by-side image: “Hussein Obama” on the left, “Trump” on the right. The subject in both images is the Washington Monument, reflected in the Reflecting Pool on the National Mall. Under Obama, the image suggests, the monument was somehow diminished. Under Trump, it gleams.

The problem — and this is not a metaphor, this is a factual, verifiable, reproducible problem that required no advanced analytical equipment to detect, merely eyes — is that it is the same photograph. Identical cloud formations. Identical water ripple patterns. Identical light angle. Identical everything. The President of the United States posted two copies of the same image and labelled them differently to demonstrate his own superiority over his predecessor. The Washington Monument, which has stood since 1884 and survived two world wars, a Great Depression, and the full catalogue of American political upheaval, remained entirely unchanged. It did not get the memo.

One might ask why a sitting president would do this. One might ask what compulsion drives a man to spend his birthday week — his 80th birthday, the oldest person ever to hold the office — posting memes about a predecessor who left the White House nearly a decade ago. One might ask what it means that this predecessor, Barack Obama, is also the subject of an AI-generated image posted in the same week, depicting his presidential library as a garbage-topped tower surrounded by a tent city. One might ask these questions and then sit quietly with the answers for a while.

A 79-year-old president, in possession of the most powerful office on earth, generated this image and posted it. The author presents it without further comment, on the grounds that further comment would be redundant.

The answer, if one is willing to look at it directly, is not complicated. It has been hiding in plain sight since 2011.

Donald Trump’s political career began not with policy but with a racial lie. The birther movement — the sustained, systematic campaign to delegitimize Barack Obama by claiming he was not born in the United States and therefore not eligible for the presidency — was Trump’s entry point into American politics. He did not invent it, but he became its most prominent and persistent advocate, tweeting about Obama’s birth certificate between 2011 and 2016 with a frequency that bordered on compulsion. “An extremely credible source has called my office and told me that Barack Obama’s birth certificate is a fraud,” he wrote in 2012. There was no credible source. There was no fraud. Obama was born in Hawaii to an American mother. These facts were available to anyone who looked, which is what made the exercise so revealing about the man conducting it.

Research is unambiguous on what the birther movement represented. Studies consistently find that belief in birther claims correlates directly with racial resentment — specifically, negative attitudes toward Black Americans — independent of partisan identity. It was not a political argument. It was a racial one, dressed in the language of procedural concern. And Trump, who had said as far back as 1989 that a “well-educated Black has a tremendous advantage over a well-educated white in terms of the job market” — a statement that was not a compliment but a grievance — was its most enthusiastic promoter.

Obama became Trump’s foil for reasons that were personal before they were political. Trump convinced himself, and his followers, that Obama was the beneficiary of what he perceived as unearned Black privilege — that the world’s admiration for Obama was racial compensation rather than genuine merit. This is a pernicious and well-documented feature of racial resentment psychology: the belief that Black achievement is inherently suspect, that it must be explained by something other than ability. Trump could not accept that a Black man could simply be better. So he searched, feverishly and publicly, for the error. Sometimes he invented it. Often he projected his own deficiencies onto it. The search, seventeen years in, continues.

The identical photograph, posted in birthday week 2026, is the same psychology in digital form. The image doesn’t matter. The clouds don’t matter. The fact that it is the same photograph doesn’t matter to the man who posted it, because the point was never factual accuracy. The point was the assertion — the repeated, compulsive assertion that he is better, that Obama was lesser, that the comparison favors him. That it demonstrably does not, even in the image he himself chose, is almost beside the point. Almost.

Let us, then, do what the President invited and make the comparison properly. He opened this door. We are merely walking through it.

On approval ratings. Barack Obama left office in January 2017 with a 59% approval rating — one of the highest exit approval ratings of any modern president. Trump left his first term in January 2021 with 34%. Obama’s lowest approval rating during his presidency was 38%. Trump’s was 34% — which was also, with a pleasing symmetry, his final number. Obama was consistently more popular, consistently more trusted, and consistently better regarded internationally throughout both their presidencies. Trump has spent years cherry-picking single polls from conservative-leaning outlets to claim otherwise, a hobby that has consumed more of his public attention than infrastructure, climate, and the opioid crisis combined, though the author declines to commission a formal study. The aggregated record is not ambiguous. The polls, unfortunately for the President, do not care about his feelings about the polls.

On the economy. Obama inherited the worst financial crisis since the Great Depression, with job losses running at 700,000 per month when he took office. He left with 75 consecutive months of private sector job growth — a record at the time. His administration oversaw the rescue of the American auto industry, the passage of the Affordable Care Act extending health insurance to twenty million Americans, the Dodd-Frank reforms responding to the financial crisis, and the Paris Climate Agreement. Trump has spent both his terms attempting to dismantle each of these achievements, with mixed results — the ACA survives, the Paris Agreement has been re-entered and re-exited, and Dodd-Frank has been weakened but not eliminated. The author notes, without further comment, that attempting to erase someone’s record is itself a form of acknowledgment that the record exists.

On global standing. Obama received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2009, awarded by the Norwegian Nobel Committee for his “extraordinary efforts to strengthen international diplomacy and cooperation between peoples.” Obama accepted it with characteristic honesty, saying he did not view it as recognition of his own accomplishments but as an affirmation of American leadership. Trump, seventeen years later, remains publicly agitated that Obama received this prize. “Obama got the Nobel Prize. He had no idea why,” Trump said earlier this year, continuing his campaign for the award after being passed over in 2025. The Norwegian Nobel Committee, when asked about the possibility of transferring Obama’s prize to Trump, noted that this is not how prizes work. The Committee did not elaborate further, which was generous of them.

On personal conduct. Obama left the White House with no criminal indictments, no civil fraud judgments, no defamation verdicts, no sexual assault findings, no classified documents scandals, and no insurrection. Trump has accumulated all of the above. The author presents this as a list rather than an argument, on the grounds that it does not require one.

On physical presentation. Obama, who turns 65 this August, runs half-marathons. He kite-surfs. He shoots hoops with the easy fluency of a man who has played the game his whole life. He appears in public looking like a person who has invested in his health across decades. Trump, who turned 79 this week, moves between golf carts and motorcades, has described exercise as a drain on the body’s finite energy reserves — a theory not endorsed by medical science at any point in recorded history — and presided over a birthday celebration that required an octagon to be built on the South Lawn of the White House, because watching other men’s physical exertion is the preferred substitute. The body keeps an honest record. The golf cart does not.

The “Hussein” is worth addressing directly, briefly, and without undue ceremony, because it deserves none. Trump has deployed Obama’s middle name as a weapon since the birther days — an attempt to make a Hawaiian-born American sound foreign, Muslim, threatening, other. In 2026, with the administration having scrubbed Pride flags from federal buildings, removed Juneteenth from the park admission schedule, and maintained a travel ban on majority-Muslim countries, the middle name deployment is not subtle. It does not need to be. It is the same signal it has always been, sent to the same recipients, for the same purpose. The recipients know what it means. So does everyone else.

What is striking, watching from Canada — where one tries not to stare and keeps staring — is not the obsession itself. Obsession with a more admired predecessor is comprehensible, if not admirable. What is striking is the futility of it. Obama is not diminished by the identical photograph. He is not diminished by the AI garbage bag image. He is not diminished by seventeen years of birtherism, poll-cherry-picking, middle name deployment, and policy reversal. He remains, by virtually every measure that can be objectively assessed — approval ratings, economic record, global standing, legal record, personal conduct, physical condition, post-presidential legacy — the superior of the man doing the comparing.

The Washington Monument knows this. It has been photographed from the same angle, in the same light, with the same clouds, and it looked the same both times. The monument is not subtle either, but in a different direction.

The President posted the photograph anyway.

Barack Obama was the 44th President of the United States, serving from January 2009 to January 2017. He was born in Honolulu, Hawaii, on August 4, 1961. The Washington Monument was not available for comment.
