CLASSIFICATION: Unclassified, though several parties present at the field test clearly wished it weren’t. SUBJECT: Donald J. Trump, President of the United States PREPARED BY: Office of the Canadian Outsider, Sunday Desk DATE OF REPORT: July 26, 2026 PERIOD UNDER REVIEW: June 13, 2026 — present

1. Executive Summary

This report was commissioned in June 2026 to determine, on the occasion of the Subject’s eightieth birthday, whether the Subject possesses a functioning sense of humour. The finding was negative: unfunny not in the ordinary sense of poor timing, but at a structural level, consistent with the absence of a second frame of reference in which incongruity might occur.

A full review of the transcript from the July 24 rescheduled White House Correspondents’ Association dinner has since produced four specimens for the record, catalogued below. The additional data does not complicate the finding. It confirms it, at length, on the record, for nearly an hour.

2. Methodology

Three instruments were applied throughout:

  • The Superiority Instrument (Hobbes): does the material make the audience feel superior to its target?

  • The Incongruity Instrument (Kant, Schopenhauer): does the material construct two incompatible frames and then collide them?

  • The Relief Instrument (Freud): does the material discharge an existing anxiety, or merely restate it?

Four specimens have now been logged, up from one, and are catalogued below in order of diagnostic value.

3. Exhibit A: The Cow

The Subject informed the room that the Health and Human Services Secretary had personally struck a cow with his car, butchered it, and served it as the evening’s tenderloin. Strong premise — an elegant tasting menu colliding with an act of roadside veterinary violence. The Subject stated the premise and stopped, offering no further construction. The room’s laughter arrived as risk management, not response. Finding: he can locate the incongruity. He cannot detonate it.

4. Exhibit B: The Raccoon, Withdrawn

Immediately following the cow material, the Subject added that the Secretary had also proposed an appetizer of male raccoon roadkill, before catching himself: “But we drew the line at that, Cheryl. We said, ‘No, we’re not going to do that.’” This is, this office must note with some reluctance, the single moment in the entire transcript where the actual mechanics of a joke are present and functioning — a second, more absurd frame introduced and then rejected in real time, which is close to textbook incongruity construction. It fails anyway, for a reason the earlier instruments don’t fully capture: nobody in the room can locate “Cheryl,” the material concerns a sitting cabinet secretary’s actual documented history of eating roadkill, and the retraction reads less like a punchline than like a man realizing mid-sentence that the true version of the story is worse than the joke. A structurally sound joke, sabotaged by having a factual floor underneath it. This office notes the phenomenon and moves on, because there is nowhere good for it to go.

5. Exhibit C: The Chandelier, Appraised

Earlier in the same remarks, the Subject paused, unprompted, to discuss the ballroom’s fixtures at length. “It’s a luxury room,” he told the assembled press corps. “See those beautiful chandeliers? I can tell you everything about them, if you’d like to know... I’m looking at them saying, I wonder if I could walk out with those chandeliers, because you can’t buy them. They’re very special.” He then specified, unprompted, that they were Czech crystal.

Applying the Superiority Instrument first: no target is identified, and therefore no one in the room is invited to feel superior to anyone — the material has nowhere to land except back on the speaker himself, admiring his own surroundings. The Incongruity Instrument fares better on paper: a sitting president, at a state dinner, in a room he formerly owned and sold, audibly pricing out the light fixtures in front of the people covering his administration. That is a real collision of frames — office and appraisal, statesmanship and estate sale — and this office credits the Subject with having, again, located one. He did not, however, do anything further with it. There is no turn, no acknowledgment that the moment is strange, only a straight continuation into the fact of the crystal’s origin. The Relief Instrument confirms the pattern established elsewhere in this report: the anxiety on offer here — that the man now occupying the White House still relates to formal settings primarily as inventory — is stated, not defused. The room received a guided tour of the fixtures. Nobody present, this office notes, had asked for one.

6. Exhibit D: The Stairs, Self-Graded

Of particular interest to this office is the Subject’s own critical judgment. Describing a senator’s texted domestic dilemma — summarized by the Subject as being told to “come up the stairs” and also asked to perform an unrelated task, the two apparently incompatible — the Subject delivered the line and then immediately reviewed it for the room: “That was actually the only thing I thought was good in this whole freaking stupid speech that they wrote. That was the only good one, and that went down with not exactly great laughter.”

This is, this office submits, the most valuable specimen in the collection, because it is the Subject’s own peer review of his own material, delivered in real time, and it is wrong. He identified the one joke in the batch with genuine incongruous structure — two incompatible instructions, one body, a real collision — correctly diagnosed it as his best line, and watched it fail regardless. The finding here is not that the Subject cannot recognize a well-built joke. He can, evidently, at least once per hour. The finding is that recognition and delivery are separate systems, and only one of them is operational. He can grade the homework. He cannot do it.

7. Exhibit E: The Fourth Term, Unresolved

Late in the address, the Subject announced “my intention to run for a fourth term as president of the United States,” a claim that, delivered by anyone else in any other register, would constitute a five-alarm constitutional event. Here it was delivered inside the same rhythm as the beef tenderloin and the raccoon — stated, not built, no visible frame separating “bit” from “plan.” This office applies the Relief Instrument and finds it does not resolve: the audience’s anxiety on the subject of term limits is not discharged by the line, because the line does not signal, at any point, whether it is a joke. A functioning joke about an autocrat’s ambitions requires the speaker to demonstrate, through construction, that he knows the idea is absurd. The Subject’s delivery demonstrates the opposite — that he has not yet decided. This is the specimen this office finds least funny and most worth flagging, since the same humourlessness diagnosed on comedic grounds throughout this report is, applied to this particular sentence, no longer a matter of comedy at all.

8. Findings — General Output

8.1 Superiority. The Subject relies on this mode almost exclusively and appears to believe it constitutes humour on its own. It requires a target who visibly loses something in front of a room that recognizes the loss. What the Subject produces instead is closer to a demonstration of rank.

8.2 Incongruity. Now confirmed present in the Subject’s toolkit as a rare capability (see Exhibits B, C, and D) but absent as a deployed one. He can build the frame. He cannot, except by his own admission, land it.

8.3 Relief. Confirmed unresolved across the full transcript. Material intended to discharge anxiety — about cabinet appointees, about term limits, about the state of the free press, about the man’s own relationship to the rooms he occupies — instead restates the anxiety at full weight and hands it back.

9. Cross-Reference: The Obama Precedent

Included for completeness: the events of April 30, 2011, in which a considerably funnier sitting president dismantled the Subject at the same dinner, to sustained laughter from a room including the Subject’s own allies. The Subject, notably, brought this evening up himself in the July 24 remarks, unprompted, insisting at length that it was not the moment that radicalized his political ambitions. This office takes no position on causation and simply logs the fact that a man secure in this belief does not usually need to argue it for four consecutive minutes.

10. Recommendations

None. This is not that kind of report. The Subject is not a policy failure awaiting correction; he is a closed system. This office’s function is diagnostic only: to note that a ninety-minute specimen collection produced exactly one line with functioning comedic architecture, that the Subject correctly identified it as such, and that it failed to land regardless — while a second-term constitutional provocation was delivered, unmarked, in the identical register as a joke about a cow.

11. Conclusion

The Subject remains unable to be reliably funny on purpose. Occasionally, rarely, he can identify what funny would require. He has simply never once been able to supply it — and the room, across ninety minutes and five specimens, laughed anyway, because laughing was cheaper than the alternative.

Status of file: Open. Further specimens anticipated, indefinitely, until the term limits question resolves one way or the other.
