MEMORANDUM — CONFIDENTIAL

FROM: Physician for the Office Of the President TO: President Trump RE: Cardiac Risk Assessment Following Recent Golf Course Incident, September 2, 2026 CLASSIFICATION: Executive Privilege / Do Not Leak (Again)

Mr. President,

It has come to this office’s attention that footage circulating online shows you engaged in what witnesses describe as a “vigorous shove” of a foreign head of government down a grassy embankment near the seventeenth hole. This office wishes to commend your enthusiasm while also, in the strongest possible terms, discouraging its repetition.

We understand the provocation. Prime Minister Carney’s short game has apparently become a subject of some irritation, particularly the third mulligan on the fourteenth, and this office does not dispute that a man in your position is entitled to feel strongly about a stroke count that seems to fluctuate with the weather, the wind, and the presence of photographers. We simply note, as your physicians, that sudden explosive exertion of this kind — the torque required to propel a sitting head of state several feet down a slope into a lake that shall not be named— places real and measurable strain on a cardiovascular system that has, in prior physicals, been described using words like “stable” and “manageable” rather than words like “excellent” or “athletic.”

We would be remiss not to mention the cholesterol panel from your most recent physical, which this office continues to describe, generously, as “a number.” We are not going to repeat the number in a memorandum that might someday be subject to a Freedom of Information request. We will simply say that a diet built substantially around the tasting menu at your own golf clubs, combined with a preferred mode of golf-course transport that involves sitting in a cart for eighteen holes and occasionally standing up to hit a ball roughly 40% of the distance a ball ought to travel, is not, strictly speaking, cardiovascular conditioning. It is closer to a Sunday drive with intermittent light exercise, and this office has advised as much for several years now, largely to no effect.

We raise this not to lecture but to flag a specific risk: shoving a Canadian prime minister down a hill is, from a purely biomechanical standpoint, an anaerobic event. Anaerobic events elevate heart rate and blood pressure rapidly and without warning, which is precisely the combination this office has spent three years trying to keep you away from. We would ask, gently, that future displeasure with foreign leaders’ short games be expressed through the traditional and considerably safer medium of a strongly worded social media post.

We remain, as ever, at your service and, increasingly, on standby.
