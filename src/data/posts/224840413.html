Every good opera house hands you a programme on the way in. It tells you who is singing, how many acts there are, and roughly when you can expect the interval. This is that programme.

The Opera Cycle began, without anyone calling it that, on November 8, 2017, when Xi Jinping closed the Forbidden City to tourists, walked Donald Trump through it himself, and sat him down inside the palace walls to watch Chinese opera. Nine years later the two men are still performing for each other. Each summit has been staged with the same care: venues chosen for their symbolism, pageantry rehearsed for weeks, and readouts written for two different audiences. The music has been magnificent. The plot has barely moved.

The cycle now runs to four operas. The Beijing Opera followed Trump’s state visit to China in May 2026, the first by an American president in nearly a decade. The Washington Opera followed Xi’s return visit in September, with its ballroom tour, its bald eagle and its truce extended to January 10, 2027. The Shenzhen Opera follows the two men to the APEC summit in November, where for the first time Xi is host to the whole region and Trump arrives weakened by the midterms. The Miami Opera will follow them to the G20 in December, with Trump as host and the truce deadline weeks away.

Each opera stands on its own, but they are written to be read in sequence. The same scorecard runs through all of them: TACO, for Threaten, Accommodate, Capitulate and Obfuscate, the four moves that have defined Trump’s dealings with Beijing. So does the same question. A summit is a performance, and every performance has an author. In each act, the reader is invited to ask who wrote the libretto, and who simply enjoyed the show.
