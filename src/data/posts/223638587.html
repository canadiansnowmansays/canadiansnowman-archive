OFFICE OF RETROACTIVE JUSTIFICATION Division of Contingency Planning CONTINUITY OF OPERATIONS PLAN No. OJR-2026-1028 Subject: Post-Election Arrangements for Senior Political Staff Classification: Do Not Forward to the Candidates Date: 28 October 2026

1. Purpose

On 28 October 2026, more than two dozen of the President’s senior strategists joined a private video call to review the final week of the midterm campaign. Four participants later described it to The New York Times. The call ran for roughly 30 minutes on what the Times called gallows humour: funeral arrangements, prison food, presidential pardons and a brief discussion of leaving the country.

This Office does not regard any of that as humour. It regards it as a requirements-gathering session. This Plan sets out the resulting procedures, so that each staff member knows exactly what to do in the event that the electorate proceeds as forecast.

2. Situation Assessment

The call opened with the President’s chief pollster asking the group: “Do you want the white pill first, or the black pill first?”

This Office has standardised the two outlooks as follows:

The White Pill (optimistic scenario). The $400 million super PAC is reaching the voters it intended to reach, and those voters turn out as expected. Probability: as presented.

The Black Pill (other scenario). A wipeout.

All staff are reminded that the pills are to be taken in the order presented, and that the White Pill is not a substitute for the Black Pill, merely a short delay before it.

3. Personal Arrangements

The Director of Midterm Strategy advised the call: “I’m getting my affairs in order. I suggest everybody else do the same.”

In support of this guidance, all staff are to complete the following checklist by 3 November:

  • [ ] Will updated.

  • [ ] Funeral preferences recorded (hymns; whether the President should be invited to speak; whether he will speak about the Reflecting Pool if invited).

  • [ ] Dietary requirements submitted to the Federal Bureau of Prisons, as a precaution.

  • [ ] Pardon request filed under Schedule P (Preemptive). Applicants are advised that the pardon queue is currently long and is processed in order of donation size.

  • [ ] Passport located.

4. Relocation Options

The call reviewed possible destinations for staff wishing to continue their careers abroad.

The Director of Midterm Strategy observed: “At least Bibi won. He’ll protect us.” The Office notes that the remark was sarcastic, and that the Israeli Prime Minister’s bloc had in fact fallen short of a majority the day before. His party subsequently claimed that its election had been stolen. The Office regards this as evidence of a strong cultural fit.

5. Recount Protocol

The Director of Midterm Strategy proposed the following contingency, citing a Trump ally in Minnesota who had demanded a recount of a primary he lost in a landslide: “I think we’ll just pull a Mike Lindell and demand a recount regardless of how many points we’re down.”

This Office has adopted the proposal as the Lindell Standard. Under it, a recount shall be requested in any race lost by between 0.1 and 100 points. Races lost by more than 100 points will be referred to the Office of Retroactive Justification for reclassification as wins.

Exception: Mississippi. The President’s advisers referred to the state on another call, mockingly, as “Battleground Mississippi.” Staff are advised that Mississippi has no legal provision for statewide recounts, and that the mockery should therefore be discontinued until 13 November, when counties certify.

6. Financial Arrangements

The President raised a war chest of approximately $400 million, which he has described to advisers as “my money.” Approval to spend it was withheld until the end of the summer. Staff are reminded that television stations raise their prices as an election approaches.

The Office finds that the President’s super PAC paid a premium of $27,300 for the privilege of waiting. This is consistent with the President’s general approach to timing, and no further action is required.

7. Root-Cause Analysis

On a call a week earlier, the Director of Midterm Strategy reviewed the party’s candidates and offered a finding: “I have seen the enemy, and it is us.”

The chief pollster added: “In many places.”

This Office has examined the finding and cannot support it. The Office’s own review, informed by the President, identifies the following causes of the result:

  1. The President was not on the ballot.

  2. Had the President been on the ballot, the party would have won in a landslide.

  3. The candidates were poor, except where the President chose them, in which case the voters were poor.

  4. Candidate quality matters, and the President did a better job promoting many of the candidates than the candidates did themselves. (Source: a White House official, speaking anonymously.)

  5. As the President told aides during the campaign: “It says something very nice about me that they just can’t win without me.”

The Office notes that items 1 and 5 together establish that the party lost because it was without the President, and also that the President’s importance is proved by the party losing. The Office has filed this under “Win-Win.”

8. Official Response

Asked about the call, a spokesman for the President’s super PAC said that the people describing it were peddling “made up quotes and false gossip,” and added: “They are cowards and losers.”

The Office notes that the call had more than two dozen participants, and that four of them described it. The Office has therefore opened a Continuity of Operations Plan for the call itself.

9. Final Instruction

Following the result of 3 November, staff are instructed to proceed to section 3. Staff who completed the checklist early are thanked for their foresight and reminded that foresight, in this administration, is not a promotable quality.

The White Pill is hereby withdrawn.

Director, Division of Contingency Planning (Acting. Affairs in order.)
