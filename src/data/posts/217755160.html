Good evening and welcome to the Sunday recap — a one-email look back at the week, alongside today’s lighter satire posts.

A warm welcome to new subscribers. The idea is simple: one categorized recap every Sunday. Start with the topic that brought you here, then browse — you’ll find the same voice throughout every series.

This was a week with two big stories unfolding at once, and I launched a new series for each. If you read anything this week, read these two.

The Washington Opera — the sequel to The Beijing Opera — was published start to finish this week. It follows Xi Jinping’s state visit to Washington from the tarmac greeting to the state dinner to tea in the Red Room, by way of a press-pool boycott, a second bald eagle and a ballroom that is still a construction site. It closed today with a short coda, and it reads best in order.

UN: The Parliament of Man traces the story from the League of Nations to today’s Security Council paralysis. This one is personal: my great-uncle Art was gassed in the First World War, and another great-uncle, Hugh Keenleyside, ran the UN’s Technical Assistance Administration in the 1950s. Alongside the history, I’ve been filing “This Week in New York” dispatches from the General Assembly as it happened. The final dispatch posts tomorrow night or Tuesday morning, once the general debate closes; after that, the rest of the series will post on a more leisurely weekly cadence.

A week of listening to speeches from the UN rostrum, together with a long conversation with Rikesh, prompted yet another new series — Nation Bullies — built on a simple premise: plenty of great powers have played the bully at some point in history, not just today’s United States. It will begin publishing in the coming weeks.

For Canadian readers, I also launched The Sovereign Country this week, a companion series to The Great Canadian Trade War. The title comes from Carney’s own words to reporters — “it means we’re a sovereign country.” It opens with his appearance in New York, where the “rupture” language from Davos now names China as well as the US, then tests his case for Canadian self-sufficiency against the actual numbers. More to come.

I’ve now finished closing out the Greenland series with the final epilogues — what was warned, and what actually happened. A series that began with invasion talk in January ended with a security agreement in September. Not the ending anyone predicted.

A reminder on Your Phone Bill and Mine: the CRTC hearing is November 30 in Gatineau, and I’ll provide a summary in December.

US politics keeps heating up with the midterms around the corner. What happens south of the border affects all of us, and I’ll keep trying to make sense of it.

Now, for the rest of the week, enjoy.
