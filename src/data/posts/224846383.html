COVER NOTE

The attached document is the final text of the U.S.–China Framework for Constructive Strategic Stability, as agreed on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen on November 19, 2026.

It was a longer document when negotiations began. The Office has preserved each clause in the form in which it was removed, together with the note of the party who removed it, so that historians will be able to see exactly how much was agreed, and by whom, and how quickly.

The Office wishes to acknowledge its debt to the 1935 film A Night at the Opera, in which Groucho and Chico Marx negotiate a contract by tearing off every clause either of them objects to. The Office had assumed the scene was a comedy. It has since been advised that it was a training film.

U.S.–CHINA FRAMEWORK FOR CONSTRUCTIVE STRATEGIC STABILITY

(Shenzhen Text)

ARTICLE 1. THE PARTIES

1.1 The Party of the First Part shall be known in this Framework as the Party of the First Part.

1.2 The Party of the Second Part shall be known in this Framework as the Party of the Second Part, and shall also be known as the host, the chair, the owner of the room and the person who decides when the cameras come in.

1.3 Any Party of the Third Part who arrives during the negotiations shall be seated at the end of the table, offered tea, and described in both readouts as a “constructive presence.”

Note (Party of the First Part): “Why can’t the first part be the second part?”
Note (Party of the Second Part): “It is not necessary. The first part has never been the first part.”

ARTICLE 2. AGRICULTURAL PURCHASES

2.1 The Party of the Second Part shall purchase American soybeans in the quantities promised at Busan in October 2025, reaffirmed in Beijing in May 2026, reaffirmed again in Washington in September 2026, and delivered at a rate the Party of the First Part describes as “tremendous.”

Torn off by the Party of the Second Part. Note: “We have already promised this three times. A fourth promise would devalue the first three.”

2.2 The Parties note with satisfaction that soybeans exist.

2.3 The Party of the First Part informs the Party of the Second Part that it has “an insatiable appetite,” and that “we can fill that appetite.”

Note (Office of Retroactive Justification): By September the appetite had been satisfied with about $3.9 billion of the $17 billion a year promised, not counting soybeans, which are counted elsewhere, by someone.

ARTICLE 3. RARE EARTHS

3.1 The Party of the Second Part shall continue to issue general licences for the export of rare-earth elements, without interruption, delay, inspection, inexplicable paperwork or sudden reconsideration.

Torn off by the Party of the Second Part. Note: “This clause contains the word ‘without’. We do not sign clauses containing the word ‘without’.”

3.2 Rare earths shall continue to be rare.

ARTICLE 4. ADVANCED SEMICONDUCTORS

4.1 The Party of the First Part shall maintain export controls on advanced chips for reasons of national security.

Torn off by the Party of the First Part. Note: “Jensen asked.”

4.2 The Party of the First Part shall relax export controls on advanced chips in exchange for a share of the revenue.

Torn off by the Party of the First Part, on the advice of counsel, who pointed out that a new Congress takes office in January.

4.3 The torn-off clauses were presented to the chief executive of a prominent chipmaker as souvenirs. He accepted both.

4.4 Each Party acknowledges that the other spies on it.

Note (Party of the First Part): “We spy on them too.”
Note (Office of Retroactive Justification): A reporter who asked whether the former chief executive had been disloyal was removed from the room. The question was not answered, and was not removed.

ARTICLE 5. TAIWAN

5.1 [This clause intentionally left blank.]

Note (Party of the Second Part): “Acceptable.”
Note (Party of the First Part): “I don’t talk about those things.”
Note (Ricardo Baroni, seated behind a pillar): “Does anyone need anything typed?”

5.2 The Party of the First Part confirms that a $14 billion arms package for a party that is not a party to this Framework remains in a drawer, where it is described as a negotiating chip. The Party of the Second Part confirms that it is aware of the drawer.

ARTICLE 6. FENTANYL

6.1 The Parties reaffirm their commitment to cooperate on fentanyl.

Note (Office of Retroactive Justification): This clause was retained from the Busan text, the Beijing text and the Washington text. It is the only clause in the Framework that has never been torn off. It is also the only clause that has never been measured.

ARTICLE 7. IRAN

7.1 The Party of the First Part, being at war with the Islamic Republic of Iran, requests that the Party of the Second Part reduce its purchases of Iranian oil.

Torn off by mutual consent. Note (Party of the First Part): "We don't need any help." Note (Office of Retroactive Justification): American intelligence has concluded that the Party of the Second Part and the Party of the Third Part have already helped, in the war, the other side.

ARTICLE 8. UKRAINE

8.1 The Parties call on the Party of the Third Part to agree to a ceasefire.

Torn off by the Party of the Third Part, who was not a party to this Framework until he sat down, and who tore off the clause with the confidence of a man who has done this before.

8.2 The Parties welcome the Party of the Third Part’s interest in peace, and note that he is a humanitarian.

8.3 The three-way meeting took place, for 80 minutes, at a lakeside villa, around a round wooden table, so that no Party could be seated at its end. The Party of the Third Part then held the news conference the Party of the First Part did not, confirmed that he would negotiate no limited ceasefires, asked whether democracy was not really "a liberal dictatorship, if you will," and, asked about a new Yalta, wondered aloud what had been so bad about the old one.

8.4 The Party of the First Part reports that "things are happening."
Note (Prime Minister of Poland, on hearing the Party of the First Part greet the Party of the Third Part as "my friend"): "God, protect us from such friends."
Note (Office of Retroactive Justification): The request has been forwarded to the appropriate jurisdiction, which has not ratified this Framework either.

ARTICLE 9. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

9.1 The Parties agree to continue the U.S.–China dialogue on artificial intelligence, established in New York in September 2026, for the purpose of continuing the U.S.–China dialogue on artificial intelligence.

9.2 Each Party shall notify the other before deploying a system it has already deployed.

9.3 At the three-way meeting, artificial intelligence was not mentioned by any Party. The Office records this as full compliance with Article 9.

ARTICLE 10. DURATION

10.1 This Framework shall remain in force until January 10, 2027, or until the Parties meet in Miami, whichever produces the better photograph.

ARTICLE 11. THE SANITY CLAUSE

11.1 Each Party certifies that the commitments in this Framework are measurable, enforceable, consistent with its previous commitments, and not contradicted by its own leader within forty-eight hours of signature.

Rejected by the Party of the First Part. Note: “Nobody told me about a Sanity Clause.”
Rejected by the Party of the Second Part. Note: “We do not recognise the jurisdiction of sanity.”
Rejected by the Party of the Third Part. Note, in the margin, in Fiorello’s handwriting: “There ain’t no Sanity Claus.”

Addendum, from the published record.

The three-way meeting was held the next day, not in Room 2B but at Kylin Villa, around a round wooden table. Rated capacity: three presidents and their interpreters. Nobody exited horizontally.

Before his own bilateral, the Party of the First Part patted the Party of the Third Part’s spokesman on the arm and told him, “You’re doing a great job.” The White House press secretary’s post is vacant.

A reporter who shouted a question about the chipmaker was barred from the room. The question was judged to exceed its rated capacity.

The social-media proprietor was confirmed to be in the country. His phone was not confiscated. He used it to join a New York conference by video.

The official photograph released by the White House showed the Party of the First Part helping the Party of the Second Part up an escalator. It was the White House’s only readout.

The Party of the Second Part sent a deputy to the airport to meet the Party of the Third Part, and seated him at a separate table at dinner. The Party of the First Part was met on the tarmac in person. On his return home he described this as the high point of the visit, and explained that it was not he who had been met, but “our country.”

Back in Washington the next day, the Party of the First Part held the news conference he had not held in Shenzhen. Asked whether the Party of the Third Part was a friend to America, he replied: “I can’t speak to America.” The Office has searched the Framework for anyone else authorised to speak for America and found no one. America has accordingly been seated at the end of the table under Article 1.3, offered tea and described as a constructive presence.

He added that the Party of the Third Part had been “very nice about” the investigation into the Party of the Third Part’s help in an earlier American election, an investigation that “probably helped me win the election—re-win the election.” The Office has filed this under Article 4.4.

NOTE FROM THE OFFICE

The Office has been asked whether the Framework represents progress. It does. In Busan, the two sides agreed to a truce. In Beijing, they agreed to extend the conversation. In Washington, they agreed to extend the truce. In Shenzhen, they have agreed to extend the extension.

In Shenzhen, the two sides also agreed to draw up a plan for a Framework, and the three Parties agreed in a joint statement to reform the United Nations, increase the supply of oil, keep talking about not having a nuclear war, and protect state officials from international courts. Of the four, the Office notes, only the last has an identifiable beneficiary, and he was in the room.

The Party of the First Part has asked the Office to record that “every move was perfectly executed,” and that “it’s too bad the media couldn’t see some of it.” The Office has recorded it, in the absence of the media.

The Office also notes, for the record, that Mrs. Claypool was not consulted at any stage, and has asked when she will be paid.

In the film, the contract scene ends with Groucho and Chico shaking hands over a scrap of paper neither of them has read. Then they go to the opera. The Office understands that this one is booked for December, in Miami, and that the host has promised a bigger theatre.

The Office of Retroactive Justification is fictional. The summit, the truce, the soybeans and the drawer are not.
