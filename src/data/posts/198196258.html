President Trump read from the Bible in a prerecorded video

Stephen Gordon CanadianSnowmanSays May 24, 2026

I should say at the outset that I have the greatest respect for prayer.

I am Canadian, which means I was raised with a constitutional commitment to the quiet management of difficult feelings, and prayer, in my experience, is one of the more effective mechanisms available for this purpose. We do not have a state religion in Canada, which means our national suffering is largely secular and therefore lacks the comfort of an official addressee, but I understand the impulse completely and I wish our American neighbours well in their efforts.

I have been reading the program from the Rededicate America rally held on the National Mall on May 17, 2026 — the day before, I note, that Canadians were lighting fireworks for a dead queen, which is our own form of national invocation, and which I have written about elsewhere.

The rally was billed as a National Jubilee of Prayer, Praise and Thanksgiving. President Trump touted it as an opportunity to “rededicate America as one nation under God.” Thousands came. Pete Hegseth appeared by video. Marco Rubio spoke. Speaker Johnson closed. The President himself, I note, was in Beijing posting about a ballroom.

Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth appeared in a recorded video message early in the day.

I have read the program carefully.

I have also read the week’s economic reports.

What follows is the program as printed, and, in my best reconstruction, the program as prayed.

OPENING: PROCESSIONAL AND WELCOME

As Printed: A celebration of America’s founding as a Christian nation, dedicated to the glory of God and the renewal of the American spirit.

As Prayed: Gas is $4.52 a gallon nationally, up more than 40 percent since February. Grocery prices rose faster in April than any month in nearly four years. Consumer confidence has reached an all-time low. The congregation has driven here from communities across America, and several are calculating, quietly, whether they can afford the drive home.

They have come, in the fullness of their faith, to the National Mall, which is free, and which does not require a tank of gas to enter once you have arrived.

They are grateful for small mercies.

They are praying for larger ones.

FIRST READING: WASHINGTON AT VALLEY FORGE

As Printed: A reflection on President George Washington’s prayer at Valley Forge in the winter of 1777-1778, delivered by Secretary of Defense Peter Hegseth, reminding us that America’s greatest leaders have always turned to God in moments of trial.

As Printed, With Annotation: The story of Washington praying at Valley Forge is, historians note, almost certainly apocryphal. It was popularized in the nineteenth century by Mason Weems, the same biographer who invented the cherry tree story, and has been described by scholars as “a pious legend.” This has not diminished its power as a touchstone, which tells us something interesting about the relationship between historical accuracy and national mythology, though this is perhaps not the moment to pursue that thread.

As Delivered: By video. Pete Hegseth was not present at the rally in person. The Secretary of Defense, who has invoked divine purpose to justify American military power and prayed to “King Jesus” at a White House dinner, delivered his exhortation to “pray without ceasing” from an undisclosed location, via recorded message.

As Prayed: The congregation prayed for the Secretary of Defense, who is managing an active war with Iran, overseeing a military whose counterterrorism strategy was produced because a journalist asked where it was, and who uses a bottle of bourbon as his professional calling card.

The congregation is not unsympathetic.

The congregation is also paying $4.52 a gallon.

The congregation prayed that someone in the building is paying attention to both of these things.¹

¹ The congregation noted that Washington’s army at Valley Forge was cold, underfed, and short of supplies, and that their commander nevertheless maintained his faith and his purpose. Several congregants found this more relatable than they had expected. Several did not say this out loud. This is what prayer is for.

SECOND READING: THE PROSPERITY OF NATIONS

As Printed: A prayer of Thanksgiving for the strength of the American economy, the resilience of the American worker, and the blessings of a nation favoured by Providence.

As Prayed: The president said, on Tuesday of this week, that he does not think about Americans’ financial situation.

He was asked whether the economic hardship Americans are feeling from the war with Iran might motivate him to seek a deal.

“Not even a little bit,” he said.

“I don’t think about Americans’ financial situation,” he said.

He doubled down on Fox News: “That’s a perfect statement. I’d make it again.”

The congregation, which thinks about Americans’ financial situation constantly, because it is their financial situation, received this news in the spirit in which it was offered.

James Carville, who is not a theologian but has spent considerable time thinking about how politicians communicate with voters, described the statement as “the greatest admission against interest in the history of the presidency.”

The congregation prayed for lower gas prices.

The congregation prayed for lower grocery prices.

The congregation prayed, in the way that congregations sometimes do when they are not quite sure whom they are addressing, that someone, somewhere, in some position of authority, was thinking about their financial situation, even a little bit.²

² A CNN poll released this week found that 77 percent of Americans, including a majority of Republicans, believe Mr. Trump’s policies have increased the cost of living in their communities. The poll did not ask whether they believed prayer would help. This is possibly an oversight.

MUSICAL INTERLUDE: GOD BLESS AMERICA

As Printed: Performed in honour of America’s 250th anniversary, reminding us that this nation was built by immigrants of faith who believed in its promise.

As Noted: Rabbi Meir Soloveichik, the only Jewish leader on the program, took the stage to tell the story of Irving Berlin, a Jewish immigrant from Russia, who wrote “God Bless America.” He said it was a reminder that antisemitism is “utterly un-American.”

The next speaker, Senator Tim Scott, opened by asking: “How many of you love Jesus?”

The list of speakers included no Muslims.

The first Muslims arrived in what is now the United States before the nation’s founding, via the trans-Atlantic slave trade.

As Prayed: The congregation prayed for the America that Irving Berlin believed in, which was large enough to contain all of them and also him, and which did not require a specific answer to Senator Scott’s question in order to participate.

Some of the congregation prayed for this more urgently than others.³

³ A professor at Michigan State noted that the event “sharpens the distinction between people who belong and people who don’t belong.” The Cardinal Archbishop of Washington said language describing America as a Christian nation “can be destructive,” particularly when used to centre culture around the exclusion of certain groups. Some 80 years ago, he noted, Catholics were also excluded. “They wouldn’t say, this is a Christian nation,” he said. “They’d say, it is a Protestant nation.” The congregation did not appear to discuss this. The congregation was singing.

THIRD READING: THE SEPARATION OF CHURCH AND STATE

House Speaker Mike Johnson described events in American history that he said showed God’s hand

As Printed: Speaker Johnson will address the foundational relationship between faith and governance, reminding us that the founders intended to protect the church from the state, not the other way around.

As Noted by a Pulitzer Prize-Winning Historian: Joseph Ellis, author of many books on America’s founding, described the idea that the founders saw America as an explicitly Christian nation as “nonsensical” and “dead wrong.” He said the founders were explicitly pushing back against the medieval assumption that states needed a common religious preference to unite people.

“It is a falsification of the meaning of the American Revolution,” he said.

As Prayed: The congregation prayed about this for varying lengths of time, depending on what they had come to the Mall to pray about, which varied.

Several had come to pray about gas prices.

THE PRESIDENTIAL MESSAGE


As Printed: A video message from President Trump, rededicating America to its founding values and expressing his gratitude to the faithful who have supported his mission.

As Delivered: The President was in Beijing.

He had attended a state banquet where the menu included lobster in tomato soup and roast duck.

He had posted extensively on Truth Social about his grand welcome, the size of the Great Hall of the People, and the handsomeness of the welcome ceremony.

On Friday, as he headed home, he posted his primary reflection on the trip.

“China has a Ballroom,” he wrote, “and so should the U.S.A.! It’s under construction, ahead of schedule, and will be the finest facility of its kind anywhere in the U.S.A.”

As Prayed: The congregation prayed for the ballroom.

Several congregants found themselves, in the privacy of their own hearts, wondering whether the $1 billion proposed for the ballroom might have other applications, given that the Senate had just ruled the funding could not be included in the budget bill, and that Americans are saving less, borrowing more, and paying $4.52 a gallon.

The congregation did not say any of this out loud.

This is what prayer is for.⁴

⁴ Eric Trump, the President’s middle son, accompanied him to Beijing. Eric Trump leads the Trump Organization, which has explored Chinese business deals over the years. The nature of any discussions held in the margins of the diplomatic visit has not been disclosed. The congregation prayed about this with varying degrees of specificity.

CABINET REFLECTIONS: WALKING IN FAITH DURING TRIAL

As Printed: Members of the President’s cabinet reflect on how their faith sustains them in service to the American people during this challenging moment.

As Observed This Week:

Sean Duffy, Secretary of Transportation: Appeared in a YouTube series promoting “The Great American Road Trip” at a moment when the average price of gas has soared to $4.52 a gallon nationally. The series has not addressed the cost per gallon. It has addressed the scenery.

Kash Patel, Director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation: Conducted a VIP snorkeling excursion at Pearl Harbor. The excursion was not, as far as can be determined, work-related. Pearl Harbor is a solemn site commemorating an attack that brought America into a world war. It has excellent visibility underwater.

JD Vance, Vice President: When asked whether he agreed that the President does not think about Americans’ financial situation, said: “Well, I don’t think the president said that.” The president had said that, on camera, two days earlier, and had subsequently confirmed it on Fox News as “a perfect statement.”

As Prayed: The congregation prayed for the cabinet.

The congregation prayed with the particular fervour of people who have spent considerable time in recent weeks thinking about their financial situation and would welcome any indication that someone in a position of authority was doing the same.

The congregation prayed that the Great American Road Trip would eventually address fuel costs.

The congregation prayed for better snorkeling conditions at Pearl Harbor, which, in the circumstances, seemed like the least complicated prayer of the day.⁵

⁵ Kevin Hassett, Director of the White House National Economic Council, said on Fox News that GDP could top 6 percent this year. Most private forecasters expect growth at less than half that rate. Pierre Yared, acting chairman of the Council of Economic Advisers, said consumer prices would “go back down” once the war ended. Scott Bessent, the Treasury Secretary, said “substantial disinflation” was coming. Stephen Moore, a conservative economist who has advised the President, said Republicans could face “a tsunami election in November if inflation continues to stay high.” The congregation prayed about the tsunami. The congregation has experience with economic weather events. The congregation voted for the man who promised to fix the last one.

CLOSING PRAYER

As Printed: Speaker Johnson closes the Jubilee with a prayer for national unity, divine guidance, and the continued blessing of God upon this nation.

As Prayed:

The congregation prayed for lower gas prices.

They prayed for lower grocery prices. Tomatoes. Seafood. The things on the list.

They prayed for wages that keep up with prices, and for prices that give wages a chance to keep up.

They prayed for someone in the White House — anyone, really, at this point — to think about their financial situation, even a little bit.

They prayed for the war to end, not because they were against defending America, but because the war is the reason for the gas prices, and the gas prices are the reason several of them are not sure how they are getting home.

They prayed for a ballroom, because the President had asked them to, in a manner of speaking, and they are a forgiving people.

They prayed for democracy, which some of them were beginning to miss in specific and articulable ways.

They prayed for each other, which is the most reliable form of prayer, because at least the addressee is present and the outcome is not dependent on anyone’s financial situation.

And then they got in their cars and they drove home, past the gas stations with the prices posted in giant numbers alongside every highway in America, and they thought about what they had come to pray for and what they had actually prayed for and whether God, in his wisdom, had noted the difference.

I believe he had.

I believe he always does.

I am Canadian. I was watching from the porch.

I was also thinking about the gas prices.⁶

⁶ The average price of regular gasoline in Canada as of May 17, 2026 is approximately $1.89 per litre, which converts to roughly $5.10 per American gallon at current exchange rates, which is higher than the American price, and which I mention only because I believe in complete transparency and also because it explains why Canadians have strong opinions about public transit that Americans sometimes find excessive. We have our own prayers. They are also largely about prices. We light fireworks for a dead queen and hope for the best. This is not so different from what happened on the Mall. This is, in the end, what all of us are doing.

The Rededicate America rally took place on May 17, 2026. The economic statistics, the presidential quotes, the cabinet activities, and the historical notes about Valley Forge, Irving Berlin, and the founders are all real. The Order of Service is reconstructed from press reports. The prayers are the author’s best reconstruction of what 77 percent of Americans, including a majority of Republicans, are currently thinking about.

The soybeans, as always, are confirmed.
