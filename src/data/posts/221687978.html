Here’s the weekly gathering of what I posted on Substack: politics written at length, history taken at a stroll, AI, science and technology for the curious, and a satire or two when the news practically wrote itself. Lately the satire hasn’t asked to be written so much as screamed to be, and some mornings I’d swear Edvard Munch is reading over my shoulder.

The subjects wander; the perspective doesn’t. It’s Canadian, a step outside the room, rooted in the record, and wry in the way Stephen Leacock was wry, on the old principle that the fellow by the door sees more of the party than anyone dancing.

American politics is the centre of gravity: its institutions, its rot, its odd knack for recovery, and the consequences for everyone pulled into its orbit, which is most of us. Around it orbit the other series, on AI governance and energy, foreign affairs, Canada’s trade fight, space, consumer advocacy, and the long story of democracies under strain. Some essays dig. Some explain. The satire is labelled, just in case.

If you’re new, welcome aboard. Start with what brought you here, then wander. It’s the same voice in every series.

Ukraine: The Country That Would Not Be Traded had a full week: what Ukraine lost when it set down its best weapon at Washington’s request, the cards it still holds, and a Ukrainian historian and a Ukrainian father describing their country in their own words. The next essay goes up on Tuesday.
