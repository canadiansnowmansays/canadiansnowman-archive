Wayne Ellerby had been thinking about the Presidential Package for three years before he launched it.

He approached it the way he approaches all his products: systematically, with reference to comparable offerings in analogous markets, with a spreadsheet, and with the particular patience of a man who knows that the right moment for a premium tier is not the moment when you think of it but the moment when the audience is ready. You do not launch the Gold Level Literary River Tour until the standard Literary River Tour has established the baseline. You do not introduce the Presidential Package until you understand who, precisely, the presidency of the package is for.

He launched it in June, at a price point he described in the Elsinore Tourism and Culture Committee meeting as “commensurate with the experience offered,” which was not the price, which he did not disclose, and which Gerald noted in a sub-folder marked Elsinore Heritage Tours — Presidential Package — Pricing — Not Disclosed.

Wayne met them at the municipal dock at seven in the evening in a jacket he had been keeping for occasions that warranted it. He had judged the Presidential Package to be such an occasion.

The Presidential Package offered the following, as described in the brochure Wayne produced, which was printed on heavier card stock than the standard Literary River Tour materials and used a slightly different shade of green:

A private Literary River Tour on the Avon at a time of the client’s choosing, outside standard tour hours, in the client’s own paddle boat — reserved, not shared. A private guided walk of the Bieber Heritage Route with commentary not available in the standard package, the nature of which Wayne described as “contextual enrichment.” A reserved table at the Avon Table for dinner, with a curated menu that Avon Table’s owner had agreed to prepare given sufficient advance notice, the curation having been developed by Wayne in consultation with the restaurant and described in the brochure as “a culinary expression of Perth County’s agricultural identity.” A personal encounter, subject to availability, with Wayne himself, who would spend thirty minutes with the client discussing Elsinore’s full cultural portfolio and answering questions. This was listed fourth in the brochure. Wayne considered it the strongest element of the package.

And, at the conclusion of the evening, a private photograph outside the Festival Theatre, taken by Wayne’s assistant — who was Wayne, with a tripod — in front of the marquee reading THIS ROUGH MAGIC — 2026 SEASON, which Wayne had assessed as a sufficiently distinguished backdrop.

The package was available to a maximum of four clients per month. Wayne had bookings through October.

The first Presidential Package client was a technology entrepreneur from Seoul whose company had been following the paddle boat photograph since the previous July and who had, in the intervening months, booked two standard Literary River Tours, the Gold Level Literary River Tour, and the Bieber Heritage Walk twice. He arrived in Elsinore with his wife and two colleagues. Wayne met them at the municipal dock at seven in the evening in a pressed jacket that was not his usual jacket — a slightly better one he had been keeping for occasions that warranted it, which he had judged the Presidential Package to be.

The private river tour proceeded without incident. The Avon was at its July best — green and slow, the evening light on the water doing the thing it does in July that makes people who see it want to come back. The client photographed the south bank, the willow trees, the Elizabethan Gardens. He asked Wayne about the Swan Warden. Wayne gave him three minutes on the Swan Warden’s role, her morning surveys, and the habitat preservation work, which was accurate and which the client found, in Wayne’s assessment, deeply affecting. One of the client’s colleagues photographed a swan. The swan did not object. The swan proceeded.

At Waypoint 3, the Avon Narrows, Wayne explained the paddle boat incident. He had a copy of Carly’s original Beacon piece, laminated, which he had added to the Presidential Package materials because he had assessed it as a document of cultural significance. The client held it carefully. He photographed it. He sent it to someone.

The Bieber Heritage Walk proceeded in the evening light along the route Wayne had established, which incorporated the relevant biographical landmarks, the proposed installation site — which the client photographed standing next to the PROPOSED INSTALLATION SITE marker with evident satisfaction, the sign now having achieved a kind of celebrity of its own following the photograph in which the swans occupied the bank behind it — and a moment of unscripted significance when Wayne pointed out that the Tim Hortons at the north end of Ontario Street had been, in a previous incarnation, a coffee establishment that the young Justin Bieber may or may not have frequented, which Wayne acknowledged was unverified but which he described as “within the reasonable bounds of biographical probability,” which the client received with the generosity of a man who has paid for a premium experience and intends to receive one.

The dinner at the Avon Table was excellent because the Avon Table is excellent, the curation notwithstanding. The owner had prepared a menu that featured corn from a Perth County farm the client’s colleagues described as the finest corn they had ever eaten, which it may have been, corn being a thing Perth County does with the quiet excellence of a region that has been doing it for a hundred and fifty years and does not require acknowledgment.

The corn is from Perth County. It is excellent. It has always been excellent. It does not carry anyone’s image and it does not need to.

The Presidential photograph outside the Festival Theatre was taken at dusk, with the marquee lit, in the specific quality of light that Elsinore gets at the end of a July day when the heat has lifted and the river is silver and the theatre is exactly what it is: a building that has been full of people for seventy years, lit up in the evening, on a street in Ontario that is exactly the right size for what it contains.

Wayne looked at the photograph on his phone. He showed it to the client. The client sent it to someone.

Wayne drove home. He updated his spreadsheet. He had six more Presidential Package bookings.

Alistair became aware of the Presidential Package in the way Alistair becomes aware of most things Wayne does: indirectly, at some delay, through a reference in the Beacon and a subsequent conversation in the Festival Theatre parking lot.

The conversation was four minutes long, which was brief for Alistair in a parking lot.

He said he was not opposed, in principle, to Elsinore’s cultural assets being offered in curated form to interested visitors from international markets. He said the river tour was Wayne’s operation and he respected the enterprise. He said the dinner sounded genuinely good.

He asked about the photograph outside the theatre.

Wayne said the clients had responded very positively to the theatre as a setting and that the marquee provided an appropriate sense of occasion.

Alistair said he noticed the package was called the Presidential Package.

Wayne said the name conveyed the premium nature of the offering.

Alistair said he saw.

He went back inside. He had a rehearsal.

In the parking lot, Wayne updated a note in his phone. He had been considering whether to rename the package. He had not decided. He filed the consideration under things to revisit in October, after the season, when the numbers were in.

Gerald filed the brochure. He filed it under Elsinore Heritage Tours — Presidential Package — Brochure — June 2026. He filed the Beacon piece about the launch under Elsinore Heritage Tours — Presidential Package — Press Coverage. He filed the Tourism and Culture Committee minutes from the meeting at which the package had been presented under Subcommittee — Tourism and Culture — 2026 — Presidential Package — Pricing Not Disclosed.

He noted, in his personal meeting notes from the committee meeting, that the pricing had not been disclosed. He noted the committee had not asked for it. He noted that he had not asked for it either, because he had not been asked to ask, and because the pricing of private tour packages was, strictly speaking, Wayne’s business.

He did not file the six subsequent booking confirmations, because he did not have them, because Wayne had not given them to him, because Wayne did not give Gerald his booking confirmations as a general practice, because that was not how their relationship worked.

Gerald noted, in his personal notes, that the Presidential Package appeared to be doing well.

He filed the note.

Jim walked the labrador past the municipal dock on a Thursday morning in August and saw Wayne on the dock at seven-fifteen, meeting what appeared to be a Presidential Package party — four people in well-pressed clothes, one carrying a camera of the kind that costs more than a paddle boat — with the smooth welcoming energy of a man who has done this before and intends to do it again.

Jim walked the labrador home. He walked the vizsla. He walked the three dogs from Festival Park Heights. He sat with his coffee. He played four hands of online poker, which went adequately.

He mentioned the Presidential Package to Maggie at Sunday dinner.

Maggie said Debi had seen the brochure. She said the corn sounded good. She said she and Debi had been meaning to go to the Avon Table.

Jim passed the bread.

He said he thought Wayne had found his niche.

Maggie said she thought so too.

The boys ate their dinner.

A Note for the Record

The premium experience is not new. There have always been people willing to pay more for access — to the table with the better view, to the earlier appointment, to the version of the thing that includes the photograph with the famous backdrop.

What is new, or newer, is the particular form the premium experience has taken to the south, where the former and current president of the United States sells gold-plated coins bearing his image, Bibles signed with his name, high-top sneakers in his preferred colour palette, and non-fungible tokens representing digital trading cards on which he is depicted in various heroic poses. The premium experience, in this version, is not a paddle boat tour on the Avon with a laminated route map and a dinner featuring Perth County corn. It is access, at various price points, to the president himself — his image, his endorsement, his proximity. It is the presidential brand as product, sold from the presidency, to people who may or may not understand the difference between the office and the man, or whether the distinction is being maintained.

Wayne’s Presidential Package offers a private river tour, a curated dinner, a heritage walk, and a photograph outside a theatre. The theatre is not named after Wayne. The package is not endorsed by any government. The pricing, while undisclosed to the Tourism and Culture Committee, does not include access to municipal decision-making processes. The photograph outside the Festival Theatre features a marquee that says THIS ROUGH MAGIC — 2026 SEASON, not Wayne’s name, not any name except Shakespeare’s, and Shakespeare has been in the public domain since 1616 and cannot be said to have endorsed the experience.

The corn is from Perth County. It is excellent.

It has always been excellent. It does not carry anyone’s image and it does not need to.

Wayne drives home and updates his spreadsheet. The river runs through the evening. The swans proceed, unbothered and unbranded, on the water that belongs to no one.

This is either the Gold Level experience or the experience itself, and in Elsinore these have always been considered the same thing.
